# Compositional Semantic Synthesis: Surah 112

**قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ / ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ / لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ / وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ** begins by assigning a human voice and ends by withholding a relational status from every candidate. Between those boundaries, two nominal predications name God, two coordinated verbs reverse the direction of birth, and a final copular clause rotates the grammatical subject away from God to the delayed **أَحَدٌۢ**. The passage is therefore not a list of attributes followed by absences. It moves from commanded declaration to self-sufficient refuge, from refuge to the denial of two genealogical directions, and from those denials to a comparison relation that no one ever occupies. The last word then returns the form of the first predicate in a different role, sending the whole movement back into **قُلْ**.

## The command makes nonoccurrence audible

The ق و ل field distinguishes a saying before and after exposure. It calls inwardly formed content **في نفسي قول لم أظهره**, “in my soul a saying I did not show” (ق و ل/B012); articulated speech is **المركب من الحروف المبرز بالنطق**, a composition of letters brought out in pronunciation (B001), and **المقول** can name the tongue (B002). Surface **قُلْ** is a 2MS imperative addressed to the Prophet. The personal issuer is not overtly named in the sentence. The imperative supplies pressure to cross from content into articulation and an exact quoted boundary: **قُلْ** remains outside, while **هُوَ** begins what is to be declared. **ٱللَّهُ** can identify that third-person pronoun through apposition, and **أَحَدٌ** supplies the predicate within the quote. A human tongue carries the declaration without becoming its subject matter.

The source wording does not repeat the imperative form itself; it returns the saying-root as **قول** and places beside it the exact particle **لَمْ**: **في نفسي قول لم أظهره**. Passage grammar independently completes the collision. The command exposes a saying, and what that saying exposes includes three governed nonoccurrences: **لَمْ يَلِدْ**, **وَلَمْ يُولَدْ**, and **وَلَمْ يَكُن**. In the source phrase, **لم** keeps the inward saying from manifestation; in the passage, the three **لَمْ** constructions are themselves made manifest in speech. The imperative does not merely bring out an affirmative name. It makes the absence of two events and one relation audible.

That act of exposure requires protected provenance. ق و ل/B005 gives **قولتني ما لم أقل**, assigning to someone what he did not say, and B006 gives **اجتر إلى نفسه قولا**, dragging a saying to oneself. The passage prevents both confusions through participant structure. The Prophet is the commanded declarer; **هُوَ ٱللَّهُ** is the third-person line inside the declaration; final **لَّهُۥ** returns to God as the one toward whom the closing relation is oriented. The carrier is granted vocal agency but not referential co-identity.

The same branch supplies a more compressed distant collision: **قال ما لم يكن**, “he said what did not occur.” The passage commands a saying whose content includes **وَلَمْ يَكُن**. Nonoccurrence inside speech is therefore not what makes speech false. Falsehood arises when saying, source, event, and attribution are wrongly joined. Here the quoted-complement boundary and the return of the third-person pronoun hold those roles apart.

The declaration can still acquire a history of production. و ل د/B005 explicitly names **المولد من الكلام**, newly produced language, and defines derivation as **تولد الشيء من الشيء حصوله عنه بسبب**. Together with the inward saying, tongue, and articulated letters, this opens a carrier process in which a declaration comes into sound and may later be repeated or reformulated. ق و ل/B007’s circulating **قالة** supplies a possible social continuation, although the passage names no audience or relay. The crucial distinction is already complete: a vocal or textual carrier may be produced without producing its referent. God is neither generated by the declaration nor made its progenitor merely because it speaks of Him.

## The repeated name turns the field

The two occurrences **ٱللَّهُ ... ٱللَّهُ** preserve one referent while changing relational phase. The first belongs inside what the Prophet is commanded to declare; the second stands as the nominative subject of **ٱلصَّمَدُ**. Speech therefore moves outward from a commanded carrier, while the ص م د field supplies movement in the other direction. Its direct source wording gives **الصمد السيد لأنه يصمد إليه في الحوائج** and **صمده قصد معتمدا عليه قصده**: need creates an approach toward an intended, relied-upon endpoint. The repeated name becomes a pivot between declaration borne outward and recourse directed inward.

“Self-sufficient refuge” makes the two sides of that inward relation unequal. Need-bearers depend upon the refuge; the refuge is not constituted by an answering need for them. The source even gives recourse an object in **الذي يصمد إليه في الأمر**: an affair can be brought toward the relied-upon one. That affair is not automatically the proposition the Prophet declares. One movement speaks about God; the other carries need or matter toward Him. They meet at the name without collapsing declarer, seeker, declaration, and affair into one role.

The name-field adds further directions at calibrated scale. Forms such as **يا ألله اغفر لي** make the name available for invocation, while **مألوه أي معبود** supplies worshipper and worshipped. The surface itself remains third-person declaration rather than vocative address. A related source sentence says **لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون معبودا**; in its own negation and inflectional construction, it organizes deity through the worship relation. The final **وَلَمْ يَكُن** will deny a different relation, comparability. Call, worship, declaration, and recourse can therefore remain real relations without becoming peerhood.

ص م د/B002 gives the self-sufficient pole a concrete secondary body in the exact phrase **الصمد الذي ليس بأجوف**, the **ṣamad** that is not hollow, together with hard ground and anchored rock. Approach can terminate at a resistant surface without entering an interior or becoming a constituent part. Related but differently formed root scenes sharpen the contrast: **الصماد** is a stopper fitted to a bottle that already has a hollow, while **الكِفاء** is a pair of panels joined one to the other to complete the rear of a dwelling. A sealed bottle depends on its plug; a made boundary depends on its second panel. The no-hollow body requires neither filler, fitted stopper, nor complementary piece. These material differences do not turn God into a body. They make self-sufficiency perceptible as integrity not achieved by reception or completion from elsewhere.

## Genealogy is denied without abolishing relation

The morphology of **لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ** creates an exact reversal. Both verbs are 3MS imperfect jussives from و ل د and each is separately governed by **لَمْ**. In **يَلِدْ**, the contextually recovered subject, God, would occupy the active source-position of birth. Passive **يُولَدْ** would place the same referent in the product-position of another source. Conjunction coordinates the clauses, but coordination does not make the roles interchangeable. One denial removes begetting from God; the other removes being begotten from His history.

The lexical field fills the apparatus around that reversal. **الوالد** and **الوالدة** differentiate father and mother; **الولادة** is the mother’s placing forth of her child; **الولد** can gather one or many, male or female, young or old. The two surface verbs compress all of these ordinary participants into two denied positions for God: source and product. A birth attendant supplied by **ولدناها أي ولينا ولادتها** remains a third possible participant outside those grammatical poles, showing that active and passive do not exhaust every role in a birth scene. The passage, however, needs only the two positions it denies.

This double denial removes genealogy, not every form of dependence. A child depends through origin; the **وليد** can also name a socially dependent slave; a worshipper depends through worship; a person in need approaches the refuge; the Prophet’s vocal act answers an imperative. These relations have different agents, directions, and consequences. **لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ** excludes the two genealogical roles of God. It does not turn the commanded declarer into no one, eliminate seekers, or make refuge relationless.

Birth also supplies a temporal measure. و ل د/B003 gives **يوم ولدت؛ يوم ولد**, the day one gave birth or was born. A related derived branch gives **لدة الرجل تربه**, an age-peer measured from a shared origin. With no birth-origin for God, that particular clock cannot establish His peer. Yet the positive ص م د field supplies another temporal shape in **الصمد الدائم والدائم الباقي** (B007), with remaining through cold and drought as its embodied image. Continuation can thus resonate as endurance across pressure rather than succession through offspring, synchronized origin, or replacement by another cohort. The passage denies birth roles, not time in the abstract; the branch supplies non-genealogical remaining as a distinct positive consequence of **ٱلصَّمَدُ**.

The command’s carrier history now returns with greater precision. Speech may come forth from an inward saying and one wording may generate another; those are production relations among carriers. God’s denied active and passive birth roles prevent that linguistic process from being transferred to the referent. Production of a declaration, generation of offspring, and occurrence of an equal are three different causal structures, and the passage gives each a different grammatical treatment.

## No one becomes “a counterpart for Him”

The closing clause has a lexical frame more exact than any image of balance or inversion. ك ف ء/B001 gives **هذا كفء له أي مثله**, “this is a counterpart for him, that is, his like,” and then states the relation as a process: **كل شيء ساوى شيئا حتى يكون مثله فهو مكافئ له**—when a thing equals another so as to be its like, it is a counterpart to it. The source phrases supply counterpart, likeness, being, and the **لـ** orientation. The surface supplies their negated architecture: **وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ**. What the source describes positively as being a like-for-another, the passage refuses to let any candidate realize for God.

The grammar makes that refusal positional and changes participant visibility. **لَمْ** governs the 3MS jussive **يَكُن**. **كُفُوًا** is its indefinite accusative predicate. The preposition **لـ** governs the 3MS pronoun in **لَّهُۥ** and completes the comparison relation toward God. But God is no longer the grammatical subject of this final verb. **أَحَدٌۢ**, arriving at the end as the delayed nominative subject, is the candidate that would have to occupy the status. The passage has rotated from God as the understood subject of **يَلِدْ** and **يُولَدْ** to God as the pronominal endpoint of comparison, while “anyone” becomes the subject whose comparable being is denied.

This exact relation sorts everything that came before it. The Prophet is related to God as declarer to referent; a caller to the invoked name; a worshipper to the worshipped; a need-bearer to refuge. Parent and child would form a genealogical relation, and the passage has already denied both directions for God. None of these structures is equivalent merely because it contains two participants. To become **كُفُوًا لَّهُۥ**, a candidate would have to satisfy the source-defined relation of likeness-for-Him. Final **أَحَدٌۢ** supplies no such candidate.

The two occurrences of **أَحَدٌ** compress this sorting into the passage boundaries. Opening **أَحَدٌ** is the nominative predicate of **ٱللَّهُ** in an affirmative nominal clause. ء ح د/B001 supplies unrestricted divine description and absolute oneness. Final **أَحَدٌۢ** is the delayed subject of a negated copula, and B002 gives its reach under negation: **لا واحد ولا اثنان فصاعدا**, neither one nor two nor upward. The same visible lemma first declares who God is and finally ranges across every possible comparator.

The other ء ح د branches show what the opening predicate does not require. One may be joined to tens in eleven, selected from a pair in **أحدكما**, placed first in a series, or distributed among many in **آحاد أحاد**. Absolute **ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ** is not produced by any of those relations: not selection from two, composition inside a larger count, first place among members, or isolation from a crowd. Read backward, the exhaustive final **أَحَدٌۢ** removes every candidate that those counting and selection structures might offer. Read forward, opening absolute **أَحَدٌ** prevents the final word from becoming merely another predicate of God.

The arranged utterance makes recurrence itself undergo the test of comparability. **ٱللَّهُ** returns; **لَمْ** returns; the و ل د root returns with reversed voice; several endings carry a final d sound; and **أَحَدٌ** returns at the last boundary. Yet every recurrence changes work. Opening **أَحَدٌ** and **ٱلصَّمَدُ** are nominative predicates, **يُولَدْ** is a passive jussive verb, **كُفُوًا** is accusative, and final **أَحَدٌۢ** is a delayed nominative subject. A related derived ك ف ء form calls differing poetic inflections **أن ترفع قافية وتخفض أخرى**; as a secondary root-field echo, it makes the passage’s actual case and mood differences audible. The governing fact remains grammatical: correspondence of sound or lemma never installs the source-defined counterpart relation.

The last **أَحَدٌۢ** therefore returns to the first **أَحَدٌ** without standing beside it as a second one. It returns as a negated candidate, after **كُفُوًا لَّهُۥ** has stated the exact status it cannot occupy. That failure sends the closing relation all the way back to the commanded voice. The Prophet may articulate the declaration, and later tongues may carry it, but a carrier does not become the referent, a produced saying does not generate God, and relation does not mature into likeness-for-Him. Heard from the end, **قُلْ** is the command to give nonoccurrence an audible form while preserving the one named from appropriation, genealogy, and counterpart. The saying completes its circuit when its final word returns—and even that exact return does not make another one.
