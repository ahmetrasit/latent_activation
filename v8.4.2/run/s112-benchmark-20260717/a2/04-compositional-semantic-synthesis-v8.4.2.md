# A voice carries outward what no counterpart can return

The passage begins by assigning the Prophet an act of declaration: **قُلْ**. Its second-person imperative stands outside the content it governs, while **هُوَ** begins the quoted interior, **ٱللَّهُ** identifies that third-person referent, and **أَحَدٌ** predicates absolute oneness of Him. The distinction is already structural. A human voice is to carry the declaration, but the one who carries it is not the one characterized within it. The final **لَّهُۥ** keeps hold of the same third-person line after the voice has passed through two predicates and three negations.

## Bringing nonappearance into speech

The lexical field of **قُلْ** makes the opening command a passage across a threshold. A saying may exist as **في نفسي قول لم أظهره**, “a saying in myself that I did not expose”; **المقول اللسان** supplies the tongue as instrument; and **المركب من الحروف المبرز بالنطق** supplies the formed letters brought out in articulation. The imperative presses this sequence toward publicity. Yet it does not command only the affirmative **هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ**. What the Prophet must expose eventually includes **لَمْ يَلِدْ**, **وَلَمْ يُولَدْ**, and **وَلَمْ يَكُن**. The phrase **لم أظهره** therefore undergoes a striking reversal across the passage: *not appearing* is itself made to appear as articulated content. The three nonoccurrences are not left in silence; they become the burden of **قُلْ**.

That burden sharpens because another saying-branch joins the opening root directly to the close: **قال ما لم يكن**, “he said what did not occur,” and **قولتني ما لم أقل**, “you attributed to me what I did not say.” The bare act of saying **لَمْ يَكُن** cannot by itself distinguish truthful declaration from fabrication. The passage does so by preserving roles. The Prophet is the commanded declarer; **هُوَ ٱللَّهُ** is the referent inside the declaration; the quoted predicates and negations remain attached to that referent. Vocal agency is granted without referential appropriation. The Prophet can bring the words into sound without dragging their subject-position to himself.

The birth root intensifies this separation from another direction. Its branch **المولد من الكلام مولدا إذا استحدثوه** names newly produced language itself as *generated*. A saying can have a history: an inward form, a tongue, articulated letters, and perhaps the **قالة**, **القول الفاشي في الناس**, that circulates among people. Its wording may be repeated or newly formed. Thus the command opens a genealogy of carriers precisely before the passage denies genealogy of God. **لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ** does not deny that the declaration comes into voiced form; it denies that its divine referent occupies either begetter or begotten position. The declaration may be generated without generating God, and God’s being named does not make the declaration His offspring.

This is the first passage-wide distinction: command-source, vocal performer, verbal carrier, and divine referent are not one source. The utterance can arise, travel, and recur while the one declared in it does not arise from that chain. Read back from the birth clauses, the quotation boundary after **قُلْ** becomes a protection of provenance.

## The name goes out; need comes in

The repetition **ٱللَّهُ ... ٱللَّهُ** turns the passage without changing its referent. In **هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ**, the name belongs to the declaration the Prophet is to bear outward. In **ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ**, the repeated name becomes the subject of the refuge-predicate. The branch prose of **ص م د** supplies its full directed scene: **الصمد السيد لأنه يصمد إليه في الحوائج** and **صمده قصد معتمدا عليه قصده**. Need produces a trajectory; a seeker intends an endpoint and relies upon it. The direct description “self-sufficient refuge” makes God that endpoint while denying that His standing depends in return upon the seekers who arrive.

The speech and refuge fields consequently form opposed vectors around the repeated name. The commanded declaration can move outward from the Prophet into later voices; **انتشرت له قالة ... في الناس** gives the report social radius, and **جاءوا آحاد أحاد** gives possible carriers or arrivals individual spacing. Recourse moves the other way: need-bearing individuals converge upon **ٱلصَّمَدُ**. A person could carry the declaration in one relation and seek refuge in another, but the apparatus does not depend on making the roles identical. What matters is the turn in direction: name borne outward, name approached inward.

The name-root itself gives voice to the inward vector through **يا ألله اغفر لي** and **اللهم بمعنى يا ألله**. The passage remains overtly a third-person declaration, not a vocative clause, but the declared name is capable of becoming the caller’s address as the refuge-predicate supplies a reason to call. Likewise, **أله ... أي عبد عبادة** and **مألوه أي معبود** supply worshipper and worshipped as directionally unequal participants. The exact source sentence **لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون معبودا** places **يكون** beside that relation and reaches forward to **وَلَمْ يَكُن**. The close does not cancel worship, call, or recourse; it denies a different relation, the occurrence of anyone as **كُفُوًا لَّهُۥ**. The passage can therefore be crowded with directed relations while remaining empty of an equal.

This changes the force of absolute oneness. **أَحَدٌ** does not mean that no one can speak, call, worship, rely, or approach. It names God without making any of those participants constitutive parts or comparable peers. Multiplicity can surround the one as voices and need-bearers without composing Him.

## An endpoint not completed by arrival

The same refuge-route acquires a concrete topology from the material prose of **ص م د**: **المكان المرتفع الغليظ**, **الصخرة الراسية**, hard ground, and **المصمت الذي لا جوف له**. The otherwise small branch **أحد جبل بالمدينة** creates an exact lock between the opening **أَحَدٌ** and the next predicate’s raised, anchored body. In this formed image, the route of reliance ends at a visible mass. Approach has a destination, but the destination has no hollow into which the arriver enters as filling. Seekers may reach its surface without becoming what makes it whole.

That no-hollow body matters because the same root offers a very different construction: **الصماد سداد القارورة**, the fitted stopper of a bottle. A stopper presupposes a mouth, an interior, and an added part that completes the closure. The cloth branches intensify the contrast. **الكفاء شقتان تنصح إحداهما بالأخرى** makes the rear of a dwelling from two panels joined one to the other; a head-wrap requires cloth, a wearer, and a bodily place. These are genuine forms of integrity, but integrity achieved through fit, complement, or enclosure. Self-sufficiency makes their dependence visible. It is not simply another name for being sealed: a sealed bottle needs its stopper, and a sewn boundary needs its second panel.

The saying-root supplies one more material limit: **امتلأ الحوض وقال قطني**, the basin whose fullness “says enough.” A full vessel indicates its state without a tongue because received contents have exhausted its capacity. The no-hollow mass indicates almost the reverse: there was no receiving capacity to exhaust. The opening **قُلْ** and the predicate **ٱلصَّمَدُ** can thus make limits perceptible by unlike means—human articulation, a filled state, and constitutive compactness—without turning them into one image.

The active and passive birth verbs then send two hypothetical directions across this boundary-field. **يَلِدْ** would place God at the outward source of generation; **يُولَدْ** would place Him at the receiving product-end of another source. Separate **لَمْ** particles stop both. The material image makes “out from” and “derived from” sensory, but the grammar remains exact: it is genealogical agency, not literal containment, that is denied. The no-hollow endpoint gives the denial resistance; the stopper and paired panels show by contrast what completion from another would require.

## Generation is removed; dependence is redirected

The symmetry of **لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ** is audible, but its two clauses distribute opposite roles. In the first, the 3MS subject would be an active source; in the second, passive voice would make that same referent the generated product. The birth prose supplies the ordinarily differentiated scene: **الوالد الأب والوالدة الأم**, **الولد المولود**, **وضع الوالدة ولدها**, and **يوم ولدت؛ يوم ولد**. The negation therefore removes more than two labels. It removes God as source of offspring, as offspring of parents, and as participant at either end of a birth-event and its origin-day.

The branches also keep this reversal from swallowing every relation. **ولدناها أي ولينا ولادتها** supplies an attendant who oversees a birth without being mother or newborn. More decisively, **الوليد** can name a newly born child and can also name a socially dependent servant or slave, while the name-root uses **عبد** for the worshipper and **ص م د** supplies the **سيد** approached in needs. These are not interchangeable hierarchies. A child depends through origin, a social dependent through status, a worshipper through worship, and a petitioner through need. The passage denies the genealogical poles, retains God as the endpoint of worship and refuge, and finally denies parity. It moves from generation to recourse, not from relation to relationlessness.

The close acts as a precise sorter. **كُفُوًا** gathers likeness, equal standing, reciprocal action, and the alignment of matched counterparts. **أَحَدٌۢ**, delayed as the nominative subject of **يَكُن**, is the candidate who would have to occupy that accusative predicate-status in relation **لَّهُۥ**. Under **لَمْ**, no candidate occupies it. But an addressee need not be an equal to the command’s referent; a worshipper need not be an equal to the worshipped; a seeker need not be an equal to the refuge. Even the two birth clauses can be coordinated without being comparable agents. The passage excludes a particular relational status rather than abolishing direction, complement, sequence, or plurality.

This sorting gives the paired textile a useful counterimage. Two panels can require **إحداهما بالأخرى** in order to make one boundary. The closing grammar likewise presents a relation “to/for Him,” but refuses the candidate panel: no **أَحَدٌۢ** is fitted as **كُفُوًا لَّهُۥ**. Self-sufficiency and non-comparability meet here as different forces. One says the whole is not completed from outside; the other says no one takes corresponding status beside it.

## The birth clock cannot measure a peer

The transition from **يُولَدْ** to **يَكُن ... كُفُوًا** becomes especially exact through **لِدَة الرجل**, the peer whose likeness is measured by shared birth-age. Such a peer requires synchronized origins. Once **لَمْ يُولَدْ** removes an origin-day for God, that clock cannot establish a comparable. The passage does not merely deny parentage and then append a general denial of likeness; the first denial dismantles one concrete measuring instrument for the second.

The being-root supplies another clock in the old man called **كُنْتِيّ**, named from **قوله كُنْتُ في شبابي كذا وكذا**. His present identity is narrated by saying “I was” in an earlier phase. This source phrase joins saying, being, and remembered change. Read against **وَلَمْ يَكُن**, it opens the question whether a comparable might once have occupied the relation even if none does now. The negated copula closes that retrospective route: the equal-status is not merely vacant at the speaking moment; it has not occurred as a past phase available to such recollection.

Other clocks remain distinct around that removal. **يوم الأحد أي يوم الأول** makes one a first day in a series; annual **كفأة** measures fruit, milk, wool, and offspring in productive cycles; **الصمد الدائم** and the camel **الباقية على القر والجدب** give endurance across cold and drought. The passage does not flatten day, year, adverse interval, and duration into one timelessness. It distinguishes continuation by remaining from continuation by successor or recurring yield. **لَمْ يَلِدْ** removes offspring as the continuation of God; **ٱلصَّمَدُ** can still gather the lexical force of enduring without replacement.

This temporal field sends pressure back into opening **أَحَدٌ**. The direct predicate is absolute oneness, not “first” as the first member of a birth-series, and not the numerical one that can be joined to tens in **أحد عشر**. Firstness seeks a subsequent member; a component seeks a larger composition; a same-age peer seeks a shared beginning. The ensuing clauses successively deprive those relations of a foothold.

## The word returns, not an equal

The passage closes by returning the very form with which its first predication ended: **أَحَدٌ ... أَحَدٌۢ**. The branch phrase **أحد أحد** invites the ear to hear emphatic duplication, but the intervening passage transforms the second occurrence. Opening **أَحَدٌ** is a nominative predicate affirming God’s absolute oneness. Final **أَحَدٌۢ** is the delayed nominative subject of a negated copula, the “anyone” whose possible range extends through **لا واحد ولا اثنان فصاعدا**. The same lemma first characterizes God and then sweeps exhaustively through candidates who fail to be comparable to Him.

The return is staged in a field of partial formal matches. **ٱللَّهُ** repeats across the two affirmative predications; **لَمْ** repeats across active birth, passive birth, and copular being; **يَلِدْ** and **يُولَدْ** share root, person, aspect, and jussive mood while reversing voice; the clause endings repeatedly offer a final *d* sound. Yet each recurrence changes grammatical work. **ٱلصَّمَدُ** is a nominative nominal predicate, **يُولَدْ** a passive jussive verb, **كُفُوًا** an accusative predicate, and final **أَحَدٌۢ** its delayed nominative subject.

The comparison root contains a formal branch for precisely this disturbance: **أن ترفع قافية وتخفض أخرى** and **اختلاف إعراب القوافي**. Its raising of one ending and lowering of another does not make the passage defective verse; it makes inflectional noncorrespondence audible inside recurrence. Sound and lemma can return while case, agency, polarity, and syntactic role refuse to align. The final denial is therefore enacted in the passage’s form: correspondence in one dimension never installs **كُفُوًا** in the decisive relation.

The delayed final **أَحَدٌۢ** sends the completed force back to **قُلْ**. The Prophet is not commanded merely to pronounce a static predicate. He is to carry into human sound a declaration that distinguishes its own repeatable carrier from its ungenerated referent, sends the name outward while need turns inward, denies both directions of genealogy, and lets the opening word return only after its role has been changed. The declaration can be voiced again; **أَحَدٌ** can recur; many can arrive **آحاد أحاد**. What never returns is a counterpart: the word comes back at the boundary, but no **أَحَدٌۢ** comes back as **كُفُوًا لَّهُۥ**.
