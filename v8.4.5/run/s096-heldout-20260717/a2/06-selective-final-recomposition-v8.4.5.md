# Semantic Synthesis — 96:1–19

## A named command gathers a formed human

The passage begins by making speech an act of relation: `ٱقْرَأْ بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ`. The imperative is attached to `بِٱسْمِ`; the name is completed by `رَبِّكَ`; the Lord is identified through creation. Recitation does not first appear as a capacity possessed by an independent speaker and later connected to a source. It begins connected. The name’s branch in س م و gives it identification and elevation of mention: the reader raises the mention of the one whose name already locates the act.

The gathering facet of ق ر ء makes that located act constructive. Reading joins letters and words and voices what has been gathered. Its supplied source wording also makes an exceptional contact with the next verse. The pregnancy branch says `لم تحمل علقة أي دما ولا جنينا` and `لم تضم رحمها على ولد`: the womb did not carry an *ʿalaqa*, blood, or fetus, and did not gather a child. The source prose for the opening root thus names the surface carrier in `خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ`. Reading and conception do not become the same event, but each changes the other’s passage-scale force. `ٱقْرَأْ` is an ordered gathering; `عَلَقٍ` is living formation that begins by being gathered, held, and attached.

The branches of ع ل ق preserve that dependence in several distinct roles: sticking to another, conception fixed in the womb, congealed blood-like matter, and a small provision by which life continues. The human is not introduced as an isolated miniature. It appears through adhesion, reception, and sustenance. Creation from clinging matter remains the direct proposition; the branch field makes dependence an operation internal to emergence rather than a later weakness added to a self-made being.

`رَبِّكَ` supplies the temporal relation through which held potential becomes capacity. ر ب ب defines nurture as bringing something forward stage by stage toward completion. Its learned-teacher branch is more exact still: `العالم المعلم الذي يغذو الناس بصغار العلوم`, the knowing teacher who feeds people with small portions of knowledge. The repeated Lord-carrier therefore already contains a mode of the repeated `عَلَّمَ`. Ownership is joined to repair, nurture, and staged intellectual nourishment.

Creation and the pen give this nurture material discipline. خ ل ق includes measuring a hide before cutting it, as well as bringing into being, giving visible form, inward disposition, and preparedness. ق ل م includes paring a hard material until it becomes a writing implement, and paring it again as use requires. Measure precedes cut; cut produces a usable edge; the edge leaves a distinguishing trace. `ٱلَّذِى عَلَّمَ بِٱلْقَلَمِ` therefore continues creation rather than departing from it. A formed instrument participates in forming a knower. The pen is neither teacher nor knowledge, just as the reader is neither name nor Lord; grammar preserves the differentiated participants through which transmission occurs.

The paired verbs unfold by filling roles. The first `خَلَقَ` identifies the Lord through an act; the second supplies human object and source, `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ`. The first `عَلَّمَ` supplies a medium, `بِٱلْقَلَمِ`; the second supplies recipient and content, `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ`. Syntactically, `مَا` is what teaching gives and what the human had not known. Non-knowledge is not a void hidden by performance. It is the open relation across which generosity acts.

The opening teaches by return as well as sequence. ر ج ع/B007 defines vocal recurrence in reading as `تكرير قول مرتين فصاعدا`, and `ٱقْرَأْ` occurs twice. The first command enters the name–Lord–creation relation; the second returns beside `وَرَبُّكَ ٱلْأَكْرَمُ`. The two `خَلَقَ` and two `عَلَّمَ` likewise add object, source, medium, recipient, and content on recurrence. Repetition is not stasis: it is staged nurture in which a carrier comes back with a missing relation now supplied.

## Received formation can counterfeit itself as sufficiency

The turn at `كَلَّا إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَيَطْغَىٰ` is sharp because the human has real capacity. The repeated `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ` was object of creation and teaching; it now bears the emphatic predicate of transgression. A formed participant mistakes participation for origination. Its action is not proof that reception was unreal; it is a misuse of what reception made possible.

The خ ل ق field supplies an exact account of that misuse. Its false-speech branch says `الخلق خلق الكذب وهو اختلاقه واختراعه وتقديره في النفس`: a lie can be fabricated, invented, and measured inwardly. The passage begins with repeated divine `خَلَقَ`, later gives `إِن كَذَّبَ`, and finally exposes a `نَاصِيَةٍ كَٰذِبَةٍ`. Falsehood is not sheer absence of formative power. It is a counterfeit exercise of it: a formed and taught being produces an inwardly measured appearance and detaches that appearance from its truth-relation. What was received as capacity to distinguish and voice can be turned into fabrication.

That counterfeit begins in `أَن رَّءَاهُ ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ`. The object suffix in `رَّءَاهُ` and the circumstantial `ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ` let sight curve toward a human figure seen or judged in a condition of sufficiency without supplying an overt reflexive noun. The lexical field can make an image, mirror, or inward opinion audible, but the grammar keeps the compression intact. What is certain is that a perceived condition becomes the occasion of transgression.

ر ء ي/B002 defines opinion with strikingly distributed wording: `الرأي ما يراه الإنسان في الأمر`, what the human sees in the matter. The passage itself supplies `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ`, `رَّءَاهُ`, three `أَرَءَيْتَ` openings, and `أَمَرَ`. Human seeing is therefore not limited to retinal inspection. It includes judgment about a condition, matter, and directive force. A real image of acquired capacity can be read falsely when the relation that produced it is excluded from the judgment.

The accepted phrase `أطغاه المال أي جعله طاغيا` gives the causal turn a further edge. The passage need not reduce `ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ` to money, but wealth, stored capacity, or possessed means can make transgression operative when they are counted as enough. ن ه ي/B005 sharpens what “enough” imagines: `ناهيك` is one who suffices so fully that seeking another ends. The self-sufficient judgment is a false endpoint. The human treats a received stage as terminal fullness, as though no further source, correction, or return were needed.

`إِنَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلرُّجْعَىٰ` breaks that enclosure before the obstructed prayer appears. The fronted predicate fixes the destination: return is `إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ`. ر ج ع can make restoration, repentance, an answer returning to a speaker, recurring water, or a retraced line audible, but none overrides that stated direction. The same `رَبِّكَ` associated with creation, generosity, and teaching now stands at the end of the human’s claim to be enough. Return reconnects capacity to a relation the self-image tried to erase.

## Water gives measure and transfer a complete process

Across the same passage runs a hydraulic process whose differentiated roles are too complete to function as decorative imagery. The accepted branches supply layered rain-cloud, fertile rain and soil, dew, a hollow that retains sky-water, a deep water-rich well, a suspended drawing apparatus, a repaired skin that holds what has been drawn, a portable vessel, urgent night movement toward water, a tract missed by rain, floodwater exceeding measure, a terminal pool where flow settles, and rain or pooled water that returns.

The decisive mechanical link comes from ع ل ق itself. Its apparatus branch names the rope, upright support, pulley, and complete means by which buckets draw water. Suspension here is neither conception nor capture. Tension enables a vessel to descend into retained depth and rise carrying what a recipient could not reach directly. The deep well supplied by ع ل م and the holding hollow supplied by خ ل ق acquire a transfer mechanism; the repaired hide supplied by ر ب ب acquires a purpose; the waterskin supplied by ق ر ب acquires content. Source, support, line, vessel, motion, and recipient remain distinct.

That distinction returns precision to `عَلَّمَ بِٱلْقَلَمِ`. The pen and pulley are materially different instruments, but they perform a common relational operation. Neither originates the content, neither becomes the giver, and neither eliminates the recipient’s action. Each makes passage possible between a retained depth and one who cannot reach it without mediation. A tethered vessel must move; a taught human must answer.

The hydraulic field also gives `ٱلْأَكْرَمُ` and `لَيَطْغَىٰ` opposed agencies within one material. ك ر م names cloud that gives rain and ground that yields well. Generosity is not sheer quantity: cloud, soil, hollow, root, vessel, and season remain capable of receiving and continuing. ط غ ي names water rising beyond its measure and sweeping things away. Transgression is abundance detached from channel and recipient. The human does not err by acquiring force; it errs when force treats its enabling bounds as dispensable.

Two failures remain distinct inside this process. خ ط ء/B003 names land missed by rain while neighboring ground receives it. A `خَاطِئَةٍ` direction may fail because it does not arrive where its action belongs, as well as because it culpably exceeds a limit. `بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰ`, from و ق ي, supplies a contrary operation: a self placed within a protective interval. Protection must keep flood from destroying its recipient without becoming the barrier that makes water miss the ground.

The two appearances of ن ه ي then acquire physical and causal precision. The root can name the pool or valley-end where moving water stops and settles. At `يَنْهَىٰ عَبْدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰ`, one person tries to impose an endpoint on another’s directed act. At `لَئِن لَّمْ يَنتَهِ`, the endpoint changes target: the one who stops the servant must himself stop. The self that imagines its own seeking finished tries to finish another’s prayer while refusing a limit for its own action. One operation dams a gift from outside; the other demands that harmful flow reach its proper end.

The water process remains active at the close without supplying its destination. ق ر ب includes the night drive toward water and the dependent creature seeking a watering place. That branch gives `وَٱقْتَرِب` bodily urgency: need moves rather than merely changing its opinion. The direct final relation, however, is Lordward nearness. Water makes the mechanism of dependence concrete; it does not turn approach into a coded command to drink.

## The praying servant occupies the center

After the return-clause the passage moves from a generic human danger to a contested act: `أَرَءَيْتَ ٱلَّذِى يَنْهَىٰ ۝ عَبْدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰ`. The one who forbids is object of `أَرَءَيْتَ` and subject of `يَنْهَىٰ`; the servant is exposed as object of prohibition while remaining the subject of prayer. Social vulnerability does not erase the servant’s grammatical or bodily agency.

The position is marked. A passage of nineteen verses has verse 10 as its single verse-center, and that center is `عَبْدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰ`. The obstructer does not occupy it; the servant in the temporal condition of prayer does. ص ل و/B005 names `الصلا وسط الظهر لكل ذي أربع وللناس`, the middle of the back. That accepted bodily branch does not replace prayer’s local sense. It makes the positional fact material: the root in the exact middle carries a branch naming middle and back.

This center reorganizes the passage’s body. The opening gathering field includes joined letters and words and, in its accepted articulation, `اجتماع عظام الظهر`. At the midpoint stands the servant’s prayer with a back/middle pressure inside its root. Later the threat targets the forelock, and the close distributes the body across prostration points. The passage moves from gathered articulation, through a centered praying body, to rival ways of directing that body.

Prayer itself joins voice and posture. ص ل و/B002 defines `الصلاة وهي الدعاء`; B003 describes the creature’s prayer through standing, bowing, prostration, invocation, and glorification. س ج د/B001 correspondingly names `سجود الصلاة`. The obstructer therefore does not interrupt a mute posture. He tries to stop an invocation and a structured act at the moment `إِذَا` makes capacity become performance. The ending will reopen that same act by selecting one of its bodily movements: `وَٱسْجُدْ`.

The three `أَرَءَيْتَ` clauses train the addressee to see the interruption correctly. The root permits `أَرَءَيْتَ` to function as “tell me,” an alerting demand for an account. The first question presents an actor and the prayer he forbids. The second asks about a subject being `عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰ` or commanding `بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰ`. The third asks about denial joined to turning away. The surrounding contrast makes the praying servant the live positive possibility and the obstructer the live negative one, while the conditional subjects themselves remain unexpressed. The grammar requires judgment across actions rather than permanent labels attached to rank.

A branch example in the visual field sharpens what is at stake in prayer’s audience: `يرآءون الناس إذا أبصرهم الناس صلوا وإذا لم يروهم تركوا الصلاة`. The example describes people praying when people see them and leaving prayer when they do not. It does not define the local act, but the passage independently supplies human sight, `إِذَا`, prayer, repeated seeing, and finally `ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ`. The example makes a danger concrete: human visibility can become a counterfeit condition for prayer.

`أَلَمْ يَعْلَم بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ` reverses that audience. `ٱللَّهَ` is the explicit subject of `يَرَىٰ`, and divine seeing is the content the obstructer ought to know. The one who behaved as watcher becomes answerable to a witness not possessed by his club or controlled by his line of sight. Prayer is no longer measurable by whether a socially dominant observer permits it or whether people applaud it.

## Marks, mediations, and commands direct the body

The passage’s sight sequence is also a route judgment. ء م ر/B005 gives `أمار الطريق معالمه`; ع ل م/B002 says `المعلم الأثر يستدل به على الطريق`; ر ء ي/B011 gives a marker raised to be seen; ه د ي distributes indication, course, and the guide who goes first; ع ب د/B005 gives a road made passable; خ ط ء gives missing the intended direction. A contextual reading-branch example, `رأيت القوم على قرو واحد`, places seeing, a group, and one followed way in the same sentence. The example does not translate `عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰ`, but its operation is independently realized by the passage’s seeing, guidance, command, and leading body-part.

The pen now does more than preserve information. Its pared edge leaves a repeatable distinction; an `عَلَم` makes one course discernible; a command can be heard as a voice or encountered as a placed sign; guidance supplies course and leader. Teaching forms a being able to read marks, judge `الأمر`, and distinguish a guiding front from a `نَاصِيَةٍ ... خَاطِئَةٍ`. Error becomes embodied misdirection at the place that presents itself as leader.

Five bāʾ constructions carry this movement across the passage. `بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ` attaches reading to a named relation. `بِٱلْقَلَمِ` gives teaching an instrument. `بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰ` gives command a protected direction. `بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ` gives knowledge content. `بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ` gives force a bodily point of contact. The repeated particle does not equate its complements. It stages relation, medium, ethical orientation, propositional recognition, and coercive handle. The early links preserve source, instrument, recipient, and act as distinct; the last threatens to reduce a person to one accessible part.

This is why the passage’s rival directives cannot be judged by imperative force alone. `ٱقْرَأْ` commands; the obstructer `يَنْهَىٰ`; the positive possibility `أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰ`; the obstructer is challenged to call; the close commands refusal, prostration, and approach. ء م ر includes deliberation, and it also names the weak-minded person who consults every voice and complies with every order. Such a person cannot perform `لَا تُطِعْهُ`. Non-obedience here is taught discrimination, not a return to self-sufficient independence.

Even falsehood can imitate obligation. The ك ذ ب field includes the idiom `كذب عليك` with the force “it is incumbent on you” or “take it upon yourself.” That idiom does not replace the surface denial, but it makes audible how a fabricated direction can present itself as binding. Guidance, protection, knowledge, participant relation, and consequence—not loudness or social rank—determine which command may move the body.

و ل ي/B007 gives the negative direction of `وَتَوَلَّىٰ` as `ترك قربه`, leaving proximity, and as leaving listening and compliance, `ترك الإصغاء والائتمار`. The obstructer turns away from the knowledge-relation even while trying to make another obey. Turning away is thus not mere physical rotation. It is abandoned proximity, hearing, and answerability.

The route need not be traversed at one heroic pace. The guidance branches include a weak person walking supported between two others, calm movement, and a hurt animal guarding its step; the service field includes an exhausted traveler. Dependence can be the means by which direction is maintained. Speed and unsupported dominance are not evidence of guidance.

## One call draws the call that follows

The late pair `فَلْيَدْعُ نَادِيَهُۥ ۝ سَنَدْعُ ٱلزَّبَانِيَةَ` belongs to the prayer movement because prayer is already الدعاء, but the three calls have different vectors. Prayer invokes within the Lord-relation. د ع و/B001 describes a call as inclining something toward oneself by voice and speech. The challenged obstructer’s third-person call draws a possessed assembly toward him. The first-person-plural future call answers with another object and another source of agency. Shared root does not make the summonses equivalent.

The formal adjacency has an exact lexical mechanism. د ع و/B003 defines `داعية اللبن` as `ما يترك في الضرع ليدعو ما بعده`, what is left so that it calls what comes after it. The pastoral material need not govern the scene; the operation is completed by passage form. `فَلْيَدْعُ` is immediately followed by `سَنَدْعُ`. The root returns while person and explicit object change: `نَادِيَهُ` in the first clause, `ٱلزَّبَانِيَةَ` in the second. One call literally draws the call that follows, and recurrence becomes reversal rather than reinforcement.

The object `نَادِيَهُ` also exposes the social contents of self-sufficiency. A نادي can be a council, public center, place of deliberation, generosity, lineage, reputation, and support. Its possessive suffix reorganizes those relations as “his” assembly, mobilized as one caller’s extended capacity. The supposedly sufficient individual finally needs answering bodies. His error is not that community exists, but that persons, alliances, public voice, and status are counted as owned proof of individual independence.

The counter-call prevents that possessed center from becoming the passage’s largest acting plurality. Z-b-n supplies pushing, repelling, striking, and defending a side; the answering object therefore carries a force opposed to the inward gathering of the نادي. Yet the passage does not command the servant or addressee to recruit a counter-club, draw a weapon, or outdo the caller. Social escalation is exposed and answered, but the final human response will be refusal, prostration, and approach.

The speech vectors remain differentiated from beginning to end. A name identifies and raises another’s mention. Reading gathers and voices ordered content. Prayer invokes. A call draws participants toward a speaker. A reply returns speech toward its source. The opening voice speaks within `بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ`; the late human voice tries to mobilize `نَادِيَهُ`. Audible force cannot erase the difference between named relation and possessed reinforcement.

## From a false front to an approaching body

The first-person-plural threat locates the obstructer’s direction in one bodily point: `لَنَسْفَعًۢا بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ ۝ نَاصِيَةٍ كَٰذِبَةٍ خَاطِئَةٍ`. س ف ع/B001 supplies taking the forepart by hand. ن ص ي/B001 supplies the front hairline that can be grasped and pulled; B003 supplies the chosen or leading part of a group. False fabrication and culpable misdirection are thus attributed to what presents itself first and attempts to lead.

The verse boundary intensifies the exposure. The definite forelock governed by `بِ` is immediately reopened by an indefinite `نَاصِيَةٍ`, which receives `كَٰذِبَةٍ خَاطِئَةٍ`. First comes the bodily handle, then the disclosed character of its direction. The social and bodily organizations correspond without becoming identical: the obstructer gathers many people beneath one possessive center, and the threat reaches his agency through one leading part.

The final `وَٱسْجُدْ` answers that one-point organization with an articulated body. س ج د/B003 names forehead, nose, hands, knees, and feet as human contact-points. The frontal surface descends, hands bear, knees bend, feet remain placed, and the gaze can be lowered. External seizure tries to govern a whole through one handle; prostration coordinates a plurality of members in one response. Both may lower the front, but only one is performed by the addressed subject and only one is joined to approach.

A contextual branch example preserves the political danger inside the visual field. Image-bearing coins are described by `إذا رأوها سجدوا لها`: when people saw them, they prostrated to them. The example is not the local definition of the final command. The passage itself supplies the stronger structure: human sight, socially represented power, contested obedience, prayer, divine seeing, and a final prostration after `لَا تُطِعْهُ`. The coin makes the boundary concrete. A visible image of rule can capture bodily compliance, but visibility does not authenticate the object before which a body lowers.

The closing sequence prevents every false identification. `لَا تُطِعْهُ` severs the obstructer’s claim on the addressee’s capacity. Without that refusal, prostration could be confused with submission to the human prohibitor. `وَٱسْجُدْ` reopens the prayer and gives refusal a bodily relation. Without it, non-obedience could become another assertion of self-sufficient uprightness. `وَٱقْتَرِب` gives the lowered body a positive vector. Without approach, descent could end in imposed humiliation rather than nearness.

The destination of that approach has direct lexical support. ق ر ب/B005 says of a قربان that it is what one uses to `تقرب إلى الله`; ر ب ب/B007 contains `الإرباب الدنو`; and ر ب ب/B001 names the Lord as `السيد المطاع`. The repeated `رَبِّكَ`, the explicit `ٱللَّهَ`, and the final `ٱقْتَرِب` converge on Lordward nearness. The object suffix in `لَا تُطِعْهُ` rejects one human claimant, while the two positive imperatives exercise the addressee’s agency within another relation.

Birth-nearing remains a secondary pressure on that movement without supplying its destination. A visual branch says `أرأت الناقة إذا أرأى ضرعها أنها أقربت وأنزلت`, making hidden pregnancy visible as birth nears; ق ر ب/B012 says `أقربت المرأة إذا قرب ولادها`. These examples touch the opening ق ر ء source that named `علقة`, the surface creation `مِنْ عَلَقٍ`, staged nurture, and final approach. They make emergence from received formation audible. They do not make the command a biological proposition. Water-seeking likewise gives approach the urgency of a dependent body without naming what the final nearness is toward.

The passage’s negations show how that body becomes capable of the close. In `مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ`, non-knowledge is the receptive lack crossed by teaching. In `أَلَمْ يَعْلَم`, negated knowing has become failure to integrate what should be known. In `لَئِن لَّمْ يَنتَهِ`, non-cessation calls forth consequence. In `لَا تُطِعْهُ`, refusal clears capacity for prostration and approach. The three `كَلَّا` interruptions perform a related work at passage scale: the first breaks closure around self-sufficiency, the second breaks continued obstruction, and the third breaks the prohibitor’s claim to obedience. Each negative opens a different next act.

The end therefore sends its gain back to the first command. Read after `لَا تُطِعْهُ وَٱسْجُدْ وَٱقْتَرِب`, `ٱقْرَأْ بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ` no longer sounds like an order given to an already self-authored voice. It is the first gathering of a dependent, teachable voice into a relation that the closing body actively inhabits. The Lord’s mention rises in recitation; the human body descends in prostration; because that descent is joined to rightly directed refusal and approach, it becomes nearness rather than defeat.
