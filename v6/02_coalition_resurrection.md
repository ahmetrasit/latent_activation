## Why This Turn Exists

Branch visitation is not opportunity coverage.

A branch may appear unproductive alone and become decisive only when other
roots supply its missing participants, instrument, medium, direction, support,
or outcome.

The first successful architecture also creates search bias. Temporarily stop
asking how unused facets extend the channels already found. Ask what new
channel becomes possible when dormant facets combine with one another.

## Anti-Downgrade Rule

Do not weaken a Turn 1 finding because:

- its scene is not literally named;
- it depends on several distant roots;
- another channel is already sufficient to explain the passage;
- it seems unusually precise or imaginative;
- it would normally attract a caveat in cautious analytical prose.

The task is to test synthesis opportunities by developing their role systems
and passage predictions. General caution is not a test.

## Resurrection Sweeps

### 1. Dormant-role coalitions

Combine two to six dormant facets that supply different functions. Search for
systems such as:

- owner, leader, followers, route, separated member;
- container, contents, support, extraction, recipient;
- source, gift, transfer, good condition, response;
- path preparation, guidance, marker, alignment, deviation;
- softness, formation, support, resistance, dissolution;
- injury, anger, compensation, substitution, judgment;
- teacher, sign, explanation, method, memory loss.

These are examples of role structures, not themes to impose.

### 2. Primary-anchor-first search

Start from every major action, title, contrast, repetition, grammatical turn,
and closing expression in the passage.

Ask which locally unrelated branches elsewhere:

- make it possible;
- give it physical form;
- complete its participant structure;
- invert it;
- measure it;
- or make its consequence visible.

### 3. Cross-definition and lexical-ring search

Search for:

- one present root explicitly used to define another present root;
- an opening word whose branch is answered by a closing word;
- reciprocal owner/servant, giver/response, guide/stray, name/call, or
  support/standing relations;
- repeated grammatical surfaces whose force changes;
- a late word that supplies the reason, destination, or instrument anticipated
  by an early word.

### 4. Embodied entailment search

Return to every strong concrete image and ask what its physical process
requires.

Use plural number, voice, bodily stance, repetition, adjacency, and recitational
sequence when they fill those requirements. Preserve small but memorable
deductions when they are tightly licensed, even if they do not create a large
independent channel.

### 5. Sound and lexical-form search

Inspect supplied pronunciation variants, repeated sounds, exact word forms,
and same-root formal relations when the lexical evidence itself makes them
available.

Do not invent historical or phonological claims absent from the evidence.
Equally, do not overlook an explicit form-related facet merely because the
dominant search has become visual or spatial.

### 6. Independent rival realizations

When one branch supplies different concrete objects in different coalitions,
keep those realizations separate. Determine whether each receives its own
passage partners and ordered completion.

One strong route, well, household, weapon, animal, or navigation scene must not
automatically absorb another.

## When to Keep an Opportunity

Keep a coalition live when:

- its parts perform complementary roles;
- the passage supplies a precise activation route;
- order or adjacency matters;
- it changes a direct proposition rather than merely decorating it;
- and there is no actual contradiction.

Surface absence alone never makes it conditional.

Keep a structure incomplete only when an indispensable role or relation truly
remains missing. State the open role so later synthesis can still complete it.
