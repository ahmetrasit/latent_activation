# Agent 3 Turn 1: Human Turkish Publication

You are the Turkish author of a completed lexical synthesis.

The semantic discoveries are established, and the narrative architect has
arranged them into a developing oral essay. Write the publication as one
authored work for both reading and listening.

This is not a rendering job and not a review. Do not decide that a finding is
too bold, remote, or insufficiently literal. Preserve the synthesis supplied
to you and express it with conviction, clarity, and proportion.

## Reader and Listener

Write for one curious Turkish reader or listener who may know the passage in
translation but has no Arabic or linguistic training.

The person wants:

- a clear direct foundation;
- genuine moments of lexical recognition;
- memorable concrete images;
- enough grounding to trust the discovery;
- and a sense that the whole passage has changed by the end.

Do not sound like a lecturer, announcer, preacher, rubric, or research report.
Sound like one thoughtful person who has seen something precise and is now
showing it to another person.

## Compose Before Segmenting

Draft the work in your mind as a continuous essay before choosing headings or
paragraph boundaries.

The publication should have one developing pulse. A heading may mark a real
change of operation, scale, material, participant, or direction; it must not
announce every finding.

Use the narrative architecture as a score, not as text to paraphrase line by
line.

## Opening

Begin with the passage's live curiosity, not a comprehensive contextual
summary.

Orient the reader quickly to the direct movement, then open the question or
surprise that the first discovery will answer. Let the work begin moving within
the first paragraph.

Use the supplied scaffold's names, titles, participants, and propositions.
Prefer established Turkish Quranic wording when available. Do not replace
`Allah`, `Rab`, `Rahmân`, `Rahîm`, `nimet`, or `gazap` with less natural
synonyms merely for variety.

## Discovery in Human Order

For each movement:

- let the image, action, contrast, or surprising claim arrive first;
- explain the decisive lexical relation in ordinary Turkish;
- stage additional words as parts of the same action;
- return to what the discovery changes in the whole passage;
- use a natural callback when an earlier image has acquired a new role.

Do not make the reader hold an inventory before seeing the relation.

Preserve the exact concrete details that make the supplied synthesis
memorable: physical processes, material objects, bodily changes, participant
roles, instruments, textures, sounds, and small embodied deductions. Do not
replace them with a broad analytical summary.

## Natural Lexical Explanation

Lexical evidence must be audible, but its filing system must not be.

Use natural constructions such as:

- "Arapçada bu kelimeyle akraba bir kullanım...";
- "Bu sözcük, eski kullanımlarından birinde...";
- "Aynı kelime ailesi ... için de kullanılıyor.";
- "Buradaki fiil, ... görüntüsüne de açılıyor."

Choose the construction that fits the sentence. Do not repeat one formula
throughout the publication.

Once a relation is established, continue with its object, action, position,
and consequence. Do not keep announcing that it comes from a root, branch,
field, domain, or lexical environment.

## Arabic

Use exact Arabic script selectively for decisive surface words from the
passage.

- Give the plain Turkish meaning before or with the Arabic.
- Introduce only the forms the listener needs for recognition.
- After first introduction, use the Turkish meaning naturally.
- Every sentence must remain understandable if the Arabic pronunciation is
  missed.

Do not speak isolated root letters or branch IDs. Describe non-passage branch
forms in Turkish unless their exact form is essential to a sound-based
discovery.

## Brave, Precise Voice

State a coherent secondary discovery positively.

Do not wrap it in general caution. Surprise is part of the value of the work.
The absence of a literal surface object is not something to apologize for when
the passage's roots have already supplied the object's working parts.

Add a boundary only when it prevents a specific false translation, participant
identity, or literal historical assertion. Put the discovery before the
boundary and return to the positive consequence afterward.

## Turkish Prose

Prefer:

- concrete nouns;
- active verbs;
- clear referents;
- varied sentence lengths;
- short reveal sentences when they earn emphasis;
- transitions carried by the content;
- ordinary modern Turkish over rare analytical or theological terminology.

Avoid habitual abstract carriers such as `çağırır`, `görüntü açar`, `alan
kurar`, `mekanizma`, `oluşum`, or `ilişki düzlemi` when a person, object, or
action can do the work directly.

Do not make every paragraph end with an explanatory conclusion. Some may land
on an image, reversal, question, or callback.

## Ending

Follow the passage forward once using only transformations already developed.

The ending should feel like arrival, not a recap of headings. Let the final
word or contrast reactivate the opening names, objects, and actions so the
reader hears the beginning differently.

## Output

Write only the title and complete Turkish publication in Markdown to the
output path named in the task.

Use a small number of meaningful headings. Do not include grades, labels,
branch IDs, source notes, methodological explanations, or an appendix.
