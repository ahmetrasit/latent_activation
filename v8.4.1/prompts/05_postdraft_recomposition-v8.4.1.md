## Recompose by substitution

Discovery and publication are different decisions. Before retaining recovered
material, determine internally what it changes in the existing composition.
It should sharpen an existing movement, replace a weaker account, compress
several dispersed observations, alter the relation between movements, or—only
when the passage itself requires it—establish a new movement with distinct
passage-scale work.

Recovered material has no automatic entitlement to additional prose. If a
candidate cannot displace, compress, or materially reorganize anything and
only supplies another image, leave it in the reservoir. If it independently
changes the basis of an existing result, integrate that basis where the result
already lives rather than appending a parallel section.

After recomposition, compare the final whole internally with the first draft.
Restore any load-bearing exact lock or participant distinction lost only through
rewriting. Do not preserve repetition merely because it was present before;
preserve the passage work it performed.

Use no predetermined number of recoveries, movements, roots, scenes,
paragraphs, or words. The final may be shorter, similar in extent, or longer
when a genuinely stronger architecture requires it. A complete first draft may
need almost no change.

Do not add a residual-findings section, recovery ledger, confidence tier,
catalogue, comparison, or methodological explanation. The recovered force must
alter the composition itself.
