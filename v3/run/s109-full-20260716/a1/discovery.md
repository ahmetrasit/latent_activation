# Whole-Passage Latent Discovery

## Passage-wide contour

The passage is launched as commanded speech: **قُلْ** is a second-person masculine singular imperative, and the vocative **يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ** is its quoted complement. Inside that commanded quotation, the deictic center changes immediately to first-person singular over against second-person plural. The middle is then built almost entirely from one root, **ع ب د**, but it continually changes the grammatical distribution of that root: first-person and second-person, finite verb and active participle, imperfect and one strategically placed perfect. The close leaves verbal action behind and gives two balanced verbless predications, each with a fronted lām phrase and a possessed **دِين**.

That contour matters because the lexical field does not merely move from one topic to another. It moves from an utterance, through repeated relations between actors and unnamed objects, into a paired allocation. The opening roots **ق و ل** and **ك ف ر** supply exposure, concealment, authoritative naming, and disavowal; the central **ع ب د** supplies submission, ownership, paths, endurance, and divergence; the final **د ي ن** retrospectively supplies obedience, judgment, debt, habit, domination, jurisdiction, and entrustment. Several of the strongest structures arise only when the final root is allowed to reopen the repeated middle and the opening address.

## Strong discovery: speech exposes a structure while leaving its objects covered

The first adjacency sets an exposure/concealment axis. **ق و ل:B001** (`root_001272/B001`) is the bringing of speech out in articulation, while **ك ف ر:B001** (`root_001307/B001`) is covering and concealment. The imperative does not merely mention speech; it requires a saying to emerge from a mouth. **ق و ل:B002** makes the tongue the instrument of that emergence, and **ق و ل:B012** supplies the rival state of a saying still held within and not manifested. Thus the movement of **قُلْ** is from possible inwardness to audible exteriority.

What emerges, however, does not uncover the concrete objects in the middle. Every object is **مَا**. According to the supplied syntax, each **مَا** is simultaneously governed by the matrix occurrence of **ع ب د** and serves as the fronted object within the relative clause containing another occurrence of **ع ب د**. The pattern is therefore repeatedly:

> ع ب د form - مَا - ع ب د form

The unnamed object is enclosed between two forms of the same root. Its lexical identity remains under the cover supplied by **ك ف ر:B001**, while its relational identity is disclosed with great precision. The speech reveals who is and is not connected to it, not what its name is. This activates **ق و ل:B016**, in which the “saying” of a thing is its definition or limit, and **ق و ل:B014**, in which a thing “says” by indicating. The unnamed **مَا** is defined by the actor whose **ع ب د**-relation identifies it; it indicates itself through a relation rather than through a noun.

The four negations intensify this selective disclosure. Each **لَا** negates the matrix relation, but the relative clause used to identify **مَا** is not itself negated. The passage therefore does not erase the entire field. It preserves one identifying relation inside each clause while refusing the crossing relation. Cover and disclosure coexist: the object stays unnamed, one attachment to it remains legible, and the opposed attachment is denied.

## Strong discovery: the middle is a system of crossed vectors, not a list of denials

The repeated **ع ب د - مَا - ع ب د** construction makes **مَا** a hinge shared by two participant structures. In the first direction, first-person matrix action is denied toward an object identified by second-person plural action. In the reverse direction, second-person plural participial predication is denied toward an object identified by first-person action. The passage then returns to the first-person side with an active participle and places the second-person action in the perfect, before repeating the reverse-direction line exactly.

This gives the middle the geometry of directed approaches that do not cross. **ع ب د:B010** (`root_000973/B010`) contributes people, things, and roads separating and going in different directions. It becomes passage-specific here because subject-object syntax supplies the directions, **مَا** supplies the attempted endpoint, and **لَا** blocks the opposed vector. **ع ب د:B005**, the trodden and leveled road, gives those vectors a path-like medium. Repetition makes the same grammatical route pass under the reader several times, while the exact recurrence of **وَلَا أَنتُمْ عَٰبِدُونَ مَا أَعْبُدُ** after the temporally different central line makes one route visibly return.

The direct distinction remains intact, but the secondary image is more exact than generic “separation”: two sets of actors repeatedly approach object-slots through the same lexical corridor, yet the negation prevents either corridor from becoming a shared crossing. The parties share grammar, root, and relational shape while not sharing the endpoint selected by that shape.

## Strong discovery: three different roots converge on one posture of submission

The opening designation, central action, and closing noun contain a distributed posture:

- **ك ف ر:B014** (`root_001307/B014`) supplies bodily lowering and submissive self-abasement.
- **ع ب د:B003** (`root_000973/B003`) supplies worship and obedience with submission.
- **د ي ن:B001** (`root_000504/B001`) supplies obedience, compliance, and yielding.

This coalition crosses all three major lexical stations after **قُلْ**. It makes the passage’s conflict less like the presence of submission on one side and its absence on the other. A posture or force of submission is latent in the very root used to name the addressees, explicit in the repeated central root, and available again in the closing **دِين**. What differs is direction, attachment, and governing center. The repeated **مَا** clauses are therefore not empty placeholders; they are the endpoints toward which a common posture is differently oriented.

The coalition is strengthened by morphology. Active participles turn **ع ب د** from a momentary event into a participant-characterization, while the closing nominal constructions stabilize **دِين** as a possessed domain. Submission moves from action, to kind of actor, to enduring affiliation. The sequence does not need to name the object literally for this scene to be complete: posture comes from **ك ف ر:B014**, enacted relation from **ع ب د:B003**, endpoint slots from **مَا**, and durable ordering from **د ي ن:B001**.

## Strong discovery: active service contains a passive ownership reversal

The central forms are grammatically active, but branches of **ع ب د** turn the apparent agent into a possible patient of power. **ع ب د:B001** supplies the owned slave in contrast with the free person; **ع ب د:B004** supplies the act of making another a slave or reducing another to slave-like labor. The same root can therefore display opposite sides of one hierarchy: the person who actively performs **ع ب د** may, in the latent participant structure, be the one owned or subdued, while another participant occupies the governing role.

The final root completes that reversal. **د ي ن:B004** supplies humiliation, coercive possession, enslavement, and ownership. Later **دِين** thus reactivates the central root not only through obedience but through the master/slave axis. The syntax of the close then makes a further transformation: the parties who could be heard as servants in the middle become grammatical possessors at the end. **لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ** marks the second-person possessor both before and after **دِين**, and **وَلِىَ دِينِ** does the same for first person through the lām phrase and possessive suffix. The potentially owned actor becomes the owner of a delimited **دِين**.

This does not cancel the submission reading. It reveals a role rotation: active worshipper, latent owned servant, and explicit possessor are superimposed. The unnamed **مَا** can consequently be heard as more than a passive grammatical object; it is also the unspoken center capable of governing the actor’s posture. **ع ب د:B006**, the honored, magnified, and served figure, supplies that complementary role without requiring the passage to name the figure.

## Strong discovery: repetition treads action into habit and solidity

The middle changes its temporal and nominal texture while repeating nearly the same words. It begins with imperfect verbs on both sides of **مَا**. It then places the second-person plural in an active-participial predication while retaining a first-person imperfect in the relative clause. Next it places first person in an active participle and second person in the perfect. It then restores the earlier second-person participial line exactly. Finally, the passage resolves into verbless nominal predications around **دِين**.

This progression activates **ع ب د:B005**, the road made level and tractable by being trodden. The repetition itself performs the treading: the same root and the same relational corridor are passed over until they acquire a stable shape. **د ي ن:B005**, habit, customary state, and familiar course, then gives the endpoint of that process. What had been repeatedly done or predicated can be heard at the close as an established way belonging to each party.

**ع ب د:B007**, strength, solidity, endurance, and persistence, adds a material transformation. The sequence moves from finite activity toward participial characterization and then toward nominal possession; what moves begins to set. This branch is not supported by a literal strong object, but it is supported by the hardening of grammatical aspect into repeated identity and by the exact return of the same line. The latent path is not merely traveled; repeated traversal makes it durable.

The sole perfect **عَبَدتُّمْ** prevents this temporal field from being flat. It inserts completed second-person action inside a first-person participial denial. The return to the earlier imperfect line after it surrounds completed action with a continuing or characteristic frame. Thus habit is made from both remembered completion and ongoing alignment, not from repetition alone.

## Strong discovery: the closing syntax physically encloses each dīn within its owner

The final balance has an unusually exact enclosure. In the first half, second-person plurality appears in **لَكُمْ** before **دِين** and again as the possessive suffix in **دِينُكُمْ**. In the second half, first person appears in **لِىَ** before **دِين** and again in the noun’s first-person possessive relation. Each noun is bracketed by the same owner:

> لكم - دين - كم  
> لي - دين - ي

The opening **ك ف ر:B001**, simple covering, is thereby reactivated at the far end as grammatical wrapping rather than semantic concealment. **ك ف ر:B010**, the sheath or envelope that covers fruit, offers a more specific but medium-strength texture for the same form: **دِين** is held inside a pronominal casing whose two sides agree. The branch need not turn **دِين** into literal fruit; what it contributes is an inside/outside structure and a fitted covering.

This final enclosure answers the middle enclosures. There, **مَا** is surrounded by two **ع ب د** forms belonging to opposed actors. At the end, **دِين** is surrounded not by opposed actors but twice by one owner. The unstable shared hinge of the middle becomes two separately closed domains. The ending therefore resolves the repeated question of crossing through a formal operation: each field is wrapped by its own participant markers.

## Strong discovery: naming becomes judgment, then allocation

The opening is a commanded act of classification. The syntax makes **ٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ** the appositional specification of the vocative addressees, while **قُلْ** governs the whole address as quoted content. **ك ف ر:B006** is precisely the branch of calling someone a kāfir, judging that person so, or attributing kufr. Joined to **ق و ل:B001**, the opening does not merely refer to a class; it stages the audible application of the designation.

Three roots then converge on judgment:

- **ق و ل:B010** (`root_001272/B010`) supplies exercising judgment or control over another.
- **ك ف ر:B006** supplies the judgment or attribution contained in naming someone kāfir.
- **د ي ن:B002** (`root_000504/B002`) supplies judgment, account, reckoning, recompense, and adjudication.

**ق و ل:B004**, the possessor of effective or penetrating speech, gives the pronouncement force, while **ق و ل:B016** gives it definitional boundaries. The repeated middle clauses can then be heard as the relational findings on which the final paired allocation rests: each object is identified by an attachment, each cross-attachment is denied, and each **دِين** is finally assigned to its possessor.

This is a latent tribunal structure, not a claim that the surface scene is literally a court. Its strength comes from role completion across roots: speech supplies pronouncement, the vocative supplies classified addressees, the middle supplies repeated relational tests, and **دِين** supplies judgment/account at the close.

## Strong-to-medium discovery: the close resembles a two-column account

**د ي ن:B003** supplies debt, lending, borrowing, deferred exchange, and mutual dealing. Once this branch is heard, the final parallelism resembles two columns: **لَكُمْ ... كُمْ** and **لِىَ ... ي**. Nothing is entered across the columns. The middle’s alternating first-person/second-person clauses repeatedly deny a transfer of relation from one side’s object to the other side’s actor. The end then places each **دِين** wholly within its own pronominal account.

**د ي ن:B002** adds reckoning and recompense to this ledger image, and the perfect **عَبَدتُّمْ** gives it one completed action that can be retrospectively “booked.” **د ي ن:B007**, entrusting a person to his own dīn and accepting or leaving him to it, supplies the settlement operation: after all cross-relations have been refused, each side is left to its own account/domain.

The financial reading remains secondary because the lām phrases are supplied syntactically as predicates and the suffixes as possessors, not as explicit loans. Yet the geometry is unusually supportive: paired parties, duplicated possession, a past item, repeated non-transfer, and a final noun whose accepted branch includes debt and mutual dealing. The branch changes the close from a static pair of labels into a settlement with no shared liability.

**ك ف ر:B009**, covering an offense until it is as though not done, can conditionally enter this account channel because an opening **ك ف ر** precedes the only perfect act and the final reckoning branch. But it should not be allowed to erase **عَبَدتُّمْ**: the passage retains that completed action grammatically and denies first-person participation in its object. The stronger image is separation of entries, not cancellation of the past.

## Strong discovery: disavowal develops into entrusted release

**ك ف ر:B005** supplies disavowal, renunciation, and dissociation. It is activated immediately by the content following the opening address: the voice repeatedly refuses cross-participation. Yet the passage does not end on **لَا**. The closing **د ي ن:B007** adds delegation, acceptance, and entrusting someone to his own dīn. Together these branches create an ordered transformation:

> disavowal of crossing -> repeated clarification of attachments -> leaving each party to its own domain

This explains why the closure belongs to the opening field. The latent force of **ك ف ر:B005** begins as withdrawal from another attachment; **د ي ن:B007** completes it as release or consignment rather than endless negation. The paired possessive syntax provides the two recipients of that release.

The transformation is compatible with **ع ب د:B010**: paths separate in their directions, but each remains a path. It is also compatible with **د ي ن:B005**: each separated direction can settle into its own accustomed course. Separation does not dissolve structure; it redistributes structure into two internally coherent sides.

## Medium-strong discovery: an unspoken court and polity is distributed across all four roots

A complete governance scene emerges from remote branches in passage order:

- **ق و ل:B004** supplies the qīl, a ruler whose word is effective.
- **ك ف ر:B015** supplies the crowning of a king and the covering crown itself.
- **ع ب د:B006** supplies the honored, magnified, and served figure, while **ع ب د:B001** supplies the owned servant.
- **د ي ن:B006** supplies the city as the place where obedience to those in authority is maintained.

The scene is not named on the surface, but its participant structure is precise. The imperative presupposes a commanding source; the quoted speaker becomes the instrument of an effective word; the central root distributes serving and served roles around unnamed objects; and the final noun can mark a jurisdiction of obedience. **د ي ن:B004** adds possession and subjugation to that jurisdiction.

The coalition makes the unnamed **مَا** audible as a possible center of authority without replacing its direct function as a relative object. The two parties are not merely performing different acts; they are aligned with distinct centers of service. The final possessives then look like jurisdictional boundaries. The crown from **ك ف ر:B015** is especially latent, but it is not isolated decoration: ruler, crown, served figure, servant, obedience, and city are supplied independently by the four roots in their textual order.

## Medium discovery: outlying settlement, road, and city form a blocked geography

A second political-spatial coalition uses different branches:

- **ك ف ر:B012** supplies a distant or cut-off tract, village, or place away from people.
- **ع ب د:B005** supplies the subdued, traveled road.
- **ع ب د:B010** supplies roads and groups dispersing toward different directions.
- **د ي ن:B006** supplies the city organized by obedience to authority.

The roots occur in exactly that broad sequence: the addressee designation at the opening, the repeated central **ع ب د** corridor, and **دِين** at the close. This permits an unspoken movement from outlying place, through road, toward civic jurisdiction. But the grammatical vectors do not arrive at one common city. Negation blocks cross-traffic, the road splits through **ع ب د:B010**, and the close yields two possessed domains.

**ك ف ر:B013**, a mountain pass, covered defile, or low wall, can add a conditional threshold to this geography. The repeated **مَا** hinges act like approached passes, while **لَا** prevents passage and the final possessives behave as boundary markers. Its support is weaker than the settlement-road-city chain because no literal elevation or wall appears, but its narrow-threshold geometry fits the blocked-crossing structure.

## Medium discovery: interruption is enclosed by return

The exact repetition of the second-person participial line on both sides of the first-person participial line forms the clearest local ring in the passage. The enclosed line contains the only perfect **عَبَدتُّمْ**. Thus a completed second-person action is set within a first-person characterization, and that entire unit is enclosed by an unchanged second-person characterization tied to first-person imperfect action.

**ع ب د:B011**, breakdown, stoppage, or a journey cut short, can enter the path channel here. The temporal route established by imperfects and participles is interrupted by the perfect, then the previous line resumes unchanged. **ك ف ر:B012** independently supplies a cut-off place, and **ك ف ر:B005** supplies relational dissociation. Together they make interruption more than a generic pause: it becomes a break in possible traffic between the two directional systems.

This remains medium because the perfect is completion, not lexical breakage. The branch contributes the shape of interruption and resumption, not a claim that **عَبَدتُّمْ** itself means failure. The enclosing repetition is what licenses the image.

## Medium discovery: the commanded saying is drawn into an “I”

The imperative **قُلْ** addresses a second-person singular, but the quotation it governs repeatedly speaks as first-person singular: **أَعْبُدُ**, **أَنَا**, **أَعْبُدُ**, **أَنَا**, **دِينِ**. This is a transfer of enunciative ownership. A word originating as an imperative to “you” becomes a declaration claimed by “I.”

**ق و ل:B006**, drawing a saying toward oneself, gives this shift a precise secondary motion. **ق و ل:B002**, the tongue as the instrument of saying, distinguishes the mouth that voices the declaration from the imperative source structurally presupposed by the command. **ق و ل:B001** completes the movement as audible articulation. The final first-person possessive on **دِين** then anchors the borrowed or commanded utterance in an explicitly possessed stance.

This does not require a theory about the unnamed source. The morphology alone supplies the second-person imperative and the internal first-person forms. The latent structure is that an externally directed saying becomes internally indexed speech.

## Medium discovery: saying becomes position, and position becomes bounded dīn

**ق و ل:B013** supplies “saying” as belief or doctrinal position rather than mere sound. **ق و ل:B015** adds sincere commitment or serious concern with a thing. These branches make the opening command govern more than vocal performance: the repeated first-person denials and final possession form a stated alignment.

At the close, **د ي ن:B001** supplies dīn as obedience, worship, confession, and way, while **د ي ن:B005** supplies habitual condition. The opening and ending therefore form a conceptual ring: a saying can be a position, and the final position is named and possessed as **دِين**. **ق و ل:B016** adds the limiting operation, so the utterance does not merely announce two positions; it draws their boundary through mirrored syntax.

This ring also explains the repetition. If **قُلْ** were only a momentary sound, one line might suffice. Through **ق و ل:B013**, repeated predications become the articulation of an enduring stance; through **د ي ن:B005**, that stance closes as an established course.

## Conditional discovery: obedience can be inverted by force

**ك ف ر:B007** supplies the specific transformation in which an obedient person is driven into disobedience. It meets **ع ب د:B003** and **د ي ن:B001**, both of which supply obedience and submission, while repeated **لَا** marks refused alignment. This creates a possible force channel: obedience is not merely absent but can be redirected or inverted under pressure.

The channel is lexically exact but structurally incomplete. No coercing participant is expressed inside the quotation, and the passage does not narrate a transition from obedience to disobedience. The branch can therefore contribute pressure and reversal to the submission axis, especially beside the opening imperative and **د ي ن:B004**’s coercive domination, but it cannot govern the whole reading.

## Weak but coherent discovery: successive clauses lay down a covering surface

**ك ف ر:B001** provides covering, while **ع ب د:B005** includes an animal or ship coated over with tar as well as the leveled road. The repeated **لَا ... ع ب د ... مَا ... ع ب د** clauses can be heard as successive layers laid over the same relational frame. The exact repeated line seals the central variation, and the final owner-markers wrap each **دِين**.

This texture is supported by recurrence and enclosure, not by a named material. It is therefore weaker than the path and pronominal-shell discoveries. Its useful contribution is material: the boundary is not only drawn as a line; repeated language thickens it into a surface.

## Weak and incomplete discovery: voiced declaration against an unvoiced response

Only one voice is commanded and supplied. The plural addressees are vocatively present and repeatedly predicated, but no reply from them appears. **ق و ل:B001** and **ق و ل:B012** provide the contrast between uttered and unuttered saying. **ع ب د:B008**, which includes pride, anger, hurt, regret, and a pride that leads to silence, can give the absent response an emotional edge.

The assignment must remain incomplete. Because **ع ب د** is repeated for both first-person and second-person relations, its anger/silence branch cannot securely be assigned only to the addressees, and silence by itself does not prove anger. What remains perceptible is an asymmetry of voicing: one side’s position is externally articulated, while the other side exists inside that articulation as addressed and described.

## Rival structures that remain active

### Negotiation versus pronouncement

**ق و ل:B009** supplies mutual discussion or negotiation over an affair. The alternating first-person/second-person clauses and final balanced allocation can initially resemble negotiated terms. Yet the passage contains no returned speech: it begins with a singular imperative and unfolds as one governed quotation. **ق و ل:B004** and **ق و ل:B010** favor effective pronouncement and judgment over mutual bargaining. The negotiation branch remains a useful rival because it highlights how formally reciprocal the content looks even while the speech situation is unilateral.

### Total separation versus a shared relational mold

**ك ف ر:B005**, **ع ب د:B010**, and **د ي ن:B007** strongly support disavowal, divergent ways, and release to separate domains. But the passage never gives the parties different grammatical machinery. Both are expressed through the same **ع ب د** root, the same **مَا** hinge, active participial forms, possessive suffixes, and the same noun **دِين**. The strongest whole-passage structure is therefore not absolute unrelatedness. It is non-crossing difference inside a rigorously shared mold.

### Static possession versus ongoing settlement

The verbless close can be heard as stable possession, especially after participles and **د ي ن:B005**. **د ي ن:B002**, **د ي ن:B003**, and **د ي ن:B007**, however, keep judgment, accounting, and entrustment active. The close both fixes a boundary and performs an allocation. Neither the static nor procedural image fully removes the other.

## Backward reactivation from the final word

Read backward, each branch of the repeated final root opens a different earlier channel:

- **د ي ن:B001** reactivates **ع ب د:B003** and **ك ف ر:B014** as one distributed obedience/submission posture.
- **د ي ن:B004** reactivates **ع ب د:B001**, **ع ب د:B004**, and **ع ب د:B006** as the owned servant, the act of enslavement, and the served master.
- **د ي ن:B005** turns the repeated central wording into **ع ب د:B005**’s trodden course and lets repetition be heard as habit formation.
- **د ي ن:B002** and **د ي ن:B003** reopen the single perfect action and the alternating pronouns as an account with separately assigned entries.
- **د ي ن:B006** reaches back through **ع ب د:B005** to **ك ف ر:B012** and **ق و ل:B004**, completing the outlying-place/road/city and ruler/jurisdiction coalitions.
- **د ي ن:B007** completes **ك ف ر:B005**: disavowal becomes entrustment of each side to its own field.

The final syntax also reaches all the way back to **ك ف ر:B001**. The passage opens with a root of covering, repeatedly keeps its objects unnamed under **مَا**, and closes by enclosing each **دِين** between matching possessor markers. At the same time, **ق و ل:B001** has made the boundary audible and **ق و ل:B016** has made it definitional. The closure belongs to the opening because it resolves the initial contest between saying and covering: the hidden objects are never uncovered, but the relations, directions, and limits around them become fully pronounced.

The most stable whole-passage result is therefore a set of intersecting structures rather than one total metaphor: exposed speech around covered objects; shared submission with opposed directions; active service reversing into ownership; repeated travel hardening into habit; judgment becoming a two-sided allocation; and disavowal maturing into entrusted separation. The same passage can sustain all of them because each is carried by repeated morphology and syntax while different accepted branches supply the otherwise unspoken medium, roles, force, and closure.
