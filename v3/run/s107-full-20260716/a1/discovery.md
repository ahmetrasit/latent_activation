# Whole-Passage Latent Discovery

## Passage-wide orientation

The passage is framed by one root of sight. It opens with `أَرَءَيْتَ`, a perfect verb addressed to a singular masculine hearer and preceded by an interrogative, and later returns to the same root in the plural imperfect `يُرَاءُونَ`. The first act of seeing is demanded of the addressee; the later act is managed by the people being described. Between those poles, the text identifies a singular figure through `ٱلَّذِى`, points him out with `فَذَٰلِكَ`, and then widens into the plural class `ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ`, twice resumed by `ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ`. The repeated relatives make conduct, rather than a supplied name, the means of recognition.

The grammatical motion is equally marked. `يَدُعُّ` takes the orphan directly as the object of force. `يَحُضُّ` would direct impulse `عَلَىٰ` the food closely bound by idafa to the poor person, but the verb is negated. The worshippers are positioned `عَن` their prayer. They then direct visibility toward other people through `يُرَاءُونَ`, while `يَمْنَعُونَ` places a final barrier before `ٱلْمَاعُونَ`. Thus the direct sequence already supplies a carrier geometry: force against a person, absent force toward provision, distance from prayer, projected appearance, and blocked utility. The lexical branches deepen that geometry without replacing it.

## Strong coalitions

### 1. The gaze reverses direction

The opening `أَرَءَيْتَ` can carry both actual or discerning sight (`ر ء ي`, `root_000531/B001`) and the alerting or eliciting force of *arāʾayta*, “tell me / attend to this” (`ر ء ي`, `root_000531/B013`). Because the relative clause is its direct object, the hearer is not merely shown a face; the hearer is asked to recognize a person through a pattern of acts. At the far side of the passage, `يُرَاءُونَ` activates the sharply specified branch of doing something so that people will see it (`ر ء ي`, `root_000531/B005`). The same root therefore changes participant structure across the passage:

- at the opening, an addressee must see through conduct;
- near the closure, the described people arrange conduct around being seen.

This is more than repetition. The first sight is diagnostic and penetrative; the second is produced and outward-facing. The reciprocal-facing branch (`ر ء ي`, `root_000531/B004`) remains audible behind the form: a field in which parties might genuinely face and see one another has contracted into a one-way presentation of the self. The direct sense of display remains primary, but the branch makes the loss of reciprocity perceptible.

Two distant roots sharpen what kind of sight is at issue. `سَاهُونَ` carries the image of the tiny, extremely faint star that is missed by sight (`س ه و`, `root_003249/B005`), while `ٱلْمَاعُونَ` can be the slight, easy, low-risk thing (`م ع ن`, `root_004706/B003`). The passage thus places conspicuous self-presentation beside failure over what is faint or small. The singular orphan also carries solitary, counterpart-less existence (`ي ت م`, `root_001692/B002`). What demands the opening gaze is not the enlarged public performance but the lone person, the easily missed need, and the minor utility at the edge of attention.

The ending makes the contrast still more exact. One branch of `م ع ن` is water that both appears and runs (`root_004706/B001`). The performers make themselves visible, yet withhold an object whose lexical field includes something naturally visible and flowing. Manufactured visibility circulates; useful visibility and flow do not.

### 2. Witness, credit, account, and the withheld small loan

The opening phrase places contrary juridical acts next to one another. `كَذَّبَ` can mean assigning a statement or person to falsehood (`ك ذ ب`, `root_001290/B002`), whereas one branch of `دِين` is crediting a person in judgment or oath and entrusting the matter to that person’s *dīn* (`د ي ن`, `root_000504/B007`). The phrase `يُكَذِّبُ بِٱلدِّينِ` therefore contains, beneath its direct proposition, a collision between discrediting and crediting.

Other `د ي ن` branches extend that collision across the passage. The root can image judgment, reckoning, requital, and settlement (`root_000504/B002`), as well as debt, lending, borrowing, deferred exchange, and repayment (`root_000504/B003`). At the closure, `ٱلْمَاعُونَ` supplies the concrete small-scale counterpart: a household benefit, gift, charitable due, or object lent for use (`م ع ن`, `root_004706/B005`). `يَمْنَعُونَ` then activates the hand held back from giving (`م ن ع`, `root_001448/B001`). The opening’s account or debt is thus reactivated at the end as the refused micro-loan. A large field of judgment and obligation is tested at the scale of a pot, bowl, bucket, tool, or other ordinary benefit.

The middle participates in this economic ring. `طَعَامِ ٱلْمِسْكِينِ` is an idafa, so the food is grammatically bound to the poor person rather than left as an unattached commodity. `ط ع م` can also denote livelihood, provision, a source of income, estate, levy, or revenue (`root_000934/B004`). This broadens the chain from account and debt, through sustenance and livelihood, to the smallest lendable household benefit. It does not by itself prove a legal claim, but it makes the absence of transfer sound less like failure to add a surplus kindness and more like an unsettled relation.

The opening observer also occupies the place of a witness. The text asks the addressee to see and then supplies behaviors as evidence; later the actors try to govern what witnesses see. The reckoning branch of `د ي ن` (`root_000504/B002`) and the staged-visibility branch of `ر ء ي` (`root_000531/B005`) meet here: the people under recognition attempt to control the evidentiary surface, but the final withheld object supplies evidence outside the staged act.

Finally, `د ي ن` can mean habit, accustomed condition, or repeated way (`root_000504/B005`). The imperfect verbs and the active participles `ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ` and `سَاهُونَ` support that branch. The passage presents not one accidental lapse but an inhabitable pattern: discrediting, repelling, not urging, being away, displaying, and withholding form a repeated economy.

### 3. The orphan’s missing household is hidden inside the aggressor’s vocabulary

The strongest role-completion occurs around `يَدُعُّ ٱلْيَتِيمَ`. Directly, `د ع ع` supplies a severe, rough push (`root_000477/B001`), while `ي ت م` supplies a child cut off from the person who would maintain him (`root_001692/B001`). Yet another branch of the verb’s own root denotes a man’s small dependents or young household (`د ع ع`, `root_000477/B009`). The verb that expels the orphan thus contains a latent image of the dependent children who belong to someone’s care. The missing household role is not imported from outside the passage; it is carried by the aggressor’s verb.

The same root contains a second reversal. `د ع ع` can be the repeated call addressed to one who has stumbled: rise, recover, be lifted (`root_000477/B004`). In the direct construction, the force does not restore the fallen person; it is applied roughly to the vulnerable object. The root offers both a raising voice and a repelling hand, and the passage selects the latter. This opposition is reinforced by `لَا يَحُضُّ`: the person who applies force does not apply the urging force that could move others toward food. Force is available, but its participant and direction are inverted.

The later vocabulary completes the household that the orphan lacks. `س ك ن` can image dwelling (`root_000726/B002`) and the household members who inhabit it (`root_000726/B003`). `م ع ن` can also name a dwelling (`root_004706/B006`), while its `ماعون` branch names the useful objects circulating within and between homes (`root_004706/B005`). The passage moves from a child cut off from a maintainer, through a root that contains dependents, to household inhabitants, dwelling, and household goods. It assembles a home lexically while depicting exclusion behaviorally.

The closing root supplies the orphan’s other missing role: protection. `م ن ع` can be protective strength, an inaccessible stronghold, or kin who surround and defend a person (`root_001448/B003`). On the surface that root does not protect the exposed person; it withholds the household benefit. Protective power has turned inward, becoming a wall around possession rather than a wall around vulnerability. `د ي ن` as subjugation, ownership, and the imposing of what is hateful (`root_000504/B004`) and `س ك ن` as poverty, weakness, and abasement (`root_000726/B006`) add the vertical power relation: force descends toward the unprotected, while protection and goods remain on the stronger side.

### 4. A blocked social circulation

The final two words form a precise latent mechanism. `م ن ع` can be a barrier interposed between a person and the thing sought (`root_001448/B002`); `م ع ن` can be running water and its channels (`root_004706/B001`). Read together, `يَمْنَعُونَ ٱلْمَاعُونَ` can sound like a dam placed across a current. The direct object may remain an ordinary useful benefit, but the water branch supplies its motion and medium.

That dam reorganizes the earlier actions. `د ع ع` gives strong outward thrust (`root_000477/B001`). `ح ض ض` gives urging and can include reciprocal encouragement, each person moving a companion (`root_000334/B001`). `ي ت م` gives severance (`root_001692/B001`). `س ك ن` can give the cessation of movement (`root_000726/B001`), and `س ه و` can also give stillness (`root_003249/B002`). The sequence is therefore not simply a list of moral failures; it is a distribution system in which:

- a person is forcibly displaced;
- social impulse toward food is absent;
- the worshippers stand away from prayer through `عَن`;
- appearance is projected outward;
- the small useful current is finally stopped.

The system is active, but its useful circulation is inactive. It spends force on exclusion, spends visibility on the self, and places stillness or blockage where food, reassurance, and tools might pass.

There is also a more concrete, medium-strength subimage. `د ع ع` can mean shaking a measure, container, or sack until it takes the thing fully and becomes packed or filled (`root_000477/B002`). The next line supplies food, and the ending’s `ماعون` branch supplies pots, bowls, and buckets (`م ع ن`, `root_004706/B005`). Later wording therefore gives the early branch both content and container. The resulting contrast is exact but latent: force that could mobilize a vessel for filling is, in the direct clause, exerted on the orphan, while the vessels and benefits that could carry provision are withheld.

### 5. Obedience and prayer form another beginning-to-end ring

The account-and-loan ring is not the only ring carried by `دِين` and `مَاعُون`. `د ي ن` can be obedience, submission, worship, and what one follows as a religious way (`root_000504/B001`). The middle names the practitioners and their prayer; `ص ل و` supplies the specified ritual of standing, bowing, and prostration (`root_000879/B003`). At the end, the accepted `ماعون` branch itself includes obedience as well as charity and household benefit (`م ع ن`, `root_004706/B005`). The passage begins with a root of obedience, passes through performed prayer, and closes with an object whose branch again contains obedience.

This ring prevents prayer from becoming an isolated ritual compartment. Another branch of `ص ل و` is prayer directed for another, blessing, mercy, intercession, and favorable acknowledgment (`root_000879/B002`). `س ك ن`, present in `ٱلْمِسْكِينِ`, names what the self rests in and explicitly includes prayer as reassurance for others (`root_000726/B004`). These branches give prayer an outward participant and an outcome: another person receives prayer, mercy, or repose. By contrast, the surface worshippers are `عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ`, and the outward relation that does remain is `يُرَاءُونَ`, directed toward viewers. Prayer’s possible benefit to another has been replaced by another’s gaze as a benefit to the performer. The final withholding confirms that the relational content of obedience has not circulated.

## Medium and conditional coalitions

### 6. The house, hearth, threshold, and implements

A detailed domestic scene accumulates in passage order even though no complete house is named on the surface. `طَعَام` supplies eating and what answers hunger (`ط ع م`, `root_000934/B001`). `مِسْكِين` carries dwelling, household, and the thing or fire by which one finds repose (`س ك ن`, `root_000726/B002`, `root_000726/B003`, `root_000726/B004`). `مُصَلِّين` carries meeting fire, warming oneself, roasting, and undergoing heat (`ص ل و`, `root_000879/B001`). `سَاهُون` carries the *suhwa*, a space before the house or crossed supports on which goods are placed (`س ه و`, `root_003249/B004`). `مَاعُون` then supplies the pot, bowl, bucket, axe, blade, and other shared household implements (`م ع ن`, `root_004706/B005`), as well as the separate dwelling branch (`root_004706/B006`). `س ك ن` adds provisions or pasture sufficient to keep a household settled (`root_000726/B010`).

Together these branches produce food, residents, a place of repose, fire for warmth or cooking, a house-front rack or threshold, and domestic tools. This coalition matters because it joins the apparent shift from the orphan and poor person to the worshippers. Lexically, the passage has not left provision for a ritual abstraction; it has walked deeper into the house. The household apparatus is present, but the person is pushed away, no one is mobilized toward the food, and the implements do not cross the boundary.

The fire branch supports a rival activation. In the domestic coalition, `ص ل و` (`root_000879/B001`) contributes hearth and roasting. Because the branch also includes suffering heat and hardship, its position immediately after `فَوَيْلٌ` can instead turn the same fire into threatened ordeal. These are not two generalized meanings blended together; they are two outcomes already held within the accepted fire-contact branch. The passage leaves unresolved whether the denied hearth returns as afflicting heat, but the thermal reversal is available.

### 7. Severance and the unrealized graft

`ي ت م` makes the orphan a child cut off from a maintainer (`root_001692/B001`) and, more generally, a solitary thing without a counterpart (`root_001692/B002`). Later, `ط ع م` supplies the unexpectedly precise image of joining a branch from another tree to a branch that accepts the graft (`root_000934/B010`). `ح ض ض` can supply mutual encouragement between companions (`root_000334/B001`), `د ي ن` can supply giving and taking on credit (`root_000504/B003`), and `مَاعُون` can supply the lendable object (`م ع ن`, `root_004706/B005`). These are several distinct mechanisms of reconnection: grafting, mutual urging, exchange, and lending.

The direct sequence negates or reverses every mechanism. The severed person is pushed, urging is absent, and the lendable benefit is stopped. This makes a latent repair scene perceptible without asserting that the surface explicitly speaks of adoption or grafting. The coalition is medium rather than certain because the botanical participant remains unspoken, but its role completion is unusually exact: a branch of the food root offers accepted attachment to the figure defined by lost attachment.

### 8. Appearance as a surface that can falsify

`ك ذ ب` includes a colored or patterned cloth that “lies by its condition,” appearing ornamented as what it is not (`root_001290/B009`). `ر ء ي` includes mirror, appearance, handsome outward aspect, and the sign visible on a face (`root_000531/B006`), while `يُرَاءُونَ` directly activates acting for people’s sight (`root_000531/B005`). These three branches can form a textile-and-mirror image: a composed visible surface covers or redirects judgment about the underlying state.

This remains secondary because no cloth or mirror is named. Its strength comes from the exact completion of roles: `ك ذ ب` supplies the deceptive surface, `ر ء ي` supplies both the surface’s appearance and its observer, and the later form supplies the act of presenting it. The opening command to see then asks for a sight not arrested by that surface.

### 9. Prayer as a set display or snare

One branch of `ص ل و` names a trap set so that bird or prey falls into it, and can extend to arranging an affair in order to bring someone into destruction (`root_000879/B004`). Joined to public display (`ر ء ي`, `root_000531/B005`) and the deceptive visible surface (`ك ذ ب`, `root_001290/B009`), it offers a conditional image of prayer arranged as an apparatus for catching sight.

The coalition should not be promoted into a claim that the worshippers are literally trapping a named victim; the passage supplies no such victim. Its narrower contribution is viable: the visible act may be spatially “set” for an audience rather than relationally directed toward mercy, food, or utility. The trap’s missing prey keeps this discovery incomplete.

### 10. Intake turned into constriction

The food root has a violent branch: taking a person by the throat and squeezing it in strangling or combat (`ط ع م`, `root_000934/B012`). That image can join the rough push (`د ع ع`, `root_000477/B001`) and the intervening barrier (`م ن ع`, `root_001448/B002`). Food ordinarily supplies something tasted and taken in (`ط ع م`, `root_000934/B001`); the secondary branch turns the same bodily passage into a point of constriction.

This is a conditional bodily analogue of the larger circulation pattern: nourishment does not enter, force reaches the vulnerable body, and a conduit is blocked. It is weaker than the water-and-barrier coalition because the throat participant is not named and the branch belongs to combat language, but it preserves a precise transformation of medium from feeding to choking rather than merely repeating a theme of harm.

## Weak but ordered constellation

### 11. A stalled course or race

Several remote branches produce an unusually ordered motion chain:

- `ك ذ ب` can image a wild animal that runs a stretch, stops, and looks behind (`root_001290/B007`), joining motion to the opening field of sight;
- `د ع ع` can be running marked by slowness and twisting (`root_000477/B005`);
- `ي ت م` can be deficiency or negligence in travel (`root_001692/B003`) and delay in a course, including the delay of care (`root_001692/B004`);
- `ح ض ض` supplies urging (`root_000334/B001`);
- `ط ع م` can mean asking a horse for its run (`root_000934/B009`);
- `ص ل و` names the second horse whose head follows at the first horse’s hindquarters (`root_000879/B006`);
- `س ه و` can supply stillness (`root_003249/B002`);
- `م ع ن` can image a horse going far in its run (`root_004706/B002`).

The sequence moves from start-stop and backward-looking motion, through twisted slowness and urging, into the race’s follower, stillness, and finally the capacity to go far. Because neither horse nor race is surface-named, this must remain weak and conditional. Yet it is more than a loose motion theme: multiple roots supply successive phases, participants, and changes of speed. If active, it gives temporal shape to the direct failures. The passage’s ongoing verbs look active, but the latent course repeatedly stalls, lags, looks toward observers, or has its far-going endpoint enclosed in the thing withheld.

A rival reading is possible. The `مُصَلِّي` branch (`ص ل و`, `root_000879/B006`) could mark following rather than lagging: the second horse is still directly on the leader’s trace. On that reading, the problem is not mere delay but following a visible line without arriving at the passage’s practical endpoint. The evidence does not decide between stalled motion and imitative following, so both remain subordinate.

## Backward reactivation

Replayed from the end, `ٱلْمَاعُونَ` is not a small afterthought. Its branch inventory reopens nearly every earlier field:

- as a lent household benefit (`م ع ن`, `root_004706/B005`), it returns to `دِين` as debt and credit (`د ي ن`, `root_000504/B003`);
- as charity or obedience (`م ع ن`, `root_004706/B005`), it returns through prayer to `دِين` as obedience (`د ي ن`, `root_000504/B001`);
- as pot, bowl, bucket, or tool, it returns through the *suhwa* before the house (`س ه و`, `root_003249/B004`) to the dwelling and household carried by `مِسْكِين` (`س ك ن`, `root_000726/B002`, `root_000726/B003`);
- as flowing water (`م ع ن`, `root_004706/B001`), it turns `يَمْنَعُونَ` into a barrier and reinterprets every earlier displacement or stillness as part of a circulation problem;
- as an easy, slight thing (`م ع ن`, `root_004706/B003`), it joins the faint star missed by sight (`س ه و`, `root_003249/B005`) and redirects the opening `أَرَءَيْتَ` toward what public vision discounts.

The house then reopens the orphan: the verb that pushes him carries small dependents (`د ع ع`, `root_000477/B009`), while the orphan is cut off from a caretaker (`ي ت م`, `root_001692/B001`). The barrier reopens protection: the withholding root could instead surround and defend (`م ن ع`, `root_001448/B003`). The tiny loan reopens the account: `دِين` can be reckoning and debt (`root_000504/B002`, `root_000504/B003`). The account finally reopens the initial act of sight as witness and discernment (`ر ء ي`, `root_000531/B001`, `root_000531/B013`).

The closure therefore belongs to the opening field. The passage begins by demanding recognition and ends by naming the smallest blocked transfer through which recognition becomes possible. Its strongest latent axes all share that reversal:

- sight becomes self-display while faint need goes unseen;
- force that could lift or urge becomes repulsion;
- protection becomes exclusion;
- household belonging becomes severance;
- prayer that could carry mercy or repose becomes an arranged appearance;
- debt, lending, water, food, and ordinary utility approach circulation and are stopped.

No single secondary metaphor exhausts the passage. The witness-and-account ring, the household scene, the hydraulic mechanism, and the weaker course image remain distinct. Their convergence lies in participant direction: resources and attention are repeatedly turned toward the already visible actor, while the solitary person and the small transferable good are left outside the operative channel.
