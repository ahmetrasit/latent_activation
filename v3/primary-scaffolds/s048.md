# Primary Scaffold: Surah 48

Surah 48 opens by declaring a clear victory granted to the Prophet, tied to forgiveness, completion of divine favor, guidance, and mighty help. It then describes tranquility sent into believers' hearts so their faith increases, while hypocrites and polytheists who harbor evil assumptions face divine anger and punishment. The passage frames the Prophet as witness, bearer of good news, and warner, and presents allegiance to him as allegiance to God.

The surah turns to those Bedouins who stayed behind, exposing their excuses and their misunderstanding of God's power, then distinguishes those excused by weakness or illness from those who refuse obedience. It recalls God's pleasure with the believers at the pledge under the tree and the near victory and spoils promised to them. It also explains that God restrained the hands of enemies and believers from one another at Mecca, so that unknown believing men and women would not be harmed, and so that God could admit whom He willed into mercy.

The passage closes by affirming the truth of the Prophet's vision of entering the Sacred Mosque securely, with heads shaved or shortened, and by naming the religion sent to prevail over all religion. It ends with Muhammad as God's messenger and his companions as firm against disbelievers, merciful among themselves, visibly marked by prostration, growing from earlier scriptural images like a crop that strengthens and pleases its sowers, while enraging disbelievers. The final promise joins forgiveness and great reward for those who believe and do righteous deeds.
