# Primary Scaffold: Surah 18:83-110

With the opening invocation included as context, this passage answers the question about Dhu al-Qarnayn by saying that God established him on earth and gave him means to everything. He follows successive ways: first to the setting place of the sun, where he finds a people and judges between punishment for wrongdoing and good treatment for belief and righteousness; then to the rising place of the sun, where he finds a people with no covering from it; then between two barriers, where he meets a people barely understanding speech who ask for help against Gog and Magog.

Dhu al-Qarnayn refuses tribute as his real support and asks instead for labor, building a barrier of iron and molten copper that Gog and Magog cannot scale or pierce. He attributes the barrier to mercy from his Lord and says that when his Lord's promise comes, it will be leveled, for the divine promise is true. The passage then moves to the final scene: people surging together, the trumpet blown, Hell displayed to disbelievers whose eyes were covered from remembrance and who could not hear.

It rejects the idea that disbelievers can take God's servants as protectors besides Him and declares their works lost, especially those whose effort is astray while they think they are doing well. Their denial of God's signs and meeting nullifies their deeds. In contrast, those who believe and do righteous deeds receive the gardens of Paradise as hospitality, abiding there without desiring transfer. The passage closes by saying that if the sea were ink for the words of the Lord, the sea would be exhausted before those words ended, even with another like it added, and by commanding the Prophet to declare that he is only a human like them, receiving revelation that their God is one God. Whoever hopes to meet his Lord should do righteous work and associate no one in worship of his Lord.
