# A2 Alternative Follow-Up: Compact Turkish Gold Synthesis

Continue in the same A2 session. The passage-scale mechanism map is complete. Render it as a compact, fluent Turkish publication without reducing the discovery field or changing its epistemic shape.

This is an alternative publication prompt. It does not replace the canonical prompt and does not alter discovery, scaffold integration, mechanism mapping, or grading.

## Epistemic continuity

Treat the mechanism map as the complete discovery reservoir. Do not rediscover the passage, re-audit the evidence, or impose a stricter local-sense gate during rendering.

Preserve every distinct graded finding that contributes a new lexical or compositional insight. Do not omit a finding merely because it is unusual, secondary, weak, or conditional. When compact inclusion and silent omission are equally defensible, include the finding with its grade. Never promote a conditional finding to certainty for rhetorical force.

Compression applies to exposition, not to discovery. The publication may merge genuinely overlapping observations, but it must not erase their epistemic distinctions or make a rich mechanism look artificially sparse.

Use labels in this form:

```text
[GÜÇLÜ / A — concise finding description]
[ORTA / B — concise finding description]
[ZAYIF / C-koşullu — concise finding description]
```

`GÜÇLÜ / ORTA / ZAYIF` describes explicit support in the supplied lexical record. `A / B / C / C-koşullu` describes activation in this passage. Preserve these dimensions. The label already performs much of the cautionary work; do not surround every medium or conditional finding with repeated generic disclaimers.

## Publication form

Write:

1. A precise, memorable title.
2. A short contextual opening that gives the passage's direct movement once.
3. Labeled findings ordered from the strongest governing coalitions through secondary and conditional discoveries.
4. A concise final replay that follows the passage in order and lets the interacting field assemble and close.

Lead with the strongest non-obvious discovery, not with a long paraphrase or methodological preface. There is no fixed word count and no finding quota.

Each finding should normally form one compact paragraph. Within that paragraph:

- state the surprising relation immediately;
- name only the decisive lexical joins that make it passage-specific;
- show what the relation changes in the hearing of the whole passage;
- stop when the discovery and its consequence are clear.

Use more than one paragraph only when an indispensable internal transformation would otherwise become unreadable. Do not reproduce the mechanism map's full branch inventory, all rival formulations, or every supporting structural observation. Those remain available in the mechanism map.

## Inclusive compression

Make the prose shorter by removing repetition, not findings.

- State the primary meaning once and preserve it throughout; do not repeatedly say that a secondary image is not the literal translation.
- Explain a lexical branch where it first becomes decisive; later references should use the established image without proving it again.
- Let the grade carry calibrated uncertainty. Add a prose caveat only when it identifies the particular missing link or rival reading.
- Give central findings enough room to become memorable. Keep secondary findings visible but proportionate to their activation grade.
- Do not turn every morphology, syntax, voice, tense, or repetition observation into an independent finding. Use it inside the lexical coalition it strengthens.
- Prefer one exact concrete relation over a list of loosely related branch possibilities.

The intended result is abundant in discoveries but economical in explanation.

## Whole-passage synthesis

Do not organize by ayah, pericope, or section. Do not produce local summaries and stitch them together.

Preserve multiple channels when the mechanism map supports them. Do not force them into one total metaphor. Show how the strongest coalitions connect distant positions, transform earlier words retrospectively, interact with rival or supporting axes, and explain why the passage closes where it does.

The final replay is not a second summary of every finding. Move through the passage once, carrying only the transformations already established, so that sequence and closure become perceptible without re-listing the evidence.

## Turkish prose

Write idiomatic, reader-facing Turkish with concrete nouns and active verbs. Prefer the language of the discovered scene to abstract analytical narration.

For example, prefer a sentence shaped like:

```text
“Nimet” kökündeki kiriş, “dosdoğru” kökündeki makarayı taşır.
```

over one shaped like:

```text
İkinci söz alanı, ilk mekanizmada açık kalan taşıyıcı rolü tamamlar.
```

Avoid letting phrases such as `söz alanı`, `rol tamamlamak`, `mekanizma`, `işleyiş alanı`, `karşı alan`, `yerel fail`, or `koşullu uzmanlaşma` dominate the prose. Use them only when no natural concrete expression is clearer. Avoid translated English syntax, unnecessary nominalization, and workflow language.

Do not expose prompts, agents, file paths, branch IDs, evidence codes, or English confidence labels. Arabic expressions and transliteration may appear sparingly when they let the reader hear the decisive lexical relation.

Do not invent a claim absent from the mechanism map. Write only the complete publication to the output path named in the task.
