# A1: Whole-Passage Latent Discovery

## Task

Discover the latent lexical structures that become available as this passage unfolds. The object is not a collection of root notes and not a direct commentary. Find cross-root coalitions, image chains, governing channels, intersecting axes, mirrored roles, transformations, rings, and later wording that reactivates earlier wording.

The direct contextual meaning must remain possible throughout, but this turn deliberately precedes the independent primary scaffold. Do not replace the passage with a secondary image and do not suppress a branch merely because it is not the local contextual sense. A supplied root branch may contribute secondary geometry, motion, force, texture, medium, participant structure, or temporal shape.

## Evidence

Use only the files named in the task:

- `passage-arabic.txt` controls wording, sequence, repetition, and boundaries.
- `morphology.tsv` provides positioned forms and grammatical features.
- `syntax.tsv` provides local structural relations.
- `lexical-branches.jsonl` provides the complete accepted, uncontaminated branch inventory. Each record has only `root_id`, `root_norm`, `branch_id`, `what_is_ar`, `branch_image_ar`, and `source_phrase_ar`.

Treat each lexical branch as its own image. Never blend all branches of a root into a generalized root meaning. Do not use translations, prior outputs, the gold reference, external sources, or remembered interpretations as evidence.

## Discovery movement

First follow the passage forward. Attend to who speaks, who is addressed, what actions and roles appear, what remains unresolved, where wording repeats, where direction or grammatical stance changes, and what later language causes an earlier word to sound differently.

Read every supplied branch once with full attention. This is exposure, not a demand to manufacture a finding from every branch. A promising seed may arise from a branch, a construction, morphology, adjacency, contrast, repetition, an endpoint, or a boundary.

Deepen selectively. Continue an image when another precise passage element:

- supplies a missing participant or role;
- changes its direction, force, medium, scale, texture, or outcome;
- creates a specific contrast or symmetry;
- makes a distant earlier or later expression newly relevant;
- joins an independently emerging channel.

Do not stop at shared themes. Let each recruited branch change the developing image and what you look for next. Keep several channels separate when the passage supports several; do not force everything into one totalizing metaphor.

The second contact need not be a literal surface noun. One latent branch may become passage-specific when branches of other present roots supply complementary participants, actions, objects, directions, textures, or outcomes and the passage sequence gives those roles an intelligible order. Search for those distributed coalitions before deciding that a remote branch is merely decorative.

Do not lower a coalition solely because its literal scene is unnamed on the surface. Latent activation is precisely the capacity of several root branches to make an otherwise unspoken scene or mechanism perceptible beneath the direct proposition. Strength comes from specificity, role completion, cross-root convergence, and ordered reactivation. The direct meaning must remain intact, but surface literalization is not required.

After the forward reading, replay the passage backward in thought. Ask which endings reopen beginnings, which later roots complete earlier latent roles, which adjacent phrases make a remote branch audible, and why the closure belongs to the opening field.

Morphology and syntax shape these discoveries. They are not preliminary permission gates that make secondary resonance impossible. Passage order and boundaries matter as activation relations, not as containers for separate summaries.

Obvious grammatical observations are supporting carriers, not the center of this notebook. Do not let pronoun shifts, tense, voice, repetition, or direct paraphrase displace the non-obvious lexical discoveries they help activate.

## Whole-passage requirement

Do not write verse-by-verse commentary, pericope summaries, section summaries, or a sequence of paraphrases. Do not produce several local summaries and join them at the end. Local observations are welcome, including weak and incomplete ones, but the main work must discover relations spanning and reorganizing the passage as a whole.

## Output

Write an expansive analytical notebook to the output path named in the task. Preserve exact branch IDs whenever a branch contributes. Describe how each substantial image forms, how multiple roots enter it, where it appears in passage order, and what it makes perceptible in the whole.

Give priority to discoveries that would remain inaudible in a direct scaffold: secondary branches that converge across roots, surprising lexical reciprocities, hidden scenes, and axes that connect distant wording. Direct contextual structure should support those discoveries rather than become a substitute for them.

Preserve strong, medium, weak, conditional, rival, and incomplete discoveries. Do not record failed seeds, branch-coverage bookkeeping, workflow narration, or an audit trail. Do not write the final Turkish publication yet.
