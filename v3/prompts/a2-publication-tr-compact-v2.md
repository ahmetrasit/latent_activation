# A2 Alternative Renderer V2: Dense Turkish Gold Synthesis

The passage-scale mechanism map is complete. Render it as a compact, fluent Turkish publication while preserving the breadth and grading of the discovery field. Whether this is the continuing A2 session or a fresh renderer, use the listed work products rather than beginning a new discovery pass.

This is an alternative publication prompt. It does not replace the canonical prompt and does not alter discovery, scaffold integration, mechanism mapping, or epistemic grading.

## Invariant

Compress exposition, not discovery.

Every distinct graded finding that contributes a lexical or compositional insight must remain visible either as a governing finding or as a compact complementary finding. Do not omit a finding merely because it is unusual, weak, secondary, or conditional. Do not promote it for rhetorical force. When in doubt between compact inclusion and omission, preserve it with its grade.

Do not rediscover the passage, re-audit the mechanism map, impose a new local-sense gate, or invent claims absent from the supplied work products.

Use the two grading dimensions exactly:

```text
[GÜÇLÜ / A — concise finding description]
[ORTA / B — concise finding description]
[ZAYIF / C-koşullu — concise finding description]
```

`GÜÇLÜ / ORTA / ZAYIF` describes explicit lexical-record support. `A / B / C / C-koşullu` describes passage activation. The grade itself carries calibrated caution.

## Form

Write:

1. A precise, memorable title.
2. One short contextual opening that gives the direct passage movement.
3. `## Ana Bulgular`: the governing discoveries in compact gold-style paragraphs.
4. `## Tamamlayıcı Bulgular`: every remaining distinct graded discovery in compressed form.
5. `## Son Geçiş`: one concise forward replay of the whole passage.

There is no fixed word count and no finding quota. Density must come from hierarchy, merging, and sentence economy rather than deletion.

## Ana Bulgular

A governing finding changes how distant parts of the passage are heard together, establishes a major channel or axis, performs a strong retrospective transformation, or materially explains sequence and closure.

Give each governing finding a vivid label and normally one compact paragraph. State the surprising relation immediately, name only the decisive lexical joins, and end with its passage-scale consequence. Do not reproduce the mechanism map's branch inventory or all of its supporting observations.

When several graded observations are extensions of the same governing channel, merge them rather than giving each a separate heading. Preserve their distinctions with a composite label or brief inline grade, for example:

```text
[GÜÇLÜ / A çekirdek + ORTA / B uzantı — concise shared description]
```

Merging must not silently upgrade, downgrade, or erase an observation.

## Tamamlayıcı Bulgular

Keep all remaining graded findings visible without turning them into full essays.

Each independent complementary finding receives its label and normally one sentence that states:

- the concrete lexical convergence;
- the limited contribution it makes to an established channel.

Use a second short sentence only when the particular missing link or rival interpretation must be named. Do not repeat generic statements such as “the passage does not literally mention this” after the grade has already marked the activation as `C` or `C-koşullu`.

Related complementary findings with the same function may share one compact paragraph. Keep their individual grades visible inline when they differ. A detail that merely illustrates an already graded finding should be folded into that finding rather than receiving another full exposition.

The result must remain generous in discoveries but light in explanatory mass.

## Synthesis

Preserve the direct contextual proposition throughout. State once that secondary activations do not replace it; do not repeat that disclaimer finding by finding.

Do not organize by ayah, pericope, or section. Do not write local summaries and stitch them together. Preserve multiple channels without forcing one total metaphor. Let governing findings connect distant positions; let the complementary findings retain genuine peripheral discoveries without controlling the whole.

Use syntax, voice, tense, adjacency, and repetition inside the lexical coalitions they strengthen. Do not publish ordinary structural observations as standalone findings unless they unlock a non-obvious lexical mechanism.

The final replay must move through the passage once. Carry forward only transformations already established; do not re-list labels, branch evidence, or all complementary findings.

## Turkish prose

Write idiomatic, listener-facing Turkish with concrete nouns and active verbs. Prefer the discovered scene to abstract analytical narration. The publication will be converted to speech.

Preserve Arabic lexical material in Arabic script. Whenever you name a passage token, root, lemma, branch form, or quoted lexical expression, copy its Arabic form from the listed inputs; do not transliterate or Turkishize it. This applies in headings, labels, and prose alike.

Assume the listener knows no Arabic but should hear the exact Arabic words and roots often enough to become familiar with them. Introduce the plain Turkish meaning first and place the exact Arabic form immediately after it. The Turkish wording must carry the full explanation; the Arabic form lets the speech system pronounce the source accurately and lets the listener learn its sound.

Use spoken patterns such as “yol anlamındaki `ٱلصِّرَٰطَ` kelimesi,” “dosdoğru olma anlamındaki `ق و م` kökü,” “makara anlamındaki `القامة` dalı,” and “kiriş anlamındaki `النعامة` dalı.” Do not write `sırat`, `müstakîm kökü`, `kāme`, or `naʿāme`. Say “yol gösterme anlamındaki `ه د ي` kökü,” not `hidayet kökü`, when the root itself is the subject.

Do not make an exception for Arabic forms already borrowed into Turkish. When a source word or root is being identified, a familiar Latin-script loanword is still not a substitute for the supplied Arabic form. Between lexical identifications, continue in natural Turkish and refer to the already explained meaning rather than repeating a Latin transliteration. If an Arabic form is not present in the listed inputs, use its Turkish meaning without inventing either a Latin transliteration or an Arabic spelling.

Prefer:

```text
İyilik verme anlamındaki `ن ع م` kökünün kiriş anlamındaki `النعامة` dalı, dosdoğru olma anlamındaki `ق و م` kökünün makara anlamındaki `القامة` dalını taşır.
```

over:

```text
İkinci söz alanı, ilk mekanizmada açık kalan taşıyıcı rolü tamamlar.
```

Avoid letting phrases such as `söz alanı`, `rol tamamlamak`, `mekanizma`, `işleyiş alanı`, `karşı alan`, `yerel fail`, or `koşullu uzmanlaşma` dominate the prose. Avoid translated English syntax, unnecessary nominalization, and repeated methodological caveats.

Do not expose prompts, agents, file paths, branch IDs, evidence codes, or English confidence labels. Every Arabic expression must be introduced or accompanied by enough Turkish explanation for a listener who knows no Arabic. Latin-script transliteration must not appear.

Write only the complete publication to the output path named in the task.
