# Compositional Semantic Synthesis — 96:1–19

## The command gathers the being it addresses

The first imperative carries an operation that the next verse unexpectedly fills with material. `ٱقْرَأْ` directly commands recitation, and the basic gathering branch of ق ر ء describes reading as joining letters and words. But the same root’s uterine branch says of an animal that has not conceived, `لم تحمل علقة أي دما ولا جنينا`—“it did not carry an ʿalaqa, blood, or a fetus”—and elsewhere describes the womb as not having gathered a fetus within it (ق ر ء/B004). The source wording itself therefore places `علقة` inside the lexical field of `ٱقْرَأْ`. When the surface immediately continues `خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ`, a distant gloss has not merely supplied another image: the opening operation has met its possible product. Reading gathers; a womb gathers an ʿalaqa; the creating Lord makes the human from `عَلَقٍ`.

This exact lock makes the opening command generative without replacing recitation by gestation. The addressee is not simply an already complete agent told to perform. The passage begins to gather and form the one who will answer it. The first `خَلَقَ` identifies the Lord by an act whose object is still unspoken; the second supplies `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ` and its attached source. The same staged completion governs `عَلَّمَ`: the first occurrence supplies the medium, `بِٱلْقَلَمِ`; the second supplies the recipient and content, `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ`. Repetition behaves like formation by successive additions rather than restatement.

The syntax keeps that formation relational at every point. `بِٱسْمِ` is attached to `ٱقْرَأْ`; `رَبِّكَ` completes the name; `ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ` describes the Lord. The name branch of س م و calls the name both a means of identification and a raising of the named one’s mention (س م و/B005). Reading thus begins by raising another’s name, not the reader’s self. `رَبّ` adds more than ownership: its source prose gives repair, care, and bringing something stage by stage to completion (ر ب ب/B002), and even a learned nurturer who feeds people with the smaller portions of knowledge (B003). The second `ٱقْرَأْ` arrives beside `وَرَبُّكَ ٱلْأَكْرَمُ`; the renewed capacity to read is placed under giving and nurture before the human can claim it as property.

Creation and the pen form a material apparatus for this staged gift. One branch of `خَلَقَ` is measuring a hide before cutting it (خ ل ق/B001); other branches distinguish visible form, inward disposition, and preparedness for an act (B003–B005). The قلم is the hard reed or other material pared repeatedly until it becomes a writing tool (ق ل م/B001, B003). Creation supplies measure and formed capacity; paring supplies the usable edge; the name identifies; the pen leaves a differentiating trace; teaching brings that trace into an inward knower. Cutting here is not the opposite of making. A measured cut makes an instrument, just as differentiation makes knowledge communicable.

`مَا` shows what this giving crosses. It is the second object of `عَلَّمَ` and also the object internal to `لَمْ يَعْلَمْ`: precisely what the human did not know becomes what teaching conveys. Nonknowledge is not a possession offered in exchange. The gift branch of ك ر م can elsewhere name a present designed to compel recompense (ك ر م/B008), but nothing in this grammar is an equivalent price; the pen is a medium, not payment. `ٱلْأَكْرَمُ` gives across an actual absence and thereby authors a capable respondent. The danger that follows is possible because the capacity is real.

## A received capacity closes around its own image

The three occurrences of `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ` enact a sharp change of agency. The human is first the object created from `عَلَقٍ`, then the object taught, and then the emphatic bearer of `لَيَطْغَىٰ`. A made and instructed object has become an acting subject. The passage does not condemn that transition; its own commands require agency. The rupture lies in `أَن رَّءَاهُ ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ`: received capacity becomes evidence, in the human’s sight, that reception never constituted it.

`عَلَقٍ` makes the misreading bodily and relational. Its branches include sticking fast to another, conception fixed in a womb, thick blood-like matter, and the small ration that keeps a creature alive (ع ل ق/B001, B003, B006, B009). The origin is therefore not merely lowly material; it is dependence capable of sustaining emergence. Yet the same root distinguishes sustaining attachment from capture. A pulley hangs by its rope so that transfer can occur (B002); affection and a precious object bind without necessarily immobilizing (B004, B007). A lawsuit or verbal claim can adhere adversarially, a person can be held between relation and release, and prey can be caught in a snare (B005, B008, B011). Created-from-attachment does not mean that every later bond is legitimate. It means that the fantasy of no relation must distinguish itself from life-giving dependence, and that liberation must distinguish itself from abandonment.

The compact sight clause stages the fantasy as a closed visual circuit. `رَّءَاهُ` has an object suffix, with `ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ` describing the state in which that object is seen; it exerts reflexive pressure without spelling out a reflexive noun. The human field can be both surveyor and surveyed. That pressure intensifies through ء ن س/B005, where “the human of the eye” is the tiny human figure visible in the pupil, and through ر ء ي/B006, where a mirror returns an appearance. The self finds a human image inside its own organ of sight and mistakes a contained appearance for a self-grounding source.

`ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ` is exact because غ ن ي can mean both wealth and one thing’s sufficing in place of another (غ ن ي/B001–B002). Its residence branch thickens the error: to “be rich in” a place is to dwell there for a long time, and a vanished settlement can be described as though it had never dwelt there (B004). Long residence in a capacity can erase memory of arrival. The human has inhabited creation and teaching long enough to redescribe them as its own self-sufficient condition.

`إِنَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلرُّجْعَىٰ` breaks the circuit before the passage identifies the social obstructer. `إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ` is fronted as the destination: return is not merely backward motion but motion determined by the same `رَبِّكَ` who created, taught, and nurtured. ر ج ع/B001 permits both a thing’s return and another’s returning it, so agency remains open while destination does not. Its wider branches include repentance, a reply returning to a speaker, recurrent rain, a line retraced with fresh darkness, a worn body restored, a weapon retrieved, failed development, and material reworked (B003, B005–B015). The destination prevents these unlike returns from becoming one celebrated cycle. A return to a sword is not the return named here; a failed gestation is not restoration. Toward this Lord, return most strongly carries reorientation, answerability, and restored relation.

The possessive `رَبِّكَ` now surrounds the self-sufficiency claim: name of your Lord, your Lord the most generous, toward your Lord the return. What the human closes into `رَّءَاهُ` is held inside a larger second-person relation it did not create.

## Gift, vessel, flood, and missed ground

A complete hydraulic process crosses this account of formation without dissolving into it. The cloud branches of ر ب ب give an abiding or layered cloud that nurtures growth, and ك ر م/B002 gives cloud generous with rain and soil generous in what it grows. Moisture descends; a rock hollow or newly dug well retains sky-water (خ ل ق/B011); the `عَيْلَم` of ع ل م/B005 is a sea or water-rich well; recurrent rain and the pool it leaves belong to ر ج ع/B006. Creatures move by night toward a watering place, water is carried in a skin (ق ر ب/B008–B009), and a repaired hide can hold what would otherwise escape (ر ب ب/B006). Gift, reception, retention, transport, and need occupy different participants.

The hanging apparatus in ع ل ق/B002 completes the process. Its source prose supplies the upright support, rope, pulley, bucket, and the combined equipment by which water is drawn. A depth can be full without reaching a drinker; the suspended line lets a vessel descend, receive, rise, and transfer. This does not turn knowledge into literal water, but it gives `عَلَّمَ بِٱلْقَلَمِ` an exact instrumental geometry. Well and teacher are sources, rope and pen are media, bucket and mark carry, human receives. Neither instrument originates its content, and suspension enables motion rather than negating agency.

The same field gives boundaries differentiated work. A hide measured and cut, a pen pared, a waterskin repaired, a vessel lidded, and a quantity stopped just short of fullness all acquire capacity because they have form. `بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰ` belongs with this enabling interval: و ق ي describes placing something between a vulnerable being and what harms it, and taqwā as placing the self within such protection (و ق ي/B001–B002). A bank can preserve the ground that receives water; it need not deny the current.

Against that formed abundance, `لَيَطْغَىٰ` becomes concrete. ط غ ي/B002 is water or sea rising beyond its measure and sweeping away what it once might have served. Divine generosity and human transgression can therefore share abundance while reversing its agency. `ٱلْأَكْرَمُ` is not simply the largest quantity: rain gives in a relation that leaves cloud, soil, plant, and recipient able to continue. Ṭughyān is force that treats receiving forms as obstacles to its own expansion.

The later root `ن ه ي` supplies the current’s endpoint. A `نَهْي` can be the pool or valley-end where floodwater finishes, settles, and becomes still (ن ه ي/B004). On the surface, the obstructer `يَنْهَىٰ` a servant; later the threat turns the same root upon him, `لَّمْ يَنتَهِ`. Stopping changes hands. Damming another’s directed prayer is not the same act as one’s own harmful force reaching its limit. The hydraulic process exposes the difference between a boundary that preserves capacity and a boundary that captures it.

`خَاطِئَةٍ` supplies the opposite failure from flood. خ ط ء/B003 names a tract of land missed by rain while the neighboring ground receives it. Error can thus be excess that erases a channel or a trajectory that fails to arrive at its proper ground. Beside `عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰ`, the false, culpable forelock is not only morally stained; it leads in a direction that does not deliver the gift where it belongs. `ٱقْتَرِب` retains, at the close, the bodily urgency of creatures moving toward water, but its destination remains the Lord-relation, not a literal pool. The water process gives need a route; the passage gives that route its address.

## The taught eye must learn a route

After return has been named, `أَرَءَيْتَ` recurs three times and makes sight an examination. Its branch can function as “tell me” and as an alerting inquiry (ر ء ي/B013). The first question presents `ٱلَّذِى يَنْهَىٰ`, with the praying servant as the object of his prohibition. The next two govern scenarios: a subject may be positioned `عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰ` or command `بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰ`; a subject may deny and turn away. The contextual movement aligns the praying servant with the positive possibility and the obstructer with denial and withdrawal, while the unexpressed conditional subjects keep the addressee moving between perspectives rather than judging by rank alone.

This examination recruits a route-making apparatus already latent in teaching. ع ل م/B002 is the distinguishing sign: a raised flag, mountain, border, garment-mark, or trace by which a road is found. ق ل م supplies the repeatable written line and also a marked lot or cut territorial division (ق ل م/B003–B004, B008). ء م ر/B005 supplies a road marker or appointed sign. ه د ي supplies gentle indication, course and manner, and the guide or leading forepart that goes ahead (ه د ي/B001–B003). Even ع ب د/B005 can be a road made level and passable. Teaching by the pen is therefore not the storage of facts; it equips a human to distinguish a way, follow it, and transmit it.

`كَانَ عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰ` gives this apparatus a grammatical surface. Guidance is something on which a subject may stand or move. The branch field refuses to make that movement a race. A weak person may walk by leaning on two companions (ه د ي/B008); a hurt animal may guard its sore hoof against rough ground (و ق ي/B003); a calm gait, a pace below full gallop, and a traveler restored after exhaustion all belong to the surrounding route field. Guidance coordinates unequal capacities. Slow protected movement may reach where an unbounded charge does not.

`وَتَوَلَّىٰ` breaks that orientation. و ل ي begins with proximity and uninterrupted succession, but its turning-away branch explicitly describes withdrawal as leaving nearness and abandoning attention or compliance—`ترك قربه` (و ل ي/B007). The last `ٱقْتَرِب` is therefore an exact directional answer, not a generic uplifting close. One participant turns away from proximity; the addressee is commanded to enter it. Between them, `لَا تُطِعْهُ` ensures that approach is not movement toward the obstructer.

The inquiry also tests what the opening teaching has produced. `مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ` first made nonknowledge a receptive opening. `أَلَمْ يَعْلَم بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ` returns the negated verb after creation, teaching, self-sight, and three questions. Nonknowledge is no longer merely the absence into which a gift may enter; it is failure to integrate what should now determine conduct. The content is syntactically explicit: `ٱللَّهَ` is the subject of `يَرَىٰ`. Human seeing depends on pupil-image, mirror, position, audience, dream, or inference; this clause supplies no such device. The obstructer who acted as watcher of another’s prayer is himself within a seeing he does not command.

The passage does not replace human discernment with that witness. The repeated `أَرَءَيْتَ` still makes the addressee answerable for seeing roles correctly. It moves from the human’s closed self-image, through trained comparative sight, to knowledge of divine sight. The taught eye can finally lower in prostration without becoming blind: one branch of س ج د is sustained, lowered gaze (س ج د/B005). Relinquishing visual dominance becomes the bodily completion of having learned how to see.

## One leading part and a many-membered answer

The obstructer’s action is first a prohibition, but `ن ه ي` itself refuses to make all restraint alike. It is the opposite of command, the endpoint reached by motion, and the reason that restrains a person from ugliness (ن ه ي/B001–B003). The positive scenario’s `أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰ` imagines direction toward protection; the threat’s `لَّمْ يَنتَهِ` demands restraint of the restrainer. The question is not whether limits exist but whose force they stop and what capacity survives them.

Prayer fixes those roles. `إِذَا` attaches to `صَلَّىٰ`: the servant is stopped at the moment prayer becomes act. ص ل و/B003 supplies the structured worship of standing, bowing, prostrating, invoking, and glorifying; its snare branch supplies a trap set at a point of passage (B004). The obstructer places restraint where the servant’s received capacity is moving. Yet the servant remains the subject of prayer even while grammatically exposed as the object of prohibition. Social power has not absorbed his agency.

The threat reaches the obstructer through the part that had presented direction. ن ص ي/B001 gives the front hairline and the act of grasping and pulling it; B003 extends `نَاصِيَة` to the chosen, leading, or elite part of a group. س ف ع/B001 supplies taking that forepart by hand. Across the verse boundary, `بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ ۝ نَاصِيَةٍ` first names the handle of threatened seizure and then catches the noun again to expose it as `كَٰذِبَةٍ خَاطِئَةٍ`. The grammar performs a linked grasp: definite bodily object, then indefinite redescription. False speech, culpable deviation, and attempted leadership are condensed at the front.

The passage then places another descent against this forced one. A forelock can be seized from outside and used as a control point for the whole person. `وَٱسْجُدْ`, by contrast, calls the addressee’s body into coordinated action. The سجود branches enumerate forehead, nose, hands, knees, and feet as contact-points (س ج د/B003), with head and gaze lowered (B004–B005). One leading part no longer monopolizes the body. Hands support, knees bend, feet remain placed, and the front touches ground as part of an articulated whole.

Material resemblance therefore sharpens relational difference. Both seizure and prostration can lower the front; only one is performed by the addressed subject, follows `لَا تُطِعْهُ`, and opens into `وَٱقْتَرِب`. Forced lowering establishes an endpoint for the obstructer’s transgression. Chosen lowering resumes the servant’s interrupted prayer and creates proximity. Force can subdue a handle; it cannot manufacture this coordinated answer.

`لَا تُطِعْهُ` is indispensable between the two. ء م ر/B008 names a person of weak judgment who consults every voice and complies with every command; ط و ع differentiates obedience from capacity, trained capacity, voluntary response, and the self’s making an act easy (ط و ع/B001–B006). The close is not command defeating freedom. It is taught capacity discriminating among commands: refuse the denier’s claim, order the body in worship, move toward the relation from which capacity came. Refusal keeps prostration from being captured by the obstructer; prostration keeps refusal from becoming self-sufficient autonomy; approach keeps lowering from ending in mere abasement.

## A call that pulls inward meets a call that pushes away

The claim to self-sufficiency finally exposes its social supports. `فَلْيَدْعُ نَادِيَهُ` gives the obstructer permission to call “his” assembly. The basic operation of د ع و is to incline something toward oneself by voice and speech (د ع و/B001); نادي is the gathered council or public meeting-place to which people come (ن د و/B001). The object suffix matters. Deliberation, lineage, reputation, alliance, and bodies are compressed into one person’s possessive resource. The supposedly sufficient self calls a collective when its own force is insufficient.

`سَنَدْعُ ٱلزَّبَانِيَةَ` answers with the same verbal root but a different object and future subject. ز ب ن/B001 gives pushing, butting, repelling, and defending a side. The two calls therefore have opposite mechanics. The first voice draws a possessed social center inward around the human caller; the answering voice summons an outward force of repulsion. Echo becomes counter-vector. The human plural is “his”; the answering plurality is definite but unavailable to his possession.

The smaller branches of د ع و disclose how a call propagates. A little milk left in the udder draws what follows (B003); falling walls can bring one another down in sequence (B005). A summons may build presence one response at a time, or one collapse may call forth the next. The obstructer assumes his voice will produce the first chain. The answering future suspends that assumption and exposes the second possibility. A public center exists only while bodies answer; the empty-house branches of both د ع و and ز ب ن remind it that possessed presence can become absence.

This does not condemn assembly. نادي also carries deliberation and generosity; alliance and companionship can sustain. The deformation lies in gathering persons as an extension of obstruction. `لَا تُطِعْهُ` prevents the addressee from becoming one more answering body that completes `نَادِيَهُ`. The repeated `رَبِّكَ` offers a contrasting possessive relation: “your Lord” creates, teaches, gives, and receives return, while “his club” is mobilized to stop another’s prayer. Possession is judged by the agency it enables.

The speech contest reaches back to the opening. `بِٱسْمِ` raises and identifies another; `ٱقْرَأْ` gathers and voices ordered content. The late human call gathers other people around the speaker’s claim. Naming and calling both make relation present at a distance, but their directions differ: the opening voice locates itself in its source, while the obstructer’s voice treats its recipients as reinforcements of itself. `لَا تُطِعْهُ` shows that audibility does not entail obedience.

A complete but peripheral armory shadows this escalation. The root of command can describe fitting a spearhead to a shaft (ء م ر/B011); ق ل م supplies a pared spear-joint or marked projectile; ه د ي a leading arrowhead; ر ب ب a skin container for arrows; ق ر ب a sheath; and ر ج ع the hand reaching backward to a sword or quiver. Preparation, containment, retrieval, blow, and collective reinforcement could form a continuous violent sequence. Yet no surface word names sword, arrow, horse, or hunt. Its passage-scale force lies in the response the close declines to make: the addressee is not told to return a hand to a weapon or call a rival club. Its three acts break escalation by changing obedience, bodily direction, and destination.

## The first word returns at the ground

`ٱلرُّجْعَىٰ` is not only announced in 96:8; return becomes the passage’s compositional practice. The first `ٱقْرَأْ` receives creation, the second generosity. The first `خَلَقَ` leaves its object open, the second fills it. The first `عَلَّمَ` supplies the instrument, the second recipient and content. Three `أَرَءَيْتَ` clauses successively expose an act, a guided and protective alternative, and denial with withdrawal. `نَاصِيَة` returns to receive its adjectives; `يَدْعُ` returns as `نَدْعُ` with changed person and object. The three `كَلَّا` interruptions do not repeat one refusal: they reopen the mistaken closures of self-sufficiency, unended obstruction, and claimed obedience.

The lexical branches of ر ج ع make this formal action audible and visible. Return can be a reply sent back to a speaker, a voice repeated in recitation, and a written or tattooed line darkened again (ر ج ع/B005, B007, B009). The passage teaches by retracing. Each renewed line adds a participant or relation, so repetition makes the earlier carrier newly legible rather than restoring an unchanged beginning. This is the same staged nurture already carried by `رَبِّكَ`: small, differentiated acts bring a capacity toward completion.

The opening root now forms an exact circuit with the end. In addition to its gathering and uterine branch—the source wording that literally includes an `علقة`—ق ر ء calls the `قارئ` an `عابد ناسك`, a reader who is a worshipper and devotee (ق ر ء/B006). Another branch says `أقرأت من سفري أي انصرفت`—I returned from my journey—and `أقرأت من أهلي أي دنوت`—I drew near to my people (B005). These are not loose thematic similarities. The opening verb’s lexical source prose supplies, in order, the operations later surfaced as human formation from `عَلَقٍ`, `ٱلرُّجْعَىٰ`, worshipful prostration, and `ٱقْتَرِب`.

The ending activates that compressed beginning with exact distinctions. `لَا تُطِعْهُ` refuses the false voice and captured collective, so the reader-as-worshipper does not become the obstructer’s servant. `وَٱسْجُدْ` gives the worship branch a many-membered body and reopens the prayer that had been stopped. `وَٱقْتَرِب` gives the reading root’s drawing-near motion a declared imperative and reverses the `ترك قربه` carried by `وَتَوَلَّىٰ`. The approach can begin the directed `ٱلرُّجْعَىٰ` without exhausting its larger certainty.

The first command can therefore be heard again only after the body reaches the ground. `ٱقْرَأْ بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ` is not the voice of a self already sufficient to master a text. It is the first received act of a being being gathered, formed, marked, taught, tested, and returned to agency. The name prevents the voice from claiming itself as source; the Lord’s nurture lets the command become capacity; the close reveals what that capacity was for. To recite in the name of the creating Lord is already to begin the worship and approach that the last two imperatives finally make bodily.
