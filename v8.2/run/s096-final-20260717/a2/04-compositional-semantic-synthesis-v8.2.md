# The Reading That Returns Its Reader

The passage does more than begin with recitation and end with worship. It lets the lexical field of `ٱقْرَأْ` disclose, at a distance, the route by which recitation becomes embodied response. One branch of ق ر ء preserves the expressions `أقرأت من سفري أي انصرفت`—“I turned back from my journey”—and `أقرأت من أهلي أي دنوت`—“I drew near to my people” (root_001210/B005). Those are not replacements for the direct command to recite. They are an itinerary latent inside its root: the opening `ٱقْرَأْ` is answered in the middle by `إِنَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ` and completed at the end by `وَٱقْتَرِب`. Reading begins a return whose destination is named before its final motion is commanded.

A second branch makes the circuit bodily. Its source wording calls the `قارئ` an `عابد ناسك` and explains `تقرأ` as becoming a devoted worshipper (root_001210/B006). The reader-role implicit in the first imperative thus finds a surface counterpart in `عَبْدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰٓ`, a servant in the act of prayer, and bodily completion in the addressee commanded `وَٱسْجُدْ`. The passage does not append worship to reading as a separate topic. It passes the reader through creation, teaching, dangerous self-perception, obstruction, judgment, and refusal until the act of reading has acquired a servant’s posture and direction.

## A gathered being is told to gather

The first command is grammatically relational before any secondary formation is heard. `ٱقْرَأْ` is attached to `بِٱسْمِ`; the name is completed by the construct `رَبِّكَ`; and `ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ` describes that Lord. The voice is therefore not told simply to produce speech. Its act is fastened to a name, that name to “your Lord,” and that Lord to creating. The name itself belongs to a lexical field of elevation and disclosure: `الاسم ما يعرف به ذات الشيء` and `به رفع ذكر المسمى`—a name identifies a being and raises its mention (س م و/B005). Reading gathers sounds under an identification it did not invent.

The gathering facet of ق ر ء makes the next verse unexpectedly intimate with the command. Its primary branch says, `قرأت الشيء قرآنا جمعته وضممت بعضه إلى بعض`: to read is to gather and join parts. But another branch uses the same root for what a womb gathers or contains and includes the exact phrase `لم تحمل علقة أي دما ولا جنينا` (root_001210/B004). Immediately after `ٱقْرَأْ`, the surface says `خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ`. The source wording of the reading root has already named an `علقة`; the passage then names clinging matter as the human’s source. Operation and product meet across the two verses: the human commanded to gather and voice was itself first gathered as attached, developing matter.

`عَلَقٍ` keeps that origin relational. Its branches include sticking to and being fastened to another (ع ل ق/B001), conception fixed in a womb (B009), and the small provision by which life is kept going (B006). The direct blood-like or clinging source remains visible, but it now carries more than smallness. Human emergence begins in adhesion, retention, and sustenance. The command is addressed not to a self-originating voice but to a creature whose very coming-to-be required being held.

That recognition changes the repeated `خَلَقَ`. The first occurrence identifies the Lord by creating while leaving the object open; the second returns with the human as object and `مِنْ عَلَقٍ` as source. The repeated `عَلَّمَ` develops in the same way. First it names the instrument, `بِٱلْقَلَمِ`; then it names recipient and content, `عَلَّمَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ`. Each return fills a role that the previous occurrence left unspoken. This is the temporal work of `رَبِّكَ` as well: the ر ب ب field includes raising and completing something `حالا فحالا`, stage after stage (ر ب ب/B002). The passage’s repetitions enact the nurture they attribute to the Lord.

The pen gives that nurture an edge. A creation branch describes measuring a hide before cutting it—`قدرته قبل القطع` (خ ل ق/B001)—while the pen root describes paring and straightening hard material and says that the writing implement is so called because it is pared `مرة بعد مرة` (ق ل م/B001, B003). Creation and pen are not synonyms: one brings and disposes a being into form; the other is a maintained instrument that leaves differentiating traces. Yet together they make the opening generosity concrete. Measure precedes cut, repeated paring makes a usable point, and the point enables teaching to cross from teacher to learner.

The grammar of `مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ` shows what crosses. `مَا` is the content taught and also the object internal to what the human had not known. Nonknowledge is not concealed as a disgrace; it is made into the receiving place of a gift. The `ع ل م` field joins knowing to `أثر بالشيء يتميز به عن غيره`, a mark by which something is distinguished, including a road-mark and visible standard (ع ل م/B002). Teaching by the pared pen therefore equips the gathered human to distinguish and follow traces. The reader is made capable of a route before the route is morally contested.

## The cut that enables becomes the limit denied

The measured hide and pared pen establish a kind of limit that produces capacity. A cut can make an implement; a hollow can retain water; a mark can distinguish one direction from another. Against this, `كَلَّآ إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَيَطْغَىٰٓ` is not merely a change from learning to bad conduct. The primary image of طغيان is water leaving its proper measure: `طغى البحر والماء إذا علا كل شيء فاجترفه`—water rises beyond bounds and sweeps things away (ط غ ي/B002). The formed capacity now behaves like abundance that destroys the forms by which abundance could have been received.

The grammar makes the reversal visible. In `خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ` and `عَلَّمَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ`, the human is the object of creating and teaching. At 96:6 the same `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ` becomes the emphatic bearer of `لَيَطْغَىٰ`. Received agency is real—the passage does not pretend the human cannot act—but it is re-described as though being acted for and taught had never structured it. The error is not possession of capacity. It is capacity claiming to be its own source and measure.

`أَن رَّءَاهُ ٱسْتَغْنَىٰٓ` names the interior mechanism. The غ ن ي field does not only name wealth; it says that something `يكفي` or `يقوم مقامه`, suffices for or stands in the place of another (غ ن ي/B002). Self-sufficiency can therefore be heard as substitution: the human treats acquired knowledge, instrument, status, or social means as able to stand in for the relation that gave them. Another branch names prolonged dwelling and can speak of a former residence as though it had never been (B004). Long residence inside a gift makes origin easy to forget. The human has inhabited created and taught capacity long enough to mistake residence for authorship.

The branch field of `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ` makes the self-seeing especially exact. `إنسان العين` is the little human figure or likeness seen in the dark pupil (ء ن س/B005). Beside `رَّءَاهُ`, the phrase supplies a concrete problem of scale: a human-shaped image appears inside the organ of sight and can be taken for the whole agent. The self sees a bounded presentation of itself and infers sufficiency from the image. Yet the image does not contain the creating Lord, the teaching act, the pen, the clinging origin, or the relations that sustain the eye itself.

This is why `إِنَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ` follows immediately. The fronted `إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ` determines destination before the noun of return lands. The ر ج ع field names both a thing returning and another being returned to the direction or condition from which it began (ر ج ع/B001). Agency may be active or undergone, but the destination is not available for revision. The repeated `رَبِّكَ` encloses the self-sufficiency episode: the Lord of the name and generosity is also the Lord toward whom return runs.

Return here does not erase what the human became. A pared pen is not restored to uncut reed; taught knowledge is not turned back into ignorance. The return root also names a line renewed by darkening it again (ر ج ع/B009). That facet matches the passage’s own procedure: `ٱقْرَأْ`, `خَلَقَ`, and `عَلَّمَ` recur with added roles; later `أَرَءَيْتَ` recurs with changed scenarios. Return retraces in order to disclose more, not to cancel formation. The problem of self-sufficiency is answered by rereading capacity back into its source.

## The little human in the eye is put under sight

After the assertion of return, the passage takes sight away from the closed self-image and turns it into examination: `أَرَءَيْتَ` appears three times. A branch of ر ء ي says that `أرأيت` can run like `أخبرني` and carries `معنى التنبيه`, the force of “tell me” and of alerting (ر ء ي/B013). The repeated phrase does not merely ask the addressee to look. It requires an account of participants and acts.

The first question presents the one who forbids and the servant whom he forbids at the moment of prayer: `ٱلَّذِى يَنْهَىٰ ۝ عَبْدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰٓ`. The next question repositions the praying subject within a positive possibility—`إِن كَانَ عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ ۝ أَوْ أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰٓ`. The third turns scrutiny toward denial and withdrawal—`إِن كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ`. Seeing must now keep actor, patient, condition, direction, and alternative distinct. The socially dominant figure cannot secure the judgment simply by occupying the front of the scene.

`عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰ` gives this examination a route. The ه د ي field includes gentle indication, the direction and manner of an affair, and the one or part that goes first (ه د ي/B001–B003). Guidance is not merely an inward label; it supplies direction, course, and leading. `بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰ` adds a different operation. The و ق ي field defines protection as putting something between a being and what would harm it, and taqwā as placing the self within such protection (و ق ي/B001–B002). The positive possibility is therefore both rightly directed and capable of extending a protective boundary toward another.

The prohibitor performs the contrary use of a boundary. `يَنْهَىٰ` stops someone else’s prayer; `لَّمْ يَنتَهِ` later demands that the prohibitor himself stop. The same root changes target and therefore force. Its water branch gives the shift a physical shape: a `نهي` is the pool or valley-end where moving water reaches its terminus and settles (ن ه ي/B004). The human who `يَطْغَىٰ` like water beyond measure tries to arrest another’s directed flow while refusing to reach his own basin. The threat does not oppose every limit; it turns limit against the action that has exceeded it.

`وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ` likewise becomes more than generic rejection. The و ل ي field holds both facing toward and, in the form of departure, turning away from proximity, attention, and compliance (و ل ي/B006–B007). Denial produces an orientation: the obstructer withdraws from what he should know while imposing his direction on the servant. By contrast, `ٱقْتَرِب` will restore positive orientation without restoring obedience to him.

The sight sequence reaches its reversal at `أَلَمْ يَعْلَم بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ`. At 96:5, `لَمْ يَعْلَمْ` named the human’s receptive lack before teaching. Here `أَلَمْ يَعْلَم` names failure after creation, teaching, self-sight, return, and three alerting scenarios. The first nonknowledge made giving possible; the later nonknowledge is a refusal to integrate what has been given. And `ٱللَّهَ` is now the explicit subject of `يَرَىٰ`: the figure who watched and obstructed another is himself within sight. Human seeing had enclosed a little self-image; divine seeing reopens the entire field of action.

## A stopped prayer changes who must stop

The object `عَبْدًا` is indefinite, but its action is precise: it prays when the prohibitor intervenes. The عبد field distinguishes an owned person, imposed enslavement, and worshipful obedience with humility (ع ب د/B001, B003–B004). Nothing in the grammar gives the prohibitor ownership of this servant. The repeated `رَبِّكَ` has already located mastery with the one who creates, teaches, gives, and is the destination of return. The prohibition is therefore an attempted reassignment of a servant’s obedience.

Prayer itself carries the structured acts of standing, bowing, prostrating, invoking, and glorifying (ص ل و/B003). `إِذَا` matters because obstruction arrives when servant-status becomes performed relation. A secondary ص ل و branch names a snare set where something will pass (B004). It does not turn prayer into a trap; it makes the prohibitor’s timing legible. He places restraint at the point where a received capacity is moving toward its end.

The conditionals refuse to leave the servant merely passive. The one being stopped may be `عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰ` and may `أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰ`: situated on a course and capable of directing protection outward. Obstruction therefore captures not only a private act but an agency that might guide and protect. When the passage later commands `لَا تُطِعْهُ`, it does not create agency from nothing. It releases an already created and taught capacity from the wrong claimant.

The threatened forelock exposes where the prohibitor’s own direction has lodged. `سفع` directly names taking the forepart by hand, and `ناصية` names the front hairline as something grasped, pulled, and mastered (س ف ع/B001; ن ص ي/B001). But `نصية` can also be the chosen, foremost, or leading part of a group (ن ص ي/B003). Thus `نَاصِيَةٍ كَٰذِبَةٍ خَاطِئَةٍ` places falsehood and culpable deviation on the bodily front that had presumed to lead.

`خَاطِئَةٍ` keeps direction and responsibility together. The خ ط ء field includes missing the intended direction and deliberate wrongdoing (خ ط ء/B001–B002). The forelock is not merely insulted; the leading part is disclosed as one that has aimed wrongly and is answerable for doing so. The phrase crosses the verse boundary by repeating the noun—`بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ ۝ نَاصِيَةٍ`—first making it the object of seizure, then reopening it to bear its moral trajectory. What the pen made distinguishable as mark and route is now readable on a false leader’s body.

## One call pulls inward; the answer pushes back

After exposure, the obstructer is told to call `نَادِيَهُۥ`. The root د ع و defines calling as inclining or drawing something toward oneself `بصوت وكلام`, by voice and speech (د ع و/B001). `نادي`, meanwhile, is a council, public meeting, or place around which people gather (ن د و/B001). The possessive suffix is decisive: the human who saw himself sufficient now mobilizes “his” assembly. His claimed independence turns out to be social dependence reorganized as possession.

This sends new force backward into `ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ`. Sufficiency may consist of allies, lineage, reputation, deliberative standing, and bodies available on call. Because they are gathered under `ـهُ`, their plurality can be misperceived as the caller’s singular power. Yet the نادي branches also include generosity and consultation. Collectivity itself is not condemned. Its deformation lies in being reduced to an obstructer’s extension after inquiry has exposed his act.

`سَنَدْعُ ٱلزَّبَانِيَةَ` answers with the same calling root but a contrary object. The primary ز ب ن branch is pushing, butting, repelling, and defending a side (ز ب ن/B001). The first call draws a possessed social center inward toward its caller; the answering call summons a force whose lexical operation pushes back. The paired clauses form a complete reversal of vectors without making the groups symmetrical. One belongs to the obstructer’s possessive claim; the other is announced by the first-person plural that has already threatened his forelock.

The contrast also changes scale. `نَادِيَهُ` appears public and secure, yet a council exists only while persons answer its call. The د ع و and ز ب ن fields both preserve formulas for an empty place in which there is no caller or no one (د ع و/B008; ز ب ن/B007). Possessed presence is therefore contingent. The answering future `سَنَدْعُ` denies the human caller control over which presence will finally fill the scene.

The last `كَلَّا` breaks this recruitment at the point where it might claim the addressee. The first `كَلَّا` interrupted the closure of self-sufficiency; the second interrupted refusal to stop; the third clears the addressee out of the obstructer’s network. `لَا تُطِعْهُ` makes the would-be commanding center into a bounded object—“him”—whom the addressee can refuse.

## The reader reaches the ground and draws near

The close gives refusal an immediate form: `لَا تُطِعْهُ وَٱسْجُدْ وَٱقْتَرِب`. Its three acts cannot be reduced to one. Refusal severs a false command; prostration reorganizes the body; approach supplies destination. Without the first, prostration could be captured by the obstructer’s authority. Without the second, approach could preserve the upright self that called itself sufficient. Without the third, bodily lowering could be mistaken for defeat rather than relation.

The contrast with forelock-seizure is material. External force reaches the person through one leading handle. The سجود field, by contrast, distributes contact across forehead, nose, hands, knees, and feet (س ج د/B003). Prostration coordinates the body as a plurality of members performing one response. The body created from a small clinging source and taught through a small pared instrument is neither annihilated nor seized into another human’s possession; it becomes the active subject of a many-jointed act.

This is where `لَا تُطِعْهُ` and `وَٱسْجُدْ` cease to look contradictory. The ط و ع field differentiates compliance, capacity, and the self’s willing facilitation of an act (ط و ع/B001, B003–B006). Refusing the obstructer protects the capacity to answer elsewhere. The عبد branches make the distinction still sharper: imposed enslavement and worshipful abasement can share an exterior language of submission while reversing agent, relation, and consequence. The passage refuses one mastery so that service can continue toward its proper Lord.

`وَٱقْتَرِب` also answers the earlier water image with unusual precision. Alongside ordinary nearness, the ق ر ب field includes a vessel `قربان` when it has approached fullness without overflowing—`قدح قربان إذا قارب أن يمتلئ` (ق ر ب/B016). The human had `طغى` like water exceeding measure and sweeping away its receivers. The final approach can therefore carry a small material correction: nearness is fullness held in form, not the obliteration of every edge. Another branch names the urgent night movement of creatures toward water (ق ر ب/B008), so approach remains the motion of a dependent being toward what sustains it.

The unnamed object of `ٱقْتَرِب` has already been supplied by the passage’s hinge: `إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلرُّجْعَىٰ`. The repeated “your Lord” has accompanied name, generosity, and return. The end does not need to restate that relation for it to govern the vector. Approach is the present bodily beginning of the return whose certainty was announced at 96:8.

Now the two distant ق ر ء phrases can complete their work. `قارئ عابد ناسك` supplies the participant the opening command was forming: the reader becomes visible as the servant who prays and the addressee who prostrates. `أقرأت من سفري أي انصرفت` and `أقرأت من أهلي أي دنوت` supply the route the surface unfolds: return from the journey, draw near to one’s relation. The passage has made those latent roles explicit without ceasing to command recitation.

The beginning therefore sounds different on return. `ٱقْرَأْ بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ` is not a command to display a capacity the reader owns. It calls a gathered, created, and taught being to gather speech within the name of its source. The pen’s cut gives that being marks to follow; the warning exposes what happens when formed capacity mistakes itself for measure; the questions teach it to see commands and participants truly; the close reopens the bodily act that obstruction tried to stop. By the time `وَٱقْتَرِب` lands, reading has become the reader’s refusal to be possessed by the false front, its chosen descent, and its movement nearer to the Lord in whose name it first began.
