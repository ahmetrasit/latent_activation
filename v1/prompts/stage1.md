# Stage 1 Pass 1 prompt: temporally conditioned reactivation

## Core task

Temporally conditioned reactivation is a state transition in which one cue reorganizes the interpretation of several earlier cues.

Find a compact latent structure that explains:

- why several words occur;
- why they occur in that order;
- why particular roles are present;
- why an earlier expression becomes relevant again;
- why the passage closes where it does.

Find a small relational model capable of reproducing the passage’s sequence of activation, expectation, completion, and reactivation.

What follows is a demonstration on S103. Your task is to do the same synthesis work for all possible findings in your assigned ayah range. Discover all possible synthesis units regardless of how likely they are, and provide a short explanation of your grade: strong, medium-strong, medium, weak, or unlikely.

Start with the very first rooted word in the given ayah interval.

## Cognitive premise

Arabic gives the hypothesis additional force because roots and patterns are psychologically active dimensions. Experiments indicate that Arabic speakers process root and word-pattern structure in both spoken and written forms. Importantly, morphological root activation can be distinguished from semantic relatedness. Arabic cross-modal priming experiments support precisely that distinction:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4673575/

The resulting cognitive model has three layers:

1. Long-term associative memory

   Root-pattern families, lexical branches, collocations, phonological neighbors, recurring Qurʾanic formulas, semantic frames, and learned scenes.

2. Temporary recitation state

   What is currently active, what is fading, unresolved expectations, referents, clause/ayah boundaries, and the opening frame.

3. Activation events

   First activation, reinforcement, competition, role completion, backward reactivation, reinterpretation, surprise, and closure.

Qnet mainly approximates the first layer. Ayah and occurrence information belong to the second. The gold demonstrates successful trajectories through all three.

The eventual research object should not be only a static network. It should be something like:

```text
long-term Qnet
  +
ordered recitation events
  =
temporary activation trajectory
  →
candidate synthesis
```

A useful hearing-based research lane must therefore preserve temporal exposure: the agent encounters the passage progressively, records predictions and unresolved activations, and then performs a backward replay asking which earlier items became newly meaningful. A simultaneous full-text agent is a separate control, not a substitute.

The strongest validation is whether the proposed synthesis explains the ordered activation trajectory better than shuffled order, static root presence, or generic theme density. This also protects against confusing semantic association with genuine prediction, which experiments show can be dissociated at the neural level:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3657387/

Human-level synthesis may be characterized by successful reactivation and retrospective organization during a temporally unfolding recitation. This is not direct “sound to concept,” and it is not root association alone. It is cascading word-form, morphology, context, memory, and relational-model construction.

The secondary reading is valuable precisely because it does not replace the first. It gives the primary proposition geometry, motion, force, duration, and causal connectivity.

## Constructive traversal

What the S103 demonstration performed was not merely traversal through a finished network. It was constructive traversal:

```text
S0: ḥaqq = inward penetration
      ↓ infer
    latent object: spear
      ↓ retrieve
    ʿamal = operating a spear
      ↓ retrieve/reinforce
    ṣabr = restraint and maintained steadiness

C1: controlled, sustained inward penetration
      ↓ exposes new questions
    Into what? Under what force? What is happening inside?

    fī khusr = contained degrading state
    ʿaṣr = pressure + successive time

C2: intervention into an internally degrading pressure-state
      ↓
    illā + four conditions

C3: a complete prevention/counter-degradation system
```

At each stage, the newly formed composite becomes the next search key. You are no longer searching for things merely “similar to ḥaqq.” You are following roles and pressures that emerge from the growing image:

- instrument;
- operation;
- direction;
- target interior;
- enclosing medium;
- force;
- duration;
- stabilization;
- prevention.

That is why a static breadth-first traversal over shared theme labels would not reproduce it. The query itself changes after every discovery.

Each recruited branch performs three jobs:

1. It supports part of the existing image.
2. It becomes a constituent of that image.
3. It changes what the mind searches for next.

For example, fī khusr does not merely confirm the spear image. It transforms the image into an internally directed intervention within a containing state. ʿAṣr then transforms a static enclosure into a dynamic pressure process through time.

```text
activation avalanche
+ specific lexical support
+ grammatical and positional fit
+ role completion
+ preservation of primary meaning
+ inhibition and stopping
= synthesis
```

The epistemic boundaries remain important:

- ḥaqq does not contextually mean “an inward psychological spear.”
- ṣabr does not literally mean gripping a spear.
- illā does not lexically mean an exit opening.
- The pressure chamber is not an alternative translation.

They are components of a secondary simulation whose relational structure matches the primary statement.

## Constitutive evidence versus corroborative evidence

Āmanū did not generate the spear image. But once the developing model predicted an internal locus, the ordinary contextual meaning of faith/inner assent independently matched that prediction. That makes the image less arbitrary.

Importantly, āmanū corroborates interiority, not the entire spear model. Evidence should support particular features rather than confer blanket validity on a synthesis.

| Cue | Role in the developing model | Feature supported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ḥaqq piercing branch | Seed and constituent | Inward penetration |
| ʿamal spear operation | Constituent | Controlled instrument/action |
| ṣabr | Constituent and reinforcement | Restraint, steadiness, continuation |
| fī | Structural binder | Containment |
| khusr | State/outcome | Continuing degradation |
| ʿaṣr | Scene transformer | Pressure plus time |
| āmanū | Corroborator and solution constituent | Internal state, security, and assent |
| ṣāliḥāt | Corroborator and solution constituent | Repair opposed to corruption |
| illā | Boundary operator | Exception from the default condition |
| repeated tawāṣaw | Structural corroborator | Reciprocal maintenance |

These roles are not permanent. A word can first corroborate one feature and then become a constituent of the expanded model. Āmanū, for example, corroborates inwardness while also occupying a functional place in the four-part prevention process.

## When the avalanche becomes synthesis

The early phase is constructive:

```text
branch
→ image
→ open scene roles
→ retrieval of compatible branches
→ enlarged image
```

At some point the process must change from finding compatible material to predicting material not used in construction.

The constructed image predicts:

- an internal state;
- repair;
- persistence under pressure;
- a containment relation;
- an exception boundary.

Unused passage features then supply:

- āmanū;
- ṣāliḥāt;
- ṣabr;
- fī;
- illā.

That transition is crucial:

> An associative avalanche becomes an explanatory synthesis when the growing image begins correctly anticipating unused properties of the passage.

Otherwise, a vivid seed may simply be absorbing everything around it.

Therefore there are at least four evidence functions:

1. Generators create the first image.
2. Constituents fill necessary roles inside it.
3. Corroborators independently confirm predicted features.
4. Constraints resist, revise, or terminate the image when something does not fit.

In S103, the independence is promising because different channels converge:

- remote lexical branch: inward penetration;
- ordinary contextual meaning: inner assent;
- grammar: being fī a state;
- morphology/semantics: repair versus corruption;
- sequence: continuation under pressure;
- discourse logic: universal condition followed by exception.

No single channel produces the whole image. Their convergence stabilizes it.

## Systematic approach

For the lexical discovery lane, every accepted branch belonging to a passage root should receive an independent turn as the starting seed. This is exhaustive singleton seeding.

Formally:

```text
lexical seeds =
  every accepted branch
  of every root occurring in the passage
```

If occurrences matter differently, the more exact unit is:

```text
seed = passage occurrence × root branch
```

The same branch at two positions may interact with different preceding activation, syntax, or ayah structure.

### 1. Every branch starts; not every branch must succeed

Most seeds should terminate quickly:

```text
seed
├── no specific passage-local complement → dies
├── small local image → retained as local
├── competing images → branches into alternatives
└── controlled avalanche → passage-scale candidate
```

For example, ḥ-q-q B009 may produce the inward-piercing model. A remote camel branch of ḥ-q-q may find no passage-specific structure and should simply return no synthesis. High refusal is necessary.

### 2. Branches are not the entire seed universe

The strongest containment observation starts from fī khusr, which is a construction, not a root branch. Therefore use separate seed families:

- lexical seeds: every accepted branch;
- construction seeds: fī khusr, exception scope under illā, repeated tawāṣaw bi-X;
- morphosyntactic seeds: agent, patient, attachment, coordination, and parallelism;
- temporal/acoustic seeds: token order, ayah boundaries, pauses, repetition, and sound recurrence.

The number of lexical seeds equals the total accepted branch count, but total research seeds are broader. Do not generate every possible branch pair. Singleton and actual textual constructions come first. Only already verified relations should later become composite seeds.

### 3. Construction and corroboration must be separated

Each avalanche must record two sets:

```text
GENERATING_SET
branches and structures used to construct the image

UNUSED_AT_FREEZE
passage features not yet used when the image was stated
```

Only the second set can provide strong corroboration.

Example:

```text
ḥaqq B009
→ predicts inward penetration
→ retrieves ʿamal B002
→ constructs spear-operation image
```

The image is then frozen with predictions such as:

- expected: internal locus;
- expected: containment;
- expected: repair;
- expected: persistence under pressure.

If previously unused āmanū, fī, ṣāliḥāt, ṣabr, and ʿaṣr subsequently satisfy those roles, they genuinely strengthen it. In any run where ṣabr has already expanded the image before freeze, the same ṣabr branch must remain `E`, not `C`. Ṣabr may count as `C` only in a different seed pass where it was unused at freeze, or when a distinct unused dimension of the root is tested after freeze.

## Seed image-branch lifecycle

Basmala may appear in the sacred Arabic text as recitational opening context. Include its words, roots, morphology, and sequence position for corroboration, constraint, and reactivation, but never initiate a seed from basmala itself. If a passage-generated image later requires a naming, invocation, mercy, or divine-source role, basmala may corroborate or constrain that role, but it must be marked as opening-context evidence rather than generating evidence.

If the orchestration prompt includes basmala as opening context, it is permitted to inspect its QAC words/morphemes and, only when needed for opening-context corroboration or resistance, uncontaminated furuq branches for its roots. These may never initiate seeds and must be labeled `opening-context`; they are outside the lexical seed count for the assigned interval.

For each focus branch or seed:

### 1. Seed formation

Start from one focused branch of one rooted word or eligible construction.

Example structure:

```text
focus branch → initial image → developing image-branch
```

### 2. Expansion phase

Read other passage roots as continuous dossiers.

Select branches only if they can expand the seed image by transforming it, completing something already suggested by it, adding a necessary mechanism, introducing a missing participant, specifying medium, direction, force, or constraint, or creating a meaningful fork.

This can create multiple image-branches:

```text
seed image
├── expansion A using root X branch B002
├── expansion B using root Y branch B004
├── expansion C using root X + root Z
└── ... all other expansion root branches
```

These are not yet corroborated just because they are possible expansions. They are candidate image-branches.

### 3. Freeze point

Once an image-branch has been constructed from selected expansion material, freeze it.

Record:

- generating seed;
- expansion roots/branches used;
- model/image created;
- predictions created by that model;
- which passage features have not yet been used.

### 4. Corroboration / constraint phase

Now test each frozen image-branch.

Corroboration or constraint can come from:

- unused roots or words;
- morphology;
- attachments;
- word order;
- repetition;
- ayah boundaries;
- later occurrences;
- other dimensions of roots already used.

The important nuance: a root or branch used for expansion may still have another dimension that later corroborates or constrains the image, but that corroboration or constraint must be named separately. Do not double-count the same fact as both construction and corroboration.

### 5. Constraint / rejection phase

If unused information resists the image, narrows it, or fails to fit, mark that too. Some image-branches should terminate.

Expansion builds candidate images; corroboration and resistance test them after construction.

### Example prose walkthrough

Begin with one occurrence and one branch. Suppose the focus seed is the ḥaqq branch that gives an image of inward penetration. At first this is only a small image: something directed inward, entering or piercing toward an interior. Do not decide in advance what the whole image must become. Let the next branches show whether the seed can move, thicken, transform, or die. If a relevant ʿamal branch supplies operating or handling a spear, it turns the bare penetration image into an enacted operation, so it is expansion rather than corroboration `(E: عمل B002)`. If a ṣabr branch supplies steadiness, restraint, or maintained holding, it expands the developing image into controlled and sustained penetration rather than a momentary strike `(E: صبر B00x)`. These branches are not yet corroboration; they are part of the construction of a candidate image. If another branch creates a meaningfully different way for the image to develop, keep it as a separate image-branch rather than blending it into the first.

Once the candidate image has enough structure, freeze it. For example, the frozen image might now be “a controlled, steady intervention directed into an interior.” At freeze, record only what the constructed image itself now appears to require or imply. Do not import known passage roles as predictions merely because they occur later in the passage. In the S103 example, if the controlled inward-intervention image itself has generated questions such as “into what?”, “under what pressure?”, or “what prevents deterioration?”, those may be written as predictions; otherwise leave them unpredicted and test them only as later observations. Only after this freeze should unused material be tested as corroboration or resistance. If the āmanū root dossier, or a specific branch within it, was not used to build the image, that unused material may corroborate the predicted inner locus if it independently supplies inward assent or security `(C: ءمن B002)`. The same material may also constrain the simulation by preventing it from becoming literal violence, bodily piercing, or a replacement meaning for ḥaqq `(R: ءمن B002)`. No branch should be counted twice for the same feature: a branch that expanded the image before freeze is not also corroboration unless a distinct unused dimension of that same root is being tested after freeze. If an unused word, morphology, attachment, sequence position, or branch resists the candidate image, mark that resistance explicitly `(R: root B###)` and narrow or terminate the image-branch. The goal is not to make every seed succeed. The goal is to let each seed generate whatever image it can, expand only through branches that actually transform or complete the developing image, freeze the result, and then test whether later or unused passage evidence actually corroborates, narrows, or defeats it.

## Multi-seed convergence

Every branch taking a turn as seed lets us ask whether distinct starting points converge:

```text
ḥaqq B009 ──────┐
ʿamal B002 ─────┤
ʿaṣr B002 ──────┼→ internal pressure/degradation model
āmanū B002 ─────┤
fī khusr ───────┘
```

Convergence from several specific, independently run seeds is much stronger than one vivid branch absorbing the passage. It also reveals the role of each branch:

- some seeds recreate the complete model;
- some reach only one subsystem;
- some corroborate without generating;
- some produce rival models;
- most die.

All accepted root branches can enter the discovery sweep, including form-remote branches such as the piercing sense of ḥaqq. Tag their morphological and contextual distance; do not exclude them in advance. Otherwise, the mechanism that produced the S103 synthesis would be filtered out.

Total accepted branches become the lexical seed count, but controlled synthesis also requires constructional seeds, early termination, frozen predictions, and convergence across independent starting points.

## Required sweep procedure

Perform an auditable cross-root sweep per seed. For each other passage root, read its returned branches as one continuous root dossier, preserving branch IDs and the exact `branch_image_ar` + `what_is_ar` content:

```text
root dossier =
  B001 branch_image_ar + what_is_ar
  B002 branch_image_ar + what_is_ar
  ...
  B00n branch_image_ar + what_is_ar
```

The purpose of this sweep is not to exploit the number of branch visits as evidence. Exhaustive reading is only the control condition that prevents missed material. Construction may continue only through branches that specifically transform, complete, or fork the developing image in a passage-local way.

For each seed:

1. Form the seed’s initial image and let subsequent branch dossiers show whether it can move, thicken, transform, fork, or die.
2. Read each other root dossier as continuous prose while preserving branch IDs.
3. Select zero, one, or several branches that specifically transform, complete, or fork the developing image in a passage-local way.
4. Fork when multiple selected branches produce genuinely different models.
5. Continue constructive traversal only through selected branches.
6. Never blend unselected branches into a generalized root meaning.
7. Freeze the model before testing unused words, morphology, attachments, sequence, and later occurrences.
8. Grade by specificity, role completion, and independent corroboration, not by branch-visit count.

Use only the resources listed here. Do not read anything else.

Primary sources:

- `resources/qac.sqlite`: inspect schema; then use only the words and morphemes for the assigned ayah range.
- `resources/attachments.tsv`: read the header and rows for the assigned ayah range only.

### How to query QAC

Use QAC to identify the rooted words, word sequence, lemmas, morphology, and morphemes for the assigned ayah interval.

Example for a whole surah:

```sh
sqlite3 -header -column resources/qac.sqlite \
  "select surah, ayah, word_index, surface_ar, root_join_keys, lemmas_ar, pos_tags, measures
   from qac_words
   where surah = 103
   order by ayah, word_index;"
```

Example for morphemes:

```sh
sqlite3 -header -column resources/qac.sqlite \
  "select surah, ayah, word_index, morpheme_index, surface_ar, lemma_ar, root_ar, root_join_key,
          pos, morpheme_role, morph_features, aspect, mood, voice, measure
   from qac_morphemes
   where surah = 103
   order by ayah, word_index, morpheme_index;"
```

For an ayah interval, constrain both endpoints explicitly:

```sql
where surah = :surah
  and ayah between :ayah_start and :ayah_end
```

### How to query attachment resources

For `final_v3`, filter by `sura` and `ayah`:

```sh
awk -F '\t' 'NR==1 || ($5==103 && $6>=1 && $6<=3)' \
  resources/attachments.tsv
```

Use attachment rows as structural evidence only. Do not let attachment rows create unattested lexical meanings.

### How to query v4 branch dossiers

QAC stores roots compactly, for example `حقق`. The v4 DB stores `root_norm` with spaces, for example `ح ق ق`. When joining or filtering, normalize with `replace(root_norm, ' ', '')`.

First get the compact roots from QAC:

```sh
sqlite3 -header -column resources/qac.sqlite \
  "select distinct root_join_key
   from qac_morphemes
   where surah = 103
     and root_join_key != ''
   order by root_join_key;"
```

Then query v4 with those roots:

```sh
sqlite3 -header -column resources/furuq_v4.sqlite \
  "select root_norm, branch_id, branch_image_ar, what_is_ar
   from branch_images
   where contaminated = 'no'
     and replace(root_norm, ' ', '') in ('عصر','ءنس','خسر','ءمن','عمل','صلح','وصي','حقق')
   order by root_norm, branch_id;"
```

Alternative combined query:

```sh
sqlite3 -header -column resources/furuq_v4.sqlite \
  "attach database 'resources/qac.sqlite' as qac;
   select b.root_norm, b.branch_id, b.branch_image_ar, b.what_is_ar
   from branch_images b
   where b.contaminated = 'no'
     and replace(b.root_norm, ' ', '') in (
       select distinct m.root_join_key
       from qac.qac_morphemes m
       where m.surah = 103
         and m.ayah between 1 and 3
         and m.root_join_key != ''
     )
   order by b.root_norm, b.branch_id;"
```

Treat each root’s returned branches as one continuous dossier, not as separate opportunities to accumulate evidence:

```text
root_norm
  B001 branch_image_ar — what_is_ar
  B002 branch_image_ar — what_is_ar
  ...
```

Do not use contaminated branches. Do not read unrelated roots.

## Output requirements

For each seed, output:

- seed occurrence and branch ID, or constructional/morphosyntactic/temporal seed description;
- short title for the finding or image-branch;
- initial image and any predictions or implications that emerged during construction;
- roots visited and selected branches;
- short prose image description with every used root + branch ID marked by role:
  - `E` = expanding / generating branch used before freeze;
  - `C` = corroborating branch or root-dimension used after freeze;
  - `R` = resisting, narrowing, or constraining branch or root-dimension used after freeze;
  - format example: `(E: ع ط و B002)`, `(C: ر ح م B001 opening-context)`, `(R: ء م ن B002)`;
- for non-branch evidence, use the same labels without a branch ID, for example `(C: morphology Form VIII)`, `(C: sequence 103:2→103:3)`, `(R: attachment row ...)`, or `(C: basmala opening-context)`;
- when the same root contributes more than one role, name the distinct feature, for example `(E: صبر B00x steadiness-before-freeze)` versus `(C: صبر B00x endurance-after-freeze)`;
- branches rejected or terminated when relevant;
- generating set;
- frozen model;
- predictions at freeze;
- unused features tested after freeze;
- corroborators;
- constraints or resistance;
- rival forks if any;
- final grade: strong, medium-strong, medium, weak, or unlikely;
- short grade rationale.

Only add a very short interpretation at the end if the image-generation process produced a coherent image. An image may begin with weak expansion or corroborating links and still become coherent through later role completion and corroboration. Do not upgrade weak lexical evidence into primary contextual meaning; describe its subordinate role.

Failed seeds are part of the audit trail. Mark them explicitly rather than omitting them.
