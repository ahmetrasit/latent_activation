# Stage 2 Pass 2 proposal v2: compactness and closure edit

Use your previous short-surah synthesis as the default axis and prose base. This pass is a compactness, primary-meaning, and closure edit—not a new discovery pass and not a comprehensive finding-recovery pass. Consult the authorized sacred text, scaffold, findings, source notes, and annotations only to verify or repair the existing essay. Omission is preferable to lexical accretion when the compact relation is already clear.

Before rewriting, answer four questions silently:

1. Can the passage's primary movement be stated in one plain sentence?
2. Is one hinge clearly responsible for turning or completing that movement?
3. Does the close make the opening newly perceptible rather than merely restate it?
4. Does every paragraph materially serve that movement, hinge, or closure?

If the answer is yes, preserve the axis and compress. Do not generate rival architectures, subdivide the passage into macroblocks, or reopen every strong and medium finding. Rebuild the axis only if it is generic, contradicts the primary grammar, loses a participant or direction, or fails to explain the closure. Even then, choose the simplest source-supported relation.

Review omissions by role, not by finding count. Add a missing element only when it repairs a primary gloss, identifies the decisive hinge, preserves a necessary participant or direction, or materially changes the backward effect of the close. A grade alone never justifies an addition. Weak findings should remain out. Any added sentence must replace at least as much lower-value prose elsewhere.

Reduce the essay to one dominant relation and at most two brief secondary projections across the whole. Remove projection chains, alternate literal scenes, catalogs of variants, repeated caveats, duplicated glosses, abstract section summaries, and a second full replay. If a secondary image does not change what the close does to the opening, omit it. Preserve evidential limits by choosing a modest verb or qualifier once.

Organize the rewrite into roughly four to six substantial paragraphs. One paragraph may follow one ayah when the ayah is a real grammatical turn; several anchors should share a paragraph when they perform the same turn. Preserve exact guillemet quotation and a natural Turkish primary gloss at each first load-bearing phrase. Keep speaker, addressee, referent, voice, direction, and contextual meaning continuously clear.

The final paragraph should normally perform both closure and replay. Select the opening and closing anchors whose relation has changed; do not inventory every word or announce that an insight has occurred. Avoid meta-terms such as “aha,” mechanism, geometry, architecture, axis, audit, finding, or movie.

The revised essay should normally be 75–95% of the previous essay's length and must not be longer. If a missing primary gloss must be restored, pay for it by deleting repetition elsewhere. Compactness must not erase the primary sequence or turn secondary resonance into the apparent translation.

Write fluent, publication-quality Turkish, not corrections, annotations, or a coverage report. Use only the sacred Arabic text, authorized primary scaffold, supplied findings and source notes, supplied annotations, and previous synthesis. Introduce no new lexical or external evidence. Return only a title and the complete standalone Turkish essay.
