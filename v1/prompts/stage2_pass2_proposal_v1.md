# Stage 2 Pass 2 proposal v1: architecture audit and mechanism recovery

Treat your previous synthesis as an evidence-bearing draft and a reservoir of strong prose, not as a fixed narrative spine or paragraph plan. Review the complete coverage appendix, source notes, permitted findings, annotations, authorized scaffold, and sacred Arabic text internally. The purpose of this pass is first to verify or rebuild the passage-scale architecture, then to recover any missing load-bearing mechanisms without sacrificing clarity, pacing, or unity.

Before rewriting, state the previous essay's governing axis to yourself in one sentence. Test it against at least one plausible rival supported by the supplied material. Divide the passage into role-complete macroblocks and ask what indispensable work each block performs for the others. If the previous essay merely follows the ayat, treats macroblocks as adjacent extended images, or postpones their relationship until its conclusion, replace its architecture. Preserve successful sentences and scenes only where they serve the selected axis; reorder, merge, compress, or discard them freely.

A valid axis must describe a transformation across macroblocks, not a shared topic. Test whether an early scene is a continuing event, an enacted model, an analogy at a verified relational level, a frame, a prediction, or the first phase of a reversal. Then identify where later wording changes its participants, domain, direction, force, agency, scale, epistemic status, outcome, or backward meaning. Do not force any of these designs; choose the one with the strongest independent anchors and constraints. Never identify distinct agents or events merely because their operations correspond.

Build an internal bridge map before touching the prose. Each accepted bridge must join at least two textual moments in different macroblocks and must preserve their primary contextual meanings. Prefer bridges that explain why the later wording is needed and why the opening is heard differently after it. If a proposed bridge is only a broad theme, a sound resemblance without functional change, or a remote literal picture, reject it.

Next group related findings by the mechanism they jointly perform. A mechanism is load-bearing when it materially changes at least one participant, direction, force, medium, constraint, temporal phase, transformation, outcome, source, knower, or backward reactivation within the selected passage architecture. Strong and medium grades should influence rhetorical weight, but they do not create an obligation to mention every finding. Several findings may be fully represented through one precise composite movement. A finding that repeats an established relation, adds decorative lexical texture, or supplies another literal version of an already audible operation need not appear separately.

Restore a missing mechanism only when it changes how the larger passage is perceived. Recover the concrete operation rather than merely its topic: entering, enclosing, penetrating, supporting, striking, extracting, separating, gathering, redirecting, maintaining, or another verified action should remain precise when that precision matters. Do not enumerate every vessel, body part, material, animal, occupation, or scene that can instantiate it. When several projections express the same relation, choose the clearest one or unite them only at their verified relational level. Admit a weak finding only when it fills a specific unfilled role, completes a necessary transition, or makes an established mechanism concretely perceptible. Never add a paragraph solely to preserve a weak or remote image.

Now rewrite the entire essay. Organize it by passage-scale transformations, not by findings or ayat. A short surah should normally have only a few substantial paragraphs. Several Arabic anchors should share a paragraph when they advance the same operation. Split a paragraph only when the active participant, domain, direction, operation, or epistemic status genuinely changes. Do not advance merely because the next ayah has arrived.

Follow the Quranic sequence when first establishing each primary development, but make cross-block reactivation audible in the body as soon as the later wording earns it. Do not save every connection for the final paragraph. Each substantive paragraph should carry an unresolved movement forward, apply or reverse an earlier operation, reveal an earlier effect's source or outcome, or close a role in a way that changes what preceded it.

At the first load-bearing appearance of an Arabic word or construction, preserve or repair its exact guillemet quotation and a brief natural Turkish primary gloss. Keep textual position, participants, direction, grammatical voice, and primary contextual meaning continuously perceptible. Absorb grammatical qualifications and evidential restraint into the sentence where they matter. Give a secondary projection at most one concise boundary. If it requires repeated explanation that the passage is not literally describing that scene, reduce it to its relational contribution or omit it.

Added depth must be paid for by removing repetition elsewhere. The revised essay should normally remain between roughly 90% and 105% of the previous essay's length and must not exceed 110% unless an entire missing passage-scale mechanism has to be restored. Even then, rebuild and compress local exposition before allowing growth. Do not preserve a sentence merely because it is polished.

Reserve the complete replay for the ending, but do not make it an inventory. Move through the Quranic order by selecting the anchors that enact the dominant transformation. Let the conclusion reveal how the close has altered the opening and why the passage can stop there. Do not announce an “aha” or name the mechanism, geometry, architecture, axis, audit, or findings. The ending must perform the recognition rather than explain that recognition has occurred.

Write fluent, publication-quality Turkish, not corrections, annotations, an audit, or a branch catalog. Use only the sacred Arabic text, authorized primary scaffold, supplied findings and source notes, supplied annotations, and previous synthesis. Introduce no new lexical or external evidence. Return only a title and the complete standalone Turkish essay.
