  OBJECTIVE

  Develop a unified synthesis explaining how the target surah’s apparently
  unrelated scenes, subsections, images, lexical choices, and secondary resonances
  belong to one larger composition.

  Working hypothesis: the surah is governed by a small number of axes, usually
  two to four. Every significant component serves one or more of these axes
  through its primary contextual function, a controlled secondary resonance, or
  an interaction between both.

  Think of the surah as a three-dimensional structure rather than a linear text.
  Elements separated by many ayahs may occupy adjacent positions in spatial,
  temporal, relational, epistemic, moral, or agency-based dimensions. Look for
  folds, reversals, nested containers, repeated trajectories, changes of scale,
  movement between exterior and interior, gathering and dispersal, concealment
  and disclosure, or shifts between human and divine perspectives. Do not force
  these particular dimensions if the surah establishes different ones.

  ANALYTICAL METHOD

  First reconstruct the surah’s overall architecture:

  1. Identify its few governing axes.
  2. Describe how those axes intersect.
  3. Model the surah as a three-dimensional form.
  4. Explain why linearly distant components become close within that form.
  5. Identify the central turn, hinge, reversal, or point of maximum depth.
  6. Determine how the ending reactivates, reverses, completes, or exposes the
     opening and middle.

  Then perform an exhaustive internal audit of every meaningful component,
  including:

  - individual rooted words and their accepted branches;
  - particles, conjunctions, prepositions, pronouns, and demonstratives;
  - tense, voice, number, gender, and changes of grammatical subject;
  - participles, verbal nouns, passives, predicates, and parallel constructions;
  - repetitions, omissions, ambiguities, temporal brackets, and delayed closure;
  - verse order, scene order, transitions, and abrupt discourse turns;
  - sensory details such as sound, light, movement, touch, dust, water, fire,
    color, bodily effort, silence, or concealment;
  - rhyme, phonetic pairing, repeated endings, and formal pacing when securely
    observable;
  - changes between singular and plural, exterior and interior, or action and
    knowledge.

  For every component, answer internally:

  1. Why is this component here?
  2. Which axis or intersection of axes does it serve?
  3. What is its primary contextual function?
  4. Which secondary branch or resonance is genuinely supported?
  5. What exactly does the secondary resonance clarify, enforce, or intensify in
     the primary layer?
  6. Why is the component expressed in this exact form and position?
  7. Why does it occur before or after the surrounding components?
  8. What later element does it prepare, mirror, reverse, or reactivate?
  9. What would disappear from the composition if it were omitted or moved?

  PRIMARY AND SECONDARY LAYERS

  Keep the following categories separate:

  - Primary contextual meaning:
    The meaning most strongly required by syntax, morphology, sequence, and the
    local scene.

  - Secondary lexical resonance:
    An accepted v4 branch that does not replace the primary meaning but adds a
    spatial, relational, sensory, or conceptual dimension.

  - Inter-surah corroboration:
    A verified occurrence elsewhere that places several relevant roots, phrases,
    images, or constructions in a meaningful local cluster.

  - Formal resonance:
    A similarity of grammar, sequencing, oath structure, repetition, sound, or
    discourse movement.

  - Weak branch shadow:
    A possible secondary texture with limited corroboration.

  - Defeated alternative:
    A branch or image-chain whose predicted participants, objects, syntax, or
    consequences are absent.

  Never promote a secondary resonance into the primary reading merely because it
  is vivid. Never import an agent, object, setting, or narrative identity that the
  target passage does not explicitly or grammatically establish.

  Every second-layer resonance included in the final synthesis must answer:

  “What does this resonance make clearer or stronger in the primary reading?”

  Do not list resonances that cannot answer that question.

  WHY THIS MODE OF PRESENTATION?

  Treat the surah’s degree of vividness, compression, repetition, ambiguity, or
  abruptness as evidence rather than decoration.

  Do not merely explain what a scene depicts. Explain why the reader must
  experience it in that particular form before receiving the explicit judgment.

  For every major scene or discourse unit, ask:

  - Why is this rendered as a sensory scene, dialogue, oath, command, question,
    parable, legal formulation, compressed image, or repeated statement?
  - Why would a direct proposition or short summary be insufficient?
  - What does the chosen form make the reader hear, see, anticipate, infer, feel,
    or reconstruct?
  - Does the passage initially withhold identity and make the reader infer a
    cause from its traces?
  - Does its pacing make the reader experience acceleration, interruption,
    enclosure, suspense, reversal, or exposure?
  - Does its form reproduce the very operation that the surah later describes?

  COUNTERFACTUAL COMPRESSION TEST

  Before writing the final synthesis, imagine replacing every major unit with a
  plain summary.

  For example:

  “This happened, and therefore the human condition is such-and-such.”

  Then determine precisely what would be lost:

  - sensory preparation;
  - spatial depth;
  - emotional force;
  - change of agency;
  - hidden-to-visible movement;
  - primary–secondary interaction;
  - inter-surah contact points;
  - formal correspondence between opening and ending;
  - the reader’s participation as observer, witness, or inferred object;
  - or the distinction between merely seeing and fully knowing.

  A component is not fully explained until the analysis shows both how it fits
  the surah and why a simpler formulation could not perform the same work.

  INTER-SURAH SEARCH

  Use broader Quranic associations selectively.

  Prioritize:

  1. exact phrase correspondences;
  2. rare combinations of multiple target roots;
  3. the same roots appearing in a similar local sequence;
  4. branch alternation involving the same root;
  5. matching grammatical or rhetorical structures;
  6. matching movement between exterior and interior, concealment and
     disclosure, action and judgment, or another axis established by the target
     surah.

  For every inter-surah association:

  - verify the actual ayah and roots in the QAC resource;
  - verify relevant branches in v4;
  - state whether it supports identity, mechanism, direction, contrast,
    intensification, or closure;
  - explain what it strengthens in the target surah’s primary layer;
  - preserve the target surah’s local grammatical constraints.

  Do not use another surah to override what the target surah itself leaves
  unspecified.

  FINAL SELF-AUDIT

  Before writing, confirm internally that:

  - every subsection has a compositional function;
  - every major word and construction has been assigned to an axis;
  - every vivid detail has passed the counterfactual compression test;
  - every secondary resonance strengthens a specified primary function;
  - every inter-surah connection has been verified;
  - weak and defeated alternatives have not been silently promoted;
  - abrupt transitions have been explained as structural operations;
  - the final verse genuinely closes the shape established by the opening;
  - the synthesis answers “why here,” “why in this form,” and “why not more
    simply.”

  details, in that order and form, to make its larger structure work.
