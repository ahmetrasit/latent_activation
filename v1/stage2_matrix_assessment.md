# Stage 2 Pass 1 matrix assessment: S100 and S108

## Matrix interpretation

All 16 requested outputs are present.

The lanes are upstream-model conditions, not replications:

- **primary Stage 1 input:** generated by `sol 5.6-max`;
- **comparator Stage 1 input:** generated by `gpt 5.5 xhigh`;
- **Stage 2 models in each lane:** `gpt 5.5 xhigh` and `sol 5.6 xhigh`;
- **Stage 2 prompts in each lane:** full v1 and compact v2-short.

Therefore:

- comparisons between v1 and v2 with the same passage, Stage 1 file, and Stage 2 model isolate the prompt variant;
- comparisons between Stage 2 models with the same passage, Stage 1 file, and prompt isolate the downstream model;
- comparisons between primary and comparator lanes measure the effect of two different Stage 1 realizations and their generating models;
- the matrix has one realization per cell, so it is a factorial screen, not a replicated estimate of prompt reliability.

The matrix-wide assessment concerns selection, rendering, traceability, and reader-facing behavior. The S100 spark mechanism is additionally checked below against the raw rows in `resources/furuq_v4.sqlite`.

## S100 result

Compact v2 is consistently shorter than full v1:

| Stage 1 lane | Stage 2 model | v1 words | v2 words | v2 change |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| primary / sol input | gpt 5.5 | 677 | 443 | -34.6% |
| primary / sol input | sol 5.6 | 867 | 463 | -46.6% |
| comparator / gpt input | gpt 5.5 | 513 | 450 | -12.3% |
| comparator / gpt input | sol 5.6 | 902 | 462 | -48.8% |

The important result is not merely compression. All four v2 outputs retain the coarse passage-scale features the prior verdict said required full mode:

- the active plural opening reaches a center;
- the text changes to singular human diagnosis without identifying the agents;
- strong attachment to the loved good supplies an inward force or orientation;
- passive grave/chest exposure reverses the opening direction;
- the closing subject reassigns the epistemic endpoint to the Lord; and
- at least one later-to-earlier connection occurs before or within the final movement.

However, this is not complete lexical synthesis. All eight S100 outputs, under both prompts and both Stage 1 lanes, omit the V4 `ح ب ب / B011` reactivation described in the next section. They render the opening `وري / قدح` spark and the contextual love at `لحب الخير`, but do not make the branch-specific hoof-spark/benefit test audible. The compact outputs therefore retain the macro-axis while dropping one of its most distinctive lexical mechanisms. The full v1 outputs do not reliably rescue it either.

The full v1 outputs usually add lexical qualification, morphological detail, and more explicit evidential limits. That can improve precision, especially in the two sol 5.6 renderings, but it also returns to local exposition and nearly doubles v2 length in two cells. This matrix does not show that the heavier v1 procedure is sufficient or necessary for S100's full lexical relation; it shows only that v2 can preserve the coarser passage-scale relation.

Because the same S100 axis is already explicit in both Stage 1 packages, the result also supports the interface diagnosis in `stage2_review.md`: the renderer may be selecting a supplied axis rather than discovering it through the v1 architecture exercise.

## S100 V4 verification: the missing second spark

Direct queries against `resources/furuq_v4.sqlite` return these accepted branches:

| Root / branch | V4 branch image | Relevant source phrase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `و ر ي / B002` | hidden fire coming out of the fire-stick | `ورى الزند خرجت ناره` |
| `ق د ح / B001` | striking out fire | `قدحت النار ... من الزند وغيره` |
| `ث و ر / B002` | stirring something from its settled state | `أثرت الأرض إثارة`; an animal stirs earth with its legs |
| `ب ع ث ر / B001` | upturning buried contents | `بعثر ما في القبور أثير وأخرج`; grave earth is stirred and its contents brought out |
| `ح ب ب / B011` | `نار الحباحب شرر لا ينتفع به` / useless sparks and firefly-glow | `نار الحباحب ما اقتدحت ... من تصادم الحجارة`; `نار الحباحب ما أورت الخيل لا ينتفع به` |
| `خ ي ر / B001` | what all incline toward as beneficial good | `الخير ما يرغب فيه الكل`; good is opposed to evil or harm |
| `ح ص ل / B002` | bringing out the inner value from its covering | extraction of gold or silver from ore and kernel from husks |

V4 therefore does contain a second spark resource for S100. It occurs in the `ح ب ب` root family: `نار الحباحب`, including the dictionary statement that it is what horses strike or kindle and that it is not benefited from. Its placement beside `الخير` permits a controlled benefit test: the opening hoof-spark returns at `لحب الخير` as the text names the human's intense attachment to wealth.

There is also a stronger horse-to-`خير` bridge outside the V4 branch row but inside the repository's sacred Quran corpus. S38:31 displays `الصافنات الجياد`, then S38:32 says `إني أحببت حب الخير عن ذكر ربي`, and S38:33 returns to the horses with the feminine plural pronoun `ردوها`. S100 places the horse operation before the same `حب الخير` collocation and opposes `لربه` to that attachment. The opening is therefore not an arbitrary force illustration: it enacts the prized good whose love the middle diagnoses. Clean V4 B001 supports the beneficial-good dimension. V4 B004 contains a wealth gloss for S100:8, but it is marked `contaminated='yes'` and is excluded under the Stage 1 evidence contract.

The boundary is equally important. V4 assigns contextual love to accepted `ح ب ب / B002` (`المحبة الملازمة للقلب`), while B011's lexical units are `نار الحباحب` and `حبحبتها`. B011 must not replace the contextual sense of `حب`, make love literally fire, or prove that the opening sparks themselves are useless. It is a form-remote root-family reactivation.

The opening also previews the final operation with unusually exact lexical support. `فأثرن` stirs settled earth and raises dust; V4's grave gloss for `بعثر` explicitly says `أثير وأخرج`, stirred up and brought out. `فوسطن ... جمعا` then carries the force from the edge into a gathered center. At the close, grave earth is stirred and its buried contents brought out, after which `ح ص ل / B002` extracts the inner content of the chests from its covering. `ح ص ل` supplies extraction, not fire.

The defensible sequence is therefore:

`horses associated with حب الخير in the Quran corpus -> وري/قدح hoof-spark -> ثور/نقع earth disturbed -> وسط/جمع center entered -> حب B011 + خير second spark/benefit test -> بعثر grave earth stirred and contents brought out -> حصل/صدور inward content extracted`.

This does not make verses 1-5 and 9-10 grammatically one event or make the horses the literal agents of the final passives. It establishes something more precise and more useful: verses 1-5 are an enacted preview. The loved horses visibly strike hidden fire, disturb earth, raise dust, and penetrate a center; the final hours apply that demonstrated operation to the human, overturning grave earth and extracting the hidden center. The close explains why this apparently separate horse scene was selected.

The SQLite build has `frozen_corpus_enabled=0`; both `corpus_forms` and `lexical_unit_corpus_links` contain zero rows. Consequently, `corpus_link_status=not_form_unit` cannot be used as corpus-link proof in this database. The distance judgment above rests on the directly queried lexical expressions and branch assignments, not on a populated Qur'anic form-link table.

Every v1 output remains lean and uses four to six paragraphs. None exposes manufactured rival architectures, macroblock terminology, or a many-scene essay. The two primary-lane v1 outputs are complete at roughly 350 words; the v2 lower word target appears to pull both primary-lane outputs upward to about 450 words.

All eight S108 outputs use more than one load-bearing hinge:

- `فـ` connects completed giving to commanded response;
- the continuing second-person chain preserves the addressee across giving, Lord-relation, commands, and hostility; and
- `هو` fixes the final predicate on the hater.

The successful v2 outputs therefore do not validate its “one decisive hinge” instruction. They succeed by using a minimal hinge set despite that instruction.

## Model and input effects

The largest downstream-model difference occurs under S100 v1. Sol 5.6 produces 867 versus 677 words on the primary input and 902 versus 513 on the comparator input. Under v2, the S100 outputs collapse into a much narrower 443-463 word band.

That stability is useful as a length-control effect, but it is not automatically a quality win. It may also suppress legitimate differences in input richness. On S108, model differences are small under both prompts.

The Stage 1 input matters visibly. S108 v1 is about 350 words on the sol-generated primary package and about 450 words on the gpt-generated comparator package. V2 again normalizes all cells to roughly 450 words. This reinforces the need to treat lane differences as upstream-package effects, not as replications.

## Reader-facing defects and cautions

- `v1/outputs/108/stage2_p1_v2short_gpt55_xhigh.md` contains the broken phrase “ona yönelen düşman başa yüklenir.” This fails publication-quality Turkish even though the surrounding relation is clear.
- Several outputs turn S100 `صُبْحًا` from raid time into a general disclosure or visibility threshold. Both prompt families do this, so it is more likely inherited from Stage 1 or the shared synthesis target than caused by v1 versus v2.
- Several S108 outputs make continuity, protection, or inability-to-cut claims stronger than the local grammar alone establishes. These may be authorized secondary syntheses, but they require Stage 3 verification and must not be scored as settled truth.
- Some v1 outputs add useful boundary language; others spend that extra space restating the same opening-to-closing relation. Word count alone cannot separate those cases.
- Two complete S100 v2 outputs and two complete S108 v1 outputs fall at or below the proposed v2 lower range. A lower word bound is not defensible.

## What this matrix changes

The evidence does **not** support activating a full-v1-for-S100 / compact-v2-for-S108 routing split.

Observed behavior points toward one adaptive, selection-first Pass 1 prompt:

1. Default to the simplest sufficient relation.
2. Preserve participant, voice, direction, and domain changes from v1.
3. Use a minimal sufficient hinge set, not exactly one hinge.
4. Escalate to macroblock and rival-axis analysis only when a visible plan records positive structural evidence.
5. Keep v2's omission permission and strict secondary-projection budget.
6. Remove word-count floors and paragraph quotas; use only a soft ceiling and a no-padding rule.
7. Do not require every paragraph to cross macroblocks.
8. Emit the synthesis plan and source IDs so the value of the heavier reasoning can be evaluated separately from essay length.

Full and compact modes may still prove useful as internal planning depths, but this matrix does not justify separate reader-facing prompt families. Neither current prompt reliably preserves the complete convergence visible in the S100 audit. The production repair must remain passage-agnostic:

1. Stage 1 retrieves exact or tightly normalized recurring Quranic formulas for every passage, recording the matched span, reference, nearby participants, and relation pattern.
2. Stage 1 detects definitional cross-links when a branch's V4 source phrase or image explicitly contains another passage root or operation.
3. The Stage 2 plan applies a counterfactual specificity test: if the proposed architecture would work unchanged with a generic actor, object, or force replacing the selected scene, it has not yet explained why that scene was chosen.
4. Every selected passage-scale relation records its independently supported links and the constraints against literalizing secondary branches.

The horse, `حب الخير`, grave-exposure, and B011 links are S100 evaluation expectations derived after the run. They must not appear in the generic prompt or routing rules.

## Next experiment

Do not proceed directly from these single samples to production Pass 2.

1. Blind and anonymize the 16 outputs.
2. Apply a faithfulness veto before prose scoring.
3. Score primary sequence, participant/voice fidelity, cross-section relation, backward reactivation, restraint, and Turkish quality separately.
4. Add at least one independent replication per cell or repeat a focused subset containing the four S100 prompt comparisons and four S108 primary-lane comparisons.
5. Test a unified adaptive prompt against both current variants on the same four Stage 1 files.
6. Record prompt, input, output, model, and settings hashes in a run manifest.

The current result is strong enough to reject the original routing claim as established, but not strong enough to choose a production prompt.
