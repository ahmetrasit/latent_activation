## Root-cause diagnosis

Pass 1 did enumerate all 55 uncontaminated lexical branches and stated that every other root dossier was visited. The limitation was nevertheless real in its **constructive depth and audit visibility**:

1. It usually froze after the first two to four compatible roots. This protected unused corroboration, but overcorrected: a selected branch was not always allowed to change the search key and trigger a second full cross-root sweep.
2. It reported “visited” roots collectively and mostly printed only selected branches. A reader therefore could not distinguish a genuinely tested-but-dead root from one merely named in the sweep boilerplate.
3. It distributed some larger images across separate seed records instead of recreating every reachable rival under each relevant seed. The full camel/provision cycle, bow–quiver conflict, food-preparation chain, reproductive-body fork, and dust-cloud/rain fork were consequently underdeveloped.
4. It sometimes treated preservation of post-freeze evidence as a reason to stop construction. The correct control is to freeze **each mature fork separately**, leaving different unused sets for different forks, not to suppress further forks.

Pass 2 corrects this by running, for every seed, (a) a complete first sweep, (b) a constructive resweep after every selected transformation, (c) explicit forks, and (d) a root-by-root ledger. `E` is pre-freeze construction, `C` post-freeze corroboration, `K` post-freeze constraint, and `Ø` a dossier tested with no passage-local transformation. A small selected set is acceptable only when every other dossier is explicitly accounted for; branch-visit count itself is never evidence.
