## Independent entry

Before opening the three formation artifacts, read the exact passage,
morphology, syntax, complete branch inventory, and primary scaffold for
yourself.

Construct an internal topology of participants, actions, changing agency,
directions, media, pressures, temporal relations, repetitions, contrasts,
delayed completions, and opening-closing returns. Do not write this as a
preliminary branch inventory.

Only then read the formation artifacts. Treat them as generative material, not
as a canonical hierarchy or a ceiling. Return repeatedly to the raw inventory.
Recruit any exact branch that completes an interaction, structure, or backward
return that the formation author did not finish.

Freshness means that you may regroup, divide, merge, rescale, or place
formations into new contact. It does not mean imposing a stricter local-sense
gate or sending a completed formation back to its initial weakness.

## Passage-scale composition

Find the interactions and architectures that make distant passage positions
part of one living activation field. Ask:

- what each formation changes in the direct contextual hearing;
- what later wording changes about earlier wording;
- which formations supply roles, limits, phases, or consequences to one
  another;
- which formations must remain independent because they perform different
  work;
- how the passage's sequence and closure become newly intelligible;
- what new passage-level consequence appears only when several formations are
  held together.

Do not require one total metaphor or mechanism. The atlas may have several
centers when the passage sustains several operations or exact architectures.
Shared subject matter does not authorize merger, and different material
realizations need not compete for selection.

Use functional hierarchy only to compose the atlas. Give broad interacting
formations enough room to become perceptible. Keep autonomous formations
complete when they add a distinct operation or architecture. Let fine
recognitions live inside the movement they sharpen. Hierarchy changes space
and placement, not whether a mature formation exists.

The lexical bridge is part of every discovery. Preserve which exact passage
word contributes each decisive facet, what role or relation it acquires, and
how the completed whole changes the primary hearing. A vivid image without
its word-to-role bridge is not sufficient synthesis.

The formation reservoir remains available beyond this atlas. You do not need
to turn every unfinished seed into an atlas section. Do not silently erase a
mature reciprocal formation merely because it is small, unusual, secondary,
or independent of the broadest movement.

## Analytical movement

Each substantial movement should earn a precise lexical recognition and, by
its end, leave some part of the primary passage sounding different. Do not
manufacture one surprise per paragraph. Allow a recognition to accumulate,
interact with another formation, and land retrospectively when that is how the
passage produces it.

Keep the direct contextual movement as the continuous subject. Secondary
formations give it spatial, bodily, temporal, material, relational, causal,
and formal resolution; they do not become alternative translations requiring
a verdict.

End with a transformed passage-scale return in which the closure sends new
force into the opening. Stop when that return lands rather than appending a
branch census or summary of headings.
