## What the rerun says about cause and quality

The results weaken a simple explanation that expanded context scopes were the
sole cause of missing v1 findings. The smaller view improves Luna's retrieval of
the optical branch and Sol's rudder integration, but it loses other findings.
V1's most integrated social chain and counted-case ledger remain missing, even
though the context cues now match v1 exactly and all focus evidence is preserved.
Discovery allocation, selection among competing branches, and integration remain
live issues. Sampling and the necessary key-composition prompt change prevent a
clean causal attribution from this one pair of samples.

The smaller view does improve input efficiency and preserves reliable citation
assembly. Both models also offer useful additions relative to v1: Sol's
work-as-track, assay/coating, and sight/steering analogies, and both models'
housing/migration contrasts. Several of these additions were already present in
v2 experimentation; they are not all discoveries unique to v3. V1 remains more
complete on its distinctive social, trust, and counted-case connections.

Against initial v2 Luna, compact v3 is substantially broader and finally retains
the integrated optical web. Against the later v2 Luna outputs, it is usable
without repairing root labels, incomplete occurrence indices, or trigger lists.
Against two-stage v2 Sol, it is a valid, much smaller one-call output, but loses
counting, cosmetic kohl, and some coverage. Thus the practical gain is a compact,
reliable workflow with a competitive set of discoveries; a blanket semantic
win over v1 or all v2 iterations is not supported.

The new analogies remain qualified. Sol's metallic-finish model uses a distant
coating image under contextual rainwater, explicitly preserving the ordinary
rain reading. Both work-as-road models infer habit or infrastructure from a
traveled-road image. The ocular and rudder models are not alternative literal
translations. Mechanical acceptance is not confirmation of these causal arrows.
