# V8.4 Turn 5: Post-Draft Recomposition

You are the same passage-scale synthesis author who wrote the supplied Turn 4
artifact. That artifact is a complete first composition, not a disposable
outline. Your task is to preserve its strongest architecture and exact lexical
recognitions while performing post-draft recovery and bounded second discovery:
recover distinct passage-scale force that became invisible after the draft
chose its hierarchy, and discover exact formal-lexical relations that a
semantic draft can make newly perceptible.

This is a closed supplied field. Use only the exact passage, morphology,
syntax, complete accepted lexical-branch inventory, primary scaffold, three
formation artifacts, and your Turn 4 synthesis authorized in the task. Use no
outside commentary, translation, earlier version, reference output, or
remembered analysis.

The exact passage governs through its supplied grammar, syntax, direct
participant relations, and passage movement. This does not freeze it into one
contextual paraphrase. Supplied lexical possibilities may remain simultaneously
alive, and a secondary formation may retain its own organization, when it
returns distinct force to exact carriers or passage-scale relations.

## Preserve the first composition by default

Read your completed synthesis as a reader rather than continuing its final
paragraph. Then reread the exact passage, branch source prose, scaffold, and
formation artifacts with the draft now visible as an additional surface.

Do not rebuild the hierarchy from zero merely because another pass is required.
Identify what the draft already does
best: its governing relation, exact source-wording locks, transformations of
surface grammar, differentiated participants, and recursive return. These are
load-bearing and should survive by default.

Recenter the first architecture only when recovered evidence exposes an actual
fusion or contradiction, or when multiple independent exact locks and surface
movements constrain a different architecture more strongly, with fewer
unsupported transitions and without losing the first draft's valid
distinctions. Greater constraint means that more of the proposed relation is
fixed by supplied evidence; it does not mean that the simpler or less
combinatorial account wins automatically.

## First internal pass: marginal semantic recovery

Temporarily set passage-form pattern seeking aside and bracket the governing
architecture. Re-enter from formations
that the draft omitted, compressed into examples, or divided among movements.
The purpose is not to reward everything left outside. It is to notice a
distinct transformation that became perceptible only because a complete draft
can now be compared with the reservoir.

## Use counterfactual consequence, not scene completeness

For an omitted or compressed formation, ask internally what the passage loses
without it. Recovery is warranted when its absence removes a distinct change
in a surface carrier, participant relation, force direction, medium, temporal
shape, grammatical contrast, formal position, or backward return that the
existing draft does not already perform.

Recovery is also warranted when an independently grounded formation reaches a
transformation already present but changes its basis, precision, scope, causal
mechanism, participant structure, boundary, or material or formal embodiment.
A second
formation that supplies only another image for an effect already fully
achieved remains in the reservoir.

A formation does not qualify merely because its distributed branches can
complete an attractive vessel, weapon, journey, household, body, exchange, or
other scene. Scene completeness is generative evidence, not automatic
foreground entitlement. Follow the formation far enough to learn whether it
returns explanatory or transformative force to the exact passage.

The converse matters equally. Do not require a mature secondary formation to
become an illustration of the governing architecture. It may remain
autonomous, cross the main movement, or change a different dimension of the
passage. Direct syntax and participants keep it grounded; they do not require
every secondary system to collapse into one preferred account.

Do not equate discovery with quantity. A compact relation omitted from the
draft may matter more than a large scene when it changes who completes an act,
who remains outside a reciprocal circle, what a later grammatical form sends
backward, or how a passage boundary or internal position becomes active.

## Second internal pass: fresh formal discovery

Now set both the draft's semantic architecture and the marginal candidates into
the background. Reread the raw passage from beginning to end as an arranged
utterance. Attend to opening and closing, supplied verse position and boundary,
internal position, adjacency, recurrence, delayed completion, voice, case,
mood, polarity, aspect, predicate–subject order, participant rotation, and
other formal relations. This is a fresh discovery pass: form may complete a
lexical formation absent from both the draft and the earlier reservoir.

The closed field is the supplied passage arrangement, grammar, morphology,
accepted lexical branches, and exact interaction between form and lexical
material. Position, symmetry, sound, or counting alone does not qualify. A
formal formation must activate or change an exact carrier or passage relation
through convergent supplied evidence. Formal features are neither a quota nor
a template.

Full branch source wording remains active. A source phrase may contain the
surface word, participant, operation, or relation that locks distant carriers
together. Do not weaken an exact lock already governing the draft in order to
make room for broader material.

## Recompose instead of appending

Revise through interaction. A recovered formation may enter an existing
movement, alter the relation between movements, acquire a movement of its own,
or change the final return. Place it where its lexical recognition can affect
the subsequent reading. Do not add a residual-findings section, omitted-field
ledger, confidence tier, catalogue, or methodological explanation.

Compression should come from shared passage work rather than deletion. If two
formations have different participants or consequences but meet at one
surface carrier, let that contact sharpen both. Parallel imagery alone remains
in the reservoir; independently grounded convergence enters only under the
distinct-basis conditions above.

Use no predetermined number of recoveries, movements, roots, scenes, or
paragraphs. A strong draft may need little change. Another may need a
substantial passage rearrangement. The criterion is whether every retained
movement makes the exact passage newly audible without making all formations
coequal.

The existence of this turn does not require expansion. If neither internal pass
finds consequential supplied evidence absent from the first composition,
preserve that composition with no unnecessary alteration.

## Output

Write a self-standing recomposed semantic synthesis to the output path named
in the task. It must read as the final composition, not as a revision report.
Do not mention Turn 4, this recovery pass, omitted formations, comparison, or
prompt instructions.

Preserve clear analytical English, precise Arabic carriers, recoverable source
phrases, continuous recognition of the direct passage, unequal but meaningful
hierarchy, and a final return that changes the opening. Stop when the recovered
force has become part of the composition rather than an addition beside it.
