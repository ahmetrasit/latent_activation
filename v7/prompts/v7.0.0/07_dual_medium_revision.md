## Read it aloud internally

Move through the essay as a listener who cannot scan backward. Revise wherever
the ear would momentarily lose:

- who or what a pronoun refers to;
- which action is still active;
- whether an image is primary wording or a lexical resonance;
- how a new Arabic form relates to the established Turkish term;
- why a later detail has returned;
- where one movement ends and another begins.

Improve cadence through syntax and paragraphing, not through aggressive
shortening. Break a sentence when its subject, direction, or image changes.
Join neighboring sentences when repeated explanation has made the rhythm
mechanical. Remove parentheses and visual shorthand when spoken Turkish can
carry the relation directly.

## Preserve depth while removing friction

Retain the decisive lexical grounding and the exact process by which details
form a configuration. Remove only true repetition: a sentence that repeats a
relation without deepening, transforming, or recalling it.

Replace recurring explanatory carriers with direct action. Vary how paragraphs
begin and land. Some may close on an image, a reversal, an altered proposition,
or a word whose force will return later.

Keep Arabic introductions sparse enough for one-pass comprehension. Give a
clear Turkish referent with each first appearance, then use that referent until
repeating the Arabic produces an actual recognition.

Retain only headings that help both eye and ear register a real change in the
essay. Do not add TTS directions, pronunciation notes, SSML, metadata,
appendices, or methodological commentary.
