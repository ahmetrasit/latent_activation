# V7.1.0 Turn 6: Canonical Turkish Publication Draft

Continue as the same Turkish author. The oral rehearsal has established the
reader's route through the semantic master.

Write a complete authored Turkish essay for both reading and listening. It
must retain the gold-level analytical depth of the semantic master while
letting discoveries arrive as human recognitions rather than report entries.
The publication should be substantially more concentrated than the semantic
master. Depth comes from completed relations, not from reproducing every
branch object named during discovery.

## Audience and voice

Write for one curious person who may know the passage through translation but
has no Arabic or linguistic training. Sound like one thoughtful person showing
another person something precise and consequential.

Do not sound like a lecturer, preacher, announcer, academic abstract, rubric,
or findings renderer. Do not mention the workflow, lexical database, branch
IDs, confidence, semantic categories, or research procedure.

## Human discovery order

Use the oral rehearsal as a living route rather than prose to paraphrase.
Begin with its genuine tension and orient the direct meaning quickly. Bring the
first governing formation into view while curiosity is active. A minor vivid
detail may sharpen that opening, but it must not displace the passage-wide
question that gives the essay momentum.

Across the essay, vary the route by which recognition happens. The audience
may encounter a familiar primary hearing, notice an unresolved detail, see a
concrete lexical formation assemble from surrounding words, and return with a
changed hearing. This recurring movement should remain flexible rather than
becoming a visible formula.

Preserve:

- every governing and independent formation;
- the exact roles by which distributed details complete one another;
- retroactive changes produced by the completed whole;
- interactions between distinct formations;
- the direct primary meaning as the continuously deepening subject.

Place memorable fine-scale details only where they sharpen an active movement,
produce a recognition, or prepare a later return. Do not append them as
miscellaneous observations, reproduce the reservoir, or give generic
categories their own movements.

## Lexical explanation

Make the relation visible before asking the audience to retain its lexical
parts. Introduce contributing words in stages as participants, actions,
materials, directions, or consequences inside an already perceptible
formation.

Explain word-family relations in varied, ordinary Turkish. Once a relation is
clear, let the object and action carry it. Avoid repeatedly announcing that a
word "opens a field," "evokes an image," "activates a meaning," or "belongs to
a root."

Use the smallest set of lexical details that lets the listener perceive the
complete configuration. Several words may arrive together when they take
distinct roles in one operation. Do not place unrelated co-branches into one
paragraph merely because they share a root, body part, material, or abstract
label.

Use exact Arabic script selectively for decisive surface wording from the
supplied passage. Give its Turkish sense before or together with it. Do not
print isolated root letters, branch prose, morphology labels, or non-passage
Arabic merely to display sourcing. Every sentence must remain intelligible if
the listener misses an Arabic pronunciation.

## Prose and cadence

Prefer concrete nouns, active verbs, clear referents, and ordinary modern
Turkish. Vary sentence length and paragraph movement. Let short sentences mark
earned turns; let longer sentences carry a stable image without losing their
subject.

Use transitions that arise from returning objects, actions, participants, and
questions. Avoid a chain of miniature explanations, repeated rhetorical
questions, repeated lexical-introduction formulas, repeated paragraph
conclusions, and abstract summary sentences that merely rename what the image
already showed.

Do not narrate semantic boundaries or repeatedly say that a resonance is not
the direct meaning, does not replace it, or should not be confused with it.
Keep the primary proposition present through the action itself. Use negative
sentences when the passage's own contrast or the discovery genuinely depends
on negation, not as methodological reassurance.

Use a small number of natural headings only when the essay truly changes
operation, scale, participant, or direction. The title and headings must also
sound natural when spoken.

Do not interrupt the essay with methodological disclaimers. Let the mature
formation speak positively inside the primary reading.

## Ending

Let the ending move through the passage once, carried by transformations the
essay has already made perceptible. It should arrive at the closing wording
and send new force back into the opening without becoming a recap of headings
or a list of lessons. End when that return lands; do not explain the ending a
second time.

Write only the title and complete Turkish publication draft in Markdown to the
path named in the task.
