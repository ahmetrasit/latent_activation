# V7.1.0 Turn 2: Recursive Configuration Maturation

Continue as the same semantic author. Keep the entire activation reservoir and
the supplied field active.

The first coherent image is the beginning of formation, not its completion.
Build provisional configurations and repeatedly let each one reorganize the
material from which it arose. As a configuration matures, give it a concise
working name and carry it forward as one whole. Do not dissolve it back into
separate branch observations during later rereadings.

## What makes a configuration

A configuration is an operation, not a category. Its parts perform distinct
roles, interact, and produce a change of state or relation:

```text
participants
-> differentiated roles
-> shared operation
-> transformation or consequence
-> changed expectation elsewhere in the passage
```

Shared subject matter, shape, organ, material, location, or mood creates an
attraction worth exploring. It becomes a configuration only when the parts do
different work inside one process. Until then, keep the attraction alive in
the reservoir and continue combining it; do not promote an umbrella label in
place of the missing operation.

## Recursive formation loop

For every live attraction, perform this movement:

```text
assemble fragments
-> inhabit the configuration concretely
-> discover the roles and consequences it creates
-> reread every contributing fragment through the whole
-> reread the passage forward and backward
-> reread unused and multiply usable branch facets
-> recruit, split, merge, rescale, rephase, or transform
-> inhabit the changed configuration again
```

Do not stop when parts merely share a topic. Let them acquire differentiated
work as participants, actions, instruments, materials, directions, pressures,
boundaries, stages, contrasts, or outcomes. Follow physical and relational
processes far enough for their implicit requirements to become visible.

When a newly recruited part initially makes a configuration less tidy, stay
with the disturbance. It may reveal:

- another phase of the same process;
- a change of scale or viewpoint;
- a shift in participant or agency;
- an inversion of direction;
- an interior and exterior relation;
- a transformation rather than a stable picture;
- a distinct configuration sharing some of the same material.

Allow a mature whole to confer new significance on an initially slight
detail. Then let that newly understood detail alter the whole again.

Keep lexical co-branches concrete. Several branches of one root may supply
alternative realizations, contrasts, phases, or formal echoes. Root membership
alone does not turn one branch object into the causal effect of another. Let
the passage's syntax, agency, order, and completed cross-root roles supply
causal direction.

## Formation searches

During every recursive cycle, actively search for relations that can make a
configuration more exact:

- one supplied lexical description defining another present word;
- an adjacent word receiving an object, direction, result, or participant
  opened immediately before it;
- an opening role completed or reversed near the close;
- a material or spatial opposition whose two sides change the same primary
  relation;
- repeated grammatical framing whose agency or outcome changes;
- several roots supplying complementary roles in one concrete process;
- a later form that changes the function, not merely the theme, of an earlier
  form.

These are routes into formation, not a checklist of required findings.

## Field plurality

Develop several configurations when the supplied material organizes different
operations. They may interact, nest, oppose one another, occur in sequence, or
share facets without being collapsed into one master metaphor.

Keep rival realizations alive long enough for each to recruit its own roles and
change the passage around it. Keep unfinished formations present with their
open relational shape; their missing role may appear only after another
configuration matures.

Read every supplied branch again after the first configurations form. An
unused facet is not outside the work merely because the initial reservoir did
not know where to place it. The reservoir preserves every still-unformed
facet; this turn does not need to recopy each one into the mature field.

The primary scaffold remains absent. Do not introduce conventional
commentary, outside knowledge, or a primary-meaning admission test.
