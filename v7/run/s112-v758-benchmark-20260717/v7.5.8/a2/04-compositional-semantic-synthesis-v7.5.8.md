# From `قُلْ` to `أَحَدٌۢ`: The Passage as a Directed Relation

```text
قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ
لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ
وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ
```

## A declaration held in the imperative

The passage begins by assigning speech before it asserts any performance of speech. `قُلْ` is an imperative addressed in the masculine singular. The supplied primary relation identifies its addressee and grammatical controller as the Prophet: Surah 112 commands him to declare God's absolute oneness. The command therefore has a determinate carrier, a determinate addressee, and a determinate content, but its performance remains open. What is present after `قُلْ` is the exact actable content `هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`, not a separately asserted event in which the Prophet has already uttered it.

That distinction is internal to the opening syntax. The command takes the quoted complement beginning with `هُوَ`; within the complement, `هُوَ` stands in the supplied appositional relation to `ٱللَّهُ`, and `أَحَدٌ` is the nominative predicate of that named subject. The Prophet controls the requested saying, while God occupies the referential and predicative structure inside its content. Speaker, addressee, named subject, and predicate are not one participant merely because the command gathers them into one line.

The lexical field gives the whole line an exact witness rather than merely recalling its topic. The source assertion `قل هو الله أحد` repeats the four terms in their order, and the accompanying description says that `أحد` is used `مطلقا وصفا في وصف الله تعالى`. The primary statement, "God's absolute oneness," fills the force of `مطلقا`: the first `أَحَدٌ` is an unrestricted affirmative description of God inside the commanded proposition. The lexical occurrence and the positioned occurrence are distinct carriers of the same exact content schema. Their identity of wording does not supply a second Prophet or turn the lexical citation into the surface command event.

The speech root makes the modality of this opening sharper. `القول من النطق` and `المركب من الحروف المبرز بالنطق` describe composed letters brought outward through articulation. That is the operation the commanded declaration could enact: content available to a speaker becomes audible expression. Here the accepting relation supplies the Prophet as controller and the exact oneness proposition as content, but the imperative still stops before completed articulation. The positive lexical articulator does not perform the Prophet's commanded act for him.

Other exact speech relations preserve the same distinction by changing one parameter at a time. In `في نفسي قول لم أظهره`, content is present inwardly while outward expression is denied. In `تقول باطلا أي قال ما لم يكن`, a saying is produced while the occurrence corresponding to its content is absent. In `قولتني ما لم أقل ... أي ادعيته علي`, content is attributed to a first-person target whose own saying is denied. These are three different absences: expression not performed, referent-event not occurring, and authorship not realized. None is the state of the opening declaration. The passage gives an actable command, not an internally withheld saying, a false statement, or an imposed authorship.

The remaining speech relations vary carrier, direction, syntax, and result without taking possession of the quoted content. `المقول اللسان` supplies a tongue as an instrument schema, but no surface edge appoints the Prophet's tongue as an occupant. A `قولة` or `قوال` is a person disposed to much speech; the single imperative does not make the Prophet `كثير القول`. The Yemenite `القيل` is a titled ruler below the greatest king whose `قول` may take effect, whereas the command supplies no royal office or efficacy result. `اقتال قولا` draws a local saying inward toward the controller's own self; `القالة القول الفاشي في الناس` sends another local saying outward among people; `قاولته ... وتقاولنا` alternates speech between partners concerning `أمره`; and `اقتال عليه تحكم` directs control over another. The declaration is directed to the Prophet, but it is not thereby appropriated, circulated, negotiated, or imposed on a controlled endpoint.

Speech can also change function without becoming another utterance of this content. In one usage, `تقول` or inflected `قلت` governs like conjecture, under different interrogative conditions for the Arabs and Banu Sulaym. In `فلان يقول بقول أبي حنيفة`, a local person accepts a possessed doctrine as belief. The filled pool in `امتلأ الحوض وقال قطني` becomes the nonhuman controller of an indication. `فلان يقول بكذا` can enact sincere concern for a thing, while `قول الجوهر` and `قول العرض` are each equated with a logical boundary, `حدهما`. These complete relations disclose possible changes from saying to conjectural government, adherence, indication, concern, and definition. The supplied surface syntax chooses none of them: `قُلْ` keeps imperative government, and its complement keeps the exact nominal predication.

Even the small written form of the command is active without being allowed to fuse its containing scenes. With vocalization removed, `قل` occurs in `أقل`, `قلت`, `القلة`, and `قلب`. The first two add person or tense material and belong to denied-authorship and conjectural-government assertions. In `القال الخشبة التي تضرب بها القلة`, the instrument name contains `قال`, while the struck object's name contains `قل`; local striker, wooden instrument, and affected object remain distinct. In `الإكفاء قلب الشيء كأنه إزالة المساواة`, a final `ب` carries the two-letter sequence into an actual overturning whose stated result is removal of equality. The Prophet is neither the person falsely quoted nor a conjecturer, struck object, or overturning agent. Yet `قلب` will matter again at the close, because the command-form that opens the passage is literally contained in a supplied operation whose local consequence concerns the equality relation the passage finally denies.

## The affirmative `أَحَدٌ`

The first line ends by completing, rather than merely naming, the command's content. `هُوَ` and `ٱللَّهُ` occupy the local referential position; `أَحَدٌ` supplies its nominative predicate. The direct proposition binds this occupant to God and this predicate to absolute oneness. Thus the movement is not from an isolated number to a theological inference. It is from a commanded declaration to an internally complete affirmative predication: God is described by `أحد` absolutely.

The supplied equation `أحد بمعنى الواحد` and the statement that it is `أول العدد` remain exact lexical relations, but the primary predicate is not reduced to arithmetic. The same record preserves an asserted origin relation, `أحد فرع والأصل الواو وحد`, and the unrestricted use `وأصله وحد`; it also gives emphatic repetition `أحد أحد`. These relations place the surface form among unity, first position, named origin, and repeated emphasis. They do not turn the one surface predicate into four events or move an original `و` into the positioned word. The historical statement `وحد -> أحد` remains its own atomic transformation, while the affirmative syntax supplies the current property relation.

The numerical field expands what must be distinguished from that property. `أحد واثنان وأحد عشر وإحدى عشرة` orders singular and compound count forms. `الواحد المضموم إلى العشرات` joins a unit to tens, and `فأحدهن أي صيرهن أحد عشر` has an unspecified controller transform a feminine-plural object into the result eleven. `أحدكما` selects one within a dual addressee field. When `أحد` is used in an additive position it can mean `الأول`, and `يوم الأحد أي يوم الأول` makes that ordinal relation a day name; `الأحد` can then pluralize as `آحاد`. Each operation has its own collection, suffix, day, or grammatical relation. None appoints God as a counted collection, one member of a pair, a compound number, or a day.

Other exact carriers alter relation rather than count. `استأحد الرجل انفرد` gives a local man a transition into separation, while `جاءوا آحاد أحاد` distributes a plural arriving group as individuals. The first is a singular separation event; the second is a manner of plural arrival. By contrast, `إحداهما بالأخرى` selects one of two local panels in order to sew it to the other. The smaller form therefore participates in opposed local outcomes, separation and joining, without transporting any man, group, or panel into the primary predicate. Absolute oneness is not an event of leaving others, arriving one by one, or being joined to a counterpart.

The consonantal form can also change its named object. `أُحُد جبل بالمدينة` keeps the consonants while changing the vocalization and predicates mountainhood of a local named place in the city. `الأَحَدِيَّة` adds article and state morphology; `استأحدت`, `أحدكما`, `فأحدهن`, and `إحداهما` add their own prefixes or suffixes; `أحدثه` continues the exact normalized sequence with `ثه` inside the separate causation `أحدثه فحدث`. These containments make the precise written deltas audible. They do not make either surface `أحد` a mountain, a panel, a feminine group, a separated man, or a caused thing.

This differentiation prepares the second occurrence of the form. At the opening line's boundary, `أَحَدٌ` is an affirmative predicate of God. At the passage boundary, `أَحَدٌۢ` will be a delayed subject under negation. Their common lemma, masculine indefinite nominative morphology, and terminal `د` carry a formal return; their occupants and syntactic work do not return unchanged. The first establishes a positive pole whose relation to the last can be understood only after the intervening name, refuge, and lineage clauses have moved God through other grammatical positions.

## The repeated name and the direction of refuge

The exact proper name appears first inside the quoted predication and then at the beginning of the second line. Primary context supplies an identity that spelling alone could not: both `ٱللَّهُ` occurrences name the same God. They remain different predication occurrences. In the first, the name is appositional to `هُوَ` and bears `أَحَدٌ`; in the second, it begins a new nominal clause and bears definite `ٱلصَّمَدُ`. The name moves from the third position of a four-word quoted line to the first position of the passage's only two-word line, bringing the referent forward while changing the predicate.

The name itself has an asserted lineage: `إله` loses its hamza, receives `الألف واللام`, and yields `الله`, a result specified for `الباري تعالى`. This is not merely the discovery of letters. It is one complete source-to-result operation, distinct from `وحد -> أحد`, from the later loss of `و` in `ولدة -> اللدة`, and from the pattern derivation `الكون -> الكينة`. The two surface name occurrences receive the same proper-name form lineage, but recurrence and derivation do not merge their clauses.

The deity relations around the name remain locally exact. `فالإله الله تعالى لأنه معبود` and `فالإله على هذا هو المعبود` place God in the named and worshipped positions, while `لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون معبودا` supplies a prerequisite relation between being deity and being worshipped. `تأله الرجل إذا تعبد`, `أله فلان يأله الآلهة عبد`, `التأليه التعبيد`, and the classification of idols and cult objects supply their own worshippers, actions, and objects. The sun is called `الإلاهة` because a local people worshipped it; in another complete event `عين الشمس` is countered or warded by an unspecified dual medium away from a first-person group. Exact `الشمس` connects those two directional organizations, but neither sun occurrence is God, and neither worshippers nor countering medium enter the passage.

Oath and invocation forms re-carry the proper name under still other controllers. `الله ما فعلت ذاك` is mapped to the intended oath `والله ما فعلته`; `اللهم` is equated with `يا ألله`; `يا ألله اغفر لي` and `لا هم اغفر لنا` supply direct address, an imperative of forgiveness, and singular or plural beneficiaries; `لاه أنت` is mapped to `لله أنت`, while `لاه أبوك`, `لهنك`, and `لهنا` remain their own complete uses. These are fresh utterance occurrences with local speakers, addressees, acts, and affixes. The surface name participates by exact identity of the named God, not because Surah 112 becomes an oath or invocation.

The second line's predicate changes the geometry of that continuous referent. The direct proposition names God as the self-sufficient refuge. The decisive lexical relation is not a broad association with mastery but the exact direction in `الصمد السيد لأنه يصمد إليه في الحوائج`, `الصمد السيد الذي يصمد إليه في الأمر`, and `صمده قصد معتمدا عليه قصده`. A need or matter is directed toward a relied-on endpoint. The primary relation fills that endpoint with God; it does not invent a seeker, a need, or a matter. In `ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ`, God is the named refuge toward which reliance may run, not the needy controller who performs the seeking.

The same source supplies a local master whose sovereignty `قد انتهى سؤدده`. That terminal-status relation gives a separate role-shape to the predicate without installing the local master as a second occupant. It also allows direction to reverse across exact `أمر` carriers. In one occurrence an agent intends and relies on `هذا الأمر`; in another an `الأمر` is directed toward the master; in `إني على صمادة من أمر` a first-person controller overlooks and cares for a matter; in `قاولته في أمره وتقاولنا` partners negotiate about a matter; and in `كان الأمر` or `الكائنة الأمر الحادث` a matter occupies temporal occurrence rather than an agentive direction. Only the route of need or matter toward the endpoint maps directly onto refuge. The other matters keep their controllers and event identities, making clear that direction toward, direction from, supervision over, exchange around, and occurrence of a matter are not interchangeable.

Self-sufficiency also receives several independent counterforms. `المصمت الذي ليس بأجوف`, `الصمد كل مكان صلب`, `الصمدة صخرة راسية`, and the descriptions of an elevated thick place, severe ground, and a hard thing without grooves provide material states of hardness and absent interior. They do not make God a place, rock, ground, or hollow body. Their contribution is relational: an intrinsic cavity vacancy differs from a need supplied from outside, while the primary predicate remains nonmaterial and belongs to God.

Applied closure supplies another, nonidentical relation. In `الصماد سداد القارورة` and `صمدتها أصمدها`, a local controller acts on a bottle and closes its aperture with a stopper. The bottle continues into a stopped state; its contents are unspecified, not denied. Intrinsic absence of a cavity and achieved closure of an existing opening are therefore different. Neither is the exhaustive absence that will close the passage.

Wrapping changes the boundary again. `صمد رأسه تصميدا` has a controller wrap a locally possessed head with `خرقة` or `منديل` or `ثوب`, while explicitly excluding `العمامة`; the resulting wrap is named `الصماد`. The alternatives are not simultaneous materials, and the excluded turban is a barred medium rather than a nonexistent object. `موضع العمامة` elsewhere names a position for that exact object without asserting an occupant. An excluded medium and an open named position remain different vacancy mechanisms.

The exact house carrier extends the boundary comparison without joining its occupants. `بيت مصمد أي مقصود` gives one local house the passive state of being aimed at. In `أكفأت البيت فهو مكفأ`, another local house receives a `كفاء`; one panel, `إحداهما`, is sewn to `الأخرى` and used to cover `مؤخر البيت`. The panels stay two through their joining, and the house continues into a furnished or covered state. A house aimed at, a bottle stopped, a head wrapped, and a rear boundary covered are not one scene. Together they distinguish target, interior, aperture, exterior, excluded medium, joined material, and completed covering around a predicate whose primary bearer is none of those objects.

The root also carries force in a direction the refuge does not. `صمده بالعصا صمدا إذا ضربه بها` has a local agent strike the same suffix-object with the same stick. It meets `القال الخشبة التي تضرب بها القلة` through the exact operation `ضرب` and the common instrument-impact topology, but the sticks and affected objects remain local to their events. This forceful outgoing act has no primary participant binding. Its difference from refuge matters: the predicate can be surrounded by a lexical force directed against an object without making God either striker or struck object.

Time provides a further independent dimension. `الصمد الدائم` and `الدائم الباقي بعد فناء خلقه` place an enduring bearer after an annihilation boundary. `كان الأمر أي مذ خلق` places another local matter from a creation boundary forward into occurrence. Creation-to-occurrence and annihilation-to-remaining point in opposite temporal directions, and no created occupant crosses between them. The she-camel called `مصماد`, remaining through cold and drought with continuous milk flow, is a third actual vector: environmental endurance joined to renewable output. It does not enter the primary predicate. The direct proposition receives persistence as a temporal relation while retaining God, not the camel or local creation, as its bearer.

Thus `ٱلصَّمَدُ` is not flattened into target, stone, stopper, wrap, overseer, blow, or camel. The refuge route supplies an exact positive direction toward God; the solidity, closure, covering, supervision, impact, and endurance relations remain differentiated changes around the same lexical carrier. Their distinctions make self-sufficiency more exact: intrinsic condition, applied completion, selective exclusion, assembled covering, care, force, and temporal persistence have different controllers and consequences.

## One referent across both sides of lineage

The third line begins a new polarity and a new kind of grammar: `لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ`. Primary context binds the same God to the implicit 3MS position in both verbs. In the first occurrence God is the active controller of `يَلِدْ`; in the second God is the passive affected subject of `يُولَدْ`. Coordination aligns the clauses, and both share the root `و ل د`, imperfect aspect, jussive mood, 3MS agreement, and negation. Voice reverses their participant orientation, and event identity remains twofold.

The active denial has an outgoing consequence. Positive birth language can appoint a parent and emit a child: `ولدت المرأة تلد ولادا وولادة` gives a woman control of a birth occurrence, and `الولادة ... وضع الوالدة ولدها` keeps her locally possessed child as the same occupant from pregnancy or source state into delivery. Under `لَمْ يَلِدْ`, no such result is emitted. God does not enter the `والد` role, and no `ولد`, `ابن`, `ابنة`, or `أولاد` occupant becomes available.

The passive denial turns the relation around. Positive `حين يولد`, `يوم ولد`, and the recent-born role place a local affected participant at or near the birth boundary. Under `وَلَمْ يُولَدْ`, God does not undergo that occurrence or acquire the born state. The external begetter is suppressed by passive voice; it is not an asserted individual who can be carried elsewhere. The two clauses therefore exclude lineage in opposite directions around the same God: no outgoing child and no incoming origin.

The parental lexicon makes these prevented appointments precise. `الوالد الأب`, `الوالدة الأم`, and the dual `الوالدان` give distinct father, mother, and grouped-parent roles. In a positive birth tuple the mother role can be re-instantiated under a local woman and her child. The surface denials supply no positive accepting event, so neither parent role nor dual pair is instantiated. The active 3MS position is not automatically "father," and the passive suppressed source is not filled by a mother or father merely because the role schema exists.

The child field likewise retains number, gender, age, and social role without sending them into the denied ports. `الولد` can be one or many, male or female; `الوليد` can name a boy near birth, while `الوليدة` can name a girl or female servant and may persist as a social designation even when its bearer is old. `ولدان`, `ولدة`, and `ولائد` preserve their plural groupings. Since the born relation is denied of God, none of these age, sex, servitude, or number parameters receives a primary occupant.

Positive birth itself has more than one controller pattern. `أولدت حان ولادها` marks the threshold at which a woman's birth time arrives. `شاة والد وهي الحامل` gives a ewe the pregnant state. `ولدناها أي ولينا ولادتها` gives first-person plural attendants charge of an animal's birth without making them parents of its child. `يوم ولدت` and `يوم ولد` place active and passive occurrences in local day boundaries. These complete events make the surface voice rotation concrete, but woman, child, ewe, attendants, and days remain local. The day interface with `يوم الأحد` carries only the repeatable temporal-boundary role, not a shared day.

Generation beyond birth supplies a distinct source-result topology. `تولد الشيء من الشيء حصوله عنه بسبب من الأسباب` has one local thing arise from another through a cause; source and result are distinct occupants. A generated Arabic variety or man may be non-pure, `المولد من الكلام` may be newly produced by plural innovators, a `كتاب مولد` may be fabricated, and `بينة مولدة` may be present yet unverified. Those outcomes are not recombined. The surface begetting denial shares a source-to-result direction but supplies no positive source, cause, or product occurrence. It does not classify speech, books, evidence, or language as divine offspring.

The exact transformation `ولدة -> اللدة` by loss of `و` opens another relation, the same-age peer: `لدة الرجل تربه` and `فلان لدة فلان وتربه`. Its measure is shared age, not lineage identity. It can meet `مثل`, `نظير`, and `كفء` as a positive comparison topology, but no local peer enters the final candidate class. The birth-time relation becomes one specific way a local pair can be comparable, while the primary clause denies a different, unrestricted comparability relation to God.

Annual production keeps still another outcome distinct. `الكفأة` names a date palm's crop for its year and is re-instantiated for camel yield. A requester asks for `نتاج إبله سنة`; milk, wool, and `أولادها` can be transferred for a year; camels can be divided into two `كفأتين` with alternating production cycles; and a yield may be present but `ليست تامة`. Renewable product, divided carrier, measured year, transfer, and deficient result are all positive local structures. `لَمْ يَلِدْ` is terminal nonproduction in its own event, not incomplete annual yield, and it emits no product that could enter a later cycle.

Speech products add one more necessary separation. A local group may innovate `المولد من الكلام`, and a local articulator may bring composed content outward. A false speaker may produce content while its corresponding event is absent. By contrast, the lineage clauses deny the generating or birth events themselves. Content present with a missing referent, product present but unverified, product present but incomplete, and no product emitted are four different states. This prevents the exact commanded quote from becoming an offspring or fabrication and prevents the lineage denial from becoming a generic absence of content.

Across the two clauses, the continuous God referent therefore changes grammatical role without becoming one event. The written surface itself marks the difference: active `يَلِدْ` does not write the weak radical that appears in passive `يُولَدْ`, while morphology supplies the common lemma `وَلَدَ`. That variation is separate from the asserted `و` loss in `ولدة -> اللدة`. Identity comes from the primary participant binding, not from a rule that turns one written form into the other.

## The delayed subject and the fixed endpoint

The conjunction at the fourth line carries negation forward, but the operation changes: `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ`. `أَحَدٌۢ` is the delayed nominative subject of `يَكُن`; `كُفُوًا` is its accusative predicate; and the pronoun in `لَّهُۥ` is the endpoint governed by `لـ`. Primary context binds that endpoint to God and the delayed subject position to the class "anyone" under negation. God is the continuous referent of the passage, but God does not control agreement in this verb. The candidate class does.

This controller change completes a five-role rotation around the same God. God is first the subject and property bearer of `أَحَدٌ`, then the subject and refuge bearer of `ٱلصَّمَدُ`, then active controller in the denied begetting relation, then passive affected subject in the denied begotten relation, and finally the pronominal endpoint of a relation whose grammatical subject is someone else. Event identity never travels across those occurrences. Occupant identity does, and line 4 makes its final position relational rather than subject-controlling.

The negative use of `أحد` supplies the scope of the delayed class. `لا أحد في الدار` and `ما في الدار أحد` give early and delayed realizations of an indefinite under negation. The source statement makes its consequence explicit: `أحد في النفي لاستغراق جنس الناطقين ولا واحد ولا اثنان فصاعدا`. `فما منكم من أحد عنه حاجزين` adds a further arrangement with a pronominal endpoint and relational material. The passage supplies its own relation, order, and endpoint: anyone who could be comparable to God. House, speakers, addressees, and blockers remain in their local assertions. What transfers is exhaustive scope from one through two and upward, so no individual is emitted from the final subject class.

The positive lexical counterpart relation makes the denied state exact. `الكفء المثل` and `التكافؤ التساوي` give explicit equations. `هذا كفء له أي مثله` places a local subject in a positive counterpart relation to a `لـ` endpoint. More fully, `كل شيء ساوى شيئا حتى يكون مثله فهو مكافئ له` traces one thing through equality, likeness, and counterpart state relative to another. The surface transforms this trajectory atomically: positive becomes negative; source `يكون` becomes jussive `يَكُن`; the subject is delayed; the candidate becomes exhaustive `أحد`; the predicate is accusative `كُفُوًا`; and the endpoint is the surface `لَّهُۥ`, bound to God. No lexical thing or pronoun crosses into the surface clause.

The result is not a vague absence of resemblance. No member of the candidate class reaches the supplied positive trajectory of equality, likeness, and counterpart status relative to the fixed endpoint. The comparison measures named in local examples, `الحسب`, `المال`, `الحرب`, marriage, or combat, remain local and unselected. The direct proposition supplies comparability without restricting it to one of those measures.

Other positive `ك ف ء` relations reveal different controllers and consequences. `المكافأة مجازاة النعم` returns a benefit; `كافأت الرجل أي فعلت به مثل ما فعل بي` re-instantiates a prior act under reversed roles, so the first-person return is a new event rather than the prior act continued. A person can be a local counterpart to another in marriage or war. An unspecified dual medium can counter `عين الشمس` away from a first-person group, and a man can use his spear to align or counter between two horsemen. These are reciprocal, social, protective, and instrumental realizations, not candidates in the primary clause. Their positive results show how many distinct relations must remain unrealized at the passage's endpoint without importing their participants or measures.

The same root also contains an explicit removal counterfield. `أكفأت الشيء إذا أملته`, overturning a vessel, diverting a group toward another route, a woman's swaying gait, and `مكفأ الوجه كاسف اللون` preserve distinct affected things and outcomes. The decisive statement is `الإكفاء قلب الشيء كأنه إزالة المساواة`: a local controller overturns a thing, with removal of equality as the stated consequence. The passage does not perform that overturning; it grammatically denies a counterpart relation. Yet the exact containment `قُلْ -> قل -> قلب` joins its opening and closing forms: the command-form is nested in the lexical operation that removes the equality whose positive trajectory is blocked at the close. This is a formal return, not an inversion act by the Prophet.

Pairing does not itself supply comparability. In `الكفاء شقتان تنصح إحداهما بالأخرى`, one panel is sewn to another and can cover the rear of a house. Their joining preserves two panel occupants. In `أكفأت إبلي كفأتين`, one herd is divided into two production groups that alternate in time. One relation materially joins a pair; the other temporally alternates paired carriers. Neither makes a panel or herd a counterpart to God, and neither turns final `أحد` into one member of a supplied pair. They disclose how one, other, two, joining, and alternation differ from equality.

The same-age peer `لدة/ترب/مثيل السن` supplies a specifically measured positive comparison, while `الكفئ النظير` supplies a general peer. The age measure stays local.
