# The Remaining Possibility: An Open Command and an Exhausted Counterpart

Everything in the passage is settled except the act with which it begins. `قُلْ` is a second-person masculine singular imperative whose controller is the Prophet. It gives that controller an exact content to articulate, but an imperative makes its continuation actable; it does not report that the saying has occurred. By the end, by contrast, three relations have been placed under `لَمْ`: God does not beget, is not begotten, and has no counterpart in anyone. The passage therefore does not move from an already completed speech act toward a series of abstract descriptions. It holds one human act open while progressively fixing every relation its content attributes to God.

The four lines are:

> قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ  
> ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ  
> لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ  
> وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ

Their participant continuity is exact but their grammatical continuity is not. In the first line, `هُوَ` and `ٱللَّهُ` can share a local referential position by apposition, and `أَحَدٌ` is the nominative predicate of the named subject. In the second, the proper name is again nominative and `ٱلصَّمَدُ` is its definite nominative predicate. In the third, the same God is first the implicit 3MS controller of an active verb and then the affected 3MS subject of its passive counterpart, with both events denied. In the fourth, God no longer controls subject agreement: `هُۥ` is the endpoint governed by `لَّ`, `كُفُوًا` is the accusative predicate of the negated copula, and delayed nominative `أَحَدٌۢ` is the subject. The final clause does not say that God fails to become a counterpart. It says that no candidate becomes a counterpart *to Him*.

That rotation gives the passage its governing asymmetry. The God-referent remains one; subjecthood, voice, polarity, and relational position change around Him. The Prophet controls only the possible declaration. The unspecified seeker implied by refuge never becomes the Prophet. The suppressed agent of `يُولَدْ` is not supplied as a parent. The final `أَحَدٌۢ` emits no individual at all. What persists is not a chain of exchanged occupants but one recognized referent carried through several incompatible grammatical offices.

## A Saying Whose Content Is Complete

The opening has an exceptional lexical lock: the complete source assertion `قل هو الله أحد` exactly reproduces the four positioned terms of the first line. Its accompanying statement, `يستعمل مطلقا وصفا في وصف الله تعالى`, identifies `أحد` as an absolute description in the description of God. The lexical witness does more than repeat the last word. It returns the entire ordered utterance: imperative, pronoun, proper name, predicate. The surface line is thus already present as a complete lexical use, while the surface syntax gives that use a fresh occurrence as the quoted content of `قُلْ`.

The distinction between content and performance is decisive. The speech field defines `القول` as `المركب من الحروف المبرز بالنطق`: something composed of letters and brought outward in articulation, whether a single expression, a sentence, a poem, or an address. That topology fits what the imperative requests. The command supplies composed verbal content and assigns a potential articulator. Yet neither the tongue named by `المقول اللسان` nor the dispositions named by `قولة`, `قوال`, `تقوالة`, and `مقوال` enter the primary scene. The Prophet is not classified as habitually talkative, and no bodily instrument is separately asserted. The lexical routes specify what outward saying can involve; the imperative alone specifies who is to perform this saying.

Other saying-tuples sharpen the same boundary by changing the controller or the status of the content. A Yemenite `قيل` is a titled ruler, `كأنه الذي له قول أي ينفذ قوله`, one whose word takes effect. That is an accomplished efficacy relation between an officeholder, his saying, and an outcome. `قُلْ` supplies no such outcome. `قاولته في أمره` and `تقاولنا` place two sides in negotiation over a matter, whereas the passage assigns one addressee a command and supplies no negotiating partner. `اقتال عليه تحكم` makes one controller exercise rule over another; it does not turn the divine content into an act of human domination. The grammatical tradition in which `تقول` or inflected `قلت` governs like conjecture changes syntactic government and interrogative scope, not the truth status of this quoted nominal clause. The opening is an imperative governing quoted content, not a conjectural verb governing two objects.

False saying creates a still different architecture. `تقول باطلا أي قال ما لم يكن` distinguishes a produced saying from an occurrence that did not happen. `قولتني ما لم أقل` and `أقولتني ما لم أقل` add a second controller who attributes content to a first person who did not utter it, while `تقول عليه` makes lying an act directed against a local endpoint. The exact sequence `لم يكن` returns at the passage close, and the detection form `قل` occurs within `أقل`; neither contact turns the opening into false attribution. In the primary passage, `لَمْ يَكُن` denies a counterpart state, not the occurrence of the commanded speech. The command has an exact content-authority relation and an unasserted performance; the false-speech tuples have asserted speakers, alleged content, and failures of correspondence or authorship. Their difference prevents the later negation from leaking backward into `قُلْ`.

Saying can also remain inward. `في نفسي قول لم أظهره` holds a content schema in the self before verbal display. `اقتال قولا` draws a good or evil saying toward oneself. Saying can name adopted belief in `فلان يقول بقول أبي حنيفة`, serious concern in `فلان يقول بكذا`, indication by a nonhuman state in `امتلأ الحوض وقال قطني`, or the logical definition of substance and accident in `قول الجوهر` and `قول العرض`. It can become a favorable or unfavorable reputation that spreads `في الناس`, multiplying `القيل والقال` without identifying an original hearer. None of these modes is automatically enacted by the imperative. The commanded proposition is not merely an inward conception, appropriated doctrine, reputation, sign from a full pool, expression of concern, or technical definition. These complete alternatives make the exact work of the opening narrower and stronger: a named addressee is to bring one supplied proposition into articulation.

The people among whom a saying spreads also appear in `الكون الحدث يكون بين الناس`, where an occurrence rather than a report is located among people. The specifically named social field licenses a relation between circulation and occurrence, but the controllers and results still differ: a reputation can propagate favorably or unfavorably, while an event can simply occur and be present. The passage names no audience, public circulation, or social event. Its one human role remains the addressed controller of an actable saying.

Even the imperative form changes controller when it returns in the name-field. `يا ألله اغفر لي` and `لا هم اغفر لنا` contain commands whose addressee is God, whose controller is an invoker, and whose beneficiary is singular or plural first person. In `قُلْ`, the addressee is the Prophet and the Prophet is the controller of the requested saying; God is the referent inside its content. Shared imperative modality does not merge invocation with declaration. It reveals a role rotation: the proper name can be the addressee of a request elsewhere while remaining the subject named inside the commanded proposition here.

The two-letter detection form `قل` also survives inside complete forms with wholly different operations. It occurs in `أقل` within denied first-person speech, in `قلت` within the conjectural-government examples, in `القلة`, the object struck by the wooden `قال`, and in `قلب`, the overturning that `الإكفاء` describes as `إزالة المساواة`. Initial hamza and person marking, final `ت`, article plus `ـة`, or final `ب` are not disposable wrappers: each takes the smaller form into a distinct tuple. Yet the last containment creates a formal return with the passage's end. The opening command is held inside a lexical word for overturning or removing equality, while the final clause denies that equality can ever mature into counterpart status. No one overturns anything in the primary scene, but the exact form of its first word is thereby implicated in the relation its last clause closes.

## Three Occurrences Before Three Denials

The four-line arrangement makes the semantic progression countable without reducing it to numerology. There are fifteen written words, nineteen morphological carriers, eleven supplied syntactic edges, and six local verbal or predicative occurrences. By line, the complete vectors are `[4,2,4,5]` written words, `[4,3,5,7]` morphological carriers, `[3,1,3,4]` syntax edges, and `[2,1,2,1]` local occurrences. The first line has the command and its quoted nominal predication; the second has one nominal predication; the third has two verbal predications; the fourth has one copular predication.

The polarity boundary after line 2 yields two real, complete groupings. The first two lines carry six words, seven morphemes, four syntax edges, and three occurrences: one modal command plus two affirmative predications. The last two carry nine words, twelve morphemes, seven syntax edges, and again three occurrences, now all denied. The occurrence structure is balanced `[3,3]`, but its embodiment expands from `[6,7,4]` to `[9,12,7]`. Negation does not merely replace affirmation term for term. It requires conjunction, repeated particles, voice differentiation, a prepositional endpoint, an accusative predicate, and a delayed subject. The passage spends more carrier material to prevent relations than to state the two properties.

The opening half also names God overtly twice. The first `ٱللَّهُ` stands at word 3 inside the quoted line; the second moves to word 1 of the next line. After that the proper name disappears from the written surface. Continuity is borne by 3MS verbal agreement in `يَلِدْ` and `يُولَدْ`, then by the 3MS pronoun in `لَّهُۥ`. Overt naming gives way first to implicit subjecthood and finally to an explicit endpoint. At precisely that final point a different subject becomes overt: delayed `أَحَدٌۢ`, the candidate class emptied by exhaustive negation.

The line beginnings register the change: `قُلْ | ٱللَّهُ | لَمْ | وَلَمْ` moves from command to proper name, then to bare negation, then to conjoined negation. Within the negative sequence, the first denial is unconjoined and the next two are introduced by `وَ`, giving `[unconjoined, conjoined, conjoined]`. The line endings move differently: `أَحَدٌ | ٱلصَّمَدُ | يُولَدْ | أَحَدٌۢ` gives two nominal predicates, a passive jussive verb, and a delayed nominative subject. Every ending terminates in written `د`, but the grammatical work changes at each boundary.

Five carriers display 3MS morphology, but only four are bound to God by the passage's participant structure. `هُوَ` is the overt pronoun sharing the named position; `يَلِدْ` places God as active controller; `يُولَدْ` places Him as passive affected subject; and `هُۥ` makes Him the final endpoint. The 3MS `يَكُن` instead agrees with delayed singular `أَحَدٌۢ`. Person and gender recurrence alone is therefore not a coreference chain; syntax decides which participant each carrier serves.

This arrangement places two nondivine roles at the extreme edges without identifying them. The first word carries an implicit 2MS human controller, the Prophet who can say. The last word carries the exhaustive class from which no counterpart can be realized. God is neither outer occupant: He first appears inside the command's content and finally stands one word before the end as the fixed pronominal endpoint. The passage opens a human act and closes a candidate relation around a divine referent that is never displaced by either.

## The Name and the Absolute Predicate

Inside the first nominal clause, `هُوَ` and `ٱللَّهُ` are linked by apposition under the supplied analysis; `أَحَدٌ` predicates absolute oneness of that local named occupant. The exact bigram `هو الله` also occurs in `اسم الله الأكبر هو الله`. There, the pronoun connects the phrase “the greatest name” to the proper name; here, the pronoun and proper name share the God-referent before the predicate. The form is exact, but the antecedent structure is not. Likewise `هو` appears in `فالإله على هذا هو المعبود`, `وهو أول العدد`, `فهو مكافئ له`, and `الولد وهو للواحد والجميع`. Each pronoun retains its local antecedent. Their recurrence makes equation and resumption available as formal relations, not as a license to exchange subjects.

The lexical range of `أحد` clarifies why the first predicate cannot be reduced to a count. `أحد بمعنى الواحد وهو أول العدد` supplies an equation with `الواحد` and an ordinal relation to the number series; `أحد أحد` supplies adjacent repetition for emphasis. The surface predicate, however, is licensed by the stronger exact assertion `يستعمل مطلقا وصفا في وصف الله تعالى`. It is an indefinite nominative description of God within an affirmative clause. Count, firstness, and repetition remain lexical counterfields that specify what changes when the same form is used absolutely.

The extended count field keeps those changes concrete. `أحد واثنان وأحد عشر وإحدى عشرة` distinguishes one, two, and compound numerals. `أحد وعشرين` joins the unit to a decade, while `فأحدهن` causes a feminine plural object to become an eleven-result. `أحدكما` selects one member of a dual field. `يوم الأحد`, named as the first day, can be pluralized as `آحاد`. None of these operations is present in `ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`: no numbered collection is supplied, no one-of-two selection occurs, no object is transformed into eleven, and no calendrical day fills the predicate.

`الواحد` itself crosses several exact equations without preserving one function. `أحد بمعنى الواحد` is a lexical equivalence; `القول والقيل واحد` equates two saying-names; `الولد ... للواحد والجميع` describes a term's singular-and-collective range; and negative `أحد` denies `ولا واحد ولا اثنان فصاعدا`. The same one-form can therefore be a definitional counterpart, a token-identity claim, a number value, or a member exhausted by scope. None of those uses turns the absolute predicate into arithmetic.

Separation creates another distinct route. `استأحد الرجل انفرد` changes a local man's relational state into being apart; `جاءوا آحاد أحاد` distributes plural arrivals one by one. The negative `ما استأحدت بهذا الأمر أي ما انفردت به` is different again: it denies that a controller acted alone with respect to a particular matter. Absolute oneness is not the aftermath of leaving a group, a distributive arrival, or exclusive control of an affair. The same consonantal form with altered vocalization names `أُحُد`, a mountain in the city, but the named-place tuple contributes no occupant to either surface `أحد`. Exact containment also reaches `إحداهما`, one of two sewn panels, and `أحدثه`, a causative occurrence form; their pairing and production relations remain local.

These differences matter again at the passage close. The first `أَحَدٌ` and last `أَحَدٌۢ` are both masculine indefinite nominative and both line-final, but the first is an affirmative predicate and the second a delayed subject under negation. They are not the lexical repetition `أحد أحد`, whose adjacency produces emphasis, nor the plural repetition `آحاد أحاد`, whose changed number distributes arrivals. The passage stretches the repeated lemma across its outer boundaries and changes polarity and grammatical role. Its repetition is an enclosure with reversal, not a copied semantic operation.

The proper name brings its own exact transformations. The lexical record relates `إله` to `الله` by deletion of hamza and addition of the article material, and restricts the resulting name to God. It also states `فالإله الله تعالى لأنه معبود`, `فالإله على هذا هو المعبود`, and `لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون معبودا`. `التأله = التعبد`, `مألوه = معبود`, and `التأليه = التعبيد` distinguish worship, the worshipped role, and causing another to be worshipped; `إلاهتك وعبادتك` keeps deity-language beside its act. The sun called `الإلاهة` is named within a people's worship, while idols and other worshipped objects can be classed as `آلهة`, and `إله` can name each worshipped object in that local usage. These are complete deity-and-worship relations with their own worshippers, objects, and causal controllers. The primary passage supplies God as the occupant of the proper name, but it does not supply those peoples, acts, the sun, idols, or a causative worship event. Naming God and predicating oneness are not themselves worship events.

Oath and invocation uses preserve the same name under further controller changes. `الله ما فعلت` is explained as `والله ما فعلته`; `اللهم` is equated with `يا ألله`; `لاه أنت`, `لاه أبوك`, `لهنك`, and `لهنا` retain their own vocative, oath, possessive, or idiomatic arrangements. The exact name can thus be spoken as subject, invocation, and oath without turning those occurrences into one event. In the passage, its recurrence between lines 1 and 2 is bound to the same God by the primary scaffold, not by form alone. That supplied identity lets a single participant bear both predicates while the lexical name-relations remain available as changed uses rather than hidden actions.

Across these relations, the same recognized God can occupy several endpoint shapes without importing their controllers. He is named as worshipped in a lexical deity proposition, addressed in the forgiveness invocations, aimed toward as refuge, and fixed as the endpoint of the final counterpart denial. Worshipper, invoker, seeker, and candidate are not successive names for one person. The common endpoint makes their directional differences explicit while every local act remains separate.

## Refuge Is an Open Direction

Line 2 is the shortest written line, but its two words are not formally simple: `ٱلصَّمَدُ` divides morphologically into definite article and stem, giving three carriers in the line. The proper name is the named nominative subject and the definite predicate is `ٱلصَّمَدُ`. The primary scaffold specifies the relevant force as God's self-sufficient refuge. Within the root field, the exact direct route is directional: `الصمد القصد`, `صمده قصد معتمدا عليه قصده`, and `الصمد السيد لأنه يصمد إليه في الحوائج`. A seeker directs an affair or need toward a relied-on master; the endpoint is the one aimed at.

This relation changes the work of predication. `أَحَدٌ` is an absolute property of the named subject. `ٱلصَّمَدُ` also predicates of the same named subject, but its lexical topology opens an incoming vector: someone can aim toward Him, rely on Him, and direct a need or affair to Him. The passage fills the endpoint with God and affirms the refuge-state. It does not fill the seeker, the particular need, or the affair. `بيت مصمد أي مقصود` shows that aimed-at status can be assigned to a house in another tuple; the house does not enter the divine predicate. `الصمد السيد الذي قد انتهى سؤدده` supplies mastery, but the relevant route to refuge remains the one in which a master is the endpoint of needs.

The open ports are semantically active precisely because they remain unspecified. They are not denials. An unnamed seeker is not the same kind of vacancy as the child prohibited by `لَمْ يَلِدْ`, the suppressed external agent in the passive `لَمْ يُولَدْ`, or the candidate exhausted by `وَلَمْ يَكُن ... أَحَدٌۢ`. Line 2 affirms a relation that can receive a seeker and a need without asserting a particular occurrence. It is open direction, not an empty world.

The other `ص م د` routes change embodiment and aftermath rather than filling those ports. Hardness and compactness produce `المصمت الذي ليس بأجوف`, a solid place, a raised thick place, a firmly set rock, hard ground, or material without grooves. The absent cavity is intrinsic to that material state. A bottle stopper produces closure by a different mechanism: an agent gives a bottle its `صماد`, while the bottle's contents remain unknown. Head-wrapping supplies an agent, a head, and a cloth, handkerchief, or garment as medium, explicitly `ما خلا العمامة`; the turban is excluded from the set of wrapping media, not denied existence. These are not alternative descriptions of the refuge relation. They show compactness, sealing, and bounded material arrangement with their own objects and controllers.

Still other routes are eventive. `إني على صمادة من أمر` places a controller over an affair in supervision and care. `صمده بالعصا ... ضربه بها` assigns an agent, a stick, and an affected object in a blow. `الصمد الدائم` supplies endurance and remaining after the annihilation of created beings, while the `ناقة مصماد` endures cold and drought and continues its milk. Supervision, impact, cosmic endurance, animal endurance, and continued yield differ in controller, scale, temporal form, and result. They deepen the predicate's field without converting God into a hard place, bottle, head covering, struck object, animal, or instrument.

The endurance route also has a temporal counterdirection in `كان الأمر أي مذ خلق`. That occurrence holds a local matter from a creation boundary forward; `الباقي بعد فناء خلقه` holds an enduring bearer beyond an annihilation boundary. Creation-to-occurrence and annihilation-to-remaining point in opposite temporal directions, while the camel's persistence through cold and drought supplies a finite environmental interval with renewable output. None of these timelines exchanges occupants. Their convergence gives persistence three different temporal mechanisms rather than one undifferentiated duration.

Exact shared terms expose these differences rather than erase them. `أمر` is aimed at and relied upon in one `ص م د` tuple, supervised in another, negotiated in `قاولته في أمره`, and made an occurrence in `كان الأمر`. The affair remains local to each event; aim, oversight, negotiation, and occurrence have different controllers and outcomes. `بيت` can be aimed at in `بيت مصمد` or have its rear covered by one or two sewn panels in the `كِفاء` field. `العمامة` is excluded as a wrapping medium here but occupies an explicitly named position in `موضع العمامة` under the place-field of `ك و ن`. The shared object relates exclusion to open position without migrating the same turban.

The two striking tuples make the same point dynamically. A stick is the instrument in `صمده بالعصا`, and the wooden `قال` is the instrument that strikes the `قلة`. Their topology aligns agent, stick-like medium, impact, and affected object. Nothing identifies the sticks, agents, or objects, and neither event enters the passage. Yet the coalition gives the opening speech-root a material counterform: a form related to saying can name an instrument of impact, while the refuge-root can name the impact itself. Against both, `قُلْ` remains a commanded articulation and `ٱلصَّمَدُ` remains the refuge predicate.

Line 2 therefore functions as more than a compact pause between oneness and negation. It is the passage's only explicitly affirmative relational opening toward an unspecified other. The next clauses will close outgoing generation, incoming generation, and lateral comparability. Refuge alone remains an affirmed direction whose nondivine participant need not be instantiated.

## Generation Turned in Both Voices

The third line repeats one lemma and one 3MS agreement pattern while reversing voice:

> لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ

Both verbs are imperfect, jussive under `لَمْ`, and rooted in `و ل د`. In `يَلِدْ`, God is the implicit active controller and a possible child or generated result is the unexpressed product port. Negation prevents that event and therefore emits no product. In `يُولَدْ`, God is the passive affected subject. Negation prevents the origin-event, while passive voice suppresses its external agent. The suppressed agent is a grammatical port, not an asserted parent who happens to be absent. The two clauses deny opposite participant appointments without collapsing them into a reciprocal event.

The birth lexicon supplies the roles with precision. `الولد`, `المولود`, `الابن`, and `الابنة` distinguish child and born-result positions while allowing the child term to range over one or many, male or female, young or old. `الوالد` names the father, `الوالدة` the mother, and `الوالدان` the two parents. The birth operation itself is `وضع الوالدة ولدها`; `ولدت المرأة`, `أولدت`, a pregnant ewe, attendants expressed by `ولدناها`, and the temporal boundaries `يوم ولدت` and `يوم ولد` each add a controller, affected body, attendant, or day. These complete roles map onto the two primary clauses only as denied appointments. No source mother, father, child, ewe, attendant, or day enters line 3.

Recent birth and social designation form a separate route. `الوليد` can be a boy near the time of birth; `الوليدة` can be a girl or a female servant, and usage can retain the servant designation even when she is aged. Singular, dual, and plural forms such as `ولدان`, `ولائد`, and `ولدة` vary number and class. The primary passive `يُولَدْ` has an exact unvocalized return in `الوليد الصبي حين يولد` and `الوليد يقال لمن قرب عهده بالولادة`, but no recent-born state follows because the primary event is denied. A denied birth does not produce an infant, servant, age class, or later designation.

Causal generation extends beyond biological lineage without becoming identical to it. `تولد الشيء من الشيء` supplies a result obtained from a source by a cause. A newly coined utterance, fabricated book, unverified evidence, non-purely-Arab man, or generated variety of Arabic each has its own producer, source, result, and status. The evidence may exist while remaining unconfirmed; a book may be produced while being fabricated; a language form may be novel without being a child. These routes specify different kinds of generated aftermath. Under `لَمْ يَلِدْ`, no biological or generalized product is licensed as a primary occupant, but the denial does not retroactively negate the lexical examples.

The same-age `لدة` field approaches the final counterpart denial from another direction. `لدة الرجل تربه` and `فلان لدة فلان وتربه` establish two local people as peers in age; `لدان`, `لدات`, and `ولدون` vary their number. That peer relation can interface with `مثل`, `نظير`, and `كفء`, but it does not supply a candidate to line 4. More importantly, its form bears an explicit history: `اللدة نقصانه الواو لأن أصله ولدة`. The peer term is produced by loss of waw from an origin form. Formal descent is asserted for the word even though participant descent is denied of God.

Three other form histories sharpen that contrast. `أحد` is called a branch whose origin is `وحد`, with the original waw named. `الله` is derived from `إله` by hamza deletion and addition of the article. `الكينة` is described as a patterned form from `الكون`. These four transformations—`وحد -> أحد`, `إله -> الله`, `ولدة -> اللدة`, and `الكون -> الكينة`—share the topology source form, explicit operation, result form, but their operations differ. One identifies an original consonant, one deletes hamza and adds material, one loses waw, and one changes derivational pattern. The passage can therefore be dense with asserted lexical origins while denying that God occupies a born-result position. A word's derivation never becomes its referent's genealogy.

The active/passive turn also meets a distinct role reversal in the counterpart field. `كافأت الرجل أي فعلت به مثل ما فعل بي` describes a fresh return act: one participant acts toward another in a manner matching what the other previously did, so controller and affected roles reverse across two positive events. Line 3 likewise rotates God from active controller to passive affected subject, but no first event causes a return, no second participant acts back, and both surface events are denied. The shared relational pattern makes voice rotation visible while preserving the difference between reciprocal recompense and prohibited generation.

Annual production supplies another nonidentical convergence. `الكفأة` can be a palm's crop or a year's camel offspring; one can request milk, wool, children, or fruit for a year, and two camel groups `كفأتين` can alternate their production. A yield may be called incomplete. Here offspring are temporal products within husbandry, with owners, animals, goods, and annual cycles. Their contact with `و ل د` specifies product type and recurrence, but it neither turns `لَمْ يَلِدْ` into an incomplete yield nor makes divine non-generation a seasonal alternation. The passage denies the generative edge itself, not merely its quantity, completeness, or timing.

## The Candidate That Cannot Become

The final clause is the most expanded line because it keeps every part of the prohibited relation visible:

> وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ

`وَ` coordinates it with the preceding denials; `لَمْ` governs jussive `يَكُن`; `لَّ` governs the 3MS endpoint `هُۥ`; `كُفُوًا` is the accusative predicate; and `أَحَدٌۢ` is the delayed nominative subject. The proper name is not repeated, but the scaffold binds the pronoun to the same God named in the first two lines. The line denies the realization of a candidate-to-God counterpart relation. Its endpoint is filled and fixed; its subject position is exhaustively vacant.

The negative use of `أحد` supplies that scope directly. `لا أحد في الدار` places the indefinite before the locative; `ما في الدار أحد` delays it after the locative. The explanation `أحد في النفي لاستغراق جنس الناطقين ولا واحد ولا اثنان فصاعدا` extends negation over the addressable or speaking class from one through two and upward. `فما منكم من أحد عنه حاجزين` adds its own plural blockers and pronominal endpoint. Line 4 reproduces neither house nor blockers, but it does reproduce negation with delayed indefinite `أحد`. The result is not one absent person; it is failure of the candidate class to emit any member capable of bearing `كُفُوًا` toward God.

The counterpart field supplies the positive relation that is being blocked. `الكفء المثل`, `الكفئ النظير`, and `التكافؤ التساوي` define likeness, counterpart, and equality. `هذا كفء له أي مثله` places a candidate in relation to a pronominal endpoint, with measures such as standing, wealth, or war supplied locally. `فلان كفء لفلان` can specify marriage or combat. The complete trajectory is more revealing: `كل شيء ساوى شيئا حتى يكون مثله فهو مكافئ له`. One thing enters equality with another, reaches a like-state, and thereby bears counterpart status toward it. The passage keeps the endpoint structure but changes every decisive parameter: positive development becomes negated jussive becoming; a generic first thing becomes delayed exhaustive `أَحَدٌۢ`; the counterpart predicate becomes accusative `كُفُوًا`; and the second thing becomes the recognized God-endpoint.

The phrase `حتى يكون` also belongs to a separate transition in the deity field: `لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون معبودا`. Both source assertions describe a prerequisite or trajectory through becoming. In one, a local term does not count as deity until it is worshipped; in the other, a thing that has become like another is its counterpart. Their exact transition spine permits relational alignment, not fusion. Worshipped status and counterpart status have different subjects, controllers, and relational results. At the surface, `لَمْ يَكُن` blocks the latter relation for every candidate; it does not deny that God is worshipped, turn a candidate into a deity, or install a worshipper in line 4.

Positive reciprocity, opposition, and alignment elaborate what the single surface predicate refuses. `المكافأة مجازاة النعم` makes recompense answer a favor, while `كافأت الرجل أي فعلت به مثل ما فعل بي` makes a fresh return act match an earlier act under reversed roles. A person can be a match in marriage or war. Two unspecified media can counter the eye of the sun, and a man can align two horsemen with his spear. These tuples supply agents, measures, instruments, orientations, temporal order, and outcomes that the final clause leaves absent. The sun is also called `الإلاهة` in a local worship tuple, but the shared named object does not make worship an act of warding or the final God-endpoint a solar endpoint. Each route converges only on the relational question of what can stand toward something else as equal, opposed, aligned, or matching.

The same root can instead remove or disturb equality. `أكفأت الشيء` tilts an object; a bowl or vessel is overturned; a group is diverted from its direction; a woman or ship sways; a face becomes cast down or altered in color. `الإكفاء قلب الشيء كأنه إزالة المساواة` explicitly makes overturning an undoing of equality. These events have physical controllers, affected objects, directions, gaits, and visible results. The surface does not tilt a candidate away from an equality it once possessed. It denies that the counterpart state ever occurs. Still, this counterfield gives the denial a second basis: equality can be blocked as an unattained trajectory, while elsewhere it can be positively removed by overturning.

Formal and material uses add further changed mechanisms. Poetic `الإكفاء` is difference among rhymes in letters, movements, or inflection—raising one rhyme and lowering another, or ending one in `ميم` and another in `نون`. `الكِفاء` is one or two panels sewn one to the other to cover the rear of a tent or house. `الكفأة` is annual yield or alternating production. These are respectively formal mismatch, material joining and covering, and temporal grouping of products. The passage's `كُفُوًا` is none of them. Yet each changes how equality or pairing can be embodied: by nonmatching endings, joined pieces, or alternating groups rather than a person-like counterpart.

The exact source form `تكفأ تكفؤا` gives a particularly narrow formal contact. After its prefix `تـ`, the normalized sequence corresponding to surface `كفوا` is recoverable, while the written hamza-seat and vocalic differences remain. The complete local tuple is swaying. The surface token is an accusative counterpart predicate. Exact containment allows the carrier to turn inside a gait expression but does not turn counterpart status into a gait or assign a swaying participant to the delayed subject.

The copular root likewise refuses to be flattened into generic existence. Its lexical field can report occurrence and presence in past or current time, add emphasis, participate in exception, or express causation in `كونه فتكون أحدثه فحدث`. It can derive `المكان`, `المكانة`, `الموضع`, and `المنزلة`, allowing a person to occupy another's place, hold rank, establish himself, or leave an open position such as `موضع العمامة`. It can name guarantee in `كنت على فلان ... تكفلت به` and `اكتنت به اكتيانا مثله`, submission in `الاستكانة الخضوع`, an aged man named from remembered speech `كنت في شبابي`, or an overnight bad condition `بكينة سوء`. These are complete occurrence, placement, responsibility, submission, naming, and condition tuples. `يَكُن` in line 4 is specifically a negated jussive copula whose delayed subject fails to bear the counterpart predicate. It creates no place, rank, guarantor, submitted person, aged speaker, or bad condition.

The guarantee phrase `اكتنت به اكتيانا مثله` is especially useful because `مثله` there qualifies the manner or form of assuming responsibility. It does not install a second counterpart occupant. Similarly, the place example `فلان مني مكان هذا` exposes a replaceable position, whereas line 4's delayed subject position cannot be filled by any member of its class. `موضع العمامة` is an open spatial slot; the turban excluded from a wrapping medium remains a separate object. `الاستكانة` has an unspecified endpoint of submission, but nothing in the imperative asserts that the Prophet submits or performs the command. Similar vacancy shapes do not share occupants or truth conditions.

## The Endpoint Hidden in the Name

Morphology splits `لَّهُۥ` into the preposition `لَّ` and pronoun `هُۥ`. Together they yield the unvocalized sequence `له`, which is also the final two-letter sequence of `الله`. The counterpart sources intensify the contact with `هذا كفء له أي مثله` and `فهو مكافئ له`. At the level of the final clause, `له` is a governed relational endpoint. Inside the proper name, the same letters are only contained form. The identity of their referent comes from the supplied passage recognition, not from those letters.

This distinction lets the passage perform a precise return. God is overtly named in the affirmative half; in the last denial He appears only as endpoint. Formally, that endpoint is already folded into the end of the earlier name. Grammatically, however, the preposition, pronominal person, and government remain new. The earlier `الله` does not acquire prepositional government, and the line-4 pronoun does not become a proper name. Form recurrence and participant continuity converge by independent routes while staying different kinds of relation.

The wider `له` fan demonstrates why this discipline is necessary. The sequence occurs in `لله`, `أله`, `إله`, `قوله`, `مثله`, `يأله`, `فالإله`, `الآلهة`, `التأله`, `قولهم`, `فأصله`, `حصوله`, and `يستعمله`. Depending on its local form, it can belong to a deity root, a saying possessed by someone, a likeness endpoint, an origin statement, an occurrence result, or ordinary pronominal government. Only the complete counterpart assertions reproduce the same typed endpoint relation as line 4. Other contacts carry letters and their local possessive or beneficiary structures, not the final clause's God-reference.

The passage's three uses of `لَمْ` generate an equally broad but form-only fan. The particle appears as itself in `ما لم أقل`, `قول لم أظهره`, and `قال ما لم يكن`; it also crosses the boundary `الـ + م...` in supplied forms such as `المثل`, `المساواة`, `المماثلة`, `المقابلة`, `المكافأة`, `المناكحة`, `المقول`, `المبرز`, `المركب`, `المصمت`, `المعتمد`, `المقصود`, `المكان`, `المكانة`, `المولد`, `المولود`, `المعبود`, and `الملك`. Every containing word retains its own definition and event. Negative government belongs only to the three surface particles. The formal spread of `لم` cannot negate likeness words, material closure, speech, place, birth, worship, or rule merely because their articles touch a following `م`.

The one-letter carriers behave similarly. Surface `وَ` is a conjunction before the second lineage denial and before the final counterpart denial. Waw also belongs to the roots `ق و ل`, `و ل د`, and `ك و ن`; it is the explicitly named origin consonant behind `أحد` and the explicitly lost consonant in `اللدة`. Those source histories license their own transformations, but conjunction never becomes a root radical and no weak letter moves from one word into another. `لَّ` is a preposition, `ٱل` on `ٱلصَّمَدُ` is a definite article, and the double lam in the proper name is part of its form. Their shared letter neither transfers government nor definiteness.

These formal circuits do not decorate an otherwise complete semantic analysis. They enact the same central transformation by another route. Overt name becomes governed endpoint; absolute predicate becomes exhaustive delayed subject; a derivational field full of beginnings surrounds denied biological beginning; and the opening command is contained in a word for the removal of the equality denied at the close. Every return alters a relation while leaving its occupants where grammar placed them.

## Four Endings, One Unfinished Act

All four lines end in written `د`, with the root sequence `[ء ح د, ص م د, و ل د, ء ح د]`. The outer root encloses the refuge and birth roots, but the endings do different work: nominative indefinite predicate `أَحَدٌ`, definite nominative predicate `ٱلصَّمَدُ`, passive jussive `يُولَدْ`, and nominative indefinite delayed subject `أَحَدٌۢ`. The grammatical vector is `[NOM, NOM, JUS, NOM]`, the category vector `[N,N,V,N]`, and the local polarity across those endings moves from quoted affirmative to affirmative to negative to negative.

The rhyme field's account of `الإكفاء` makes this boundary structure legible without turning the passage into an instance of poetic defect. Its source contrasts rhymes by final letters, vowels, and inflection. Here the written terminal consonant remains constant while definiteness, case or mood, category, voice, and syntactic role change. The first and last endings look most alike morphologically, yet they are farthest apart in function. Final sound supplies continuity; grammar supplies the reversal.

The reversal is the passage's deepest enclosure. In line 1, a recognized named subject receives `أحد` as an affirmative absolute predicate. In line 4, delayed `أحد` is the exhaustively denied candidate for a different predicate, while the recognized referent stands as endpoint. The first use tells what God is; the last prevents anyone from standing toward Him in the relation of a match. Oneness is not proved by counting away rivals, and exhaustive noncomparability is not a second assertion of the same predicate. Each route changes the other backward: the final vacancy shows the relational consequence of the first absolute predicate, while the first predicate prevents the final `أحد` from being mistaken for a negated property of God.

Between them, `ٱلصَّمَدُ` affirms the one outwardly open relation: God as the endpoint of reliance and need. Then `لَمْ يَلِدْ` closes a possible outgoing product, `وَلَمْ يُولَدْ` closes an incoming origin-event, and `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ` closes every lateral equality trajectory. These closures are not one generic absence. The refuge seeker is unspecified but admissible; the begotten product is prevented; the passive origin is prevented with its agent suppressed; the counterpart class is exhausted. Material no-cavity, sealed bottle, excluded turban, unfilled position, incomplete annual yield, unverified evidence, and covered house rear remain their own vacancy or closure mechanisms. None can stand in for another.

What remains open is exactly where the passage began. The source can define saying as articulated letters, distinguish it from inward conception, belief, reputation, definition, conjecture, false attribution, negotiation, rule, and indication, and even place its smallest command-form inside impact and overturning words. None of those routes reports the Prophet's performance. The final denial therefore returns not to a completed declaration but to an imperative whose content has become more exact through everything that follows.

Read from the end, `قُلْ` is no longer merely the first member of an exact four-word quotation. It is the sole uncompleted occurrence after the God-referent has been fixed as absolute property-bearer, refuge, nonbegetter, nonbegotten subject, and incomparable endpoint. The passage closes every relation that could generate, derive, match, replace, or reciprocate that referent, but it does not close the commanded act of articulation. Its last word empties the field of counterparts; its first word leaves one speaker with something still to do.
