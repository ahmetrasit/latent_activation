# The Declaration and Its Unreturned Relations

## 1. The declaration crosses into audibility

The passage begins by making speech happen:

```text
قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
```

`قُلْ` is a second-person masculine singular imperative. The supplied primary
relation identifies its controller as the Prophet, while the Arabic surface
does not name either the source of the command or the first hearer. The speech
verb stands outside its quoted complement. Inside that complement, `هُوَ`
holds the appositional carrier, `ٱللَّهُ` is the named nominal subject, and
`أَحَدٌ` is its nominative predicate. The exact lexical assertion `قل هو الله
أحد` therefore returns not a loose collection of words but the complete
surface order: command, pronoun, name, predicate.

The command acquires a material trajectory from the relations of saying.
`المتصور في النفس قبل الإبراز باللفظ قول` and `في نفسي قول لم أظهره` place a
saying on the interior side of manifestation. `المقول اللسان` supplies the
tongue as instrument, not as speaker. `المركب من الحروف المبرز بالنطق`
supplies ordered letters and their passage into pronunciation. These roles
compose the opening act:

```text
command -> Prophetic controller -> internally formed content -> tongue
        -> ordered هُوَ | ٱللَّهُ | أَحَدٌ -> audible declaration -> hearer
```

The hearer remains unnamed, but the declaration does not remain inert at that
open edge. A saying can be drawn toward the recipient's own self in `اجتر إلى
نفسه قولا من خير أو شر`; it can be held as an inherited belief in `فلان يقول
بقول أبي حنيفة`; it can pass between interlocutors in `قاولته في أمره
وتقاولنا أي تفاوضنا`; and it can spread as `القالة القول الفاشي في الناس`,
returning to a bearer as a good or bad public report. Repeated output can make
its bearer `قوال`, `تقوالة`, or `لسن كثير القول`, while circulation moves
quantity from one speaker into a population. A word can also take effect,
`الذي له قول أي ينفذ قوله`, so that reception becomes action rather than mere
hearing. None of these later controllers replaces the Prophet as controller
of the first `قُلْ`; the utterance gives later participants new agency without
transferring its original agreement.

The range of saying also changes the kind of medium that can bear the
declaration's effect. A filled pool indicates its own limit in `امتلأ الحوض
وقال قطني`; material condition becomes source, sign, and content. `فلان يقول
بكذا` can enact `العناية الصادقة بالشيء`, turning saying into sustained care.
Among logicians, `قول الجوهر` and `قول العرض` are `حدهما`, so a saying can
become the defining boundary of its object. Interrogative `تقول` and inflected
`قلت` can even be made to govern like `تظن`; that syntactic possibility
exposes a path from utterance to supposition without reducing the commanded
declaration to supposition. Emission, belief, negotiation, public report,
effective word, material sign, care, and definition are distinct afterlives of
the one produced content.

Two other afterlives sharpen the opening by supplying failures of speech.
`تقول باطلا أي قال ما لم يكن` gives a speaker, a claim, and an occurrence that
did not take place. `قولتني ما لم أقل وأقولتني ما لم أقل أي ادعيته علي`
gives an attributor, a claimed speaker, and speech the claimed speaker denies
having uttered. The passage itself places positive `قُلْ` before final `لَمْ
يَكُن`, but it supplies neither the contradiction nor the false attribution
needed to enact those failures. Its `لَمْ يَكُن` belongs to what the Prophet is
commanded to declare. The exact contact with `قال ما لم يكن` makes speaker,
content, occurrence, and truth-bearing relation more sharply differentiated;
it does not turn the command into false speech.

The same precision governs generated language. `المولد من الكلام مولدا إذا
استحدثوه` makes a saying a product of people, and `كونه فتكون أحدثه فحدث`
supplies a causer, an affected thing, and an occurring result. The Prophet
really produces a pronounced proposition, but God is its referent, not its
product. The command thus opens the passage with one successful crossing while
already distinguishing the production of speech from the production of the
one spoken about. That distinction will govern every later vacancy.

## 2. The named subject becomes the destination of need

The third term of the opening declaration immediately changes position:

```text
قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ
```

The first `ٱللَّهُ` lies inside quoted content; the second begins a new nominal
predication. Repetition holds the referent continuous while moving the name
from the declaration's interior to the head of the next line. `ٱلصَّمَدُ` is
its definite nominative predicate. What the first line sends outward as a
name, the second line turns into a destination.

The name itself carries a directed relation. `الإله الله تعالى لأنه معبود`
and `لا يكون إلها حتى يكون معبودا` place a worshipper on one side, worship as
the operation, and the worshipped endpoint on the other. `أله فلان يأله
الآلهة عبد` keeps the actor distinct from the affected object whose role
licenses the name. The statement that the sun was called `الإلاهة` because a
people worshipped it makes this direction especially clear: the group acts,
the sun receives the act and the role-name. The passage names God, not that
sun or an idol, but those complete relations make the absent worshipper
perceptible around the named surface subject.

The word-form history remains a history of the name, not of its referent:
`الله قيل أصله إله فحذفت همزته وأدخل عليها الألف واللام فخص بالباري تعالى`.
Hamza is deleted, the article is introduced, and the resulting form is
specialized. The name then re-enters action through speech and address.
`قولنا الله` makes it spoken content; `اللهم بمعنى يا ألله` turns it toward an
invoked endpoint; `الله ما فعلت` and its equation with `والله ما فعلته` place
it within an oath over a negated deed. These are not the same act as the
Prophetic declaration, but they show that the name can move through assertion,
address, request, and binding speech while its referent remains the named
subject.

`ٱلصَّمَدُ` adds a second, differently directed relation. `الصمد السيد لأنه
يصمد إليه في الحوائج` supplies a person with needs who aims toward a master.
`صمده قصد معتمدا عليه قصده` adds deliberate direction and reliance, and
`الذي يصمد إليه الأمر` lets an affair itself occupy the moving position. God
is the grammatical subject of the surface line and the destination in this
relation. The seeker, need, route, and affair do not control the clause. The
primary description of God as self-sufficient refuge therefore has a
directional grammar: dependence moves inward toward Him, while the later
clauses refuse to send a matching dependence, genealogy, or equal return back
from Him.

The hearer left open by `قُلْ` can now enter this geometry without being fixed
to one identity. The name and predicate move outward from Prophet to hearer;
need or worship can then move from a hearer toward God. A recipient may become
believer, repeater, seeker, worshipper, or bearer of an affair. The passage
does not say that the first hearer performs all those acts, but the two
directions form a complete route: declaration identifies the destination, and
the received destination reorients the affected participant.

The remaining relations of `صمد` articulate response around that route rather
than providing competing names for God. `المصمت الذي ليس بأجوف`, `الصمدة صخرة
راسية`, and `المصمد الصلب الذي ليس فيه خدد` render resistance at the scale of
material, rock, and severe ground. `إني على صمادة من أمر إذا أشرف عليه وحفلت
به` gives an attendant who stands over an affair and cares for it. `الصماد
سداد القارورة` gives an opening and a stopper that guards it. Wrapping a head
`بخرقة أو منديل أو ثوب ما خلا العمامة` gives flexible protection while
explicitly excluding the turban. `الصمد الدائم` and the `ناقة مصماد` that
remains through cold and drought while milk continues give adverse duration,
persistence, and output. Hardness, attention, closure, wrapping, and endurance
are different operations, yet they fill distinct positions in one movement
from need through dependable response.

The response reaches a beneficiary when the continuing output meets the
annual relation `أعطاني لبنها ووبرها وأولادها سنة`. The first-person
recipient receives milk, wool, and offspring; `سألته نتاج إبله سنة` and
`سألته ثمرها سنة` preserve asker, owner, herd or palm, one-year interval, and
product. `أكفأت إبلي كفأتين` divides production into alternating groups, so
duration becomes recurrence rather than immobility. Milk from the enduring
camel can enter the bottle as content and the annual gift as provision, while
wool, offspring, and fruit retain their different producers. The nonhollow
solid cannot be the bottle whose cavity is needed, but it can occupy the
resistant stopper or supporting ground. The mismatch gives the response
separate working parts.

Self-sufficiency fixes the force direction of this composition. God is the
refuge toward whom need travels, not the annual beneficiary who requires
milk, wool, children, or fruit. The material participants remain camel,
bottle, cloth, rock, earth, house, affair, and recipient. Their work makes
dependability, guardedness, attention, and provision perceptible around the
primary relation without turning the refuge into any of those objects.

An affair can approach that refuge only after several kinds of human
government have been differentiated. `ما استأحدت بهذا الأمر أي ما انفردت به`
denies that one participant monopolized it. `تقاولنا أي تفاوضنا` gives the
affair a reciprocal speech medium. `اقتال عليه تحكم` replaces exchange with
control over another, and `الذي له قول أي ينفذ قوله` gives such force an
effective word. `الاستكانة الخضوع` supplies a changed posture that force can
produce. Yet the same vertical relation can become custody rather than
domination: `كنت على فلان أكون عليه إذا كفلت به` means taking responsibility
for the person; `أشرف عليه وحفلت به` means oversight and attention to the
affair; `العناية الصادقة بالشيء` makes care the sustained orientation. A
person spending the night `بكينة سوء`, in a bad condition, can become the
affected participant who calls forth that care.

At the end of this human rotation, `يصمد إليه الأمر` makes the affair itself
move toward God. Negotiation, control, submission, sponsorship, attention, and
recourse are not one relation. Their differentiation changes how the surface
command and predicate meet: the Prophet's imperative is not thereby defined
as negotiation, coercion, or custody, and God as refuge is not a ruler whose
word is merely one human force among them. Speech supplies the route by which
a destination is known; recourse, not control, completes the affair's
direction.

## 3. Openings, coverings, and the interior that is not there

The movement from `قُلْ` to `ٱلصَّمَدُ` also changes what a boundary does. The
tongue is a transmitting boundary. An interior saying crosses it and emerges
as the ordered clause `هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`. The stopper in `الصماد سداد
القارورة` does the opposite: it stands at a mouth and retains content. The
head wrap surrounds an exterior surface, and the sewn `كِفاء` of a tent or
house joins one or two strips, `إحداهما بالأخرى`, to cover `مؤخر البيت`.
Transmission, interception, wrapping, and joining preserve different
controllers, media, and outcomes.

The house gathers several of these roles without merging them. `بيت مصمد أي
مقصود` is a destination for an implied visitor; `عملت له كفاء` makes the house
the beneficiary or endpoint of a covering operation; the joined strips give
it a protected rear. The covering differentiates front from back. In the head
scene, excluding the turban as wrapping material does not erase it, because
`موضع العمامة` gives it a location. `المكان والمكانة الموضع` and the relation
of `المكان` to `كان يكون` make place an outcome of being positioned. Covering
therefore creates orientation, and exclusion creates a distinct place for
what is not used.

Other boundaries communicate rather than cover. The full pool says `قطني` by
reaching capacity; its edge is an indicating condition. `قول الجوهر ... أي
حده` makes an analytical statement the limit of what it defines. A stopper
enforces a vessel limit. The line ending stops an utterance and carries
grammar. These boundaries all delimit, but the pool signals, the definition
determines, the stopper intercepts, and the line ending terminates. Their
differences keep the passage's closure from becoming merely a general image of
containment.

`المصمت الذي لا جوف له` drives the boundary movement to a more radical state.
A stopped bottle presupposes an interior and a content; a nonhollow solid
removes the interior position itself. `ليس فيه خدد` likewise removes furrows
from the resistant surface. The anchored rock and high thick place make the
absence stable at larger scale. The named mountain in `أحد جبل بالمدينة` can
occupy that mass and scale, but its proper name remains distinct from both the
absolute predicate `أَحَدٌ` and the adjective `صمد`. The contact changes the
scale of resistance, not the identity of the mountain.

Birth supplies the bodily boundary against which these states become
decisive. `الولادة فهو وضع الوالدة ولدها` gives a mother, a contained child,
the act of placing or delivery, and an output that crosses from within to
without. A mother cannot occupy the nonhollow material role in that enactment;
the birth scene requires an interior. A stopper would prevent rather than
perform the crossing. The passage's next line does not identify God with any
container. It takes the source and product positions that birth opens and
negates them in both directions.

The whole boundary sequence can now be heard through the passage rather than
beside it:

```text
قُلْ: an actual content crosses outward
ٱلصَّمَدُ: need reaches a dependable endpoint; openings can be guarded
لَمْ يَلِدْ: no child crosses outward from God
وَلَمْ يُولَدْ: God does not cross into the product position
وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ: no comparable relation closes upon Him
```

The declaration remains the one enacted production. Stopper, wrap, house,
place, full pool, definition, solid, and maternal boundary successively expose
what it means to release content, retain it, cover it, orient it, signal a
limit, remove an interior, or produce an output. The later negations do not
withdraw the first crossing. They distinguish a produced proposition from a
produced referent.

## 4. Source and product exchange places under negation

The third line holds one referent across a controlled reversal:

```text
لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ
```

Both verbs are third-person masculine singular imperfects, jussive under
`لَمْ`, and forms of the same lemma and root. In `يَلِدْ`, God is the implicit
grammatical controller and occupies the semantic source or progenitor
position; the child is unexpressed. In `يُولَدْ`, God is the implicit passive
subject and occupies the affected product position; the progenitor is
suppressed. The conjunction and repeated negator preserve coordination while
voice changes direction:

```text
God -> child                 negated
parent or initiator -> God   negated
```

The complete birth relations show exactly what each vacancy excludes.
`الوالد الأب والوالدة الأم وهما الوالدان` separates father, mother, and dual
pair rather than supplying an undifferentiated parent. `ولدت المرأة` and
`وضع الوالدة ولدها` give the mother grammatical and semantic control of
delivery, the pregnancy or child as contained participant, and the child as
output. `ولدناها أي ولينا ولادتها` introduces plural overseers of the event
without turning them into mother, father, or child. Managing a birth is not
begetting, and affected product is not event supervisor.

On the output side, `الولد المولود` names the born participant. Son and
daughter, singular and collective, male and female remain available ranges;
the singular surface agreement does not absorb them. `الوليد الصبي حين يولد`
returns the exact unvocalized writing `يولد` and continues the product into a
young participant. The uses of `وليد` and `وليدة` for boy, girl, male or
female slave, and even an aged woman preserve age, sex, status, and usage as
different axes. None of those product states can be assigned to God once the
passive event is negated.

Birth also normally opens a long temporal continuation. `يوم ولدت` and `يوم
ولد` position an event at a day. `يوم الأحد أي يوم الأول` makes a separately
named day a first boundary. `لدة الرجل تربه` then turns shared birth-time into
a measure between peers; its exact history, `اللدة نقصانه الواو لأن أصله
ولدة`, keeps the missing consonant attached to the age relation. A child can
become a same-age counterpart, pass through youth and age, and finally become
the `كُنْتِيّ`, the old man named from `قوله كُنْتُ في شبابي كذا وكذا`.
Remembered past state then becomes new spoken content. Day, birth-time, age,
and recollection form a participant history, while annual fruit, offspring,
alternating herds, and persistence through cold and drought supply different
clocks.

The passive negation blocks that entire downstream chain from beginning for
God. No divine birth day, birth-age peer, aging-from-birth, or remembered
youth follows. The active negation likewise prevents a child from entering
the chain from Him. The local child, woman, parents, peers, camel herds, and old
speaker continue within their own assertions; their concrete histories reveal
the continuations interrupted around the primary referent.

General generation extends the same participant topology beyond biology.
`تولد الشيء من الشيء حصوله عنه بسبب من الأسباب` gives a source thing, causal
route, cause, and resulting thing. New speech can be `مولد`; a book can be
fabricated, evidence generated but not verified, and a person or Arabic usage
non-pure or mixed. Those outcomes keep social producer, material source,
authenticity, and perceiver distinct. `كونه فتكون أحدثه فحدث` then turns a
caused result into an occurrence. An occurred affair can become something
negotiated, overseen, cared for, or reported; a generated saying can be
pronounced, believed, circulated, or falsely attributed. Product becomes a
new input.

The movement from birth to occurrence reaches the last line through `يَكُن`,
but it does not make the closing negation a denial of existence. `كان الشيء
يكون كونا إذا وقع وحضر` supplies occurrence and presence; `الكائنة الأمر
الحادث` supplies an occurring affair. Surface `يَكُن`, however, has its own
predicate and delayed subject: the state being denied is comparability to
God. `أَحَدٌۢ` is not denied occurrence; `كُفُوًا لَّهُۥ` is denied as its
state. The exact containment of reduced `أحد` in `أحدثه` brings the candidate
into the making-of-occurrence circuit, then syntax prevents that circuit from
swallowing the final relation.

Active and passive birth also resemble a return only until reciprocity is
fully enacted. In `كافأت الرجل أي فعلت به مثل ما فعل بي`, a prior actor's deed
to the first-person participant becomes the input for a later like deed back
to him. That operation requires two times, a deed, changed agency, and a
measure `مثل`. The voice pair supplies none of those. It changes God from
source-side controller to product-side affected subject while both events are
absent. Voice reversal is therefore not repayment, and generation is not
equality. A lexical child can later become a measured peer; the two relations
remain successive rather than interchangeable.

## 5. Anyone arrives after the relation

The last line delays its grammatical controller until the final word:

```text
وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ
```

`أَحَدٌۢ` is the nominative subject of `يَكُن`. `كُفُوًا` is its accusative
predicate, and the pronoun in `لَّهُۥ` is the endpoint of that predicate. God
is continuous as the referent of `هُۥ`, but He does not control the final
verb. Forward order presents endpoint B, relation, then candidate A:

```text
لَّهُۥ | كُفُوًا | أَحَدٌۢ
B       | relation | delayed A
```

The exact affirmative model `هذا كفء له أي مثله` supplies the roles in the
opposite order: A, counterpart relation, endpoint B. `كل شيء ساوى شيئا حتى
يكون مثله فهو مكافئ له` supplies a process in which one thing approaches a
measure, becomes like another, and acquires counterpart status. The surface
changes demonstrative A into delayed indefinite `أَحَدٌۢ`, changes the
affirmative nominal relation into a negated copular state, and leaves its
measure unexpressed. Status or lineage, wealth, war, marriage, combat, and
shared birth-age can each enact a measure without becoming the one surface
measure.

The delayed subject makes the denial comprehensive. `أحد في النفي لاستغراق
جنس الناطقين` extends through `لا واحد ولا اثنان فصاعدا`; `ما في الدار أحد`
supplies the same topology in which relational or locative material precedes
a delayed `أحد`. The last word is therefore not an isolated individual but
the candidate position into which one and more-than-one can enter. The primary
relation names it “anyone”: no candidate occupies comparable relation to God.

Once counterpart is enacted as a deed, the denied state's consequences become
clearer. `كافأت الرجل أي فعلت به مثل ما فعل بي` reverses initiator and
affected participant through a like return. `المكافأة مجازاة النعم` lets a
received benefit initiate recompense. `كافأ الرجل بين فارسين برمحه` positions
two riders by an instrument, and `نكافىء بهما عنا عين الشمس` places two
intercepting things between a group and the sun. These scenes supply return,
pairing, alignment, opposition, and cover. The surface close installs none as
an equal counteraction against God because the comparable A on which matched
return depends is denied.

That denial does not eliminate every direction toward God. A seeker may rely
on the refuge without becoming its equal; a worshipper may act toward the
worshipped without entering parity; a hearer may believe, repeat, or seek
without becoming counterpart. The distinction between recourse and equality
gives self-sufficiency its relational force. Incoming dependence remains
available, but no reverse equal dependence or like-for-like exchange is
installed.

The same root also supplies a counter-transformation: `الإكفاء قلب الشيء كأنه
إزالة المساواة`. A vessel is tilted or overturned, people are redirected
`إلى غيره`, a woman sways in walking, and a face changes posture or color.
These are physical and directional removals, not the grammar of surface
negation. They nevertheless complete the comparison trajectory: equality can
be approached, enacted, reciprocated, overturned, or absent. The passage gives
the absent-state realization. Its own forward arrangement also inverts the
affirmative source order by placing B before relation before A. Read backward,
`أَحَدٌۢ | كُفُوًا | لَّهُۥ` restores A -> relation -> B, but `لَمْ يَكُن`
continues to deny the restored proposition.

The outer occurrences of `أحد` now become one passage-scale transformation.
Opening `أَحَدٌ` is an affirmative predicate of God and carries the absolute
descriptive use in `يستعمل مطلقا وصفا في وصف الله تعالى`. Final
`أَحَدٌۢ` is a negation-scoped delayed subject and universal candidate. Both
are masculine, indefinite, nominative, and line-final; the final form carries
an additional Quranic boundary mark. Stable form makes changed syntax and
polarity more, not less, audible:

```text
أَحَدٌ:  what is predicated of God
أَحَدٌۢ: anyone tested for relation to God
```

`أحد أحد` supplies adjacent repetition, while the passage distributes the
same form over its first and last boundaries. `جاءوا آحاد أحاد` supplies a
plural population arriving as separate units, so distributed recurrence can
move from word to participants. Count relations add further transformations:
`أحد واثنان وأحد عشر وإحدى عشرة`, `الواحد المضموم إلى العشرات`, and
`فأحدهن أي صيرهن أحد عشر` distinguish unit, dual neighbor, composition with
tens, gendered form, and the making of a counted total. `أحدكما` selects one
from a pair; `يوم الأحد أي يوم الأول` turns firstness into a day-name;
`استأحد الرجل انفرد` turns it into solitary action. The opening predicate is
not reduced to numeric firstness, and the final candidate is not merely one
person. The intervening operations show how singular form can select,
compose, collectivize, separate, and range upward.

The close returns all those candidates to the opening claim. Absolute oneness
is first declared positively; source and product roles are then opened and
negated; every comparable candidate finally reaches the delayed subject
position and fails to enter relation. The closing `أَحَدٌۢ` therefore gives
the opening `أَحَدٌ` an operational consequence without defining it by a
single comparison measure or arithmetic count.

## 6. The utterance is distributed through its own letters

The passage's architecture begins with the exact assertion `قل هو الله أحد`
and expands its terms across four lines. `ٱللَّهُ`, the third term, recurs at
the opening of the next line and acquires a second predicate. `أَحَدٌ`, the
fourth term, remains at a line ending but returns only at the passage's final
boundary, where it changes from predicate to delayed subject. The free
`هُوَ` does not recur unchanged, yet its third-person masculine reference
continues through both implicit birth subjects and emerges bound in
`لَّهُۥ`. `قُلْ` remains a unique surface command, while its exact letters
travel through longer lexical forms.

The movement of `له` makes referential continuity formally visible. After
vocalization and annotation are removed, each `الله` ends in the exact sequence
`له`, and final `لَّهُۥ` independently reduces to `له`:

```text
ٱللَّهُ -> الله -> [له]
ٱللَّهُ -> الله -> [له]
لَّهُۥ  -> له
```

The two earlier carriers are proper nouns; the last is preposition plus bound
pronoun. The shared letters do not segment the name. They let a sequence
embedded twice in the named subject emerge as a grammatical endpoint. The
endpoint then receives several exact relational realizations: counterpart in
`هذا كفء له`, report-bearer in `انتشرت له قالة`, possessor of an effective
word in `الذي له قول`, house-beneficiary in `عملت له كفاء`, and possessor of
an absent cavity in `لا جوف له`. At the surface close, comparison selects its
primary work. Read backward, the independent endpoint is absorbed into both
name tokens and the bound pronoun returns toward free `هُوَ`, carrying relation
back into the declaration without confusing morphology.

Waw supplies a second, mobile architecture. The root waw of `قُلْ` is absent
from the imperative surface. `أحد فرع والأصل الواو وحد` places an origin waw
behind both outer forms. The root waw of active `يَلِدْ` is absent; the
conjunction `وَ` then appears independently; passive `يُولَدْ` displays waw
inside the root-shaped surface; the next `وَلَمْ` places it at a line
boundary; jussive `يَكُن` hides the medial waw present in source `يكون`.
`اللدة نقصانه الواو لأن أصله ولدة` supplies a separate explicit loss from an
age-peer form. None of these waws substitutes for another, but their order
performs concealment, conjunction, interior visibility, and renewed
concealment around the active-to-passive pivot.

The mobile interior crosses a fixed outer consonant. Every line ends in dāl,
and the active verb `يَلِدْ` adds another dāl inside the third line:

```text
أَحَدٌ -> ٱلصَّمَدُ -> يُولَدْ -> أَحَدٌۢ
دٌ     -> دُ         -> دْ       -> دٌۢ
```

The first ending is an indefinite nominative predicate, the second a definite
nominative predicate, the third a passive jussive verb, and the fourth an
indefinite nominative delayed subject. Roots `ء ح د`, `ص م د`, `و ل د`, then
`ء ح د` carry the fixed consonant through description, refuge, negated birth,
and negated candidate. `الإكفاء في الشعر`, with `اختلاف إعراب القوافي`, one
rhyme raised and another lowered, and even differing final letters such as
mim and nun, makes boundary variation an explicit relation. The passage does
not reproduce those examples; it keeps dāl fixed while inflection,
definiteness, mood, voice, and syntactic role change around it. Its boundary
both unifies and differentiates.

The exact seed `قل` survives another series of changes:

```text
ما لم أقل
متصرف قلت مجرى الظن
القال الخشبة التي تضرب بها القلة
قلب الشيء كأنه إزالة المساواة
```

In `أقل`, it lies inside a first-person speech act explicitly denied and
available for false attribution. In `قلت`, it lies inside a form that can
govern a proposition like supposition. In `القلة`, it lies inside the affected
game-object struck by `القال`. In `قلب`, it lies inside inversion and the
removal of equality. The containers change controller, polarity, syntactic
force, physical affectedness, and direction while the ordered letters remain
recoverable.

The impact scene releases a distinct cross-scale movement rather than turning
the command into a blow. `القال الخشبة التي تضرب بها القلة` gives actor,
wooden instrument, target, and contact. `صمده بالعصا صمدا إذا ضربه بها`
repeats the directed strike with a staff and affected participant. A vessel
can then tilt or overturn; a people can be redirected; a spear can stand
between two riders; a needle or seam joins one strip to another; cloth wraps a
head. Against hard ground, anchored rock, and mountain scale, the supplied
result is resistance or absence of furrow rather than a claimed strike. The
same topology of instrumented direction therefore yields impact,
displacement, alignment, joining, wrapping, or no registered mark according
to medium and target. The Prophet remains controller of surface `قُلْ`, and
God remains referent and refuge, not striker or struck object.

`لَمْ` forms a complementary containment structure. On the surface it occurs
three times as a negator governing jussive verbs. Its exact reduced letters
also cross the article-to-stem boundary in nouns that fill the passage's
opened roles: `المقول` and `المبرز` supply speech instrument and manifested
state; `المعبود` supplies the worshipped endpoint; `المقصود` and `المعتمد`
supply intended and relied-on destination; `المصمت` and `المصمد` supply
nonhollow resistance; `المولد` supplies product; `المثل` and `المماثلة`
supply comparison; `المكان` supplies place. In those nouns, the sequence is
not a negator. Its exact persistence makes the grammatical operator inhabit
the written forms of the medium, endpoint, material, product, measure, and
place exposed by its negations.

The two short forms consequently cross without fusing. `قُلْ` is spoken
through `المقول`; it can become `المولد من الكلام`; its proposition includes
`لَمْ يَكُن`; its letters enter `قلب` in the field that removes `المثل`; and
its declaration identifies `المقصود` or `المعتمد` as refuge. The larger words
do not manufacture a hidden sentence. They distribute the command and negator
through the exact roles by which the passage externalizes one content and
vacates three relations.

Finally, reduced `أحد` lies at the opening of `أحدثه` in `كونه فتكون أحدثه
فحدث`. The contact places the outer form inside a causative occurrence, while
surface grammar gives it two explicit positions: first as the state predicated
of God, last as the participant controlling negated `يَكُن`. One form can thus
be embedded in making something occur, name an affirmative predicate, and
govern the denial of a relation. The architecture preserves difference at
every point: `أحد` is not `أحدث`, the final subject is not denied existence,
and formal containment is the route by which causation makes the surface
roles more exact.

## 7. The ending reopens the command

Forward, the passage withholds the last clause's controller until its last
word. A hearer first meets God as endpoint `لَّهُۥ`, then the comparable
predicate `كُفُوًا`, and only then candidate `أَحَدٌۢ`. Reaching the boundary
completes the proposition being denied. The line ending therefore does more
than stop sound: like the full pool that indicates capacity and the saying
that defines a limit, it communicates which relation has closed.

Backward movement performs a different operation. `أَحَدٌۢ | كُفُوًا |
لَّهُۥ` restores the A -> relation -> B order of `هذا كفء له`, while the
negator continues to hold the state absent. The endpoint pronoun then returns
through God as the passive affected subject of `يُولَدْ`: a possible parent is
restored only as the suppressed initiator of a negated event. The same God
returns through active `يَلِدْ`: a possible child is restored only as the
vacant output of another negated event. Neither backward restoration fills
the genealogy.

Continuing backward, God reappears as `ٱلصَّمَدُ`. The child and parent
vacancies give way to a different asymmetry: seeker or affair may move toward
the self-sufficient refuge. A relationship remains open, but it is recourse,
not derivation or parity. Material resistance, guarded opening, attention,
persistence, and provision restore the affected participant around the
destination; they do not supply a source on which God depends. The possible
hearer of the declaration divides here into two routes. As seeker,
worshipper, believer, repeater, or beneficiary, the participant can remain in
relation to God. As comparable A, the same human scope cannot complete the
closing state.

The repeated `ٱللَّهُ` then moves from line-initial subject back into the
quoted clause. The independent `له` endpoint is reabsorbed into its written
ending, and bound `هُۥ` returns toward free `هُوَ`. Final `أَحَدٌۢ`, already
heard as anyone under negation, reopens initial `أَحَدٌ` as God's affirmative
predicate. Distributed `أحد أحد` contracts from the outer boundaries into the
first declaration without equating the two grammatical roles.

Only after all those returns does the opening command reassemble:

```text
قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
```

It now sounds with every participant position differentiated. The Prophet is
the producer of the declaration, not the subject of its predicates. God is
the declared referent and destination of recourse, not a product of speech,
parent, child, needy beneficiary, or comparable endpoint in a relation of
parity. The unnamed hearer is not left as an empty acoustic slot: hearing can
become inward reception, belief, repetition, negotiation, care, worship, or
reliance, while the closing candidate relation remains denied. The utterance
moves outward so that need may move toward God; genealogy and equal return do
not move back.

The final boundary therefore changes the first imperative. `قُلْ` is not only
an instruction to pronounce an isolated predicate. It initiates a four-line
arrangement in which the name becomes refuge, the continuous referent crosses
source and product positions under negation, and the outer form `أحد` returns
as every candidate barred from comparability. When the ending sends those
relations back into the opening, the commanded declaration is heard as the
one completed production that announces a destination without producing,
deriving, or matching the One it names.
