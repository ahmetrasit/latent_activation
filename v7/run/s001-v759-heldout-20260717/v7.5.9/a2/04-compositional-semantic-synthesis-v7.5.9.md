# From the Name to the Differentiated Path

The passage opens by making its eventual addressee identifiable before it addresses him. In `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ`, the first carrier is explicitly articulated as `بِـ + سْمِ`, with the recoverable lexical base `اسم`. The supplied description `أصل اسم سمو` joins naming to elevation, while `الاسم ما يعرف به ذات الشيء` makes the name a sign through which the named is known. The iḍāfa gives that sign its exact endpoint: `سْمِ -> ٱللَّهِ`. A second formal history meets it there. `الله` is supplied as deriving from `إله` through deletion of the hamza and addition of `الألف واللام`; the altered form is then specialized as the name. The opening therefore contains two distinct but interlocked operations: a name raises and identifies its bearer, and the bearer’s written name preserves a stated history of deletion, addition, and specialization. The name does not become the named, but the grammar makes the one the possessed carrier of the other.

That identification is the beginning of a passage-wide change of reference. In the first four lines the same endpoint is carried through `ٱللَّهِ`, `لِلَّهِ`, `رَبِّ`, the repeated mercy epithets, and `مَٰلِكِ`. At line 5 it is not renamed; it is rephased as `إِيَّاكَ`, twice. Naming, praise, predication, title, and direct address remain fresh speech relations, yet the passage expressly makes God in the opening the `You` of the later address. Thus the movement “from naming and praising God to direct address” is enacted as a preservation of referent under a change of grammatical position: the named genitive endpoint becomes the fronted second-person object of two first-person performances, then the controller of the imperative `ٱهْدِ` and of the perfect `أَنْعَمْتَ`. The exact name prevents `You` from floating free of the opening; the later `You` makes `بِسْمِ` audible as the first act in an address trajectory rather than as an isolated label.

The other supplied speech carriers clarify what kind of act this trajectory is without merging their local events. A name identifies; a physical `عَلَم` distinguishes and can guide a traveler; a good reputation or voice spreads among an audience; an oath binds a proposition; `نعم` affirms or promises; testimony is entrusted to a verifier; praise evaluates; and a poem can be sent as praise or blame. Projected together, they form a relay from sign to recognition to address and possible response. In the passage, however, the opening is a name-and-praise carrier, line 5 is direct address, and line 6 is a request. No oath, answer, testimony, or reply is asserted. Their contribution is to make the exact succession perceptible: a recognized endpoint can be praised, addressed, and asked, while each new utterance retains its own controller, content, and result.

The first four lines build that endpoint almost entirely without finite predicates. Line 1 is a governed genitive chain: `بِـ` governs `سْمِ`; `سْمِ` takes `ٱللَّهِ`; and `ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ` and `ٱلرَّحِيمِ` are parallel adjectives of the named endpoint. Line 2 changes the architecture. Nominative `ٱلْحَمْدُ` receives `لِلَّهِ` as its predicate; `رَبِّ` stands appositionally with that endpoint and takes `ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ` as its complement. Line 3 consists wholly of the exact pair that ended line 1. Line 4 compresses authority, time, and judgment into the nested genitives `مَٰلِكِ -> يَوْمِ -> ٱلدِّينِ`. Only after these carriers have named, praised, qualified, and titled the endpoint do two imperfect first-person-plural verbs appear. The referential preparation and the onset of direct action are consequently two parts of one formal movement.

## Praise folds mercy into the opening

`ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ` does more than recur. At the end of line 1 the two words are parallel dependents of `ٱللَّهِ`; at line 3 the same ordered pair becomes the whole line, with `ٱلرَّحِيمِ` appositional to `ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ`. A four-word governed chain therefore releases its exact two-word suffix as an autonomous carrier. The ending of the first line becomes the body of the third, and the syntax changes as it moves. This fold gives the opening praise an internal pulse: the mercy pair is reached, line 2 names praise and Lordship, and the pair returns in full.

Its lexical operation also has direction. The source explicitly says `الرحمن الرحيم مشتقان من الرحمة`, and describes mercy as `رقة تقتضي الإحسان إلى المرحوم`: a softness or compassionate disposition that entails good to an affected receiver. That assertion opens a complete relation behind the two epithets: initiating tenderness, a receiver, conveyed benefit, and an improved consequence. Other supplied operations differentiate how that consequence can be produced. Benefaction conveys good to another; nurture brings an incomplete thing state by state toward completion; teaching feeds small portions of knowledge; provisions, repair, and rain benefit a household or land; support makes a body or affair stand; praise then evaluates a good act or satisfactory result. These are not one source event. Their shared directed geometry allows the epithets to project an enacted process of care rather than remain merely static descriptions.

Several independent embodiments give that process scale and material. An upper shading field, a cloud layered upon itself, enduring rain, a soft moist south wind, watered land, a plant that remains green, and gathered fresh water form one atmospheric division of labor. The atmosphere descends; the wind moves laterally; land and people receive; vegetation persists; water gathers as an aftermath. This projected mechanism changes how mercy and later favor can be experienced: benefit may have medium, duration, direction, and sustained result. It does not assert that the epithets are clouds or that `أَنْعَمْتَ` is rain.

The written carrier `ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ` also contains the complete supplied forms `الرحم/رحم` before its final `ن`. Those forms locally name kinship, the womb as `بيت منبت الولد ووعاؤه في البطن`, and the painful postpartum condition in which swelling or an unreleased remainder follows birth. Their formal inclusion opens another projected process: a bounded interior holds a developing occupant; nurture and small increments form it; cohesion supports it; release completes the process; retained afterbirth instead produces pain. Knowledge, material, or another primary carrier can fill the developing role only projectively, and successful release remains different from retained remainder. The surface epithet continues to mean the mercy-derived adjective. What changes is its passage work: beside the above-to-below atmosphere, it can carry an interior-to-exterior geometry of care, formation, emergence, and aftermath.

These mechanisms return to `ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ` without turning mercy, favor, and praise into one act. `ٱلْحَمْدُ` is a nominal praise carrier; its supplied field opposes praise to blame, distinguishes praise from thanks, allows repeated praise, names the praised bearer, marks the limit of what is praised, and warns against claiming praise from people when one has benefited only oneself. `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ`, much later, is a completed benefaction by an expressed second-person controller to other receivers. Between them lies a repeatable content relay: good is conveyed; a fresh evaluator finds an act, person, place, or affair satisfactory; a fresh speaker praises; praise may circulate or recur; a claim to credit remains constrained by who actually received the good. The later favor can therefore return as possible content for the earlier praise, but it cannot become its historical cause merely because the schema fits. Mercy supplies an initiating affect, favor supplies a transfer to another, and praise supplies evaluative speech. Their distinct phases give the opening positive density.

## Lordship and the Day form a distributed circuit

The appositional `رَبِّ` in `رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ` opens several differentiated controller relations. The supplied `رب` can be owner, master, obeyed authority, reformer, or one who governs a people. It can also be the one who stands over an incomplete thing, repairs it, and brings it state by state to completion; the teacher who nourishes another or himself with portions of knowledge; the caregiver who continues care across a prior relation; and the support by which another system persists. Custodianship, ownership, command, nurture, and material sustenance share a controller-to-dependent interface, but their consent, purpose, medium, and result remain distinct. The primary phrase “Lord of all worlds” gives these operations their passage-scale relation: `all worlds` is the actual complement of Lordship, not a class to be redefined through an unrelated sense of the root `ع ل م`.

Collective formations sharpen that scope. Tribes can gather numerically; treaty people can be bound by covenant; arrows can be held by a leather enclosure; herds can follow a leader; a council can share a place; a ruler can be installed by a group; and a scattered collective can lose its common direction and power. These actualities do not turn “all worlds” into a tribe, herd, quiver, or council. They disclose the parameters that any collective relation must specify: who its members are, what binds them, whether a controller is installed or inherited, what sustains them, and what happens when cohesion is lost. Against those variants, `all worlds` remains universal scope under the title `Lord`, while the plural classes at the end remain three differently bound path relations.

The phrase `رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ` also returns inside the exact supplied description `يوم يقوم الناس لرب العالمين`. Two lines later, `مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ` meets an even fuller source collision: `يوم الدين` is the day of judgment, account, and recompense, and the source explicitly gives both `مالك يوم الدين` and `مالك يوم الجزاء`. The two nonadjacent surface titles are therefore joined through two independent source sentences. One supplies the Master and the day of account; the other supplies people standing before the Lord of all worlds. Their titles and events do not merge, but the shared day-role closes a distributed circuit across the opening.

The Day of Judgment consequently becomes a participant-bearing process. `يَوْم` can retain the bounded unit from sunrise to sunset, expand to any duration, or take its boundary from a great event or hardship. `ٱلدِّين` at this locus supplies judgment, account, recompense, and returned consequence. `قيام` supplies the standing of people before `رب العالمين`. Valuation can disclose a price; a balanced coin can meet a standard without excess; debt can hold a due open until its term; compensation can replace retaliation; ownership can pass; and blood can remain without the vengeance that would have returned consequence. These local relations form a measured-return field: prior relation, participant, held or completed due, measure, judgment, return, substitute, or terminal vacancy. Judgment is thereby heard not simply as a label inside a date but as an architecture of standing, measure, and consequence.

The time field prevents that architecture from becoming uniform. Sunrise and sunset bound one scale; midday makes a balanced internal position at which the day is half and the shadow nearly stops; an intermediate age denies both old and young; equal weight denies excess; freshness begins a duration; old age and repeated habit occupy other positions. Standing likewise branches into chosen or compelled posture, resolved rising toward an affair, preservation by a custodian, bodily support, exhausted arrest, diseased compulsion, and even an intact eye whose sight has gone. Projected into the Day, these variants distinguish posture, initiative, measure, and function; they do not replace the exact source standing of people. The Judgment circuit has room for a collective, a bounded event, an authority, a measure, a returned result, and a still-open aftermath after recompense.

Recurrence adds another temporal dimension without turning every return into the same event. Praise may be performed again and again; a war fought after an earlier war carries accumulated conflict; habit preserves a familiar course; gifts can recur; the particle `رب` can mark fewness or occurrence time after time even though one source explicitly leaves its derivation unknown. Recent birth and first youth, an animal retained after birth for milk, balanced strength catching up with age, intermediate age, and an old palm occupy different positions on beginning-to-duration scales. These actualities let “opens,” “moves,” and “then turns” be experienced through repetition, persistence, and event-bounded time, while the passage itself asserts no war, biological maturation, or habitual repetition. Exact recurrence in the passage remains a fresh occurrence with its own work.

The form `مَٰلِكِ` reinforces this differentiation without changing its active-participial actuality. Compared without vocalic decoration, it contains the supplied `ملك` field: tightening and cohesion, possession and transfer, command and sovereignty, contract, central support, the middle or main portion of a road, water that lets a traveler master his affair, and a leading animal or body part followed by the rest. The exact phrase `مالك يوم الدين` fixes the primary carrier. The contained field then gives authority several orthogonal dimensions: strength to hold a structure, control of a possession, command over a collective, central support, leadership, and the capacity to sustain a journey. Mastery of the Day remains authority over the named day; the chamber makes the material and relational work of authority audible around it.

## The named endpoint becomes `You alone`

The formal hinge is line 5: `إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ`. The passage has used eleven explicit definite prefixes in its title, epithet, path, and class carriers, but line 5 is the unique line with none. Definite framing yields to person framing. The fronted object is stated twice rather than left understood after the first predicate, and the English rendering supplies `alone` to both: “You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help.” Exclusivity is therefore not a property borrowed by the second verb from the first. Each fresh operation begins again with the same explicit sole endpoint.

The exact source sentence `إياك نعبد إياك نطيع الطاعة التي نخضع معها` gives the first performance its content: obedience with submission. The first-person plural is nevertheless its grammatical and semantic initiator. This active, exclusive orientation becomes sharper against the complete service counterfield held inside and around `نَعْبُدُ`. The word contains the complete written form `عبد`; source actualities distinguish the owned human from voluntary worship, coercive enslavement from honored service, anger that ends in silence from quick action, and the paved or tarred material made tractable from every human relation. Other branches distinguish obedience to law, ownership, group-installed rule, custody, and delegated decision. The passage selects the active first-person worship performance. The counterfield contributes by making its controller, consent, direction, and dignity exact: the worshippers act; they are not grammatically acted upon inside this verb.

The second operation exposes need without erasing that agency. `نَسْتَعِينُ` is an imperfect measure-X first-person-plural verb; its supplied base and root lead to `الاستعانة طلب العون`. A requester initiates speech or action toward a prospective helper; the helper, aid medium, assisted task, result, and aftermath remain open until a response occurs. Anything used for help may fill the medium, cooperation may distribute the labor, and support may enable another affair to stand. The passage’s description of this request as `dependence` names the relation positively. Dependence here is not inert lack: `we` controls the request, `You alone` is the requested endpoint, and another operation remains available to the respondent.

The support field makes that openness precise. Need can be answered by state-by-state nurture, inherited care, small portions of knowledge, central structural support, water carried for travel, cooperative labor, or guardianship that preserves a group. Each route changes a different parameter. A caregiver transforms the dependent over time; a helper answers a request; a custodian continuously maintains; water sustains without intention; a central support bears load; delegation can release control rather than increase dependence. Coercive ownership produces imposed work instead of nurtured capacity. The request for help can therefore open toward completion, continued support, released agency, or still-unfilled response, but it cannot silently contain the help it asks for.

Even the exact suffix-position contact `نَسْتَعِينُ ⊃ عين` preserves that distinction. The surface word remains rooted in help. The supplied `عين` actualities describe swelling around an eye, aching eyes, an intact eyeball whose sight is absent, and an eye brought to contentment. Projected around the request, they create a concrete need field: an impaired perceiver may require an external guide or support; guidance can compensate for absent sight by providing orientation, but no supplied operation restores the sight itself. Eye-contentment is a different possible result. The tiny formal chamber thus changes dependence from a generic deficiency into a distinction between compensation, continued impairment, pain, and satisfaction while leaving the verb’s morphology untouched.

Line 5 is therefore neither a descent from agency into passivity nor a single blended act. It is a double exclusive orientation by an active collective: service is performed; help is requested. The repeated endpoint holds the two together, and their different predicates keep the results apart. That disciplined double relation is the material the passage next “turns” into guidance.

## The turn redistributes agency and gives dependence direction

`ٱهْدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ` makes the passage’s transformation grammatically visible. In line 5 the first-person plural controls two imperfect verbs and the second-person singular is their fronted object. In `ٱهْدِنَا`, the imperative stem `ٱهْدِ` has a second-person controller, while suffixed `نَا` makes the same first-person collective the affected receiver. The exact person vector is thus `2MS: object -> object -> imperative controller -> perfect controller` and `1P: controller -> controller -> imperative object`. The passage itself controls the summary verb “turns,” but inside the actions no controller is transferred carelessly: `we` perform worship and request; `You` would perform guidance; `we` would receive it.

Because the passage explicitly turns “that worship and dependence into a request for guidance,” adjacency becomes a causal content succession. Worship contributes exclusive orientation toward the endpoint. Dependence contributes an acknowledged need and an open responder role. The guidance request carries both into a new operation: the formerly addressed endpoint is asked to act, and the formerly acting collective is repositioned as receiver. Agency persists on both sides but changes event-local form. The requesters do not lose initiative by needing guidance; they use their agency to request a transformation whose performance belongs to the addressee.

The supplied guidance assertion fixes the first possible result. `هديته الطريق والبيت هداية أي عرفته` makes guidance an operation by which a receiver comes to know a path or house, and `الهداية دلالة بلطف` gives the indication a gentle medium. Guide, receiver, route content, indication, and recognition are all present. `ٱهْدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ` binds the same roles on the surface: the addressed controller, the first-person receiver, and the accusative route object. Dependence has therefore acquired a direction and an epistemic change. The request is not merely for something helpful; it asks for a relation in which this particular route becomes known.

Knowledge and marking divide that operation further. Knowledge replaces ignorance or brings news into awareness. A mark is an effect by which one thing is distinguished from another, and `المعلم الأثر يستدل به على الطريق` makes a road trace a basis for inference. A guide actively indicates; a marker can remain still while a traveler reads it; a leading neck, staff, arrowhead, first rank, or lead animal precedes a following whole. These source events retain their occupants, but projected together they give guidance several nonidentical roles: controller, forward part, stationary sign, observer, follower, route, and recognized direction. The path may materially exist while remaining unknown; a marker may exist while the observer cannot use it; a human or bodily support may compensate where sight or balance fails. Guidance is consequently an epistemic state change embodied in differentiated relations, not a synonym for movement.

The appearance field begins even earlier. A raised object can become visible from afar; a name identifies; a physical sign distinguishes; a good voice spreads. A projected sequence of appearance, mark, knowledge, and continued course makes the initial `اسم` and later `ٱهْدِنَا` endpoints of one recognition architecture. At the opening, the named endpoint is made available for address. At the request, the desired route is to be made available for action. The final class `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ`, whose source relation includes failure to find the way, will invert that availability. The passage thus moves from a named and recognized addressee to a requested known path and finally excludes a class whose relation to aim and route has broken.

The response remains genuinely open. The passage asserts a turn into a guidance request, not a grant of guidance. The lexical guidance operation is complete in its own assertion; its primary enactment is still requested. No surface verb carries the first-person collective along the route, no duration of traversal appears, and no arrival is stated. This vacancy is productive because it keeps four different things from collapsing: asking, responding, making known, and traveling. Each can receive a projected mechanism, but none can be smuggled into the preceding event.

## A known path must also be held, borne, and followed

The exact object is `ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ`. One supplied definition gives `الصراط: الطريق المستقيم`; another gives straightness as alignment with a level line and human adherence to a stable method. The morphology marks `ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ` as an active participle agreeing with the accusative path. These exact locks make straightness a state of this route, while the wider field differentiates what can bring such a state about or preserve it.

A maintained-road projection assigns a guide or leading part, a marker, a central corridor, a straight measure, a prepared bearing surface, support for the traveler, and a possible stable destination. `ملك الطريق` supplies the middle or main portion that may be kept to or cleared; `ق و م` supplies alignment, balance, stability, and the custodian who stands over an affair; the trodden `طريق معبد` supplies a surface made tractable; tightened material and cohesive walls supply bearing capacity; water and aid supply continued motion. The source occupants remain road, wall, dough, traveler, custodian, and helper. In their division of labor, straightness becomes a maintained relation among line, material, controller, traveler, and continued use.

The complete forms contained in `ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ` reinforce this maintenance field. `تقيم` occurs in assertions of residence and of installing one thing in another’s place; `قيم` names one who governs and preserves a people. The primary carrier remains the straight participle. Projectively, however, it can house a custodian who keeps alignment, a stable site in which the route’s result persists, and a substitute that occupies a damaged or vacated position. Maintenance is thereby more than static correctness: it can require oversight, habitation, and repair, with each operation preserving its own controller and result.

Material routes make that work tangible. A thick concentrate strengthens a skin or container; tar coats a ship, camel skin, or road; kneading gives a yielding material cohesion; folded hide becomes shield-like; inherent solidity resists load; softness may be an intended pleasant result rather than a failure. These vectors distinguish the target state of each carrier. A road may need smoothing, a waterskin sealing, a wall cohesion, a shield resistance, and a body softness. Projected onto the straight path, they make it a conditioned bearing surface whose capacity to carry passage is produced, while preventing a one-note equation of hardness with goodness.

The cutting and engulfing counterroutes expose what that maintained condition must resist. One source calls `السراط` a cutting sword that penetrates in a blow; another derives the swallowing field from material entering a throat and disappearing; the road-name itself is accompanied by the proposal that the traveler disappears in it. Projected as a cutting track, a wielder, forward point, resistant rock or folded hide, penetrating force, visible split, and divided aftermath produce a line by breaching a boundary. Projected as an engulfing corridor, an entrant crosses a path-mouth, enters a medium, and vanishes from an observer, with return guidance or terminal loss as alternatives. Straight alignment preserves a traversable relation; cutting creates a passage through resistance; engulfing consumes visibility. Their distinct consequences deepen the requested path by making boundary behavior explicit.

Other source cuts keep intention and result differentiated. A kin relation may be joined or severed; an upper lip may be cut into a visible mark; body hair may be deliberately shaved away. Those enacted removals have controllers, affected carriers, and produced boundaries. They are unlike an object that simply becomes lost, material that disappears in a medium, or a route that cannot be found. Projected at the path boundary, they distinguish purposeful marking or removal from uncontrolled loss.

The full route state field extends farther. A route may be unknown and then made known; unaligned and then straightened; unprepared and then made bearing; peripheral and then centered; traversed and then inhabited; continued and then interrupted; visible and then hidden; possessed and then lost; cohesive and then scattered. A failed or departed mount cuts off the traveler. An exhausted animal stops. A place cannot be found. A camel or object leaves its owner. A stray beast remains where its owner is unknown. Groups and roads disperse in every direction, and a collective can lose its unified word or power. These are complete local transitions, not a single narrated journey. They supply exact failure parameters against which the primary straight path can be heard: knowledge, alignment, material, centrality, locomotor capacity, visibility, ownership, and common direction can fail independently.

The path can also arrive at a maintained place. Land may agree with a traveler and become a pleasant residence; `مقام` can name the site or duration of staying, while `لا مقام لكم` denies stability; a city can be constituted by obedience to command-holders; a council gathers people; a market “stands” when goods circulate and “sleeps” when they stagnate; water makes an encampment and livelihood possible. No destination beyond the straight path is named, so residence, city, council, and market remain projected outcomes. They change the force of arrival: the end of a route might be not merely a coordinate but a site sustained by water, order, gathering, and exchange, or a vacancy in which no stable place is available.

## Dependence receives material and bodily divisions of labor

The passage’s turn from help to guidance admits independent support routes without making any one of them the hidden literal scene. The well mechanism is especially complete. A well or deep container holds much water; an ostrich-named crossbeam spans it; a standing pulley or held wooden component transmits force; gathered water is raised; a strengthened waterskin carries it; the traveler or group thereby controls its stopping and livelihood. Material that already exists still requires apparatus, an operator, lifting, a carrier, and later use. If the apparatus is absent, the water remains inaccessible; if the water freezes, the material remains while its function changes; if the container is porous, transport fails. Dependence can thus locate its vacancy in resource, mechanism, controller, carrier, or accessibility rather than in one undifferentiated lack.

A voyage projection changes the controller map again. The chief of sailors, the forward guide or leading part, the following crew or whole, carried water, and route continuity occupy different roles. A lost or exhausted locomotor medium, frozen water, or absent destination produces a different failure. The vessel itself remains only typed by the sailor relation, not independently described. When this assembly enters the guidance request, it makes command, forward orientation, follower, sustenance, and continuation separately audible. The straight path supplies direction to the projection; the projection does not turn the path into a voyage.

Supported gait gives the same primary transition a bodily scale. A weak walker can proceed between two helpers while swaying right and left; another gait is calm rather than the rush of defeat; walking on foot can be light and purposive; upright stature supplies a bodily axis; pain, leg disease, heaviness, or imbalance can arrest motion. Help and guidance can therefore fill different roles in one enactment: the helpers bear bodily weight, while a guide or marker supplies orientation. Support enables the receiver’s own movement rather than replacing it. Straightness becomes an achieved relation among body, two supports, line, and direction.

The eye chamber in `نَسْتَعِينُ` makes one limit exact. If the visible eye carrier remains but sight is absent, a mark alone cannot be used; an external guide, staff, or human support can compensate. This is not restoration. The distinction between present form and missing function also maps to memory loss, where content can be absent from memory or memory from its bearer, and to concealment, where an object persists in a medium but disappears from an observer. Guidance can reintroduce content or orientation as a fresh operation, yet it cannot retroactively erase the vacancy. The primary request thus contains a rigorous difference among healing, compensation, teaching, and support.

Atmospheric water, well water, and carried water can be projected into a longer material succession: rain descends and gathers; apparatus raises; a container bears; a traveler carries laterally; a body or group continues. At every handoff the controller, direction, medium, and scale change. No source says it is numerically the same water token. The assembly’s force lies in its completed transformation of relations: above becomes below, stored becomes lifted, lifted becomes portable, and portable support becomes route capacity. This is an independent material explanation of how dependence might become capability.

The support-to-standing field supplies a nonmaterial counterpart. Need opens a role; a nurturer, caregiver, helper, or custodian answers; small increments and central supports enable; cooperative labor distributes; the body or affair stands. A coercive controller produces imposed labor instead, while delegation can return a decision or evidentiary role to the dependent. The primary first-person collective is consequently neither self-sufficient nor inert. It acts, requests, receives, and can be enabled to act again. The controller exchange at `ٱهْدِنَا` is the grammatical concentration of that larger relation.

## The quality of the path becomes a participant history

At the next line boundary the exact base `صِرَٰط` recurs under changed grammar. Line 6 has the definite, qualified object `ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ`; line 7 opens with bare construct `صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ`. Article, attachment, and specification type all change. The description “the straight path, specified as the path of those who have received favor” supplies the cross-line bond. What was initially a formal recurrence therefore becomes primary contextual specification. Quality first answers what state the path bears; a participant relation next answers which path it is.

This does not replace straightness with people. The adjective remains exact, and the construct adds a second axis. `ٱلَّذِينَ` opens a plural class; its relative clause `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ` gives that class a completed history. The form-IV perfect has an expressed second-person controller and a third-person-plural endpoint. Its source relation is `الإنعام إيصال الإحسان إلى الغير`: conveying good to another. The English clause paraphrases the class as those who have received favor, and the Arabic surface restores the giver left unexpressed in that clause. The specified path is thus associated with people who bear a prior directed benefit relation.

That relation makes the class a precedent rather than an identity for the requesting `we`. One can follow another’s manner; a guide or first rank can precede followers; a lead animal or body part can orient the rest. Those source events project model and leader roles onto the favored class, while the passage supplies only path association. The first-person requesters are not said to be the same occupants. Their requested reorientation now has a social and historical specification: the route is not only geometrically straight but associated with recipients of a completed good from the very addressee who is being asked to guide.

The favor event also completes an opening-to-closing relay. The same second-person referent who was named and praised, then addressed as the sole endpoint of worship and help, is now the expressed controller of a completed benefit to another plural class. This past operation serves as precedent for the requested future operation without becoming its fulfillment. The perfect `أَنْعَمْتَ` shows what the controller has done in one event; the imperative `ٱهْدِنَا` asks what that controller should do for another participant relation. The temporal and participant difference keeps precedent from turning into possession of the result.

Several exact inner forms thicken this positive transfer. `أَنْعَمْتَ` preserves the stem `أَنْعَمْ`, the final controller suffix `تَ`, and the complete internal `نعم`. The same stem has supplied assertions of benefaction, saying yes, and adding or intensifying an act; `نعم` can be praise opposed to blame, affirmation opposed to `لا`, ease, softness, or eye-contentment. The surface perfect and its syntax fix benefaction here. The enclosed forms create a formal constellation in which affirmative utterance, praise, increment, ease, and good transfer surround the act without becoming it. Near the end of the line, explicit `وَلَا` will expose the very negative operator against which `نعم` is defined. The movement from inner affirmative form through otherness to explicit negation is real as arranged form, though no dialogue of yes and no is asserted.

Favor also admits transfer mechanisms that clarify its direction. A gift moves gentle good to a loved receiver on a carrier; an offering travels to an appointed destination; debt holds value for later return; ownership or decision can be transferred; contract changes a relation; covenant binds parties; compensation can take the place of retaliation; a substitute can stand in a prior position. These are not synonyms for benefaction. They expose the parameters that `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ` actually fills: expressed giver, other-directed good, plural receiver, completed transfer, and improved relation. Judgment later returns a measured consequence; favor sends good outward; exclusion changes which participant relation may specify the path. Their distinct temporal directions prevent a generic exchange image from consuming the passage.

Reception itself branches by controller and consent. A gentle gift may invite reciprocal giving; an offering travels until it reaches a prescribed destination; a bride is transferred and joined to a husband, with the supplied descriptions retaining both ordinary joining and a captive comparison; a refuge-seeker arrives to request protection before it is granted, whereas a captive does not control the destination; delegated judgment or decision can release agency. These local events project received good, appointed arrival, changed relation, open protection, and compelled placement as separate reception modes. The favored class actually receives conveyed good. It is not an offering, bride, refuge-seeker, or captive, but those variants make the giver, route, consent, reception, and resulting status of favor exact.

Even the small prepared-product assembly preserves that difference. A perfume slab receives material; intensified grinding makes it fine; a carrier can take the resulting fragrance as a gift; reputation may then spread. Preparation, giving, receiving, and social aftermath are fresh operations. Projected beside favor, the sequence makes production and transmission separately audible, while the passage never asserts perfume. Likewise, analogical animal and tool names -- the ostrich-named beam, herds, a falcon-modeled quick person, a male hyena, a surly serpent or camel, and a place-attributed product -- retain their source naming relations. Their positive contribution is formal precision: a shared name or contained form can open a role, as the well beam does, without importing the source occupant into the favored class or the path.

## The same `upon` frame carries opposed outcomes

Line 7 repeats `عَلَيْهِمْ` at positions 4 and 7, but it does not repeat an occupant or a force. The first occurrence completes `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ`: an expressed second-person initiator conveys good to the plural relative class. The second completes `ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ`: a passive participle presents a different plural class as affected while leaving the semantic initiator unexpressed. Exact surface and prepositional government are stable; referent, controller distribution, voice, polarity, and result change.

The complete form `عليه` appears in several supplied local assertions, each with its own topology: mercy directed toward an affected receiver, anger directed toward an endpoint, affective anger that can lead to silence, an affair depending on its support, and a gift resting on the plate that carries it. Projected together, they make `عليه` a directed-relation socket rather than a payload. Controller, transmitted force or content, medium, endpoint, and consequence determine what arrives. The passage enacts two of these possibilities with unusual clarity. Benefit and passive adversity can use the same directional frame, so direction alone cannot determine value.

The English clause gives the contrast a second grammatical realization. Its “those who incur anger” makes the class control an active incurrence relation; the Arabic `ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ` remains passive, with no expressed anger initiator. Inside that passive form, the exact complete `غضوب` activates source descriptions of an active anger-bearing disposition, including one who is much or quickly angry and the separate surly animal or person. Three vectors therefore surround the same primary class without collapsing: surface passive affectedness, English active incurrence, and lexical active disposition. The English predicate contributes liability or receipt; the Arabic preserves external affectedness and an unknown source; the embedded form exposes an anger-bearer only in its own source events.

The supplied anger field further differentiates direction. `غضب عليه` directs intensified displeasure or vengeance toward an endpoint. `غضبت لفلان` and `غضبت به` alter the preposition according to whether the represented person is living or dead. Reciprocal `غاضبه` introduces opposition between controllers. Hard rock, thick skin, folded hide, swelling near the eye, redness, a great serpent, and a surly camel or woman bear separate material, bodily, and dispositional results. The actual line uses the `على` topology under passive voice; neither the living/dead distinction nor the rock, hide, eye, color, or animal occupants enter its event. Their mapped parameters make the exact primary relation more precise: anger can change preposition, controller symmetry, material resistance, body state, or represented participant, and none of those variants licenses filling the passive initiator.

Conflict assemblies give the adverse field further causal possibilities while staying projected. Rivals can attempt to match one another; opponents can stand to each other in contest; people can reciprocally defy; a protector can orient to family and threat; a difficult animal can resist a human controller; a hunter can go into open land with the supplied hunting tool, use a quick falcon-modeled observer role, and pursue visible or marked quarry that may escape and become lost. These topologies introduce reciprocal, triangular, and asymmetric controller maps. Around the anger-incurring and straying classes, they make opposition, pursuit, resistance, and escape available as differentiated countermechanisms. The passage does not state that its classes fought, hunted, or were hunted. Their work is to prevent adverse force from being imagined as a single undirected pressure.

The material counterfield also returns to the path. Hard rock, layered hide, and shield resist penetration; a cutting sword makes a track through them; a swelling eye obstructs perception; body pain or leg disease arrests movement. Favor, by contrast, can soften, repair, nourish, and sustain. The closing class relation is consequently not only a lexical polarity. It can be projected through force, material, body, controller, and result: what bears and supports passage differs from what blocks, wounds, compels, or overwhelms. Each material source remains local, but its role changes what the primary contrast can embody.

## Straying breaks a relation from within

`وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ` introduces a third participant geometry. The morphology makes `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ` an active participial plural: the class itself bears the state. The source says `الضلال ضد الهدى` and describes every one who deviates from an intended aim as `ضال`; it also includes failure to find a way or affair. This exact opposition returns directly to `ٱهْدِنَا`. The guidance request is no longer heard against an empty alternative but against an active counterstate in which the needed relation to aim and route fails.

The other loss branches show that “go astray” is not an undifferentiated bad state. One relation fails when material disappears into water, milk, or earth and is no longer visible. Another fails when an object, camel, house, or place leaves the possessor’s knowledge. Another makes blood go without vengeance, leaving a return unfilled. Another loses content from memory or memory from its bearer. Another leaves an animal in waste with no known owner. Across them, the carrier may move or remain; the vacancy may be spatial, epistemic, mnemonic, proprietary, or consequential; recovery may be open or terminal. Their shared structure is a broken relation between occupant and aim, route, observer, owner, place, memory, or due result.

The path context selects aim and route for the primary proposition, while the other mechanisms sharpen its consequences. A road can exist and still be unknown. A traveler can move and still deviate. An object can persist while its location is unavailable. A visible carrier can remain intact while function is absent. A collective can remain plural while its common direction and power dissolve. Straying therefore differs from passive adverse affectedness at the level of causal geometry: the anger class is presented by the Arabic as bearing an externally formed passive result with its source vacant; the straying class actively bears a failed relation to direction.

Motion parameters keep even that active failure from becoming one picture. A walker may proceed lightly, sway through weakness, move calmly rather than flee, run with some speed, stop from exhaustion, or stand because disease has seized the legs. A stray animal may lack an owner rather than a path. A group may scatter across many routes. A hunter’s quarry may escape. These complete source motions project speed, balance, support, intention, control, and ownership as independent axes. The primary `go astray` remains deviation from the desired path, but the field makes clear that speed is not direction, standing is not necessarily success, and motion is not necessarily arrival.

Collective mechanisms give the three closing classes equally precise boundaries. The favored `ٱلَّذِينَ`, the passive `ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ`, and the active `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ` are not one plural population under different labels. A group can be gathered by number, kinship, shared site, covenant, enclosure, leader, species, or common direction; it can then scatter and lose cohesion. The passage supplies three different binding relations: completed favor as path precedent, passive Arabic and active-English anger under exclusion, and active deviation under negation. No quiver, treaty, herd, ruler, or council is their actual binder. Those projections make the principle exact: plurality never supplies its own relation.

## One excluded field is built by two different operators

The English clause gives a unified negative scope: the straight path is not the path of those who incur anger or go astray. The Arabic constructs that unity in two stages. `غَيْرِ` is a genitive relational noun that qualifies and excludes the favored relative class, then governs `ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ`; `وَلَا` coordinates the active straying class under an explicit negator. The supplied lexical description says both that `غير` can mean otherness, exception, or negation and that `معنى غير معنى لا`. Their meanings can meet; their grammar and operation remain different.

The first stage forms an alternative class through otherness and relation. The second adds a class through conjunction and scoped denial. This two-stage architecture matters because the classes already have different voices. Nominal exclusion first receives the passive affected class; explicit negation then receives the active divergent class. One primary boundary is thus distributed across operator type, attachment, voice, controller visibility, and causal result.

Replacement actualities enlarge the operator field without becoming the surface operation. One image may change while an essence remains; one object may replace another; truth may displace wrong; blood-money may replace retaliation; a substitute may stand in a prior position. Other vacancies behave differently: debt waits for return; a helper response remains prospective; a passive actor is unknown; a location cannot be found; sight is absent; afterbirth is retained; stable residence is denied. These are not interchangeable absences. `غَيْر` actually excludes here, but its wider field makes a position’s possible fates perceptible: it may be contrasted, negated, vacated, delayed, replaced, or left unknown. The surface’s exact choice is therefore sharper, not blurrier.

The internal `نعم` of `أَنْعَمْتَ`, positioned before `غَيْر` and `وَلَا`, creates a formal polarity sequence across the line. `نعم` can be affirmation and promise opposed to `لا`; it can also be praise opposed to blame. Enclosed within the benefaction verb, it is not a spoken answer. Yet the ordered forms enact `affirmative seed inside positive transfer -> otherness/exclusion -> explicit no`. This formal circuit intensifies the passage’s movement from a received good to differentiated excluded relations while preserving the actual grammar of every term.

The whole close is consequently a three-position field, not a binary moral list. The favored class receives good from an expressed giver and specifies the path. The anger class is excluded through `غَيْر`, appears passively affected on the Arabic surface, actively incurs in the English clause, and retains an unknown anger source. The straying class is additionally denied through `وَلَا` and actively bears broken route or aim. Benefit, external adversity, and internal deviation are three distinct mechanisms. The requested reorientation seeks the first relation and is bounded against the latter two without making either one a synonym for the other.

## The passage’s form carries the transformation

The seven lines make this semantic differentiation through complete, indexed formal vectors. Their word counts are `[4,4,2,3,4,3,9]`. The first two four-word lines build the name/epithet chain and praise/Lordship predication. The two-word third line folds out the mercy pair. The three-word fourth line compresses authority, day, and judgment. The four-word fifth line doubles direct address across two predicates. The three-word sixth line performs the imperative and supplies the qualified route. The nine-word final line expands the route into relative class, completed favor, exclusion, passive affectedness, repeated endpoint, negation, and active deviation. Quantity does not create meaning, but the final expansion provides the distinct positions without which the participant field could not be enacted.

The morpheme vector `[7,7,4,4,5,6,15]` independently moves through two equal opening plateaus, two equal compact title units, and then increasing person and class articulation. The last line’s fifteen morphemes include the perfect stem and controller suffix, two `عليهم` constructions, definite passive and active participles, conjunction, and negation. The supplied syntax-edge vector `[4,4,1,2,3,3,6]` moves from dense governed openings through the minimally attached exact return, then grows to the six relations needed by the close. These complete groupings make the phase verbs “opens,” “moves,” “then turns,” and “specified as” perceptible as formal operations: opening chains, predicate onset, person reversal, and terminal differentiation.

The finite-predicate vector is still more decisive: `[0,0,0,0,2,1,1]`. Four nominal/title lines precede two imperfect first-person performances, one second-person imperative, and one perfect second-person benefaction, after which participial voices distinguish states. The aspect sequence `IMPF-1P / IMPF-X-1P / IMPV-2MS / PERF-IV-2MS` carries the passage from current or repeatable worship and dependence, through a requested future guidance operation, to a completed precedent of benefit. “Turns” is therefore not merely an editor’s summary; morphology redistributes controller and receiver while aspect differentiates present performance, prospective request, and prior completed good.

Articles and pronouns reinforce the hinge. The explicit definite-prefix distribution `[2,2,2,1,0,2,2]` makes line 5 the unique zero, precisely where fronted `إِيَّاكَ` and first-person verbs replace nominal framing. Line 6 restores articles on path and quality. Line 7 drops the article from construct `صِرَٰطَ` while marking the two participial counterclasses. Definite title field becomes personal exchange, and personal exchange becomes path and class field. The change is grammatical before it is thematic.

The line endings weave the remote phases together without merging them. The exact carriers are `ٱلرَّحِيمِ / ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ / ٱلرَّحِيمِ / ٱلدِّينِ / نَسْتَعِينُ / ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ / ٱلضَّآلِّينَ`, giving `-يم / -ين / -يم / -ين / -ين / -يم / -ين`. The first four alternate; the help request repeats the second class; straightness restores the first; straying returns to the second. `ٱلرَّحِيمِ` is an exact endpoint twice, whereas the other carriers distribute the sounds across epithet, plural noun, account noun, verb, straight participle, and divergent plural. Mercy and straightness can thus answer one another at the boundary, while world, judgment, help, and straying circulate through another ending class. The shared sound changes formal perception, never root or occupant.

Exact recurrence does the same work at larger scales. The mercy pair repeats with new syntax. `إِيَّاكَ` repeats with the same referent but a new performance. `صِرَٰط` repeats with changed article and specification. `عَلَيْهِمْ` repeats with changed occupant and force. Repetition is therefore not one operation called emphasis. In each locus it preserves one parameter while changing another: token and order, referent, base form, or directional frame remain; attachment, action, specification, occupant, voice, or polarity changes.

## The ending reopens the beginning

The final `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ` first returns to `ٱهْدِنَا` through the exact mutual opposition of guidance and error. What had been a positive request gains a fully articulated counterstate: without the requested relation, aim, route knowledge, ownership, memory, visibility, or common direction can fail. The active participle makes the failure participant-bearing. The preceding passive class then adds a different danger, adverse affectedness whose controller remains unknown. The close does not merely say which path is wanted; it specifies the controller and vacancy structures against which guidance matters.

That differentiation returns farther back. `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ` makes the addressed endpoint of line 5 audible as an already acting benefactor and provides a precedent for the requested guidance without claiming fulfillment. The favor relation returns to the mercy epithets as one possible consequence of compassion and to `ٱلْحَمْدُ` as possible content for fresh praise. The path’s favored class gives Lordship a receiver field for care and support. The projected Day of Standing Account can receive the closing classes as differentiated participant or outcome roles, while no passage clause states that these particular classes are judged there. The Day gives class contrast possible measure and consequence; the classes give the Day possible participant differentiation. Their relation remains a projected passage-scale field.

The closing collectives also return to `رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ`. Universal scope under Lordship now stands beside, but does not merge with, three path-related classes. Leadership, covenant, support, and scattering show how collectives can be held or lose direction; the passage itself assigns different binding relations. The line-2 title and line-4 Day circuit thereby become the broad authority horizon within which the later request and class distinctions can be heard, while their actual syntactic distances remain intact.

Most importantly, the ending returns to the name. The supplied `اسم` identifies and raises the mention of its endpoint; guidance makes a route known; a mark distinguishes a way; straying breaks the relation to an aim or route. The passage begins with an exact operation of recognition, moves to the recognized referent as `You`, asks that referent to perform another recognition operation for `us`, and ends by excluding a class in whom recognition and direction fail. The opening and closing do not share an occupant or lexical meaning. They form a carried schema: identifiable endpoint, direct relation, requested known path, and excluded loss of relation.

Seen backward from the close, `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ` is therefore more than the first named carrier. It establishes the source to whom every later operation is oriented: the endpoint of praise, the Lord whose collective scope is all worlds, the Master whose Day gathers standing and return, the sole `You` of service and dependence, the prospective controller of guidance, and the expressed giver of prior favor. The repeated mercy pair supplies a benefit geometry; the Day supplies consequence; line 5 supplies exclusive active relation; line 6 redistributes agency toward a known path; line 7 gives that path participant history and two causally different boundaries.

The request remains open toward a journey it has not narrated, while the passage completes its own transformation of relations. It moves from a name that makes the addressee present, through praise that differentiates mercy, Lordship, and mastery, into an exclusive exchange in which active dependence asks to become directed reception. It then transforms straightness from quality alone into a path specified by received good and bounded against passive adversity and active loss. That final differentiation sends the hearing back to the first words: `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ` now opens not only a named praise field, but the entire controller, receiver, route, consequence, and counterstate architecture that the name will sustain.
