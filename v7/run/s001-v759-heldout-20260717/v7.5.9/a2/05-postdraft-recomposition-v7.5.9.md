# The Named Source Enters the Exchange

The passage begins by identifying a source and ends by distinguishing relations to a path. Between those limits, its opening descriptions become operations. The God named in `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ` becomes the repeated `إِيَّاكَ` whom the speakers worship and ask for help, the controller addressed by `ٱهْدِنَا`, and the benefactor expressed in `أَنْعَمْتَ`. Lordship becomes audible as care, governance, and support; mercy receives the directional shape of good conveyed to another; mastery of the Day opens an account-and-consequence field; and deity is realized as the endpoint of worship. The titles are not dissolved into the later verbs. Each retains its own grammar and actuality, but each acquires participants, directions, and possible consequences as the passage proceeds.

That movement gives the middle of the passage unusual force. The three consecutive endings `ٱلدِّينِ`, `نَسْتَعِينُ`, and `ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ` belong to three different constructions: a nominal account scene, an active request for help, and a qualified route. Yet the supplied lexical relations join them without making them synonymous. `ٱلدِّينِ` actually means judgment, account, and recompense in `يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ`; another branch of the same word names obedience and worship, a guidance assertion says that God guides to `الدين`, and the standing field calls a religion `قيما`, straight and stable. Judgment, worship, dependence, guidance, and steadiness thereby form a relay across lines 4-6. The opening authority is not merely announced and left behind. It enters an exchange whose requested result is a differentiated way of proceeding.

## A name makes address possible

The first relation is exact and grammatical. In `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ`, prefixed `بِـ` governs `سْمِ`, and `سْمِ` takes `ٱللَّهِ` as its genitive endpoint. The recoverable base `اسم` is supplied both as what makes a thing known and through a lineage connecting naming with elevation. A name can therefore function as an identifying sign and as a raised carrier of mention. It does not become its bearer, but it makes the bearer available to recognition and speech.

The written name has its own supplied history. `الله` is related to `إله` through deletion of the hamza, addition of the definite material, and specialization as the divine name. More consequentially, the `ء ل ه` field states that something is not an `إله` until it is worshipped: `لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون معبودا`, and `فالإله الله تعالى لأنه معبود`. This source relation does not need to be invented later. Line 5 supplies its passage-level performance when the same referent is made the sole object of `نَعْبُدُ`. The name at the beginning thus contains a relation that the passage eventually enacts: named deity becomes actual worshipped endpoint.

The first two lines give that endpoint a quiet formal stability. Each has four words, seven morphemes, and four registered syntactic edges. In each, the divine name occupies the second word. The relation changes, however. In line 1, `ٱللَّهِ` completes the construct `بِسْمِ`; in line 2, `لِلَّهِ` completes the predication of `ٱلْحَمْدُ`. The same positional center is first the bearer of a name and then the endpoint of praise. The repeated location preserves reference while changed government begins the larger succession from identification to evaluation to address.

Other speech relations clarify the succession. A physical `عَلَم` can distinguish something and guide an observer; a voice or reputation can spread; an oath binds a speaker to a proposition; testimony entrusts content to verification; `نعم` can affirm or promise; a poem can be sent as praise or blame. These remain different events with different controllers and results. Together they expose the stages implicit in the passage: a referent is made recognizable, praise is directed to him, the referent becomes vocatively available, and a request is made. The `ء ل ه` material itself includes the invocation forms `اللهم` and `يا الله`, so the name field already admits the transition from designation to appeal. When `نَا` later appears as the object in `ٱهْدِنَا`, the appeal gains an explicit beneficiary.

The later pronoun therefore does not replace an earlier identity. In the first four lines the referent is carried through `ٱللَّهِ`, `لِلَّهِ`, `رَبِّ`, the mercy pair, and `مَٰلِكِ`. In line 5 the same endpoint is rephased twice as `إِيَّاكَ`; in line 6 the implied second-person subject of the imperative becomes prospective guide; in line 7 the second-person suffix of `أَنْعَمْتَ` identifies the already acting benefactor. Name, predicate, epithet, title, object, imperative controller, and perfect controller are fresh grammatical roles occupied by one continuous addressee. The opening name secures the continuity; the later exchange reveals what kind of relational source has been named.

## Praise gives the titles a common carrier

Line 2 begins with the nominative nominal carrier `ٱلْحَمْدُ` and directs it to `لِلَّهِ`. `رَبِّ` stands appositionally with that endpoint and takes `ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ` as its genitive complement. The praise field distinguishes evaluation from the good that may occasion it. It can oppose praise to blame, distinguish praise from thanks, recur as a new performance, name a praised bearer, and mark a boundary against claiming public credit when no good has reached another. Praise therefore has a speaker, an evaluated content, an endpoint, an audience, and a claim condition. It is not identical with mercy or favor merely because both are positive.

The mercy pair is nevertheless folded tightly into this carrier. At the end of line 1, `ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ` are parallel adjectives of `ٱللَّهِ`. Line 3 repeats the exact ordered pair as its whole body. What was the suffix of a four-word governed chain becomes an autonomous two-word line, and its internal syntax changes to an appositional pairing. The opening thus reaches mercy, names praise and Lordship, and returns to mercy before introducing mastery and the Day. Repetition does not merely add emphasis; it changes the scale and syntactic work of the same forms.

The supplied definition gives mercy a directional mechanism: `رقة تقتضي الإحسان إلى المرحوم`, a tenderness that entails good toward an affected receiver. That relation can be set beside, but not equated with, later benefaction. Mercy supplies initiating compassionate disposition; `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ` supplies a completed transfer of good by an expressed controller; praise supplies a fresh evaluation. Their coalition has three phases: good is disposed toward or conveyed to another, a result becomes available for evaluation, and praise can be newly performed. Because the phases remain separate, the later favor can return as possible content for the earlier praise without being declared its narrated cause.

Several material processes give this directed good a body. A cloud layered upon itself, sustained rain, a moist wind, watered ground, persistent green growth, and gathered fresh water distribute source, medium, receiver, duration, and aftermath. Another formation lies inside the written `ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ`, which contains the supplied forms `الرحم/رحم`: kinship, the womb as a bounded house and vessel of growth, and a painful retained remainder after birth. Atmosphere gives mercy an above-to-below direction; the womb gives it an interior-to-exterior process of formation and release. Neither changes the epithet into weather or anatomy. They show that care can have a medium, a boundary, an occupant, incremental development, successful emergence, or a painful failure of completion.

The opening pair also prepares the benefit field at the close. Favor can nourish, soften, repair, or sustain; praise can follow conveyed good; blame can oppose praise; and the line-ending negation will distinguish relations that do not specify the requested route. Positive content is therefore not a single undifferentiated value. It moves through initiating affect, material or social mediation, receiver, consequence, and evaluation. The repeated mercy pair gives the opening an internal pulse capable of carrying that later history back into praise.

## Lordship anticipates need, support, and favor

`رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ` fixes a universal scope: the Lord is related to all worlds, not to a collective secretly substituted from another lexical branch. Within that exact title, however, `ر ب ب` differentiates several controller relations. The lord can be owner, master, obeyed authority, governor, reformer, or the one who stands over an incomplete thing and brings it state by state to completion. A teacher nourishes another with small portions of knowledge; a caregiver continues an inherited relation; a custodian preserves an affair; a central support lets a body or system stand. Ownership, nurture, governance, teaching, and structural support share a controller-to-dependent interface while differing in consent, medium, duration, and result.

Two source assertions connect this title especially closely to the later exchange. One says `رب الرجل النعمة يربها`: a person tends or completes a favor. Another places need, a firm knot, favor, and benefaction in the `الربى` field. These do not make `رَبِّ` mean need or make `أَنْعَمْتَ` a repetition of Lordship. They allow the title to hold both sides of a developing relation: a dependent need that can be answered and a good that can be cultivated to completion. Line 5 exposes dependence through the request for aid; line 6 gives the requested response a direction; line 7 presents prior benefaction as a precedent. Lordship thus begins as a title and later becomes legible as a capacity for sustaining, directing, and completing another.

The collective field makes the scope more exact. Tribes may gather, treaty partners may be bound, arrows may be held in an enclosure, a herd may follow a leader, a council may share a place, and a group may install a ruler or scatter until it loses a common direction. These source events do not redefine `ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ`. They identify parameters in any governed collective: membership, bond, controller, support, direction, and the consequence of lost cohesion. The universal collective under Lordship remains distinct from the three path-related classes at the end. The title supplies their broad horizon without collapsing universal scope into a favored or excluded group.

Material branches keep nurture from becoming purely abstract. A thick fruit concentrate or sediment can strengthen a skin; medicine can be prepared with honey; a porous vessel can be repaired; rain can sustain vegetation; stored water can support a traveler. Each has a different carrier and immediate controller. Their shared result is not goodness in general but improved capacity: a vessel bears, a body receives medicine, a plant persists, a traveler continues. When later help and guidance are heard beneath Lordship, support becomes the enabling of another operation rather than the erasure of the dependent participant's agency.

## The Day opens the central relay

`مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ` is a nested genitive chain: the active-participial `مَٰلِكِ` governs `يَوْمِ`, which governs `ٱلدِّينِ`. The supplied lexicon explicitly gives both `مالك يوم الدين` and `مالك يوم الجزاء`. At this locus, `ٱلدِّينِ` means judgment, account, recompense, and returned consequence. That local sense is primary. It joins authority to a bounded or event-defined time and makes the Day a process with an assessor, participants, measure, due, result, and aftermath.

The title `رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ` returns inside another exact source assertion, `يوم يقوم الناس لرب العالمين`. The two nonadjacent opening titles are thereby joined through a day-role: the Master owns the Day of account, while people stand before the Lord of all worlds. Standing can be chosen or compelled, bodily or institutional, initiating or preserving. Valuation can establish a price; balanced weight can meet a standard; debt can hold a due open; compensation can substitute for retaliation; ownership can pass; a consequence can be returned. These are distinct local operations, but they combine into a Day-of-standing-and-account architecture with differentiated posture, measure, authority, and result.

The contained form `ملك` widens the mechanics of mastery without changing the active participle. Its field includes cohesion and tightening, possession and transfer, sovereignty, contract, central support, the middle or main portion of a road, travel water, and a leading part followed by the rest. Mastery of the Day remains authority over that Day. Around it, the contained operations specify what authority may require: power to hold a structure, control a due, leadership of a collective, a sustaining medium, a central route, or a transferred position. Mastery is consequently not reducible to force; it is a relation capable of holding measure, participants, and return together.

Yet `ٱلدِّينِ` does not stop at the account scene. A separate branch states `الدين لله طاعته والتعبد له`: religion as obedience and worship directed to God. The next line enacts precisely that orientation in `إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ`. The guidance field then gives `هداه الله للدين هدى`, placing God as guide, a receiver as guided, and `الدين` as destination or content. Finally, the standing field supplies `دينا قيما`, explains `القيمة` as `الملة المستقيمة`, and calls a straight religion stable. `قوام الدين والحق` names what lets religion and truth stand. These contacts preserve the line-4 actuality of judgment while opening a passage-wide relay:

`يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ -> إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ -> ٱهْدِنَا -> ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ`.

The relay is also formal. Lines 4-6 have word counts of three, four, and three, a balanced frame around the doubled address. Their morpheme counts rise from four to five to six. The first is a noun chain with no finite verb; the second has two first-person-plural imperfect verbs; the third opens with a second-person singular imperative whose suffixed object is first-person plural. Their final words are `ٱلدِّينِ`, `نَسْتَعِينُ`, and `ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ`. The audible ending relation and the lexical bridges agree: the Day's account field passes through enacted worship and acknowledged need into a request for guided steadiness.

The Day also reaches toward the closing classes without annexing them. Judgment supplies possible measure and returned consequence; the final line supplies favored, anger-affected, and straying participant relations. One source even places `غير` and recompense together in `غير مدينين`, with separate readings of not recompensed and not owned. The passage does not thereby state that its closing classes are being judged in line 7. It makes the Day and the class field mutually articulable: authority can receive differentiated participants, and differentiated paths can be heard beneath a horizon of consequence.

## Exclusive address realizes the named relation

The passage's formal hinge is `إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ`. Eleven definite prefixes occur across the passage, but this is the sole line with none. Article-framed titles yield to person-framed interaction. The explicit object `إِيَّاكَ` stands before its verb and is repeated after the conjunction rather than being understood from the first clause. The endpoint is therefore freshly stated for each action. Worship and the request for help are held together by identity of addressee, while their controllers, media, and results remain different.

The first verb supplies the clearest completion of the opening name. `نَعْبُدُ` is an imperfect first-person-plural performance, and the source assertion `إياك نعبد إياك نطيع الطاعة التي نخضع معها` fixes its content as obedience with submission. The `ء ل ه` field had defined the deity through being worshipped; the passage now places the named God exactly in that role. The name is not merely exchanged for a pronoun. Its defining relation is made actual when the speaking collective actively orients worship to `You alone`.

That agency matters because the written `نَعْبُدُ` contains the complete form `عبد` and therefore opens a broad service counterfield. An `عبد` may be an owned human opposed to the free; a controller may enslave another or impose labor; the same root can name voluntary worship and submission; `معبد` can mean honored and served; a road, camel skin, or ship can be made tractable by treading or tar. Anger followed by silence, quick motion after little delay, strength, and scattered groups occupy further branches. The line selects none of the coercive or material events as its surface meaning. Their work is to sharpen the actual event: the first-person plural controls the worship verb, chooses its endpoint, and is not grammatically acted upon. Voluntary service is heard beside, and distinguished from, possession, forced labor, material conditioning, and honored reception.

The second clause changes the operation while preserving initiative. `نَسْتَعِينُ` is an imperfect measure-X first-person-plural verb. Its field gives seeking aid as `طلب العون`. A requester initiates the relation toward a prospective helper; a task exposes a need; aid may arrive through a medium; cooperation may distribute labor; and the result remains uncompleted until a response. Dependence is therefore active. The speakers perform the request even though they cannot themselves fill the requested helper role.

This distinction prevents the two clauses from becoming a descent from agency into passivity. Worship is a performed orientation; asking for help is an initiated exposure of need. The first operation has its object and act; the second opens helper, aid-medium, assisted task, result, and aftermath roles. Repeated `إِيَّاكَ` makes the same addressee exclusive in both without pretending that worship itself is help or that asking already contains an answer. The named deity has become worshipped endpoint and requested source of enabling action.

The direction of line 5 runs from `we` toward `You`. That direction will reverse at the next boundary. The exchange is reciprocal in orientation but not symmetrical in operation: worship and request move toward the addressee; guidance is requested from the addressee toward the speakers; prior favor in line 7 has moved from the same controller toward another plural class. The passage thereby differentiates offering, appeal, response, and benefit by who initiates each event and where its result is meant to arrive.

## The addressee becomes prospective guide

`ٱهْدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ` turns the established relation by redistributing grammatical roles. In line 5, first-person plural controls both imperfect verbs and second-person singular is their fronted object. In `ٱهْدِنَا`, the imperative `ٱهْدِ` has a second-person singular controller, while the suffix `نَا` makes the first-person plural the affected receiver. The addressee who was the endpoint of action is now asked to initiate action; the collective that acted becomes the requested recipient. Agency persists on both sides, but it changes event-local form.

Worship and dependence supply the content of this turn. Worship contributes exclusive orientation to the addressee. Seeking help contributes acknowledged need and an open responder role. The imperative combines them in a new request: the same endpoint is asked to guide, and the same speakers ask to be repositioned. Their dependence has acquired a direction. It is no longer an unspecified need for assistance but a request concerning `ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ`.

The guidance field specifies what such a response could do. `هديته الطريق والبيت هداية أي عرفته` gives guide, receiver, route or destination content, and a result in which the receiver comes to know it. `الهداية دلالة بلطف` adds gentle indication as a medium. Other branches make a leading neck, staff, arrowhead, first rank, or forward animal a `هادي` because it precedes a following whole. A physical sign can remain stationary while an observer infers a way from it; a leader can move ahead; a teacher can supply knowledge; a staff or person can support a body whose orientation is impaired. Guidance is thus not identical with locomotion. It can transform an unknown route into a recognized relation before any traversal occurs.

The same root differentiates how something moves toward a destination. A `هدية` is a gentle gift sent to a loved receiver; `الهدي` may be an offering from `النعم` conveyed to the House or sanctuary; a bride may be joined to a husband; a person seeking refuge may approach a group before protection is granted; a captive is carried without controlling the destination; reciprocal giving moves benefit in both directions. These source events do not turn the imperative into a gift, offering, marriage, refuge scene, or captivity. They reveal the parameters that direction alone leaves open: controller, consent, carrier, appointed destination, reception, and whether the result is already secured. In the passage, the speakers control the appeal, the addressee is prospective guide, the speakers are intended receivers, and the known straight path is the requested content.

That reception remains genuinely prospective. The imperative enacts the request, not the grant. No finite verb says that the first-person collective is guided, travels, persists, or arrives. The path is supplied and specified, but the requested response remains an open relation between the addressee's possible action and the speakers' possible reorientation. This openness lets support, teaching, marking, bodily aid, and route maintenance enter as differentiated mechanisms without being mistaken for narrated results.

The eye chamber inside `نَسْتَعِينُ` makes the distinction concrete. Decoration-free written contact exposes `عين` inside a verb whose actual root remains `ع و ن`. The eye fields include swelling around the eye, aching eyes, an intact eyeball whose sight is absent, and an eye brought to contentment. If the carrier remains while sight is gone, a marker by itself cannot supply its function to that observer. A guide, staff, or human support can compensate by providing orientation, but compensation is not restoration. Knowledge may be reintroduced; the missing function is not retroactively erased. The request for help thereby admits pain, impaired perception, external guidance, bodily support, and satisfaction as different need or result states.

The positive eye result joins the passage even more exactly. The `ن ع م` field gives `نعمك الله عينا` and `نعم الله بك عينا` for eye-contentment and honor. Across the passage, `الله` is named at the opening, `عين` is enclosed in `نَسْتَعِينُ`, and `نعم` is enclosed in `أَنْعَمْتَ`. The three forms do not reproduce the source sentence in surface order, and the help verb does not change roots. Their distributed contact nevertheless creates a complete positive perception circuit: divine source, a person through whom good is related, an experiencing eye, and contentment as result. It complements the impairment route. Aid and guidance can answer lack through compensation; favor can project a satisfied receiver; neither result should be silently substituted for the other.

## Straightness becomes a maintained relation

The requested object is exact: `ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ`. `ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ` is an active participle agreeing with the accusative path. One supplied definition gives `الصراط: الطريق المستقيم`; another describes straightness as alignment with a level line, steadiness, and adherence to a stable way. The path therefore bears straightness as its actual quality before any further participant history is added.

The `ق و م` field turns that quality into a maintenance problem. A straight object is aligned; an affair stands through its `قوام`; a custodian stands over a group and preserves it; a body stands by support; an instrument or standard establishes measure; a stable religion is `دينا قيما`. The word `ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ` also contains the supplied written forms `تقيم` and `قيم`. Their source events include taking up residence, installing one thing in another's place, and governing or maintaining a people. The containing adjective remains the straight participle. Projectively, its internal forms make straightness capable of oversight, stable habitation, and repair by replacement.

A maintained road therefore requires more than a correct line. A guide or leading part provides forward orientation; a mark makes the route inferable; a central track keeps movement away from destructive margins; a custodian preserves condition; a prepared bearing surface supports repeated passage; water and aid sustain the traveler; a stable place can receive arrival. `ملك الطريق` supplies the middle or main part of a road; `قوام الدين والحق` supplies supporting structure; `الطريق المعبد` supplies a trodden and tractable surface. These roles remain source-local, yet their coalition gives straightness controller, material, user, duration, and repair conditions.

Material branches test those conditions. Tar can coat a ship, camel skin, or road; a thick concentrate can strengthen a skin or container; kneading gives yielding material cohesion; layered hide becomes shield-like; hard rock resists force; a soft result may be desirable in a body or medicine while disastrous in a wall. A route may need smoothing, a waterskin sealing, a wall cohesion, a shield resistance, and a body flexibility. The relevant value is not hardness or softness alone but fitness of a carrier for its operation. A straight path becomes a conditioned bearing relation rather than an abstract line detached from continued use.

Two route counterimages expose its boundaries. The `ص ر ط` field includes a cutting sword that penetrates in a blow and a swallowing relation in which material enters a throat and disappears; the road-name is also associated with the traveler's disappearance into it. A cutting track creates passage by breaching resistance, with wielder, edge, force, boundary, and split aftermath. An engulfing corridor takes an entrant across a threshold and removes it from an observer, with return orientation or terminal loss as alternatives. Straight maintenance preserves a traversable relation; cutting produces one through division; engulfing consumes visibility. None of these mechanisms replaces the actual path, but each makes boundary, observer, and consequence explicit.

The route can fail at other interfaces. A mark may exist but remain unreadable; a path may materially persist but be unknown; a traveler may lose a mount; an exhausted animal may stop; a place may be impossible to find; memory may lose its content; a group may disperse in many directions; a formerly possessed object may leave its owner. These are not one generic failure. They locate vacancy in guide, perception, locomotor medium, memory, ownership, collective cohesion, or destination. The request for guidance becomes exact because it asks for one relation whose failure can occur through many distinct mechanisms.

Support can likewise be divided. A weak person may walk between two helpers while swaying; a calmer gait avoids the rush of defeat; upright stature supplies a bodily axis; pain, heaviness, leg disease, or imbalance can arrest movement. The helpers bear weight, while a guide or sign supplies direction; the person's own body still performs movement. On a larger scale, a chief of sailors, a forward part, crew, carried water, vessel, and continuous route occupy different roles. A leader cannot replace sustenance, and sustenance cannot replace direction. Guidance and help may cooperate without becoming the same operation.

The water-lifting formation gives dependence a complete material sequence. A deep container holds water; an ostrich-named crossbeam spans the well; a standing pulley or wooden component transmits force; an operator raises the water; a strengthened skin carries it; a traveler or group later uses it. Existing resource still requires access, apparatus, controller, carrier, and continued function. If the mechanism is absent, water remains inaccessible; if the carrier leaks, transport fails; if the water freezes, material persists while usability changes. Need can therefore reside in resource, mechanism, controller, medium, or accessibility. Help answers different vacancies differently.

Atmospheric water, well water, and travel water can be arranged as a longer transformation: descent and gathering, lifting, containment, lateral carrying, and sustained passage. The source events do not establish one numerically continuous water token. Their interaction matters because the controller and direction change at each handoff. What comes from above becomes stored below; what is stored becomes raised; what is raised becomes portable; what is carried becomes route capacity. This material sequence embodies the same larger movement as the grammar: a dependent relation can be enabled to stand and proceed without surrendering its own participant role.

## The path acquires a favored history

The complete final line is `صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ`. At its boundary with line 6, `صِرَٰط` recurs under changed grammar. Line 6 has the definite qualified object `ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ`; line 7 begins with bare construct `صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ`. Article, attachment, and specification type change. Straightness first answers what condition the path bears. The construct then answers which participant history specifies it. Quality is not replaced by people; it is joined by a social and temporal axis.

`ٱلَّذِينَ` opens a plural class, and its relative clause `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ` gives that class a completed relation. `أَنْعَمْتَ` is perfect measure IV with an expressed second-person singular controller; `عَلَيْهِمْ` supplies the plural endpoint. Its exact actuality is `الإنعام إيصال الإحسان إلى الغير`, the conveying of good to another. The class is therefore not simply called good. Its members bear the history of a completed positive transfer from the very addressee who is now being asked to guide.

That history makes them a precedent, not an identity for `نا`. A guide, first rank, leading animal, or exemplary manner can orient followers, but the requesters are not said to be the same occupants as the favored class. The perfect event establishes what the controller has done for others; the imperative asks what that controller should do in a new relation. Precedent gives the requested path a received history while leaving the current request open.

The favor event also completes several opening capacities. Mercy had supplied compassionate good toward a receiver. Lordship had supplied nurture of `النعمة`, need, support, and completion. Praise had supplied evaluation of good delivered. Here the same referent appears as expressed giver. The titles have acquired an event without becoming reducible to it: compassionate disposition, sustained care, conveyed benefit, and praise remain differentiated phases around one passage-level source.

The internal form of `أَنْعَمْتَ` makes that positive transfer unusually dense. The word retains `أَنْعَمْ`, the controller suffix `تَ`, and the contiguous `نعم`. Its source branches include benefaction, saying yes, praise opposed to blame, affirmation opposed to `لا`, ease or softness, eye-contentment, and addition or intensification. The surface perfect and its syntax select benefaction. The other operations give its carrier a positive counterfield of response, evaluation, refinement, bodily ease, and satisfactory result.

One source assertion contacts the entire vocalized form after decoration is removed: `دققت دواء فأنعمت دقه أي بالغت وزدت`, grinding a medicine and making its grinding finer through added effort. This exact contact joins favor to preparation without changing its meaning. The Lordship field supplies medicine improved with honey or concentrate; the worship-root field supplies a `صلاءة الطيب`, a perfume-grinding slab; the guidance field supplies gift and carrier relations. Projected together, raw material is placed on a working surface, refined by repeated action, made fit for use, carried, and given. Preparation, benefit, transmission, reception, and later reputation are distinct stages. Favor is not medicine or perfume, but the assembly prevents benefaction from being imagined as contentless transfer: a good may have to be produced, refined, borne, and only then received.

The same root connects favor with the Day through `نعم فلان في اليوم إذا نزل`, praise of a person when the great event descends or occurs. The passage positions `يَوْمِ` in line 4 and encloses `نعم` in line 7. The source event supplies evaluator, performer, difficult occasion, and praise after performance. It does not turn Judgment Day into that event or the favored class into its praised performer. It creates a measured positive circuit alongside the account field: a Day can expose consequence and evaluation, while favor can become content for praise.

Transfer branches specify the direction further. A gentle gift moves good to a loved receiver; an offering travels toward an appointed destination; debt holds value for return; ownership or decision can change hands; covenant binds parties; compensation occupies the place of retaliation; a substitute fills a prior position. Reception may be voluntary, requested, prescribed, delegated, or compelled. `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ` fills only some of these parameters: expressed giver, other-directed good, plural receiver, and completed event. The surrounding transfer field makes clear why favor differs from account, exchange, refuge, captivity, and replacement even when all involve a change in relation.

## One directional frame carries opposed outcomes

Line 7 repeats `عَلَيْهِمْ` at words 4 and 7, but the two occurrences neither identify the same class nor transmit the same content. The first completes `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ`: an expressed second-person controller conveys good to the plural relative class. The second completes `ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ`: a passive participle presents another plural class as affected, while the initiator of anger is not expressed. Preposition and plural suffix remain stable; occupant, voice, controller distribution, polarity, and consequence change.

The complete form `عليه` occurs in several local relations: mercy toward an affected receiver, anger directed toward an endpoint, an affair resting on what supports it, and a gift resting upon the plate that carries it. These do not share one payload. They make the prepositional form a directed-relation socket whose value depends on controller, transmitted force or content, receiver, medium, and result. The passage fills it once with benefaction and once with passive adversity. Direction alone therefore cannot decide whether what arrives supports or harms.

The contrast is also a contrast of voice. `أَنْعَمْتَ` is a perfect active verb with an expressed second-person controller. `ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ` is a passive participle whose affected class is visible but whose anger source is vacant. Inside the passive carrier, decoration-free form contact exposes `غضوب`, an active disposition describing one who is much or quickly angry, along with source-local descriptions of a surly person or animal and a great serpent. Surface passive affectedness, embedded active disposition, and the general relation of incurring anger remain distinct. No embedded bearer is entitled to fill the unexpressed initiator.

The wider anger field makes this discipline necessary. `غضب عليه` directs intensified displeasure or vengeance toward an endpoint. `غضبت لفلان` and `غضبت به` change preposition according to whether the represented person is living or dead. Reciprocal `غاضبه` introduces opposed controllers. Hard rock, thick or folded skin, severe redness, swelling around the eye, a surly camel or woman, and a great serpent supply material, bodily, and dispositional states. The actual clause selects the passive `على` relation. The other events expose parameters - direction, reciprocity, resistance, body state, and disposition - while leaving the actual source of anger unexpressed.

Conflict mechanisms add more possible controller geometries. Rivals may try to match one another; opponents can stand in reciprocal contest; a protector can orient simultaneously toward family and threat; a resistant animal can oppose a human controller; a hunter, tool, quick observer, visible quarry, and possible escape can form an asymmetric pursuit. These scenes do not become the history of the closing class. They distinguish adversity that arrives from outside, resistance between active parties, protection of a third participant, and escape that becomes loss. The passive class remains defined by affectedness rather than by an imported conflict narrative.

The same distinction can be embodied materially. Hard rock and layered hide resist penetration; a cutting edge may breach them; a swelling eye impairs perception; bodily pain can arrest movement. Favor can instead soften, nourish, repair, or sustain. Support and adversity can both be directed toward a receiver, yet they produce contrary capacities: one enables standing or passage; the other obstructs, burdens, wounds, or overwhelms. The repeated `عَلَيْهِمْ` concentrates that difference in one unchanged surface frame.

## Straying breaks the route relation from within

The final class is `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ`, an active participle in the masculine plural. Its voice differs decisively from the passive `ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ`. The anger-affected class is presented as bearing an externally structured relation whose initiator is hidden. The straying class actively bears a state of deviation. Coordination places the classes under one excluded scope, but morphology prevents them from becoming one kind of failure.

The primary `ض ل ل` relation is deviation from aim, guidance, or the road: leaving a goal, failing to find the way, or falling into wandering. Other branches separate several mechanisms that can produce a lost relation. A thing can disappear into another medium, as material vanishes in water; a dead body can be buried until hidden; an object or camel can leave its owner; a person can fail to locate a house, mosque, or place; a content can disappear from memory, or memory from its bearer; an animal can remain where its owner is unknown. These are complete source events, not synonyms.

Their common structure is a broken pairing. Traveler and route no longer align; seeker and place do not meet; owner and possession separate; observer and object lose visibility; memory and content fail to coincide; animal and owner lack identification. The missing role changes in each event. Sometimes the path exists and is not known; sometimes the object persists but is hidden; sometimes movement carries it away; sometimes the relevant content is no longer retained; sometimes the owner relation itself cannot be recovered. Active straying at the close thus has a far more precise countergeometry than undifferentiated badness.

That geometry reaches backward to guidance. Naming made an endpoint identifiable; a road mark can make a direction inferable; guidance can bring route content into knowledge. Straying reverses those successful pairings by severing aim, sign, observer, memory, owner, route, or destination. An external guide may compensate for absent sight; a marker may reintroduce information; a support may enable gait. None guarantees continued relation if the participant departs from the aim or if another indispensable role remains vacant. The close therefore tests the requested guidance against failures of recognition, retention, movement, and attachment.

It also reaches into collective life. A group can scatter in every direction and lose its common word or power; roads themselves can branch apart; a herd may lose its leader; a vessel may lose forward control; a traveler may lose the medium that carries him. These project different scales of disorientation without making `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ` a tribe, herd, crew, or dispersed road network. The active participle remains the class's own route-state, while the surrounding mechanisms disclose how individual and collective alignment can fail.

The closing contrast consequently has three causal geometries rather than one moralized polarity. `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ` has expressed giver, completed positive transfer, and plural receivers. `ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ` has a passive affected class and an unexpressed initiator. `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ` has active bearers of a broken relation to aim or way. Benefaction, externally structured adversity, and internally borne deviation specify three different histories around the path.

## Otherness and negation build one boundary differently

`غَيْرِ` and `لَا` cooperate in excluding the two closing classes, but they do different grammatical work. `غَيْرِ` is a genitive noun that qualifies `ٱلَّذِينَ` and takes `ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ` as its complement. It introduces otherness and exclusion through a nominal relation. `لَا` is an explicit negative particle coordinated with `وَ` before `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ`. One boundary is built through an attached category of nonidentity; the other through exposed negation.

The `غ ي ر` field confirms that distinction. `غير` can mean what is other than or contrary to something, function as noun, adjective, or exception, and approach the scope of `لا` without sharing its form. Other branches describe changing a state, substituting one thing for another, exchanging goods, replacing retaliation with compensation, repairing a mount, supplying provisions, watering people or land, and protective concern for family. These operations remain separate from the line's nominal exclusion. They show that difference can concern identity, image, occupant, condition, value, or benefit. The passage chooses class differentiation: this is the path of the favored, other than the anger-affected, and not the straying.

The line also arranges a formal positive-to-negative sequence. The exact `نعم` is enclosed in `أَنْعَمْتَ` at word 3; `غَيْرِ` appears at word 5; exposed `وَلَا` appears at word 8. In its own source events, `نعم` can be affirmative response opposed to `لا` or praise opposed to blame. On the surface it is only an inner written form within a benefaction verb, not a spoken answer. The sequence nevertheless moves from positive transfer containing an affirmative form, through otherness, to explicit negation. Form and grammar cooperate without inventing a dialogue.

Transfer and substitution clarify the boundary's consequence. A position can change occupants; a due can be converted to compensation; a contract can bind a new relation; an affair can be repaired; a damaged segment can receive a replacement. Such changes preserve something while altering something else. The path repetition does exactly that at the level of specification: `صِرَٰط` is preserved, while definiteness, complement, and participant relation change. The closing negatives then delimit which histories can occupy that specification. Difference is not empty refusal; it is controlled participant selection around a stable route carrier.

## The passage's form performs the exchange

The seven lines contain 29 words, 48 morphemes, and 23 registered syntactic edges. Their word-count vector is `[4, 4, 2, 3, 4, 3, 9]`; the morpheme vector is `[7, 7, 4, 4, 5, 6, 15]`; the edge vector is `[4, 4, 1, 2, 3, 3, 6]`. The form first establishes compact governed carriers, introduces verbal exchange at the center, and expands sharply when the path must be specified by a favored history and bounded against two differently formed classes. The last line's size is the structural cost of expressing relative class, perfect benefit, repeated directional frame, nominal exclusion, passive affectedness, coordination, negation, and active deviation.

The first four lines contain no finite verbs. They name, predicate, qualify, and title through prepositions, constructs, adjectives, apposition, and active participle. The first two share the same 4-word, 7-morpheme, 4-edge dimensions and place God in second position under changed grammar. Line 3 releases the exact mercy suffix of line 1 as a whole line. Line 4 compresses authority, event-time, and account into three words and two nested edges. The opening is therefore not static in form: it moves by changing how nominal carriers depend on one another before it introduces personal action.

Line 5 initiates that action with two imperfect first-person-plural verbs, `نَعْبُدُ` and `نَسْتَعِينُ`. Repeated `إِيَّاكَ` at words 1 and 3 gives each its own fronted object. Line 6 changes to the imperative `ٱهْدِ`, whose second-person controller is paired with first-person object `نَا`; two definite accusative carriers then name and qualify the route. Line 7 changes again to perfect `أَنْعَمْتَ`, an expressed second-person action completed toward a third-person class, followed by passive and active participles. The person-and-aspect succession is a controller history:

`1P acts toward 2MS -> 2MS is asked to act toward 1P -> 2MS has acted toward 3MP -> a passive carrier over a 3MP endpoint and an active MP carrier mark counterclasses`.

This history gives direction to the passage's conceptual movement. The endpoint of praise becomes object of worship and appeal; the object becomes prospective controller; the requesting speakers become intended receivers; a prior receiver class specifies the route; two other classes delimit it under different voices. No single participant is simply active or passive throughout. Roles rotate by operation.

Article distribution reinforces the hinge. The definite-prefix vector is `[2, 2, 2, 1, 0, 2, 2]`. Line 5 alone suspends article-framed description and works entirely through pronouns, verbs, and conjunction. Definite form returns in line 6 with the specified object `ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ`, then changes at the next boundary when bare `صِرَٰطَ` enters a construct with `ٱلَّذِينَ`. Person first replaces title framing; the path then receives definite quality and finally participant history.

Exact repetitions likewise change work rather than halting movement. `ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ` moves from adjectival suffix to autonomous line. `إِيَّاكَ` preserves its referent while taking a new predicate. `صِرَٰط` moves from definite qualified object to bare construct head. `عَلَيْهِمْ` preserves direction while changing class, voice, payload, and result. Repetition is therefore the passage's method for holding one parameter stable while transforming another.

The line endings weave two sound families: `-يم` in lines 1, 3, and 6, and `-ين` in lines 2, 4, 5, and 7. They do not mark semantic identity, but they carry returns and transitions. The repeated mercy line returns the first ending; the central sequence `ٱلدِّينِ / نَسْتَعِينُ / ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ` crosses the two families while its lexical relations move from account through dependence to steadiness; the last `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ` returns the dominant plural-like cadence after maximal differentiation. Sound binds the phases without erasing their grammatical differences.

The central `3-4-3` word frame deserves the last emphasis. Line 4's three nouns establish Master, Day, and account. Line 5's four words double the addressee around worship and help. Line 6's three words reverse the controller relation and specify the requested route. At the same time the morphemes rise `4-5-6`, because increasingly explicit relations require conjunction, pronoun suffix, and articles. Balance at the word level accompanies growth below it. The passage does not simply pivot at line 5; it builds a compact formal chamber in which authority becomes worshipped endpoint, requested helper becomes prospective guide, and `الدين` becomes articulable with `المستقيم`.

## The ending changes the beginning

Once the closing path relations have been heard, `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ` can no longer function as an isolated label. The `اسم` has identified a referent who will sustain every later role. The `إله` relation has been realized by `نَعْبُدُ`. The mercy pair has received the receiver-and-benefit geometry of `أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ`. Lordship has unfolded into nurture of favor, acknowledged need, support, guidance, and maintained standing. Mastery of the Day has acquired people, measure, due, consequence, and a possible relation to the differentiated classes. Every title remains what it was, but the ending supplies the participant structures through which it can act.

The return also changes what praise can contain. The favored class offers a completed good as possible evaluative content; the anger-affected and straying classes mark causally different counterrelations; the Day supplies a horizon of account; the straight path supplies a maintained standard. Praise at the opening can now be heard as directed toward a source whose goodness is not merely adjectival. It may be expressed as compassionate orientation, care over incomplete life, aid requested by an active dependent, gentle indication of a route, good conveyed to others, or authority holding consequence together. These are distinct operations gathered by one named endpoint.

The path, in turn, returns to the name through recognition. A name makes its bearer known; a sign distinguishes a way; guidance asks that a route become known; straying breaks relation to aim, place, owner, memory, or path. The passage begins with an identifiable endpoint, changes identification into direct relation, asks that the identified source make another relation knowable, and ends by excluding participants in whom direction is externally burdened or internally broken. Recognition is not the sole meaning of the passage, but it is a carried architecture linking first word to last class.

The exchange remains dynamically open. Worship is performed and help is requested; guidance is requested and a straight path is specified; prior favor is complete for another class. The current speakers' guided traversal and arrival are not narrated inside these seven lines. Nor is the initiator of the passive anger relation expressed. These vacancies preserve the difference between request and response, precedent and present possession, route knowledge and travel, affectedness and source. The passage completes its articulation of relations without pretending to complete every event those relations make possible.

Heard backward from `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ`, the opening name is therefore the first operation in a whole causal field. It names the sole worshipped endpoint, the requested helper, the prospective guide, and the prior benefactor; its epithets distribute mercy and care; its titles hold collective scope and account. The middle changes those descriptions into reciprocal directions, and the close gives the requested path quality, history, and boundaries. `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ` now opens not only by naming God, but by making present the source from whom worship, dependence, guidance, favor, consequence, and the differentiation of ways will all take their relational form.
