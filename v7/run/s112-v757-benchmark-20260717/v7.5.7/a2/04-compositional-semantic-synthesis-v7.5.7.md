# From `قُلْ` to `أَحَدٌۢ` and Back

The passage begins by assigning speech before it names its subject:

```text
قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ
لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ
وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ
```

`قُلْ` is an imperative addressed to a grammatically encoded second-person
masculine singular controller. Surah 112 supplies the directive source, and
the Prophet is the commanded declarer. The syntax gives the command one exact
quoted complement, `هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`; the later lines continue the
passage's declarations about the same God, but they are not silently inserted
into that first syntactic payload. The audience remains unstated. Thus the
opening has a source, an utterer, an operation, and a complete content without
inventing a hearer.

## The Utterance Brought Out

The lexical relation of `قُلْ` makes declaration a concrete transition. The
source describes `القول` as `المركب من الحروف المبرز بالنطق`: composed
letters are brought out through articulation. `المقول اللسان` names the
tongue as the instrument. The Prophet therefore does not receive an abstract
instruction merely to hold a proposition; the command moves the exact
four-word carrier from content to performed saying. The complete source
occurrence `قل هو الله أحد` meets the complete first line, so command, pronoun,
name, and predicate enter articulation together and in their given order.

That outward transition becomes more exact beside `في نفسي قول لم أظهره`.
A conceived content is already `قول` while it remains in the self, and `لم
أظهره` explicitly withholds its manifestation. The commanded quote occupies
the opposite realization: its particular content is bound to the outgoing
side of speech. A third realization, `امتلأ الحوض وقال قطني`, lets the state
of a full basin indicate “enough” without a vocal organ. Articulation and
indication can therefore produce operative content by different media; here
the imperative and the equation of the tongue fix the medium as voiced
declaration.

The wider saying-field differentiates what a saying can do after or apart
from that emergence. `رجل قولة وقوال كثير القول` places abundance on a human
bearer, while the Yemeni `القيل`, `الذي له قول أي ينفذ قوله`, joins a ranked
ruler to a saying that takes effect. `اقتال قولا أي اجتر إلى نفسه قولا من
خير أو شر` directs a good or bad saying inward; `انتشرت له قالة حسنة أو
قبيحة في الناس` sends a report outward through a population; `قاولته في أمره
وتقاولنا` gives saying a reciprocal route around an affair. In `اقتَال عليه
تحكم`, the direction becomes control over another. In the interrogative use
of `تقول`, and in the Banū Sulaym use of inflected `قلت`, saying takes the
grammatical work of `تظن`. Belief in another's `قول`, sincere care in `فلان
يقول بكذا`, and the defining use `قول الجوهر ... وقول العرض ... أي حدهما`
give content, concern, and delimitation different results. These roles do not
become attributes of the Prophet merely because he fills one saying-position;
rather, their different bearers, routes, and outcomes specify the realized
primary act as one exact bearer bringing one exact content outward.

False attribution sharpens that exactness. `تقول باطلا أي قال ما لم يكن`,
`قولتني ما لم أقل`, and `تقول عليه أي كذب عليه` distinguish actual utterer,
alleged utterer, unspoken or unoccurred content, attribution agent, and target.
The passage later contains the ordered form `لم يكن`, and its opening `قل`
also occurs inside the supplied `أقل` and `قلت`; those exact contacts keep the
scope distinction audible. The Prophet's quoted complement is explicitly
`هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`, whereas the later `وَلَمْ يَكُن` heads the final
no-comparable clause. The latter is a direct denial of a relation, not content
smuggled into the earlier quotation or assigned to a false speaker.

Even the material homonym `القال الخشبة التي تضرب بها القلة` contributes a
precise contrast in operation. It supplies an agent, a wooden instrument, a
blow, and an affected object; it does not carry speech as its material. When a
second stick-mediated blow appears under `صمده بالعصا ... ضربه بها`, the two
complete impact patterns align while `القال` remains different from `العصا`
and `القلة` remains different from the suffixed target. The opening word can
therefore support both articulated force and, through a distinct exact form,
material force without confusing utterance with impact. The command's force
is fixed by its own syntax: it produces a saying.

## The Named One

Inside the quote, `هُوَ` occupies the first position and can take `ٱللَّهُ`
as its apposition; `ٱللَّهُ` then heads the nominative predication whose
indefinite nominative predicate is `أَحَدٌ`. The exact ordered pair `هو الله`
also occurs in `اسم الله الأكبر هو الله`, but the source equation and the
surface apposition retain their different grammatical work. It is the
passage's local syntax, together with its direct subject, that binds the
pronoun and name to God.

The name itself carries a completed prior relation. The source begins with
worship: `تأله = تعبد`, `مألوه = معبود`, and `لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون
معبودا`. Worshipper, act, worshipped endpoint, and resulting qualification
remain distinct. The narrower assertion `فالإله الله تعالى لأنه معبود`
selects the named God-instance from the generic field, while the sun, idols,
and other things made objects of worship retain their own local occupants.
The same selected entity passes from the worshipped state into the qualified
`إله` position. Then a declared form operation follows: `الله قيل أصله إله
فحذفت همزته وأدخل عليها الألف واللام فخص بالباري تعالى`. Base `إله`, deleted
hamza, added `الألف واللام`, specialized result `الله`, and surface
`ٱللَّهُ` all remain visible. The passage's name is therefore not a loose
root resemblance; it is the exact output of a supplied qualification and
naming sequence.

The same name can bear an oath or an address in `الله ما فعلت = والله ما
فعلته`, `لاه أنت = لله أنت`, and `اللهم = يا ألله`; `لا هم اغفر لنا` and
`يا ألله اغفر لي` retain speaker, addressee, negated deed, and requested
forgiveness. In the passage, however, the name is first the head of a
predication inside a commanded declaration. Those other uses make the
speaker/addressee axis of the name explicit without replacing this nominal
syntax.

At the quote's close, the exact source phrase supplies the decisive lexical
lock: `أحد بمعنى الواحد ... قل هو الله أحد ... يستعمل مطلقا وصفا في وصف
الله تعالى`. The surface `أَحَدٌ` is accordingly an absolute description
predicated of God. The numerical statement that `الواحد` is first of number
remains present, but the passage has not placed God as the first member of a
counted series. The form's other complete operations show why this positive
predicate cannot be reduced to a thin numeral. `الواحد المضموم إلى العشرات`
forms such expressions as `أحد عشر`; `فأحدهن أي صيرهن أحد عشر` carries the
same feminine-plural set through a transitive change into an eleven-count
state; `إحدى عشرة` preserves the feminine form. Additive placement can mean
first, as in `أحدكما`, and `يوم الأحد أي يوم الأول` gives firstness a day
name. `ما استأحدت بهذا الأمر أي ما انفردت به` makes the operation solitary,
while `جاءوا آحاد أحاد` distributes plural arrivals one by one. Exact `أحد`
can also name `جبل بالمدينة`. Number, firstness, transformation, solitude,
distribution, and proper naming are fully different realizations. Their
co-presence makes the surface binding decisive: here `أَحَدٌ` predicates
God's absolute oneness.

The source also gives `أحد أحد` as adjacent emphasis. The passage gives a
different arrangement: `أَحَدٌ` closes the first line and `أَحَدٌۢ` returns
only at the end of the fourth. Exact form and order survive, but the
intervening lines change adjacency into a passage-wide return. That return
begins to act already at the first close, because the saying-field supplies
the explicit term `حد`. In `قول الجوهر ... وقول العرض ... أي حدهما`, the full
`حدهما` releases base `حد` while retaining its dual suffix and its source
definition. Those exact ordered letters occur inside both complete `أحد`
forms. The containing words keep their initial hamza, full spelling,
inflection, and one-relations; `أحد` is not equated with `حد`. Formally,
however, the commanded saying reaches a word that contains the supplied term
for a saying's delimitation. Its first syntactic payload closes there, and the
whole passage will close by returning the same contained term.

## The Direction of the Refuge

The name that stood at the third of four positions inside the quote returns
exactly as the first of two positions in `ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ`. Here exact name
recurrence is joined by contextual identity: the God declared one is the same
God named the self-sufficient refuge. The participant continues across the
line boundary, while the predicates remain distinct. Absolute oneness does
not become `ٱلصَّمَدُ`, and the refuge predicate is not transported backward
as the grammar of `أَحَدٌ`.

The directional assertion of `ٱلصَّمَدُ` gives “refuge” its internal roles:
`الصمد السيد الذي يصمد إليه في الأمر`, and `صمده قصد معتمدا عليه قصده`.
There is a seeker or orienting initiator, a need or affair, an intentional
route, reliance established along that route, and the master as intended
endpoint. Surface predication and the direct description of God as
self-sufficient refuge fill the endpoint with God. No phrase supplies a
particular seeker or need, so those incoming positions remain open but
exactly directed. The house-scale `بيت مصمد أي مقصود` preserves the same
direction with a house as its own local endpoint; it neither turns the refuge
into a house nor supplies a seeker to the passage.

The exact form `أمر` helps keep this openness articulate. It is negotiated in
`قاولته في أمره`, intended and relied upon in `صمدت صمد هذا الأمر`, watched
with concern in `إني على صمادة من أمر`, made the object of solitary action in
`ما استأحدت بهذا الأمر`, and named as an occurring affair in `الكائنة الأمر
الحادث`. Each occurrence changes controller, preposition, direction, and
result. A generic affair does not migrate through them as one participant;
consequently the need directed toward the refuge is genuinely typed without
being filled by a nearby lexical scene.

Other `صمد` relations alter different dimensions of the same primary
predicate. `المصمت الذي لا جوف له`, the fixed rock, hard place, elevated thick
site, ground without furrows, and severe earth supply material bearer,
density, elevation, and an absent cavity. Applied through the predicate, this
is a source topology of self-sufficiency, not an assertion that God is one of
those material occupants. Its exact `له` relation will meet the final
`لَّهُۥ` around the same God: an absent interior item in the nonhollow image
will stand beside an absent exterior counterpart in the closing proposition,
while cavity and comparator remain categorically different.

Two uses of exact `الصماد` make that interior/exterior distinction tangible.
`الصماد عفاص القارورة` and `الصماد سداد القارورة` place a stopper at a
bottle's aperture and produce a closed vessel; `صمدتها/أصمدها` retains the
feminine object through the placement. In `صمد رأسه تصميدا`, a rag,
handkerchief, or cloth wraps the exterior of a head, expressly `ما خلا
العمامة`, and the wrapping is also called `الصماد`. Bottle, opening, stopper,
head, cloth, and result all stay local. Together they distinguish closing an
interior opening from covering an exterior boundary, a distinction the
passage later replays around God as center without importing either object.

Direction itself also changes payload without losing its vector. In `صمده
قصد معتمدا عليه`, the suffixed object is the intended and relied-on endpoint;
in `صمده بالعصا صمدا إذا ضربه بها`, the same exact full form places its
object at the end of a blow carried through a stick. Intention/reliance and
impact are separate operations, just as `العصا` and the qawl-field's `القال`
are separate instruments. The common outward agent-to-object topology makes
clear that direction does not by itself determine whether the transmitted
relation is reliance or force. The primary `ٱلصَّمَدُ` receives the first
direction through the refuge relation; no surface event makes God the struck
object of the second.

`إني على صمادة من أمر إذا أشرف عليه وحفلت به` adds oversight and sustained
concern toward an affair. `الصمد الدائم` adds duration: the lasting bearer
remains after the passing of its creatures, while the `ناقة مصماد` remains
through cold and barrenness and continues its milk-flow. Observer, affair,
adverse conditions, vanished others, retained existence, and continuing
output remain differentiated. In the passage they change the internal
composition of self-sufficiency: destination, density, closure, attention,
force, and persistence are distinct axes gathered by one predicate, not a
single hybrid material scene.

## Lineage Turned Through Voice

The passage then changes from two affirmative nominal predications to renewed
verbal negation: `لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ`. The first `لَمْ` governs an
active imperfect jussive, 3MS; after the conjunction, the second `لَمْ`
independently governs a passive imperfect jussive, also 3MS. The direct
subject makes the covert controller in both clauses the same God, but voice
puts that continuous participant at opposite semantic ends.

In `يَلِدْ`, God is grammatical controller and active semantic initiator.
The birth-field gives the full operation: `الولادة فهو وضع الوالدة ولدها`.
A parental source, specifically a mother in this realization, places a child
as output; `أولدت` marks birth becoming due, a pregnant `شاة والد` retains
the incoming state, and `ولدناها` introduces attendants who undertake her
delivery. Under `لَمْ`, that operation yields no child. The suppressed output
is structured by the lexical relation but not populated by one of its local
examples.

In `يُولَدْ`, the same God is again the grammatical controller but now
occupies the affected or born position; the semantic begetter is suppressed.
Exact unvocalized `يولد` meets `الوليد الصبي حين يولد`, while `يوم ولدت؛ يوم
ولد` supplies an explicit active/passive turn. The surface vocalization and
passive morphology retain their own force: the event is denied, so no source
and no born-state emerge. Moving from the active to the passive clause turns
the role map, not an occupant. The absent child from the first clause does not
become the controller of the second.

The relations surrounding birth make both denied directions concrete. `الولد
= المولود` can designate one or many, male or female, son or daughter;
`الوالد = الأب` and `الوالدة = الأم`, together `الوالدان`, separate the two
parent roles. `الوليد/الوليدة` varies proximity to birth independently from
social status: a mature bondwoman can still be called `وليدة`. None of those
number, gender, age, or status classes fills a negated participant. The exact
day contact between `يوم ولدت`, `يوم ولد`, and `يوم الأحد` likewise preserves
three local temporal relations rather than making them one day.

`تولد الشيء من الشيء حصوله عنه بسبب` widens the topology from biological
delivery to source plus cause producing a result. Generated Arabic or a
`رجل مولد` can be non-pure; `المولد من الكلام` is newly introduced speech;
`كتاب مولد` is fabricated, and `بينة مولدة` is not verified. These outcome
parameters stay separate. Exact `الكلام` meets the articulated content of the
opening, so performed declaration and generated language can be compared as
two output operations, but no identity makes the Prophet's quote newly
coined, impure, fabricated, or unverified.

Positive production elsewhere sharpens what the two negations prevent. A
year's `كفأة` can be palm fruit or camel offspring; `أعطاني لبنها ووبرها
وأولادها سنة` yields milk, wool, and the herd's young over a bounded year.
Recovering `أولاد` from `أولادها` lets the same local output enter the plural
child class defined by `جمع الولد أولاد`. `أكفأت إبلي كفأتين` divides the
herd into two alternating production groups. These performed local yields do
not enter the passage's denied lineage, but they expose source, interval,
production, result, and phase as separate dimensions of an output relation.
Against them, `لَمْ يَلِدْ` denies outward product from God and `وَلَمْ
يُولَدْ` denies an inward source producing God.

The birth-field also produces a tightly measured counterpart: `لدة الرجل
تربه`, with `اللدة` explicitly recovered from `ولدة` after loss of waw. Two
are `لدان`; the relation is likeness in age or birth-time, not equivalence in
every domain. This completed peer relation will meet the broader comparison
field of the final line by preserving its two places and narrowing its
parameter to age. It supplies no person to the surface comparison.

## A Relation That Does Not Occur

The third renewed negative frame is `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ`.
Its small grammatical pieces perform a major role rotation. `يَكُن` is the
imperfect jussive copular head under `لَمْ`. `لَّ + هُۥ` represents God as
the prepositional endpoint of comparison; `كُفُوًا`, whose supplied base is
`كُفُو`, is the accusative predicate; delayed nominative `أَحَدٌۢ` is the
grammatical subject and controller. Continuous reference to God therefore
survives precisely when grammatical control passes to “anyone.”

The occurrence relation of `كان الشيء يكون كونا إذا وقع وحضر` specifies what
is denied: not God's occurrence, but the occurrence of a candidate in the
comparable state relative to God. The source causal chain `كونه فتكون أحدثه
فحدث` carries one affected thing from causation into occurrence, but the
surface negation produces no such participant or state. Other `كون`
relations vary the result without seizing the surface subject. `المكان`
derives from `كان يكون`, while `المكانة = المنزلة` turns presence into site or
rank relative to another. `كنت على فلان ... أي تكفلت به` makes standing over
another a guarantee and supported state. `الاستكانة = الخضوع` supplies
submission with its own unstated endpoint, and `بكِينة سوء = بحال سوء`
supplies a bad condition through a form derived from `الكون`. The final clause
selects none of those occupants; it uses occurrence to deny one exact
predicate relation.

The aged `كُنْتِيّ` makes the return from occurrence to speech locally
explicit. `يقال للرجل إذا شاخ كُنْتِيّ؛ كأنه نسب إلى قوله كُنْتُ في شبابي
كذا وكذا`: the same man reaches old age, reports an earlier state in the form
`كُنْتُ`, and receives a label derived from his own utterance. Speaker,
remembered youth, past state, uttered form, derived name, and aged bearer form
an identity-preserving loop. Surface `قُلْ` and `يَكُن` touch its two lexical
fields without making the Prophet that aged speaker or the final copula his
past-tense report. The local loop nevertheless clarifies a larger possibility
of arranged speech: a produced form can return to redescribe an earlier
participant when identity is explicitly sustained. The passage will perform
its own distinct return through repeated name, repeated `أحد`, and the same
God.

The predicate `كُفُوًا` opens a two-place relation. Its source equations are
exact: `الكفء المثل`, `التكافؤ التساوي`, `الكفئ النظير`, and `هذا كفء له أي
مثله`. A candidate, an endpoint, a comparison parameter, and an attained
likeness state must all remain distinct. Surface word order places `لَّهُۥ`
before `كُفُوًا`, while the source says `كفء له`; the reversal brings the God
endpoint forward without making it controller.

The comparison field demonstrates how much a parameter matters. A person can
be `كفء` to another in lineage or standing, property, war, marriage, or
combat. `كافأت الرجل أي فعلت به مثل ما فعل بي` transports the pattern of a
prior act into a returned like act while rotating actor and recipient;
`المكافأة مجازاة النعم` gives benefaction a reciprocal result. Two things can
counter the sun from the eyes, and `كافأ الرجل بين فارسين برمحه` places two
horsemen in a matched or opposed relation through a spear. The age-peer
`لدة/ترب` supplies another two-person relation whose parameter is only shared
age. Each scene fills two places under its own measure. The surface supplies
no one of those measures and denies occupation across the candidate class.

The same lexical field also changes equality rather than attaining it.
`أكفأت الشيء إذا أملته` tilts an object; a bowl or vessel is tipped over;
people are diverted from one destination to another; a woman sways in her
walk; a face becomes downcast or changes color. `الإكفاء قلب الشيء كأنه
إزالة المساواة` explicitly presents inversion as removal of equality.
Grammatical negation in the passage is not a physical overturning, yet the
declared aftermath makes absence of the equal state perceptible as a changed
relation rather than an unspecified void. Vessel, group, body, face, and
orientation remain where their own operations put them.

Two further pairings give the relation material and temporal embodiments.
`الكفاء شقة أو ثنتان` places one or two pieces at the rear of a tent or house;
in `شقتان تنصح إحداهما بالأخرى`, one feminine piece is sewn to the other and
the joined material becomes a covering. Full `إحداهما` retains feminine base
`إحدى` and dual suffix `ـهما`, and its contact with `إحدى عشرة` carries form,
not the cloth or counted set. The annual `كفأة` places palm crop, camel young,
milk, wool, requester, owner, one-year measure, completeness, and alternating
groups into a production relation. A seam and a production phase are thus two
different ways of relating parts; neither becomes comparability to God.

`الإكفاء في الشعر` brings ordered endings into the same field: one ending can
be raised and another lowered, consonants can differ, or the inflection of the
line endings can change. Its complete topology of boundary parameters meets
the passage's four line closes. It does not name the passage as that poetic
condition and does not import its `ميم` and `نون`; it lets retained consonant
and changed grammar be inspected together. That structural consequence
becomes visible once the final candidate is fixed.

The candidate `أَحَدٌۢ` receives its scope from `أحد في النفي لاستغراق جنس
الناطقين ولا واحد ولا اثنان فصاعدا`. The house examples `لا أحد في الدار`
and `ما في الدار أحد` show that position can change while the absent class
remains exhaustive; `فما منكم من أحد عنه حاجزين` retains its population,
endpoint, and barrier role under the same scope. In the passage, “anyone” is
therefore not a particular missing counterpart. One, two, and upward are
ranged through the eligible class, and no member comes to occupy the
`كُفُوًا` relation to God. The final clause is complete precisely as a denied
relation: candidate class, predicate, endpoint, controller, and polarity are
all explicit, while no comparator or equality state is emitted.

This exterior absence returns to the earlier interior one around the same
God. `لا جوف له` had placed an absent cavity inside the nonhollow `صمد`
topology; `لَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ` places an absent counterpart
outside the God endpoint. Contextual identity carries God from refuge bearer
to comparison endpoint. It does not carry `جوف` into `كُفُوًا`, or material
hardness into equality. Self-sufficiency becomes internally and externally
articulate through two different absences centered on one participant.

## The Four Boundaries

The arranged utterance has four lines and six clause-level movements: the
imperative frame; its nominal quote; the second nominal predication; active
birth negated; passive birth separately negated; and the final copular
relation separately negated. Its line openings move `قُلْ -> ٱللَّهُ ->
لَمْ -> وَلَمْ`. Its line endings move `أَحَدٌ -> ٱلصَّمَدُ -> يُولَدْ ->
أَحَدٌۢ`. The opening command yields to a repeated name, then to bare
negation, then to coordinated renewed negation. The endings preserve one
consonant while changing nearly every grammatical parameter.

All four lines close on `د`. Their ordered roots are `ء ح د / ص م د / و ل د
/ ء ح د`. The first boundary is an indefinite nominative affirmative
predicate; the second a definite nominative predicate; the third a passive
imperfect jussive verb; the fourth an indefinite nominative delayed subject
under a negated copula. The line-ending relation from `الإكفاء في الشعر`
makes the parameter map exact: consonant remains `د`, while lexical category,
definiteness, case or mood, voice, polarity, syntax, and root change. The
sound does not flatten those relations. It carries their transformations to a
common boundary.

The outer pair is tighter. `أَحَدٌ` and `أَحَدٌۢ` share lemma, root, gender,
indefiniteness, nominative case, and closing position, while the last written
surface retains its additional recitational decoration. At the first close,
God controls the nominal predication and receives absolute oneness. At the
last, anyone controls the copula as delayed subject while God is only the
prepositional endpoint; universal negation prevents comparability. Exact form
returns, but participant and predicate do not travel with it. The first
positive `أحد` is not recruited as the missing second comparand of the last
line.

The same distinction governs the passage's smaller morphemes. The `وَ` before
the second and third negative clauses genuinely coordinates them; root-internal
waw in `واحد`, `قول`, or `ولد` does not. Each `لَمْ` renews its own
government rather than lending one undifferentiated negation to the remainder
of the passage. In `لَّهُۥ`, `لَّ` supplies the comparison endpoint and
`هُۥ` supplies the continuous God-referent; the many other source suffixes
`ـه` retain their own local antecedents. The determiner `ٱل` belongs overtly
to `ٱلصَّمَدُ`, while the addition of `الألف واللام` in the declared history
of `الله` remains a named form operation. Exact small-form recurrence keeps
position visible, but grammar determines what each occurrence carries.

The three `لَمْ` frames form a role sequence around one God without becoming
one event. First, God is controller at the source side of an outward birth
relation, and output is denied. Second, the same God is controller at the
affected side of an inward birth relation, and source and born-state are
denied. Third, God remains referential center but yields grammatical control
to the universally ranged candidate, and lateral comparability is denied.
The passage therefore draws a directional geometry around the self-sufficient
refuge:

```text
open need or affair -- قصد and اعتماد --> God as refuge
God -- begetting --> offspring                         denied
source -- begetting --> God as born participant       denied
anyone -- comparability --> God                       denied
```

The positive route and the three closed routes preserve different agents,
objects, and outcomes. No seeker becomes a child, no absent child becomes a
source, and no suppressed source becomes a comparator. The source image of no
interior cavity adds another axis around the same center while remaining a
material topology rather than a fourth primary event.

## The Opening Heard from the End

Read backward, the closing `أَحَدٌۢ` first names no individual at all. It is
the delayed controller whose scope ranges across anyone. Moving left reaches
`كُفُوًا`: likeness, equality, returned action, age-peer, opposed horsemen,
sewn pieces, annual phases, and removal of equality reopen as differently
parameterized two-place relations, but no one of their occupants enters this
one. Moving left again reaches `لَّهُۥ`, where God is recovered as endpoint.
That exact relation returns to the earlier `لا جوف له` around the same refuge
bearer and makes exterior non-comparability answer interior nonhollowness
without equating their missing terms.

At `يَكُن`, what has failed to occur is now exact: a candidate's comparable
state, not God. The exact source collision `قال ما لم يكن` retains its own
utterer and content, so the denial does not recast the opening declaration as
false. Passing into `يُولَدْ` puts the continuous God first on the affected
side of a denied lineage; moving farther left into `يَلِدْ` turns the same
participant to the active source side. Backward order makes voice maximally
audible: identity continues while semantic position reverses.

`ٱلصَّمَدُ` is then heard after all three closed relations. Its direction
remains positive: need can be aimed toward and rested upon God as the
self-sufficient refuge. Its open seeker and affair are not filled by anything
negation has failed to produce. Persistence, attention, boundary closure,
solid interior, and the distinction between reliance and impact give this
positive destination multiple exact dimensions without combining their
local bodies.

The repeated `ٱللَّهُ` carries the same participant back across the line
boundary into the first quote. The refuge bearer becomes again the named head
of `ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`, and the name reopens its supplied passage from worshipped
state through `إله` to specialized `الله`. The final universally negated
`أَحَدٌۢ` then changes back into first-line `أَحَدٌ`, an affirmative predicate
of God. The outer recurrence sends no candidate backward; it sends the
closing relational consequence backward. Absolute oneness is heard after
both directions of lineage and every comparable position have been denied.

Inside that first `أَحَدٌ`, exact `حد` returns once more to the defining use
of saying. The passage has supplied its own primary realization of the
relation: the Prophet as declarer, God as the declared entity, absolute
oneness as delimiting content, and the complete quote as manifested result.
The two-letter containment does not define the one-word by subtraction; it
marks how the utterance's first boundary and the passage's final boundary
answer one another. The four closing `د` sounds carry the route between them:
oneness, refuge, denied begotten state, and denied candidate comparison.

Only then does the backward movement arrive at `هُوَ` and exit through
`قُلْ`. The opening imperative is newly audible because its result has
returned to it. Surah 112 commands the Prophet to bring out the exact saying
`هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`; the same God then occupies refuge, active source,
passive affected participant, and final comparison endpoint, while grammar
carefully changes who controls each relation. The passage's ending does not
merely stop the declaration. By closing again on `أَحَدٌۢ`, it sends the
absence of origin, product, and counterpart back into the affirmative
`أَحَدٌ`, and through that completed content back into the force of `قُلْ`.
