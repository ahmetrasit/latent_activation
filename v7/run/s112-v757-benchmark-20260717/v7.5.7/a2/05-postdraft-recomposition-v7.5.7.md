# The One Declared Through Changing Relations

The passage gives its subject through an action of speech and then keeps that
subject continuous while grammar repeatedly changes His position:

```text
قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ
لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ
وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ
```

Surah 112 supplies the directive source, and the Prophet is the second-person
masculine singular controller of `قُلْ`. Its exact quoted complement is
`هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`: the Prophet is to declare God and predicate absolute
oneness of Him. The same God then heads `ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ`, occupies the
active side of denied begetting and the affected side of denied begottenness,
and finally appears as `هُۥ`, the endpoint relative to whom anyone is denied
the state `كُفُوًا`. Continuous reference belongs to God; grammatical control
does not remain with Him in the final clause. That distinction lets the
passage move from positive declaration to three differently directed denials
without turning them into one event.

## `قُلْ`: A Complete Saying Is Brought Out

The opening word assigns an utterer before it names the declared entity.
`قُلْ` is outside its quotation and governs the complete payload after it;
the content does not control the command. The lexical assertion makes the
operation concrete: `القول` is `المركب من الحروف المبرز بالنطق`, a
composition of letters brought out through articulation, and `المقول
اللسان` names the tongue as instrument. The exact source occurrence `قل هو
الله أحد` meets the complete first line, so the Prophet brings out these four
ordered carriers rather than a paraphrase. The eventual audience remains
unstated.

Speech has a genuine incoming state. `المتصور في النفس قبل الإبراز باللفظ`
is already `قول`, while `في نفسي قول لم أظهره` keeps a conceived content
inside the self and explicitly prevents its manifestation. The imperative
places its exact quote at the other end of that transition: the content is
assigned to voiced output. Yet sound is not the only way a state can become
operative. In `امتلأ الحوض وقال قطني`, the full basin itself indicates its
limit or sufficiency. Basin, fullness, indicated “enough,” and perceiver stay
distinct; no tongue is required. The passage selects articulation for the
Prophet's declaration, while the basin scene establishes that a completed
state can also declare itself by what it has become.

The saying-relations then vary bearer, direction, and result. `رجل قولة وقوال
كثير القول`, together with `منطيق` and `لسن`, places abundance and fluency on
a human producer. The Yemeni `القيل`, a ruler below the greatest king, bears
a saying that takes effect in `الذي له قول أي ينفذ قوله`; singular `القيل`,
feminine `قيلة`, and the collective or plural forms retain their number and
gender. `اقتال قولا أي اجتر إلى نفسه قولا من خير أو شر` draws a saying
inward toward the self. `انتشرت له قالة حسنة أو قبيحة في الناس` sends a
report outward through a population and allows it to multiply. `قاولته في
أمره وتقاولنا` makes the route reciprocal around an affair, whereas `اقتَال
عليه تحكم` directs control toward another. The Prophet fills one exact
outgoing bearer-role; abundance, rulership, inward drawing, circulation,
negotiation, and control retain their own agents and contents.

Other uses alter what saying does. `تقول` can perform the grammatical work of
`تظن` in interrogation, and inflected `قلت` can do so outside interrogation
in the supplied Banū Sulaym construction. `فلان يقول بقول أبي حنيفة` places
a person in a belief-position; `فلان يقول بكذا` directs sincere care toward a
thing; `قول الجوهر ... وقول العرض ... أي حدهما` makes saying the delimiting
definition of what it names. The full basin makes a state indicate content.
These are not successive phases of the Prophet's quote. They make saying a
family of exactly differentiated operations, within which `قُلْ` realizes
articulation with a fixed declarer and content.

False saying fixes the boundary of that content. `تقول باطلا أي قال ما لم
يكن` gives an utterer content that did not occur; `قولتني ما لم أقل` and
`أقولتني ما لم أقل` distinguish an actual speaker from an alleged one;
`تقول عليه` gives lying a target. The passage later contains exact `لم يكن`,
and the shorter `قل` occurs inside supplied `أقل`, `قلت`, and `القلة`, but
form contact does not extend the quoted-complement edge. `هُوَ ٱللَّهُ
أَحَدٌ` remains the command's quotation, while `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا
أَحَدٌۢ` remains a later direct denial. The Prophet's utterance therefore has
an actual bearer and exact content even where the same letters enter scenes
of nonoccurrence or misattribution.

The material `القال الخشبة التي تضرب بها القلة` carries the qawl form into a
different complete event: agent, wooden instrument, blow, and affected
`القلة`. Later, `صمده بالعصا صمدا إذا ضربه بها` supplies the same
instrument-mediated impact shape with another stick and another target.
Neither instrument nor target crosses between the scenes. Their alignment
separates force from speech while making direction itself available as a
parameter: something may be carried outward as articulated content or as
physical impact, and the surface command fixes its own result as declaration.

## `هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`: Name, Predicate, and One

Within the quote, `هُوَ` occupies the first position. The supplied syntax
allows `ٱللَّهُ` to stand as its apposition, and `أَحَدٌ` is the nominative
predicate of `ٱللَّهُ`. The source phrase `اسم الله الأكبر هو الله` contains
the same ordered `هو الله`, but its equation about the name does not replace
the surface apposition. Local grammar and the direct subject bind the passage
pronoun and name to God.

The name carries a complete qualification. `تأله = تعبد`, `مألوه = معبود`,
and `التأليه = التعبيد` separate worshipper, worship, worshipped endpoint,
and the act of making something worshipped. The condition `لا يكون إلاها حتى
يكون معبودا` carries the same local entity from the worshipped state into the
qualified `إله` position. The narrower assertion `فالإله الله تعالى لأنه
معبود` binds that relation to the named God-instance; the sun, idols, images,
and other locally worshipped things do not enter the specialized name.

The next operation is explicitly one of form: `الله قيل أصله إله فحذفت
همزته وأدخل عليها الألف واللام فخص بالباري تعالى`. Base `إله`, deleted
hamza, added `الألف واللام`, specialized output `الله`, and surface
`ٱللَّهُ` remain distinguishable. Oath and address enact the same name under
other roles: `الله ما فعلت = والله ما فعلته`, `لاه أنت = لله أنت`, and
`اللهم = يا ألله`; `لا هم اغفر لنا` and `يا ألله اغفر لي` keep speaker,
addressee, negated deed, and request separate. Here the specialized name is
the head of an affirmative predication inside the Prophet's quote.

The adjacent `أَحَدٌ` also carries a source-declared form history: `أحد فرع
والأصل الواو وحد`, and the absolute descriptive use likewise states `وأصله
وحد`. The name and its predicate therefore stand together as the surface
outputs of two independent histories. For `الله`, the source supplies base
`إله`, hamza deletion, and article addition. For `أحد`, it preserves the full
surface form beside base `وحد` and the original waw. One history cannot be
used to manufacture steps in the other, but backward attention through
`ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ` reaches both declared bases without dissolving the surface
words.

The decisive semantic assertion is exact: `أحد بمعنى الواحد`, and the form
`يستعمل مطلقا وصفا في وصف الله تعالى`; the source itself repeats `قل هو الله
أحد`. Surface `أَحَدٌ` is thus an absolute one-predicate of God. Its relation
to number remains articulated rather than erased. `الواحد` is first of
number; joined to tens it yields `أحد عشر` and `أحد وعشرين`; `فأحدهن أي
صيرهن أحد عشر` keeps one feminine-plural set through a transitive change into
an eleven-count state, and `إحدى عشرة` retains the feminine form. Additive
position can mean first in `أحدكما`; `يوم الأحد أي يوم الأول` names the first
day and pluralizes it as `آحاد`. `ما استأحدت بهذا الأمر أي ما انفردت به`
makes an actor solitary in an affair, while `جاءوا آحاد أحاد` distributes
plural arrivals one by one. Exact `أحد` also names a mountain in the city.
Absolute description, counting, firstness, solitary action, distribution, and
proper naming have different participants and results. The surface predicate
receives the absolute description, not a member of any other scene.

An additional exact route runs through `واحد`. The saying-source states
`القول والقيل واحد`, using `واحد` in an equation between two names of saying;
the divine-name source says `أصل واحد وهو التعبد`, where one counts a lexical
origin; the one-source gives definite `الواحد` as the meaning of `أحد`; and
the offspring-source later says `الولد ... للواحد والجميع`, where one marks
number scope. Indefinite `واحد` remains distinct from article-bearing
`الواحد`, and each assertion retains its subject. Even so, the term passes
formally through the lexical fields of `قُلْ`, `ٱللَّهُ`, `أَحَدٌ`, and
`يُولَدْ`. The first line therefore reaches its one-predicate by two supplied
routes: the complete phrase `قل هو الله أحد`, and a second circuit from the
saying equation through `واحد` to `أحد بمعنى الواحد`. Neither route turns
saying, lexical origin, or offspring number into God.

The defining use of saying supplies a still smaller term. Full `حدهما`
releases base `حد` while preserving its two possessors and the assertion that
`قول` can delimit a thing. Those ordered letters occur inside both complete
surface forms `أَحَدٌ` and `أَحَدٌۢ`. Initial hamza, full one-form,
inflection, local syntax, and meaning remain intact; `أحد` is not reduced to
`حد`. Yet the commanded content closes on a word that formally contains the
supplied term for a saying's boundary, and the whole passage will close on the
same containment.

## `ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ`: Destination, Sufficiency, and Duration

The exact name moves from the third of four positions inside the quote to the
first of two positions in the next line. Context supplies more than form
recurrence: the God declared one is the same God named the self-sufficient
refuge. The participant continues, while `أَحَدٌ` and `ٱلصَّمَدُ` remain two
predicates with different operations.

`ٱلصَّمَدُ` first gives refuge a direction. `الصمد السيد الذي يصمد إليه في
الأمر`, `الذي يصمد إليه في الحوائج`, and `صمده قصد معتمدا عليه قصده`
supply a seeker, an affair or need, a route of intention, reliance established
along it, and a master as intended endpoint. Surface predication and the
description “self-sufficient refuge” fill that endpoint with God. The seeker
and need remain unstated, but their motion is no longer vague: it is `قصد`
joined to `اعتماد`. `بيت مصمد أي مقصود` enacts the same route with a house as
its own local destination. The exact house form later enters `أكفأت البيت`
and the covering of `مؤخر البيت`; form connects intended house and covered
house, while no house or inhabitant enters the primary predication.

The full basin's `وقال قطني` supplies a second route to the word
“self-sufficient.” There a state of fullness indicates its own limit or
enoughness without articulation. Here `ٱلصَّمَدُ` names a relied-on endpoint
whose self-sufficiency belongs to the predicate bearer. Basin and God are not
identified, and the basin's indicated content does not become the Prophet's
quote. The distinct route nevertheless changes the predicate's composition:
self-sufficiency can be heard not only as independence at a destination, but
as a state whose sufficiency is manifest in the state itself. This joins the
opening's nonvocal saying relation to the second line without transferring its
container.

Material `صمد` assertions give that state an interior. `المصمت الذي لا جوف
له`, `الصمد الذي ليس بأجوف`, the fixed rock, hard place, elevated thick site,
severe earth, and solid bearer without furrows supply density, elevation,
interior position, and explicit absence of cavity. These remain source images,
not literal identities for God. Because the same God later appears in
`لَّهُۥ`, the `لا جوف له` arrangement can return around the same primary
referent while its missing item stays a cavity, not a counterpart.

Exact `الصماد` places material at two kinds of boundary. `الصماد عفاص
القارورة` and `الصماد سداد القارورة` make it the stopper of a bottle;
`صمدتها/أصمدها` retains the feminine bottle through the closing operation.
`صمد رأسه تصميدا` wraps a head with rag, handkerchief, or cloth, expressly
`ما خلا العمامة`; the medium covers an exterior rather than stopping an
aperture. The excluded `العمامة` has its own exact source position in the
occurrence-field, `موضع العمامة`. The two generic objects are not one
occurrence, but the repeated form makes the local distinction exact: exclusion
from one wrapping medium does not erase the object's separately named site.
That differs from the final universal denial, which emits no comparator at
all.

Exact `صمده` then changes what travels along an outward vector. In `صمده قصد
معتمدا عليه`, the suffixed object is intended and relied upon. In `صمده
بالعصا صمدا إذا ضربه بها`, it is the affected endpoint of a blow through a
stick. The qawl relation `القال ... تضرب بها القلة` supplies a second complete
strike with independently filled instrument and target. Direction, reliance,
force, medium, and aftermath are therefore separable. God as refuge occupies
the intended endpoint; no surface event binds Him to either blow.

`إني على صمادة من أمر إذا أشرف عليه وحفلت به` supplies oversight and concern
toward an affair. Exact `أمر` also appears as negotiated subject matter,
relied-on direction, object of solitary action, and occurring event; each
keeps its own preposition and controller, so no single affair migrates into
the passage. `الصمد الدائم` supplies a further dimension: the lasting bearer
remains after the passing of its creatures, and a `ناقة مصماد` remains through
cold and barrenness while continuing its milk-flow. This exact duration
changes the continuity of God across the passage. The same referent does not
merely recur grammatically from name to refuge to the later verbs; under the
`ٱلصَّمَدُ` predicate, persistence becomes one positive temporal relation of
the bearer. The camel and its milk remain local, but adversity, duration,
retained existence, and continuing output stay distinct and available when
the passage next denies production and origin.

## `لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ`: Source and Product Are Both Denied

The two affirmative nominal clauses yield to two verbal clauses. Bare
`لَمْ` governs active imperfect jussive `يَلِدْ`; `وَ` then coordinates a
second clause whose own `لَمْ` governs passive imperfect jussive `يُولَدْ`.
Both verbs are 3MS and share lemma `وَلَدَ`, but voice reverses the semantic
side occupied by the same God.

In `يَلِدْ`, God is grammatical controller and active initiator. The complete
birth relation is `الولادة فهو وضع الوالدة ولدها`: mother, operation, child,
and completed placement are separate. `أولدت` marks birth becoming due; a
`شاة والد` is in pregnancy; `ولدناها` introduces attendants who undertake
her delivery. Negation prevents the operation from yielding a child. The
suppressed output is typed by these positive scenes but receives none of their
occupants.

In `يُولَدْ`, the same God controls the passive verb while occupying the
affected or born position; the begetter is suppressed. Exact unvocalized
`يولد` occurs in `الوليد الصبي حين يولد`, and `يوم ولدت؛ يوم ولد` supplies
an explicit active/passive turn. The passive event remains under its own
negation, so neither semantic source nor born-state results. The active
clause's absent child cannot cross into the passive clause as its controller;
the coordination carries ordering, shared lemma, and changed voice, not a
participant.

The child and parent assertions fill out every local parameter without
occupying either denied event. `الولد = المولود` can be one or many, male or
female, son or daughter; plural `أولاد` remains distinct. `الوالد = الأب`,
`الوالدة = الأم`, and together `الوالدان` keep father and mother separate.
`الوليد/الوليدة` varies proximity to birth independently from social status:
boy, girl, male or female bondperson are different uses, and an older
bondwoman may still be called `وليدة`. The exact day shared by the forms `يوم
ولدت`, `يوم ولد`, and `يوم الأحد` carries a day-position only; no assertion
makes the birth days the first day.

Generic generation changes the medium while keeping source, cause, and result
distinct: `تولد الشيء من الشيء حصوله عنه بسبب`. A generated Arabic usage or
man may be non-pure; `المولد من الكلام` is newly introduced speech; `كتاب
مولد` is fabricated; `بينة مولدة` is not verified. Exact `الكلام` meets the
opening's articulated content, but the Prophet's declaration is not handed
into the generated example and acquires none of its novelty, impurity,
fabrication, or verification states.

Several positive outputs now surround the two denied lineage directions.
Articulation produces the Prophet's manifest quote. The enduring camel under
`الصمد` continues milk through adversity. A year's `كفأة` produces palm fruit
or camel yield, and `أعطاني لبنها ووبرها وأولادها سنة` emits milk, wool, and
young over a bounded interval. Base `أولاد` from full `أولادها` classifies
the same local young under `جمع الولد أولاد`; `أكفأت إبلي كفأتين` divides
the herd into two alternating production phases. Generated language supplies
another caused output. These scenes differ in medium, duration, agency, and
result. None crosses into the passage's negated lineage, but together they
make its polarity exact: outward child from God is denied, and inward source
producing God is denied, while declaration, persistence, annual yield, and
local generation remain positive elsewhere.

The birth forms themselves open a measured relation that anticipates the
last line. `لدة الرجل = تربه` defines a peer in age; the source states that
`اللدة` lacks waw and has origin `ولدة`, and two peers are `لدان`. Elsewhere
`ولدان` is a plural of `الوليد` for boys or bondpersons. Written `لدان` is
contained in full `ولدان`, but the initial waw, dual versus plural number,
age measure, and social meanings remain. The formal contact does not turn
children into the two age-peers. It carries the complete age-relation forward
as one locally filled way two people can be alike, ready to meet the broader
`كُفُوًا` relation without supplying a surface candidate.

## `وَلَمْ يَكُن ...`: Qualification Changes Its Subject

The third negative frame is
`وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ`. Here `يَكُن` is an imperfect jussive
copular head under a newly supplied `لَمْ`; `لَّ + هُۥ` is the prepositional
endpoint governed through the likeness predicate; `كُفُوًا`, with base
`كُفُو` and accusative indefinite ending `ـًا`, is the predicate; delayed
nominative `أَحَدٌۢ` is subject and grammatical controller. God remains the
continuous referent only in `هُۥ`. Anyone now controls the clause.

This negative qualification answers the positive one embedded in the name's
source. In `لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون معبودا`, an entity that has become
worshipped can pass into the `إله` qualification, and the narrower assertion
binds the named God-instance before the form operation yields `الله`. The
final `يَكُن` opens the same broad category of occurrence or qualification
with the participant map reversed: God is not the subject being qualified;
He is the endpoint relative to whom another subject would have to become
`كُفُوًا`. That state never occurs. The passage therefore runs from a
supplied positive state-transition behind the name to a surface denial of any
candidate's relational state beside the named God.

`كان الشيء يكون كونا إذا وقع وحضر` identifies occurrence as event and
presence in past or present. `كونه فتكون أحدثه فحدث` explicitly carries the
same affected thing from causation into occurrence. The final clause denies
neither God nor the candidate as entities; it denies occurrence of the
candidate in the `كُفُوًا` state. The supplied exception use `لا يكون زيدا`
keeps exclusion as another local work of `يكون`, while reinforcement remains
separate from this governed copula.

Other kawn relations change result and scale. The source explicitly derives
`المكان` from `كان يكون`; `المكانة = المنزلة` and `المكان / المكانة =
الموضع` turn occurrence into place or rank relative to another. `كنت على
فلان أكون كونا أي تكفلت به` gives guarantor, beneficiary, responsibility,
and supported state. `الاستكانة = الخضوع` supplies submission with an
unstated endpoint, and `بكِينة سوء = بحال سوء` gives an overnight bearer a
bad condition through a pattern from `الكون`. None of those persons or states
controls the surface `يَكُن`.

The aged `كُنْتِيّ` completes a local return between being and saying:
`يقال للرجل إذا شاخ كُنْتِيّ؛ كأنه نسب إلى قوله كُنْتُ في شبابي كذا وكذا`.
The same man reaches old age, utters an earlier condition in `كُنْتُ`, and
receives a derived label based on his own saying. A speaker, remembered youth,
past state, uttered form, and later name remain connected by the explicit
same-man relation. Surface `قُلْ` and `يَكُن` touch the two lexical families
without making the Prophet that aged man. The local loop shows exactly what
an identity-bearing return requires; elsewhere, formal repetition alone
cannot provide it.

`كُفُوًا` supplies the denied relation. `الكفء = المثل`, `التكافؤ =
التساوي`, `الكفئ = النظير`, and `هذا كفء له أي مثله` distinguish two
relata, a measure, equality or likeness, and an attained state. Surface word
order places `لَّهُۥ` before `كُفُوًا`, whereas the source places `كفء` before
`له`; the endpoint is brought forward but does not become controller.

The local likeness scenes display their measures rather than filling the
surface. A person may be equal to another in status or lineage, property, war,
marriage, or combat. `كافأت الرجل أي فعلت به مثل ما فعل بي` carries the
pattern of a prior act into a returned like act while rotating actor and
recipient; `المكافأة مجازاة النعم` gives benefaction a corresponding return.
Two things counter the sun from the eyes, and a spear places two horsemen in a
matched or opposed relation. The age-peer `لدة/ترب` narrows the measure to
birth-time. No one of these measures is assigned to the absolute surface
denial.

Exact `الشمس` also links two complete local scenes across the name and
likeness lexicons. In the first, a group worships the sun and it is called
`الإلاهة`; in the second, two things counter the sun from the eyes. Worshipper,
worshipped status, countering instruments, sun, and eyes retain their local
roles, and exact form alone does not identify the two occurrences as one
event. The circuit nevertheless sharpens the primary distinction: being made
worshipped and being countered are relational outcomes of their own agents;
neither transfers the sun into the specialized surface `الله` or into the
unoccupied `كُفُوًا` position.

The same root also changes or materializes a relation. `أكفأت الشيء إذا
أملته` tilts an object; a bow, dish, bowl, or vessel changes orientation;
people are diverted to another destination; a woman or ship-like motion
sways; a face is downcast or altered in color. `الإكفاء قلب الشيء كأنه إزالة
المساواة` explicitly makes overturning as though removal of equality. The
passage's grammatical negation is not a physical inversion, but the stated
aftermath supplies a distinct route from an attained equal state to its
removal.

`الكفاء شقة أو ثنتان` gives a material pair: in `شقتان تنصح إحداهما
بالأخرى`, one piece is sewn to the other and covers the rear of a tent or
house. `أكفأت البيت فهو مكفأ` changes the house by making such a cover for it.
Full `إحداهما` retains feminine base `إحدى` and dual suffix `ـهما`; it also
contains exact `حد`, already supplied as the term in the defining
`حدهما`. These contacts carry form only. Yet they give the final adjacent
surface carriers `كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ` an additional exact circuit: the likeness
lexicon contains a one-of-two form from the `أحد/إحدى` family, and that form
itself carries the same boundary letters found in passage-final `أَحَدٌۢ`.
The sewn pieces, house, and dual possessors never enter the denied relation.

The annual `كفأة` supplies a temporal pair or cycle rather than a seam:
requester, owner, palm or herd, one-year interval, fruit, milk, wool, young,
complete or incomplete yield, and two alternating groups remain separate.
`الإكفاء في الشعر` supplies another ordered set: line endings can vary in
consonant, movement, or inflection, with one raised and another lowered. Its
named examples do not become the passage's endings, but its parameters make
the passage's retained and changed boundary features inspectable.

The delayed subject receives exhaustive scope from `أحد في النفي لاستغراق
جنس الناطقين ولا واحد ولا اثنان فصاعدا`. The house examples `لا أحد في
الدار` and `ما في الدار أحد` vary position without shrinking the absent
class; `فما منكم من أحد عنه حاجزين` preserves population, endpoint, and
plural barrier role. The count assertion adds exact internal stations to
`فصاعدا`: one, two, eleven, twenty-one, and sets transformed into eleven show
how cardinal extent can be composed. No counted set becomes the delayed
subject. Rather, “anyone” ranges through the eligible class so completely that
no single member, pair, or higher number occupies the likeness relation to
God.

The final denial also preserves the opening's unfilled audience as a separate
role. The lexical scope of negative `أحد` concerns a class fit to be
addressed, while `قُلْ` requires a declarer and allows an eventual hearer. The
class description does not identify an actual audience, and no final
candidate is handed backward as a hearer. Addressability and grammatical
control therefore remain distinct: anyone controls the final copula under
negation; the Prophet alone controls the opening command.

## Four Lines, Six Movements, One Returning Boundary

The arranged utterance has four complete lines and six clause-level
movements: imperative, nominal quote, second nominal predication, active
birth denial, passive birth denial, and final copular denial. Its openings are
`قُلْ -> ٱللَّهُ -> لَمْ -> وَلَمْ`; its endings are `أَحَدٌ ->
ٱلصَّمَدُ -> يُولَدْ -> أَحَدٌۢ`. The first boundary after the command begins
with `هُوَ`; the next line begins by moving exact `ٱللَّهُ` from an internal
third position to a line opening. Context turns that form recurrence into
continuity of God while leaving the two predicates apart.

Every line closes on `د`, in the root order `ء ح د / ص م د / و ل د / ء ح
د`. The first close is an indefinite nominative affirmative predicate; the
second a definite nominative predicate; the third a passive imperfect jussive
verb under renewed negation; the fourth an indefinite nominative delayed
subject under a negated copula. The line-ending assertion from `الإكفاء في
الشعر` supplies the exact parameter set: consonant remains `د`, while root,
lexical category, definiteness, case or mood, voice, polarity, and syntactic
role change. The sound carries the role sequence without replacing it.

The outer forms are closer still. `أَحَدٌ` and `أَحَدٌۢ` share lemma, root,
gender, indefiniteness, nominative case, and closing position; the final form
retains its additional written recitational decoration. The first is an
affirmative predicate of God. The last is a universally ranged delayed
subject whose candidate relation to God does not occur. Source `أحد أحد`
places two forms adjacently for emphasis; the passage distributes them across
four lines. Position and form recur, but adjacency, participant, polarity,
and grammatical work change. Both full forms contain `حد`, so the
declaration's first boundary and the passage's final boundary also carry the
same smaller source term.

The small morphemes keep these transformations exact. Each of the three
`لَمْ` occurrences governs its own imperfect jussive. The two `وَ` prefixes
coordinate the second and third denials; internal waw in `واحد`, `قول`, or
`ولد` does not become conjunction. In `لَّهُۥ`, `لَّ` supplies the
prepositional endpoint and `هُۥ` the continuous God-referent, while other
source suffixes `ـه` retain their own antecedents. The overt determiner in
`ٱلصَّمَدُ` and the source-declared addition of `الألف واللام` in `الله`
remain different operations.

The three negative clauses rotate control around one referent. In `لَمْ
يَلِدْ`, God controls and occupies the source side of an outward relation; no
child is produced. In `وَلَمْ يُولَدْ`, the same God controls but occupies
the affected side of an inward relation; no begetter or born-state is
produced. In `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ`, God remains the endpoint,
but anyone controls; no likeness state is produced. `ٱلصَّمَدُ` supplies the
positive counter-movement, a route of need and reliance toward God, and
`الصمد الدائم` supplies positive persistence through time:

```text
open need or affair -- قصد and اعتماد --> God as self-sufficient refuge
God -- begetting --> child                              denied
source -- begetting --> God as born participant        denied
anyone -- likeness relation --> God                    denied
God as الصمد -- duration through passing and adversity --> remains
```

The operations do not exchange occupants. Their convergence on the same God
changes the direct propositions into a geometry of relations: a positive
destination and positive duration stand amid denied origin, denied product,
and denied counterpart. The material no-cavity assertion adds an interior
absence, while the final clause supplies an exterior relational absence. The
same God centers both, but cavity and comparator never become equivalent.

## The End Reopens `قُلْ`

Backward reading begins with `أَحَدٌۢ` as anyone, not with the earlier
one-predicate. The delayed subject controls `يَكُن`, and its exhaustive scope
fills no candidate position. Moving left reaches `كُفُوًا`, where status,
property, war, marriage, returned action, age, opposed horsemen, sewn pieces,
annual phases, orientation, and boundary inflection reopen as different
measures and embodiments of a two-place relation. Their populated local
scenes make the surface vacancy precise without supplying an occupant.

Moving left again reaches `لَّهُۥ` and identifies God as endpoint. Exact
`له` then returns to `لا جوف له` inside the earlier `صمد` source around the
same primary referent: absent exterior counterpart answers absent interior
cavity, while their nouns and domains remain apart. At `يَكُن`, the positive
qualification behind `الله` also reopens. A named entity there passed from
worshipped state into `إله`; here no other subject passes into `كُفُوًا`
relative to that God. The name and final denial become two opposed outcomes
of qualification rather than merely distant lexical contacts.

The backward path meets passive `يُولَدْ` before active `يَلِدْ`. The same God
first appears on the affected side of denied birth and then on its source
side. Exact birth forms reopen parent, child, delivery, generated output,
annual young, and age-peer relations, but negation still emits none of their
participants. The final `د` of `يُولَدْ` enters the four-boundary sequence
before the root supplies its wider production scenes.

`ٱلصَّمَدُ` is then heard after the relations of origin, product, and likeness
have all been closed. Its incoming route remains open and positive: need can
move toward God in intention and reliance. Its self-sufficiency is indicated
from a second lexical direction by the full basin's state, articulated
internally by absence of cavity, materialized locally as aperture and exterior
boundary, and extended temporally by `الدائم`. No negated child, source, or
candidate is used to fill the seeker or need.

Exact `ٱللَّهُ` carries the same God back from line opening into the quote's
interior. The source-declared name history returns from specialized `الله` to
`إله` and its worshipped prerequisite; the adjacent one-predicate returns
from `أحد` to source base `وحد`. Then final universally negated `أَحَدٌۢ`
changes back into first-line affirmative `أَحَدٌ`. Form returns without its
final controller, and the ending sends exclusivity backward into the declared
oneness.

Inside that first `أَحَدٌ`, exact `حد` sends the close through the defining
use of saying and back to `قُلْ`. The Prophet is the declarer, God the declared
entity, absolute oneness the delimiting content, and `هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`
the manifested result. The alternate `واحد` circuit also reaches the same
close from the lexical equation `القول والقيل واحد`. Neither formal route
turns the utterance into God; both make the opening and its result internally
connected before the later lines act upon them.

At the end of the backward movement, `قُلْ` no longer sounds like an isolated
command followed by a list. It commands a declaration whose named participant
will remain through positive refuge, positive duration, active and passive
lineage denials, and a final relation controlled by anyone else. The passage
closes on `أَحَدٌۢ`, returns the same one-form and the contained `حد`, and
sends the absence of origin, product, and counterpart into the affirmative
`أَحَدٌ`. That completed relational field is what the Prophet is commanded to
bring out when the passage begins: `قُلْ`.
