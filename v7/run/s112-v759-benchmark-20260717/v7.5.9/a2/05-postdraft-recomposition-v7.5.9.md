# The Declaration and Its Returning Boundary

`قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`

`ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ`

`لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ`

`وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ`

Surah 112 commands the Prophet to declare God's absolute oneness, names God as
the self-sufficient refuge, denies that He begets or is begotten, and closes by
denying that anyone is comparable to Him. The passage arranges those assertions
as one command, two affirmative nominal predications, and three negated
imperfect clauses. Its grammatical forces thus form a complete progression:
an act is commanded, God receives two positive determinations, and three
possible relations around the same God are denied. The first denial blocks an
outward lineage route, the second an inward lineage route, and the third a
lateral peer relation.

The arrangement is also enclosed by one returning form. `أَحَدٌ` first stands
as the affirmative predicate of `ٱللَّهُ`; final `أَحَدٌۢ` is the delayed
subject whose entire candidate class falls under the denial of comparability.
Between those positions the one referent becomes refuge, occupies both sides
of the blocked lineage schema, and returns as the endpoint in `لَّهُۥ`. The
last clause therefore completes two returns at once: `هُۥ` recovers the God
named near the opening, while `أَحَدٌۢ` recovers the word that first predicated
absolute oneness. Neither return is ornamental. One preserves participant
identity; the other transforms a positive description into an exhaustive
relational limit.

## A Saying That Reaches a Boundary

The imperative `قُلْ` has an implicit second-person masculine singular
controller, identified as the Prophet. Supplied syntax keeps the speech verb
outside the quotation and binds the complete three-word clause
`هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ` as its complement. The command, commanded performer,
proposition, and referent are consequently distinct. The command occurs; a
later declaration token is actable but is not separately asserted as completed,
and no audience or response is named.

The whole unvocalized first line, `قل هو الله أحد`, occurs as a complete
utterance in the supplied `أحد` source. This is an exact content circuit. The
lexical quotation and the positioned passage remain different occurrences,
but the four-word order and proposition recur intact. A second exact circuit
lies inside the quote: the name source says `اسم الله الأكبر هو الله`, and its
`هو الله` is the same two-word sequence carried by 112:1:2-3. The source
assertion about the name and the passage's pronoun-apposition grammar are not
one assertion type, yet the exact phrase strengthens the movement from pronoun
to named referent.

Morphology relates `قُلْ` to lemma `قَالَ` and root `ق و ل`. Because the lemma
has a medial alif, this is an inflectional lineage rather than literal
containment. Exact `قل` occurs inside supplied `أقل`, `قلت`, and `القلة`.
Those contacts retain their full local operations: `ما لم أقل` concerns a
performance not made and a possible false attribution; `قلت` can receive the
supposition-like government assigned to it by a named speech community; and
`القلة` is the object struck by the wooden `القال`. The command inherits none
of those neighboring events, but each exact form specifies a different route
available to the speech root.

The root's primary carrier operation makes the command concrete. A supplied
account calls `قول` a complex of letters brought out through pronunciation,
whether a single expression, sentence, poem, or address, and another equates
`المقول` with the tongue. A third locates a saying inside the psyche before
expression: `في نفسي قول لم أظهره`. Projected through `قُلْ`, the exact
proposition may be internally held, reach the vocal boundary, pass through
tongue and pronunciation, and become a fresh declaration token. If the
`لم أظهره` state fills the route, content remains present while performance is
absent. The command therefore opens a carrier transition without confusing
proposition continuity with a new occurrence.

Every changed carrier preserves that distinction. A fluent or frequent
speaker can multiply performances, and supplied `أحد أحد` can fill the
reiterated form without multiplying the proposition. A title-holder whose word
`ينفذ قوله`, or a controller whose saying acts over another, can project an
effective declaration with a receiver, consequence, and consent position.
A saying can be drawn toward oneself with good or evil content, circulate
among people with good or bad valence, become one party's contribution to a
negotiation, or continue as adopted belief. These operations produce
appropriation, social recurrence, reciprocal response, and adherence under
new controllers. They are possible downstream lives of a proposition, not
fresh identities for the Prophet or an asserted audience.

Other source operations alter the very kind of performance. In one, saying
works like supposition; in another, a filled basin `قال قطني` by the indication
of its state, without tongue or pronunciation. Saying may also name sincere
care for a thing or the definition of it. The wooden `القال`, by contrast,
cannot carry content: it transmits a strike to `القلة` and therefore moves into
a force topology. These complete alternatives make the primary action exact.
`قُلْ` commands a declaration, while indication, care, supposition, definition,
control, and impact preserve different controllers, media, and results.

The definition source opens an exact relation that passes through the whole
surah. It says that logicians use a thing's `قول` as its `حد`, and the
full supplied term `حدهما` names the boundary or definition of two defined
objects. Releasing its recoverable base preserves both terms:
`حدهما = حد + هما`. Exact `حد` occurs after the initial alif-hamza in both
surface forms `أحد`. It also remains terminal in the supplied origin `وحد` and
in `واحد`, although all containing forms and their initial material remain
distinct.

This contact does not make the surah a source-local exercise by logicians. It
does create a complete projective mechanism. The commanded `قول` reaches a
quote-final `أحد` that contains the supplied definition term; after refuge and
the three denials have specified the proposition's relations, the passage
again reaches `أحد`, containing the same term at its formal close. The first
word opens an act of saying, the first `أحد` bounds its explicit quoted
predication, and the final `أحد` returns the boundary after exhaustive
comparability has been denied. A lexical definition, a syntactic quote
boundary, and the passage boundary converge without becoming identical. The
ending changes the opening because `قُلْ` now launches a declaration whose
limit is not merely stated once but completed by the returning word.

False speech provides the opposite carrier result. The exact source phrase
`قال ما لم يكن` contains the later surface sequence `لَمْ يَكُن`; the same
branch has an attributor assign `ما لم أقل` to another. In the source event, a
speech token can occur while the represented event did not, or an attribution
can occur while the claimed performance did not. In the passage, the denied
event is the occurrence of comparability, and no false report is asserted.
The form circuit therefore separates command, performance, attribution,
represented occurrence, and truth. It sends the final denial back to the
opening without turning either the Prophet or the proposition into the
source-local liar.

## One God, Two Positive Determinations

The continuous God referent travels through six grammatical stations:

`هُوَ -> ٱللَّهُ -> ٱللَّهُ -> Ø in يَلِدْ -> Ø in يُولَدْ -> هُۥ`.

`هُوَ` is the independent third-person pronoun at the head of the supplied
apposition relation; first `ٱللَّهُ` names its referent and heads the
predication to `أَحَدٌ`; second `ٱللَّهُ` begins a new predication to
`ٱلصَّمَدُ`. The same referent is then grammatically exposed as active source
and passive affected participant under two negations before becoming the
suffix endpoint of comparability. Occupant continuity never erases the role
changes. The Prophet controls `قُلْ`, not these third-person stations. Final
`يَكُن` agrees with delayed `أَحَدٌ`, not with God; God is present there only
as the endpoint encoded by `هُۥ`.

The repeated name carries its own asserted form history:

`إله -> deletion of hamza + insertion of الألف واللام -> الله`.

The passage repeats the result twice. The source also preserves the name in
oath, address, and invocation, including `والله`, `اللهم`, and
`يا ألله اغفر لي`. These are fresh local speech events, not alternative
grammars for the surface name. The worship source likewise preserves a
worshipper, act, and worshipped object, and makes prior worship a prerequisite
for the local role in `لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون معبودا`. Its sun, idols, and
other objects remain local occupants. When worship and submission are
projected beside the refuge route, they offer dependent-role variants, but the
passage itself names refuge rather than asserting one of those source events.

Line 2 places `الله` beside `الصمد`, so exact alif-lam material performs two
different kinds of work in adjacent words. In `الله`, insertion of
`الألف واللام` belongs to the supplied lexical formation and specialization of
the name. In `ٱلصَّمَدُ`, morphology gives `ٱل` as a present determiner before
the stem `صَّمَدُ`. The invariant letters therefore move from name formation
to grammatical definiteness. The same participant who received an indefinite
nominative predicate `أَحَدٌ` now receives a definite nominative predicate
`ٱلصَّمَدُ`; the final clause will instead combine indefinite accusative
`كُفُوًا` with indefinite nominative `أَحَدٌۢ` under negation.

The first predicate has its own complete source lock. The `ء ح د` account
quotes the whole opening line, permits unrestricted `أحد` as a description in
this named context, equates it with `الواحد`, relates it to origin `وحد`, and
supplies reiteration `أحد أحد`. Under unvocalized comparison, terminal `حد`
persists through `أحد`, `وحد`, and `واحد`, while the full forms and the written
`أ/ا` difference remain. The primary clause predicates God's absolute oneness;
the form relations give that predicate derivational, reiterative, and boundary
dimensions without converting it into arithmetic.

The other `أحد` operations preserve their distinct results. A unit can join
tens and produce eleven; firstness can occur on either side of an إضافة or in
the day-name `الأحد`; one actor can stand apart, and a plural group can arrive
`آحاد أحاد`; `أحد` can name a mountain in the city. Composition, ordinal
position, individual distribution, and located mass enter count, time, and
terrain roles elsewhere in the passage, but none displaces the affirmative
predicate. Their independence matters at the close: joining, being first,
standing alone, or occupying a mountain position does not itself make a
candidate comparable to God.

The second predicate changes the positive axis from description to relation.
The source repeatedly defines `الصمد` through a seeker who intends and relies,
an affair or need carried along that orientation, and the lord or house sought
as endpoint: `الصمد السيد لأنه يصمد إليه في الحوائج`. Joined to the supplied
meaning of self-sufficient refuge, the complete primary geometry is

`dependent or seeker -> need -> قصد واعتماد -> God as refuge`.

Need and dependence belong to the approaching side; self-sufficiency belongs
to the endpoint. Absolute oneness and refuge therefore act successively on one
participant without becoming synonyms. Oneness prevents the endpoint from
being placed inside a peer set; refuge makes that same endpoint positively
available to an asymmetrical approach.

## Refuge, Request, and Return

The passage does not name a particular seeker, need, answer, or beneficiary,
but the supplied source field can fully inhabit each role as a projected
variant. The clearest verbal route is not the Prophet's declaration about God
but the independently supplied invocation toward God:
`يا ألله اغفر لي` and `لا هم اغفر لنا`. Here a speaker addresses the named
endpoint, a request supplies the need, and `لي` or `نا` supplies the
beneficiary. Joined to the `صمد` direction, invocation travels toward refuge;
joined to the `قول` carrier, it crosses a vocal boundary. The distinction in
direction remains exact: `قُلْ` assigns the Prophet a declaration whose content
is God, whereas the invocation assigns another source speaker an address and
request directed to God.

The response position can also be completed projectively in several
source-distinct ways. Reciprocal `قاولته ... وتقاولنا` can return a saying from
the endpoint to the first speaker and closes after one exchange cycle. The
title-holder's effective word can supply an outcome rather than mere reception.
The annual-yield source gives an asker, owner or giver, one-year measure, and
fruit, milk, wool, or offspring delivered to a beneficiary; its result may be
complete, incomplete, or renewed by two alternating cohorts. The enduring
camel source continues to send milk through cold and barrenness, and the
guarantee source has one controller assume continuing responsibility for
another. These routes yield verbal response, effective action, provision,
continuing sustenance, and protective obligation. None is asserted as the
particular aftermath of `ٱلصَّمَدُ`, but none remains an empty mechanism.

A returned word or provision does not erase the refuge relation's asymmetry.
Negotiation may reverse the direction of speech, annual giving may move a
product to a requester, and guarantee may bind a protector to a beneficiary;
the participant roles remain different. This distinction returns at the end
of the surah: bidirectional exchange, answered request, or benefit does not by
itself make the dependent a `كُفُو` of the endpoint. Refuge can admit approach
and a projective return while comparability remains denied.

Self-sufficiency receives several independent material geometries. One source
supplies hard, compact material, an elevated thick place, anchored rock,
severe ground, absence of cavity, and absence of grooves. Projected through
refuge, it gives stability and no inward hollow. The mountain `أحد` and the
`ك و ن` place field can fill terrain and location, while social `مكانة`, an
overseer's station above an affair, a ruler's rank, and a guarantor's position
retain different physical, attentional, social, and obligational measures.

The filled basin that indicates `قطني` opens a countergeometry. Its sufficiency
is signified by a complete state rather than a spoken controller: an interior
is full and its fullness says enough. This is not the cavityless solid. A
third source applies `صماد`, a stopper, to a bottle and retains an interior
behind a changed boundary. The medial alif in `صماد` remains; it is not the
exact surface form `صمد`, though supplied verbs `صمدتها` and `أصمدها` preserve
the base. No cavity, filled capacity, and retained capacity are three different
ways of having no open inward lack. Projected through self-sufficiency, they
remain material images rather than bodily assertions about God.

Boundary media branch further. One operation wraps a head with cloth,
kerchief, or garment while expressly excluding a turban. `موضع العمامة` can
fill the body site but not the excluded medium. Another uses one or two sewn
pieces to cover the rear of a house or tent. The sought `بيت مصمد` can thereby
be approached and covered; one piece acts alone, while two are sewn as
complements rather than proved equals. A stopper closes a mouth, a wrap covers
a body region, a sewn piece shelters an architectural rear, a vocal boundary
controls expression, and `حد` defines a conceptual object. Their distinct
interiors, agents, media, and results give refuge protection several complete
forms without merging physical closure with relational denial.

Force changes those boundaries dynamically. `صمده بالعصا ... ضربه بها` and
`القال الخشبة التي تضرب بها القلة` share a controller-instrument-impact
topology. A spear between two horsemen supplies mediation without asserting a
strike; inclination can tilt a bow or plate, overturn a vessel, divert a group,
produce swaying, or leave a face's color changed. Warding turns pressure away
from a protected endpoint, while an effective word changes another's agency
without bodily impact. When these roles meet refuge, resistance and warding
provide a protection geometry; when they meet a stoppered vessel, the boundary
determines retention or a spill opening. Literal strikers, targets, and force
remain in their source events.

Duration completes the refuge field without making endurance a reproductive
act. One source calls `الصمد` enduring and remaining after others' annihilation;
another gives a camel that survives cold and barrenness while continuing milk.
The primary refuge can receive persistence through an adverse or terminal
boundary. The camel, annual products, requester, and alternating cohorts remain
their own concrete production system. Together they show that persistence,
outgoing benefit, and lineage are independent. Refuge may project protection,
response, or continuing provision; the next line still denies the exact
begetting and begotten relations rather than turning God into an inert
endpoint.

## Two Lineage Directions, Three Negative Gates

`لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ` carries one `و ل د` event schema through a change
of voice. Both verbs are imperfect jussive third-person masculine singular and
both are governed by `لَمْ`. In `يَلِدْ`, God is exposed as active grammatical
controller and semantic source; an offspring or product position lies
downstream, but the event and product are denied. In `يُولَدْ`, God occupies
the affected passive-subject position; the initiating parent is suppressed by
voice, and the event is independently denied. The unknown initiator remains an
unknown grammatical role, not an opening that another vacancy may fill as
passage actuality.

Positive birth sources reveal the whole operation being blocked. They supply
mother and father as distinct parental roles, a mother placing a carried child
into born state, an impending birth time, a day boundary, and attendants who
oversee without becoming the mother. Offspring terms may denote one or many,
male or female, young or older. The separate `وليد/وليدة` usages distinguish
nearness to birth from enslaved status, even allowing an older woman to retain
the status term. Every participant and aftermath remains actual in its own
source event; under the passage negations none becomes God's parent or child.

The two surface clauses thus block opposite transitions:

`God as source -> begetting -> product` is denied;

`source -> begetting -> God as product` is denied.

Placed after `ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ`, this bidirectional block gives
self-sufficiency an exact lineage surround. An outward begotten result and an
inward derivation from a begetting source are absent, while refuge remains a
positive state and relation. General causal production does not broaden the
scope of the clauses. The source expression `تولد الشيء من الشيء` can yield a
new saying, a mixed or environment-formed person or language, a fabricated
book, or generated but unverified evidence. Those results distinguish cause,
novelty, purity, fabrication, and verification; they make the source-result
topology visible without converting the passage into a denial of every cause.

The forms themselves enact the voice difference. Passive `يُولَدْ` contains
the released base `ولد` after its initial `ي` and exactly meets supplied
`حين يولد`. Active `يَلِدْ` belongs to the same lemma and root through
morphology but does not contain `ولد` in that order. The clause that suppresses
its initiator visibly retains the base, while the clause that exposes its
controller changes the internal sequence. The separately asserted loss
`ولدة -> لدة` produces a same-age peer relation; it does not restore a parent
or child to either denied clause.

The negator sequence expands the directional block:

`لَمْ -> وَ + لَمْ -> وَ + لَمْ`.

The first particle opens line 3 and governs active birth. The second renews
negation inside the line and coordinates the passive reversal. Complete
`وَلَمْ` then recurs as the opening of line 4, carrying the negative force
across the boundary to `يَكُن`. The governed complements rotate from God as
source, to God as affected result, to anyone as the subject of a possible
peer relation. Outward lineage, inward lineage, and lateral equivalence are
therefore three different closures around one referent.

Their vacancies are equally differentiated. The active denial retains an
exposed controller but no event or product. The passive denial retains an
affected role but no event and no expressed initiator. The final denial retains
endpoint, predicate, and exhaustive candidate schema but no obtaining relation.
Elsewhere, `لم أظهره` preserves internal content without performance;
`لا جوف له` preserves solid material without a cavity; `ليست تامة كفأة`
preserves a partial yield; `ليست بمحققة` preserves generated evidence without
verification; and `لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون معبودا` withholds a role until a
prerequisite. The consequences are positive and unlike one another. None can
be poured into a primary denied event to make it occur.

The passage also distinguishes time and modality. The declaration remains
commanded; the lineage schemas are negated imperfects rather than two positive
moments; the last denial blocks occurrence; passage closure is positional, not
calendar time. The named first day `الأحد`, a birth day, annual yield, coeval
age, an old man's `كُنْتُ في شبابي`, a night spent in a bad condition, and
open-ended endurance retain their own temporal scales. They make the positive
transitions and continuities precise without arranging them into a biography
for the primary participant.

## The Empty Peer Position

The final clause is grammatically complete before its result is denied:

`وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ`.

`يَكُن` is a jussive imperfect of `كَانَ`; `كُفُوًا` is its indefinite
accusative predicate; `أَحَدٌۢ` is its indefinite delayed nominative subject;
and `هُۥ` in the prepositional complement is God, the endpoint of comparison.
Surface order establishes endpoint `لَّهُۥ` and relation `كُفُوًا` before the
candidate reaches the passage boundary. When `أَحَدٌۢ` arrives, its negative
use ranges from one through more than one and exhausts the class of anyone;
no candidate occupies the comparable position.

Morphology releases lemma `كُفُو` from surface `كُفُوًا` while preserving the
final accusative material. The lexical field writes hamza-bearing forms such
as `كفء`, `كفئ`, `كفاءة`, and `تكافؤ`; their spellings are not flattened into
the lemma. The complete relational topology provides the bridge. Source
`هذا كفء له`, `الكفء المثل`, and `الكفئ النظير` supply two comparands, a
measure, an endpoint, and a positive peer result. The passage changes candidate
to exhaustive `أحد`, advances endpoint and predicate, delays the subject, and
negates occurrence.

A longer source formula creates a second exact pronoun circuit:

`كل شيء ساوى شيئا حتى يكون مثله فهو مكافئ له`.

In that positive source event, `هو` bears the equal-result relation and `له`
marks its other endpoint; the initial `ف` in `فهو` remains part of the full
form. The passage places exact `هُوَ` near its opening and exact `لَّهُۥ` near
its close, but assigns both pronouns to the one God referent. A different
participant, delayed `أَحَدٌۢ`, would have to bear the comparison predicate,
and its occurrence is denied. The mapping therefore changes a positive
two-comparand formula into a passage-wide counterconfiguration: God persists
from opening pronoun to closing endpoint, while the second comparand never
materializes.

`كَانَ` supplies occurrence and presence in a past or current time, caused
occurrence, report, emphasis, and an exception use. Surface `يَكُن` is related
to the lemma morphologically; lexical `يكون` retains a medial waw and is not
an exact containment. Here the direct proposition fixes what would have
occurred: anyone's being comparable to God. `لَمْ يَكُن` denies that relation,
not place, rank, guarantee, submission, remembered state, or every occurrence
named in the wider root field. Its exact contact with `قال ما لم يكن` remains
a form circuit to the false-speech event, not an attribution of falsehood to
the surah.

The positive counterpart field fully differentiates the denial. Equality may
be tested in lineage, wealth, war, or marriage; a benefit can receive a
like-for-like return; two horsemen can be related by a spear; a dual medium can
ward the sun. Another operation begins with equality and removes it through
tilting or inversion. That outgoing inequality differs from a peer who never
occurs. A same-age `لدة` is a peer under birth age; two sewn pieces are
complements; two herds alternate production phases; one joins tens to make a
larger number. Peerhood, reciprocity, opposition, complementarity, succession,
and composition remain distinct relations.

The distinction returns to refuge. A dependent may approach God, address God,
receive a projected answer or provision, or stand under protection. Even a
returned word creates a directional exchange, not an equal endpoint relation.
The closing denial therefore does not prohibit access; it prohibits
comparability. Conversely, the positive refuge relation cannot supply a hidden
candidate to `أَحَدٌۢ`. Shared destination transports no participant from one
topology to the other.

## The Form of the Whole Declaration

The passage has four lines, fifteen word tokens, nineteen morphological
carriers, eleven supplied syntax edges, ten root-bearing stems, six
clause/event carriers, and forty-seven normalized Arabic letters. These totals
remain attached to their complete positioned units. Their line vectors expose
different operations:

- word tokens `[4,2,4,5]`;
- clause/event carriers `[2,1,2,1]`;
- morphological carriers `[4,3,5,7]`;
- syntax edges `[3,1,3,4]`;
- root-bearing stems `[3,2,2,3]`;
- normalized letter totals `[11,9,12,15]`.

The six clause/event carriers also form the complete force grouping
`[1 command, 2 affirmative nominal predications, 3 negated imperfect clauses]`.
This is not an arithmetic interpretation of `أَحَدٌ`; it is the exhaustive
distribution of the passage's actual clauses. The command stands outside its
explicit quote, the two positive clauses predicate oneness and refuge of the
same God, and the three negative clauses block outward, inward, and lateral
relations. The grammar thus enacts the semantic movement without making every
clause syntactically subordinate to `قُلْ`.

Lines 1 and 3 each contain four words, but their groupings differ. The first is
one imperative plus a three-word quotation; the third is two coordinated
two-word denials. The clause vector `[2,1,2,1]` alternates pairs and singles,
yet the first pair embeds content while the second rotates voice, and the
first single is positive while the last is negative and delays its subject.
The God referent follows the same line count `[2,1,2,1]`: `هُوَ/ٱللَّهُ`, then
`ٱللَّهُ`, then two implicit verbal stations, then `هُۥ`. This convergence
distributes one participant at the scale of the clause architecture without
equating a carrier with a clause.

The difference between morpheme and word counts is itself the complete vector
`[0,1,1,2]`. Line 1 has no multi-morphemic word in the supplied analysis. Line
2 adds the determiner in `ٱل + صَّمَدُ`; line 3 adds the conjunction in
`وَ + لَمْ`; line 4 repeats that split and adds `لَّ + هُۥ`. Relational
material is increasingly packed inside words as the passage moves from
positive determination to coordinated negation and finally to a comparison
endpoint. The final single clause is therefore the densest, with four syntax
attachments and seven morphemes separating governor, endpoint, predicate, and
delayed subject.

Exact recurrence organizes the line transitions. `ٱللَّهُ` moves from word 3
of line 1 to word 1 of line 2, renewing the named participant under the refuge
predicate. `وَلَمْ` moves from word 3 of line 3 to word 1 of line 4, renewing
negative government under the comparable relation. These are parallel
third-to-first handoffs with different payloads: participant continuation in
the positive half and force continuation in the negative half. `أَحَدٌ`
instead remains a closing word while moving from the quote boundary to the
passage boundary.

The line openings progress
`قُلْ -> ٱللَّهُ -> لَمْ -> وَلَمْ`: command, named participant, negation,
and renewed negation. The endings progress
`أَحَدٌ -> ٱلصَّمَدُ -> يُولَدْ -> أَحَدٌۢ`: affirmative indefinite
predicate, affirmative definite predicate, negated passive verb, and delayed
candidate under negation. Every line ends in written `د`. The exact boundary
vector `[د,د,د,د]` holds the consonant while final grammar changes from
nominative tanwin, to nominative damma, to jussive sukun, and back to
nominative tanwin.

The supplied poetic `إكفاء` source compares complete ending distributions by
letter, movement, and inflection. Its source examples include raised and
lowered rhymes and different consonants, but the passage retains `د` throughout.
What varies is definiteness, case or mood, category, voice, syntactic role, and
polarity. Acoustic constancy therefore carries grammatical transformation

Final `لَّهُۥ` activates several exact source routes without merging them.
`هذا كفء له` supplies the direct positive counterpart topology;
`الذي له قول أي ينفذ قوله` supplies possession of an effective word;
`انتشرت له قالة` supplies a socially circulating saying associated with an
endpoint; and `عملت له كفاء` supplies a covering made for a house. In the
passage, `لَّهُۥ` remains only preposition plus coreferential pronoun in the
comparison complement. Projectively, those local routes send declaration
effect, circulation, and refuge cover backward to their proper passage
relations. Form provides convergence; grammar prevents semantic transfer.

The ending now reopens every element of the first line. `هُۥ` returns to the
exact `هُوَ/ٱللَّهُ` identity circuit; `أَحَدٌۢ` returns to the first
predicate and to the contained definition term `حد`; the `د` boundary returns
the sound with changed grammar; and the exhaustive no-peer result completes
the positive oneness that the Prophet is commanded to declare. The command is
therefore heard anew. It does not launch an isolated predicate. It launches a
bounded utterance in which God is positively named as absolutely one and as
self-sufficient refuge, may be approached without becoming a peer, is neither
source nor product in lineage, and has no comparable candidate. The final
word closes those relations and, by returning the boundary inside `أَحَدٌ`,
sends the completed declaration back to `قُلْ`.
