# From Command to Exhaustive Closure

`قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`

`ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ`

`لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ`

`وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ`

The passage commands the Prophet to declare God's absolute oneness, names God
as the self-sufficient refuge, denies both begetting and being begotten, and
closes by denying that anyone is comparable to Him. Its four lines do not
merely place these propositions beside one another. They distribute action,
reference, dependence, lineage, and comparison around one continuous God
referent. The opening sends a command toward the Prophet and gives him a whole
proposition to declare. The second line presents God as the endpoint of an
asymmetric refuge relation. The third blocks lineage in both directions. The
fourth blocks a lateral peer relation. At the same time, exact recurrences
carry the reader back through the passage: `ٱللَّهُ` is renewed across the
first line break, `وَلَمْ` is renewed across the last, `لَّهُۥ` returns the
closing relation to the named God, and final `أَحَدٌۢ` returns to the exact
form that closed the opening declaration. Absolute oneness is thus first
predicated and then enacted as an exhaustive relational closure.

## A Command With Bound Content

The imperative `قُلْ` has an implicit second-person masculine singular
controller. That controller is identified as the Prophet, while the syntax
keeps the verb outside the quotation and makes the complete
nominal clause `هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ` its complement. Prophet, proposition, and
God are therefore three distinct positions: the Prophet is commanded to
declare; God's absolute oneness is what he is commanded to declare; God is the
referent within that content. The command occurs, but the passage does not
separately assert a later completed performance, name an audience, or report a
response. This modality remains part of the line's meaning. `قُلْ` opens a
performance without pretending that the opened performance has already become
an event.

Morphology binds `قُلْ` to lemma `قَالَ` and root `ق و ل`; the link is
inflectional rather than literal containment because the lemma has a medial
alif. Exact `قل` does occur inside supplied `أقل`, `قلت`, and `القلة` forms.
Each contact reopens its complete local relation, respectively an utterance
not made, supposition-like government, or a struck object, without transferring
that relation into the imperative.

The lexical field of `ق و ل` makes the opened performance materially exact.
One supplied account calls a saying a complex of letters brought out through
pronunciation, whether the resulting unit is a single expression, a sentence,
a poem, or an address; another equates `المقول` with the tongue. A further
source calls an inwardly conceived content a `قول` before it is brought out in
words and says `في نفسي قول لم أظهره`. Projected through the command, these
relations yield a complete carrier transition: the exact proposition can be
held as content, reach a bodily and vocal boundary, pass through tongue and
pronunciation, and emerge as a fresh declaration token. Proposition and
referent can remain stable while carrier and occurrence change. In the
`لم أظهره` variant, the content is still present but the outward crossing does
not occur; this is an unperformed expression, not empty content. The command
therefore specifies both what must cross and the point at which performance
remains open.

The exact content is secured twice. The whole unvocalized first line,
`قل هو الله أحد`, occurs as a complete supplied utterance in the lexical
account of `أحد`. That source occurrence and the positioned passage line are
not one historical performance, but their ordered proposition is identical.
The contact locks the command to the whole quote, not merely to `أَحَدٌ`, and
allows the same content to persist across a new carrier without confusing
content identity with token identity.

Other complete `ق و ل` operations expose what changes when a proposition
moves beyond its first carrier. A fluent or frequent speaker can multiply
tokens, and the supplied reiteration `أحد أحد` can give that repetition an
exact form; multiplication increases performances, not propositional content.
A title-holder whose `قول` takes effect, or a controller whose speech acts over
another, supplies a consequence-bearing word; projected after `قُلْ`, this
makes declaration capable of changing a receiver's relation or agency, while
rank, compulsion, consent, receiver, and effect remain unstated. A saying may
be drawn toward oneself, spread among people, exchanged reciprocally about an
affair, or adopted as a belief. The appropriated saying retains its good or
evil content branch, and the socially circulating saying retains its good or
bad valence. Each route supplies a new controller and a new aftermath:
appropriation is not original conception, social circulation is not an
asserted performance by the Prophet, negotiation is not passive reception,
and belief continuity does not prove a new utterance.

The remaining saying-relations sharpen the command by changing the kind of
carrier. In one construction a form of saying works like supposition in an
interrogation, with the named community extending inflected `قلت` beyond that
setting; in another a filled basin indicates `قطني` without a tongue;
elsewhere saying names sincere care directed to a thing or the definition,
`حد`, of a substance or accident. These are complete local performances of
cognition, signification, attention, and delimitation. Their changed
controllers and media show that the primary action is specifically a commanded
declaration. The lexical `القال`, by contrast, is a wooden stick used to strike
`القلة`. It cannot transport the proposition at all. Its completed route leaves
the speech system and enters a force system with controller, implement,
affected object, and impact. Root association alone does not turn an
instrument into a saying.

The false-speech branch makes the distinction between content and controller
especially consequential. It includes `قال ما لم يكن`, saying what did not
occur, and `ما لم أقل`, attributing to someone what he did not say. The later
surface sequence `لَمْ يَكُن` is an exact formal contact with the first phrase.
In the lexical event, a speech token occurs while the represented event does
not; in the passage, a comparison relation does not occur, and no false report
is asserted. Returning this distinction to `قُلْ` keeps the commanded Prophet,
the actual commanded proposition, a possible alien attributor, and the truth
of a represented event in separate positions. Exact wording joins the forms;
it does not transfer the liar or false outcome into the declaration.

## One Referent Through Changing Stations

Within the quote, `هُوَ` is an independent third-person masculine singular
pronoun, `ٱللَّهُ` stands in apposition to it, and `أَحَدٌ` is the nominative
predicate of the named God. The same God then begins the second line as the
head of a new nominal predication. In the third line the referent is carried by
the implicit agreement of both verbs, first as active grammatical source in
`يَلِدْ` and then as the affected passive subject in `يُولَدْ`. In the fourth
line it returns explicitly as the suffix `هُۥ` in `لَّهُۥ`, now the endpoint
of comparison. This gives the continuous path

`هُوَ -> ٱللَّهُ -> ٱللَّهُ -> Ø in يَلِدْ -> Ø in يُولَدْ -> هُۥ`.

The occupant does not change, but every grammatical station does. God is first
identified within the content to be declared, then bears two predicates, then
occupies opposite sides of a denied lineage schema, and finally becomes a
relational endpoint rather than a clause controller. The Prophet never enters this
third-person path. Nor does `يَكُن` carry God as its subject: that verb agrees
with delayed `أَحَدٌ`, the candidate `anyone`, while `هُۥ` refers to God.
The final clause is intelligible only if agreement controller, semantic
referent, comparison endpoint, and delayed candidate remain distinct.

The repeated name has a separate supplied form-history. The lexical account
states `إله -> حذف الهمزة + إدخال الألف واللام -> الله`: hamza is removed,
the alif-lam material is inserted, and the resulting form is specialized as
`الله`. The passage places that result at 112:1:3 and again at 112:2:1. The
same account also preserves oath, address, and invocation forms such as
`والله`, `يا ألله`, and `اللهم`; these show the name entering fresh speech
acts without turning any of those local speakers or requests into passage
participants. Another lexical relation makes being worshipped a prerequisite
for the local role `إله` in `لا يكون إلاها حتى يكون معبودا`. That conditional
availability is distinct from the passage's terminal denials and does not make
the named primary participant depend on an unstated passage event. The sun,
idols, and other worshipped objects in those lexical examples remain their own
local occupants and never enter God's referential path.

After ordinary vocalization marks are removed, terminal `له` is also an exact
ordered sequence within `إله` and `الله`. The closing surface word `لَّهُۥ`,
however, is independently a preposition plus a coreferential pronoun. A full
return can therefore be followed without collapsing its links:

`إله -> الله -> contained له -> surface ل + ه -> Him -> God`.

The first arrow is an asserted lexical formation, the second is exact formal
containment, the third is surface grammar, and the last is referential
continuity. Together they make the comparison endpoint a formal and
referential return to the earlier name. They do not make an internal two-letter
sequence a preposition or import worship semantics into `لَّهُۥ`.

## The Two Positive Predications

The first line closes with `أَحَدٌ`, masculine, indefinite, and nominative as
the predicate of `ٱللَّهُ`. The supplied `ء ح د` account directly recalls
`قل هو الله أحد`, equates `أحد` with the one, gives it unrestricted descriptive
use in this named context, relates it to the recovered origin `وحد`, and
permits emphatic recurrence `أحد أحد`. The direct proposition fixes the
passage-local assertion as God's absolute oneness. Number, derivation,
description, and reiteration remain distinguishable facets: the grammar
asserts one nominative predicate, not a count performed on the passage and not
two occupants created by repetition.

The source independently supplies the semantic equation with `الواحد`. Under
unvocalized base-letter comparison, `احد` also occurs after the initial waw in
`واحد`, but the written difference between `أ` and `ا` remains. The semantic
equation and the normalized formal contact reinforce one another without
being mistaken for the same kind of relation.

The same root field also contains unit-plus-tens composition, firstness in
either side of an إضافة, the named first day `الأحد`, acting alone or arriving
`آحاد أحاد`, and the mountain named `أحد`. These operations do not replace
absolute oneness. They return more precisely through the passage's form. Unit
composition shows that one may enter a larger expression without becoming its
peer; firstness supplies an ordinal relation but no calendar for the four
lines; distributed individuals retain separate occupants rather than merging
a group; and the mountain supplies a concrete located mass only in the
terrain assembly opened by the next predicate. The form's complete field is
active, but the first `أَحَدٌ` remains the exact positive pole of the passage.

Line 2 moves `ٱللَّهُ` from the third word of a quoted clause to the first word
of a new clause and predicates the definite nominative `ٱلصَّمَدُ`. The two
positive clauses thus act successively on the same participant. Absolute
oneness establishes God without a peer set; self-sufficient refuge gives that
same participant an outwardly addressable relational function. The predicates
are not synonyms. Their interaction creates a positive axis: the one God is
also the endpoint toward which dependence may be directed, while the closing
comparison will prevent that endpoint from being recast as one member among
equals.

The decisive lexical lock for refuge is the repeated source relation
`الصمد السيد لأنه يصمد إليه في الحوائج`: an intending seeker carries an
affair or need toward an endpoint relied upon. Bound to the passage's naming
of God as the self-sufficient refuge, its complete geometry is

`open seeker or dependent -> need -> قصد واعتماد -> God as self-sufficient
refuge -> particular response open`.

Refuge is therefore an asymmetric direction, not only a static title. Need and
reliance belong on the approaching side; self-sufficiency belongs to the
endpoint. The lexical lords, affairs, and `بيت مصمد` retain their own local
occupants while supplying this topology. The passage fills God as the primary
endpoint but does not name a seeker, a concrete need, or an answer. A saying
can be projected as carried material on the route, yet the commanded
declaration does not thereby become a request and the Prophet does not thereby
become the open seeker.

Other directional operations make the asymmetry more exact. A saying can be
drawn inward to oneself; a group can be diverted away from its intended route;
an overseer can stand over an affair and attend seriously to it; a person can
direct sincere care toward a thing; a guarantor can stand for another in a
continuing obligation. These yield appropriation, failed arrival, oversight,
care, and responsibility as distinct projected routes around need. An
attended or guaranteed refuge is a complete possible division of roles, but no
oversight event, guarantee, beneficiary, or response is asserted by the
primary line. Direction and endpoint are complete even where aftermath remains
open.

## The Geometries of Refuge

The other `ص م د` relations give self-sufficiency and refuge several
independent material and temporal geometries. The source field first supplies
hardness, compactness, an elevated or thick place, an anchored rock, severe
ground, absence of a cavity, and absence of grooves. Projected through
`ٱلصَّمَدُ`, compact resistance figures a stable destination with no inward
hollow or deficit. The mountain named `أحد` can occupy the large elevated
place role, and the `ك و ن` field can supply location or establishment, but
mountain, rock, ground, and God remain different occupants. Material height is
also distinct from social `مكانة`, from an overseer's position over an affair,
from a ruler's rank below a greater ruler, and from a guarantor's obligation
over another. Their common positional affordance does not erase the operation
that makes each station physical, attentional, social, or juridical.

A second source operation applies a stopper, `صماد`, to a bottle. Here an
interior and opening exist; a controller changes an open vessel into one whose
boundary retains what is inside. This geometry is not the cavityless solid.
The noun `صماد` retains its medial alif and is not the exact written form
`صمد`; the connection is supplied by the complete stopper operation and by
base-bearing verbs such as `صمدتها` and `أصمدها`.
Putting a stopper against what has no cavity yields no applicable opening;
putting it on a vessel closes a real boundary. A separate inclination or
overturning force from the `ك ف ء` field produces two further outcomes: a
stoppered vessel resists an outward route, whereas an open one acquires a
spill position. Contents, opener, and later restoration remain unspecified,
but the difference between no interior, retained interior, and exposed
interior is complete.

A third source operation wraps a head with cloth, kerchief, or garment while
explicitly excluding a turban. The `ك و ن` expression `موضع العمامة` may
supply the body site, but it cannot supply the excluded medium. A fourth
boundary operation uses one or two sewn pieces to cover the rear of a house or
tent. With two pieces, joining is complementary rather than proof of equality;
with one, a partner is not required. Projected through refuge, these operations
make protection an applied cover around a body region or an architectural
rear. The sought `بيت مصمد` can become a reached, covered shelter, yet the
maker, material, inhabitant, and seeker remain in their source relations or
unnamed. Refuge can thus be destination, resistance, retention, bodily cover,
or sheltering boundary without becoming a literal rock, bottle, cloth, or tent.

The exact difference among these geometries also clarifies the passage's later
negations. No cavity is a structural absence. A stopper blocks a route while
preserving an interior. A wrap or sewn piece protects a region. An unspoken
content remains behind an expressive boundary, and a definition closes a
conceptual field. `لَمْ`, by contrast, denies an event or relation. These are
different boundary operations with different surviving participants and
aftermaths. The concrete assemblies make the three negative clauses more
precise without converting relational denial into physical sealing.

The source field also names `الصمد` as enduring and remaining after others'
annihilation. This gives the self-sufficient refuge a temporal dimension:
the destination does not cease at the adversity or terminal boundary through
which the source relation carries continuance. A separate source tuple speaks
of a camel enduring cold and barrenness while continuing to send milk. Joined
with the annual-yield field, that local animal operation can branch into full
or incomplete production, milk, wool, offspring, a recipient, and two herd
cohorts alternating by year. It is a concrete counterfield, not a divine
lineage. It proves that endurance and production are independent parameters:
a carrier may persist and send products, may persist with an incomplete yield,
or may renew production by phases. God's refuge can receive duration from the
endurance relation while the next line separately denies begetting and being
begotten. Persistence does not entail lineage, and blocked lineage does not
make refuge momentary.

Force supplies one more independent geometry. The exact lexical tuples
`صمده بالعصا ... ضربه بها` and `القال الخشبة التي تضرب بها القلة` map a
controller through a stick or wooden implement to an affected target and
impact. A spear between two horsemen supplies mediation but not, by itself, a
strike; projection of strike force through it branches toward either target.
Tilting, overturning, diverting, swaying, and a changed face replace impact
with altered orientation or state. An effective word replaces a material
implement with changed agency, while the source expression about warding the
sun redirects pressure away from a protected endpoint. Against
`ٱلصَّمَدُ`, resistance and warding can project a refuge that receives or
redirects pressure. These force routes remain distinct from directed need:
need approaches an endpoint, pressure meets counterforce, and literal impact
has no passage target. The distinction allows the commanded word to be
potentially consequential and the refuge to be protective without inventing a
striker, victim, or coercive outcome in the passage.

## The Double Lineage Block

The passage turns from two positive nominal predications to two coordinated
negated verbal clauses:

`لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ`.

Both verbs are imperfect jussive third-person masculine singular forms of
`وَلَدَ`, and both are governed by `لَمْ`. The first is active. God is exposed
as grammatical controller and semantic source, while a child or product role
would lie downstream; negation prevents the event and does not mint an
occupant for that role. The second is passive. God is exposed as the affected,
begotten position, the initiating parent is suppressed by voice, and negation
again prevents the event. Suppression and denial are not the same operation:
the passive grammar leaves its initiator unexpressed, while `لَمْ` denies that
any birth event occurred.

The positive lexical topology makes the two blocks concrete. A mother can
give birth, a carried child can cross into a born state, an attendant can
oversee without becoming the mother, and a day can mark the boundary. Father
and mother are distinct parent roles and may be named together as a dual.
Offspring terms can range over one or many, male or female, young or older.
The vocabulary of a newborn and the separate vocabulary of an enslaved-status
boy or girl, including an older woman still called `وليدة` in the status use,
show that age and social status are independent. None of these source-local
participants enters the passage. They specify what an active or passive birth
would require, and therefore what each denial prevents.

The resulting primary operation is bidirectional. `لَمْ يَلِدْ` closes an
outward lineage transition with God as source. `وَلَمْ يُولَدْ` closes an
inward lineage transition with God as product. Read together, they prevent a
child from being produced from God and prevent God from being produced from a
parental source. The same participant has occupied both sides of one complete
schema without undergoing either event. The second clause changes the first
backward by revealing its source direction; the first changes the second by
revealing its affected direction.

Placed immediately after `ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ`, the double block gives
self-sufficiency an exact lineage surround. The refuge is positively
established, while an outward begotten result and an inward derivation from a
begetting source are both denied. These exclusions delimit the two lineage
sides of the positive state; they neither reduce refuge to non-generation nor
extend the denial to every possible causal relation.

The formal bodies of the verbs enact a further asymmetry. Passive
`يُولَدْ`, after its initial `ي`, visibly contains the released base `ولد` and
exactly meets supplied phrases such as `حين يولد`. Active `يَلِدْ` is bound to
the same lemma and root by morphology but does not contain the base in that
letter order. The direction that suppresses its initiator preserves the base
letters; the direction that exposes its controller transforms them. This does
not add a new denial, but it makes voice and controller distribution audible
inside the forms.

The wider `و ل د` field maps the same source-to-result topology beyond
biological birth. `تولد الشيء من الشيء` names a result arising from a source
through a cause; a newly coined saying, a mixed or environment-formed person
or language, a fabricated book, and generated but unverified evidence are
different possible products and aftermaths. Production, novelty, purity,
fabrication, and verification remain separate parameters. The general mapping
makes source and result directions intelligible in line 3, but the primary
denial remains exactly begetting and begotten; it is not silently expanded
into a denial of every causal operation. Likewise, the recovered form
`ولدة -> لدة` and the relation `لدة الرجل تربه` produce a same-age peer, not a
parent or child. That peer belongs to the positive comparison counterfield of
the final line.

The whole passage now distributes control over results with unusual
precision. In line 1, command source, intended controller, and exact content
are supplied, but a new declaration token remains commanded rather than
separately completed. In line 2, the refuge predication is positively
established and is not a generated material product. In line 3, outward and
inward lineage results are denied at opposite ports. In line 4, the occurrence
of a comparable relation will be denied although its predicate and endpoint
remain present. An internal saying that has not crossed, an incomplete annual
yield, and generated but unverified evidence show still other result states:
present content without performance, existing product without completion,
and existing artifact without verification. None can be substituted for the
terminally denied birth events.

## Three Negative Gates

The complete negator sequence is

`لَمْ -> وَ + لَمْ -> وَ + لَمْ`.

The first `لَمْ` opens line 3 and governs active `يَلِدْ`. The first complete
`وَلَمْ` renews negation inside the line and changes voice to passive
`يُولَدْ`. The exact word `وَلَمْ` then returns at the opening of line 4 and
carries the negative sequence across a line boundary to `يَكُن`. Its governed
relation changes from production by God, to production of God, to comparison
with God. The passage's negative architecture is consequently directional:
an outward lineage route is denied, an inward lineage route is denied, and a
lateral peer route is denied.

Each gate leaves a different structure in view. The first retains God as
exposed source but has no event or product. The second retains God as affected
role, has no event, and also has an unexpressed passive initiator. The third
retains God as endpoint, `كُفُوًا` as predicate, and `أَحَدٌ` as an exhaustive
candidate class, but no comparison relation occurs. These are not increasing
degrees of one generic absence.

The other supplied vacancies make that distinction exact. `في نفسي قول لم
أظهره` preserves content while withholding performance. `لا جوف له` preserves
a resistant object while making closure inapplicable. `ليست تامة كفأة`
preserves a yield but withholds completion. `بينة مولدة وليست بمحققة`
preserves a generated result but withholds verification. `لا يكون إلاها حتى
يكون معبودا` withholds a role until a prerequisite relation occurs. False
attribution can occur even when the claimed speaker's performance did not.
Against these variants, the primary `لَمْ` clauses are terminal for the exact
events and relation in their scope. The passive unknown remains unknown; it is
not a vacancy that another branch may populate.

Time and modality follow the same differentiation. `قُلْ` makes a declaration
actable but not separately accomplished. The two imperfect jussives encode
negated event schemas, not two moments in a positive biography. The passage's
closing is formal succession through arranged lines, not a calendar period.
The lexical first day `الأحد`, a birth day or impending birth, a one-year
yield, coeval age, an old man's report `كُنْتُ في شبابي`, a night spent in a
bad condition, and open-ended endurance all retain their own time scales.
They delimit command, denial, formal order, and persistence without turning
the four lines into one invented chronology.

## The Candidate Who Arrives Only as a Class

The closing clause is ordered

`وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ`.

`يَكُن` is an imperfect jussive of `كَانَ`, governed by the third `لَمْ`.
`كُفُوًا` is its accusative predicate, `أَحَدٌۢ` its delayed nominative
subject, and the pronoun in `لَّهُۥ` the complement endpoint of comparison.
The semantic route is therefore candidate `anyone` to comparable predicate to
God as endpoint, with occurrence denied. Surface order presents endpoint and
relation before it delivers the candidate at the last word of the passage.
The delay is itself an enactment: when the subject finally arrives, it arrives
as an exhaustive class under negation and supplies no comparable occupant.

Morphology releases lemma `كُفُو` from the accusative surface `كُفُوًا`; the
full form retains its final accusative material. The lexical relations use
hamza-bearing forms such as `كفء`, `كفئ`, `كفاءة`, and `تكافؤ`. Their root and
complete counterpart topology connect them to the surface, but their exact
spellings are not flattened into `كفو`. The decisive bridge is therefore the
whole relation `candidate -> counterpart predicate -> له endpoint`, not a
manufactured substring.

The positive lexical topology is explicit. `الكفء` is `المثل` or `النظير`;
`التكافؤ` is equality; `هذا كفء له` places one comparand in relation to an
endpoint. Other source examples test equality in lineage, wealth, war, or
marriage, return an act with an act like it, place two horsemen in relation
through a spear, or use a dual medium to ward the sun. These source-local
relations specify candidates, measures, directions, and possible reciprocal
aftermaths. The passage supplies none of those restricted domains. Instead,
the negative use of final `أَحَدٌۢ` ranges through anyone, from one through
more than one, and `لَمْ يَكُن` denies that the relation obtains. The search
has a present endpoint, a present predicate schema, and a complete candidate
domain, but no successful candidate.

The `ك و ن` field further specifies what the denial targets. It defines
occurrence and presence, `كان الشيء يكون كونا إذا وقع وحضر`, and also supplies
caused occurrence, place and station, guarantee, submission, remembered former
state, and a bad condition through a night. In the passage, the direct
proposition fixes the possible occurrence as anyone's being comparable to
God. `لَمْ يَكُن` blocks that relation's occurrence; it does not deny place,
social station,
responsibility, submission, memory, or every state named elsewhere in the
root field. The source-local guarantor, submissive participant, aged reporter,
and person in a bad night retain their own controllers and open aftermaths.
The surface `يَكُن` is related morphologically to `كَانَ`; lexical `يكون`
retains a medial waw and is not an exact containment. Its exact external
contact remains the supplied sequence `لم يكن` in the false-speech relation.

The other `ك ف ء` operations distinguish an absent comparable from relations
that merely resemble comparison. An inversion may begin with equality and
end in `إزالة المساواة`; lost equality presupposes an earlier equal state,
whereas the passage admits no comparable occurrence. Two sewn pieces are
complementary in making a cover, not necessarily equal. Two herd cohorts
alternate by year, not simultaneously. One joined to tens forms a composite
number rather than a peer pair. The same-age `لدة` relation yields a positive
peer under the measure of birth age, but that lexical peer cannot populate the
negated subject. Reciprocal action makes one complete like-for-like return;
negotiation makes a different exchange cycle. Each is a formed positive
counterfield that clarifies what the passage does not instantiate.

The contrast with refuge is decisive. Both dependence and comparison terminate
at God, but their other participant is differently related. An open dependent
may direct a need toward God as refuge; every possible peer candidate is denied
comparability with Him. Access to an endpoint is not equality with it. The
shared endpoint transfers neither the seeker into the candidate class nor the
candidate into a dependent role. Thus `ٱلصَّمَدُ` and `كُفُوًا` define two
opposed relational geometries around the same God: positive asymmetrical
orientation and absent lateral equivalence.

## The Exact Return of `أَحَدٌ`

The last word is the exact lexical, morphological, and case return of the
first line's predicate: masculine, indefinite, nominative `أَحَدٌ`. Its
syntax and polarity have reversed. At 112:1:4 it is an affirmative predicate
of God and names absolute oneness. At 112:4:5 it is the delayed subject of a
negated copular clause and names the exhaustive class `anyone`. The complete
passage lies between these two positions.

This recurrence turns absolute oneness into a two-boundary operation. The
opening pole positively predicates God as `أَحَدٌ`. The closing pole places
every candidate under the topology of `كُفُوًا لَهُ` and returns no occupant.
The same form therefore changes from description to search domain, from
predicate to subject, from affirmative quote-close to negative passage-close.
The final denial does not merely repeat the first proposition in negative
language. It supplies the lateral relation whose exhaustive absence completes
the positive predicate. Read backward, no comparable anyone reaches the
opening one God; read forward, absolute oneness passes through refuge and both
lineage blocks before closing every peer position.

Two returns intersect in the final clause without merging. `هُۥ` returns by
coreference to `ٱللَّهُ`; `أَحَدٌۢ` returns by exact form to the opening
predicate. One carries the same God participant, the other carries a changed
word-role and a different candidate class. Their intersection in
`لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ` gives closure two axes: the endpoint is recovered,
and the enclosing form is recovered. God and anyone remain categorically
distinct even as the two returns meet.

## The Passage as an Arranged Utterance

The formal groupings enact the same participant architecture. Across four
lines, the complete word-count vector is `[4,2,4,5]`. Line 1 divides into one
imperative plus a three-word quotation; line 3 also has four words but divides
into two balanced negative clauses. Equal word count thus changes grouping
from command-with-embedded-content to active/passive coordination. Line 2
compresses the positive refuge predication into two words. Line 4 expands to
five so that the endpoint and predicate can precede the delayed subject.

The complete clause/event vector is `[2,1,2,1]`: command plus quoted
predication, one refuge predication, two coordinated lineage denials, and one
closing comparison denial. Its alternating pairs and singles are internally
different. The first pair embeds content under a command; the second rotates
voice under negation. The first single is a positive nominal predication; the
last is a negative copular relation whose subject is delayed. The continuous
God referent is carried by a matching line vector `[2,1,2,1]`: `هُوَ` and
`ٱللَّهُ` in line 1, `ٱللَّهُ` in line 2, two implicit verbal stations in
line 3, and suffix `هُۥ` in line 4. This formal convergence does not identify
clauses with pronouns. It shows that the participant spine is redistributed at
the same alternating scale through which the passage groups its actions.

Morphological carrier counts `[4,3,5,7]` and syntax-edge counts `[3,1,3,4]`
make the closing movement increasingly explicit in a different way. Line 2
splits the determiner `ٱل` from `صَّمَدُ`; line 3 splits conjunction `وَ` from
its negator; line 4 retains that split and also separates preposition `لَّ`
from pronoun `هُۥ`. The final single clause is therefore the most
morphologically and syntactically articulated: government, comparison
complement, copular predicate, and delayed subject all have their own formal
work. Its density enacts the separation between the candidate controlling
agreement and God serving as endpoint. The root-bearing-stem vector
`[3,2,2,3]` also returns to three, changing the opening roots of command, name,
and oneness into the closing roots of occurrence, comparison, and anyone.

At the letter scale, the complete normalized line totals are `[11,9,12,15]`,
for a passage total of 47. They contract at the positive two-word line and
then increase by three in each negative line. This pattern is distinct from
word, morpheme, clause, and syntax counts; it does not supply an arithmetic
interpretation of oneness. It shows instead that different complete unit
systems organize the same passage without being averaged into one measure.

Exact word recurrence articulates the two halves. `ٱللَّهُ` moves from the
third word of line 1 to the first word of line 2, carrying the named referent
from the oneness predicate into the refuge predicate. `وَلَمْ` likewise moves
from the third word of line 3 to the first word of line 4, carrying negation
from the passive lineage clause into the comparison clause. These parallel
third-to-first handoffs perform opposite work: the first renews the participant
under a new positive predicate; the second renews a negative governor under a
new relation. `أَحَدٌ`, by contrast, remains a line-ending form while moving
from the end of the first line to the end of the whole passage. Continuity is
therefore enacted by both displacement and fixed boundary position.

The line openings progress
`قُلْ -> ٱللَّهُ -> لَمْ -> وَلَمْ`: imperative, named participant, negator,
and conjunctively renewed negator. The line endings progress
`أَحَدٌ -> ٱلصَّمَدُ -> يُولَدْ -> أَحَدٌۢ`: noun predicate, noun predicate,
passive verb, delayed noun subject. Every ending has written `د`, so the
complete boundary consonant vector is `[د,د,د,د]`, while grammar changes from
nominative tanwin, to nominative definite damma, to jussive sukun, and back to
nominative tanwin. The lexical account of poetic `إكفاء` explicitly compares
ending letters, movements, and inflections. Applied here, its comparison does
not yield the source example's discordant `م` and `ن`; it reveals constant
`د` with changed case, mood, definiteness, category, voice, role, and polarity.
The acoustic return keeps each line's work distinct.

These arrangements complete two passage-wide systems at once. The
source-to-result system distributes a commanded output still open, a positive
refuge predication, two blocked lineage transitions, and a blocked comparison
occurrence. The directional system sends command from the passage to the
Prophet and possible declaration outward from him; admits need inward toward
God as refuge; blocks lineage outward from God and inward toward God; and
blocks lateral equivalence with God. One referent holds the field together,
but controllers and directions rotate around it. The Prophet is commanded to
declare but is not the content. God bears the positive predicates but does not
control `يَكُن`. The passive source is unexpressed. Anyone controls the closing verb's
agreement as delayed subject but never becomes an actual comparable.

The ending therefore returns to the command with more than a summary. Final
`هُۥ` recovers the God named in the quote; final `أَحَدٌۢ` recovers the form
that first predicated absolute oneness; repeated `وَلَمْ` has carried the
lineage block into lateral closure; and the four `د` boundaries have made the
whole content a composed utterance ready for the carrier opened by `قُلْ`.
What the Prophet is commanded to declare is now heard as the entire arranged
movement: God is positively named as one and as self-sufficient refuge,
outward and inward lineage are denied around Him, and every comparable
candidate is exhausted at the returning form. The last word thus sends the
passage back to its first act. `قُلْ` opens not an isolated label but this
complete declaration.
