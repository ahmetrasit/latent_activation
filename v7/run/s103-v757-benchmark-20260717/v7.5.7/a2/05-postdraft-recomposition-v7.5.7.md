# The Oath's Measure: From Contained Loss to Reciprocal Keeping

> وَٱلْعَصْرِ
>
> إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ
>
> إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ

The passage moves through three sharply different constructions. An oath first
places `ٱلْعَصْرِ` under invocation. An emphatic predication then places the
human being `فِى خُسْرٍ`, inside loss. An exception finally identifies a plural
group through four coordinated perfect verbs: believing, doing righteous
deeds, mutually counseling one another with truth, and mutually counseling one
another with patience. Its movement is therefore not merely from a bad state
to a list of good qualities. It is a redistribution of relations. The singular
human is first the affected term of a containing condition. The excepted group
then becomes a plural agent, its activity passing from assent, to an acted-on
object, to two reciprocal exchanges whose contents are explicitly governed by
`بـ`.

The lexical fields sharpen that movement. Belief can be described as
`إذعان النفس للحق`, the self's assent directed to truth. Counsel can be defined
as an advance toward another concerning something to be acted upon, and its
reciprocal form as some people counseling others. Patience can be described as
`حبس النفس عن الجزع`, the holding of the self away from distress. These are not
three interchangeable inward virtues. They give the same plural group a
sequence of different orientations: a self toward truth, a deed brought into
effect, truth passed between members as content, and a self held away from a
countermovement. The oath by `ٱلْعَصْرِ` surrounds this sequence with a word
whose field includes time, pressure, extraction, restraint, refuge, yield, and
succession. The final patience does not simply end the series. It returns the
passage to the opening problem of what is held, what is released, and what an
interval makes visible.

## Three constructions and a changing center

The small scale of the passage makes every grammatical change consequential.
Its three lines contain fourteen orthographic words and thirty analyzed
morphemes. The first line is one orthographic word made of the oath particle,
the definite article, and the genitive noun `عَصْرِ`. The second has four words:
`إِنَّ`, its definite accusative noun `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ`, the emphatic `لَ` joined to
the preposition `فِى`, and the indefinite genitive `خُسْرٍ`. The third has nine
words but twenty-one morphemes. Its density comes from conjunctions, articles,
prepositions, and the repeated third-person masculine plural suffixes that
keep attaching the four predicates to one group.

The three openings establish three kinds of force. `وَ` opens the oath and
governs its genitive complement. `إِنَّ` opens an emphatic clause, governs the
case of `ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ`, and receives `لَفِى خُسْرٍ` as its predication. `إِلَّا`
opens an exception whose relative expression is not complete at the relative
pronoun. Its scope extends through the final `بِٱلصَّبْرِ`. The reader must
therefore carry `ٱلَّذِينَ` across the whole verbal span before the identity of
the group is fully given. The closing word is part of the exception's
definition, not an optional comment added after it.

The four predicates are all perfect and third-person masculine plural. The
first is form IV, the next is a simple verbal form, and the last two repeat the
same form VI verb. The shared person, number, and aspect make one coordinated
agency audible, while the different constructions prevent the predicates from
collapsing into synonyms. `ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ` is a definite feminine plural
accusative direct object of `عَمِلُوا۟`. By contrast, `ٱلْحَقِّ` and
`ٱلصَّبْرِ` are definite masculine genitive nouns governed by the two instances
of `بـ`. Action reaches an explicit object. Reciprocal counsel is specified by
content. The grammar does not supply either counsel's individual addressee,
because reciprocity already distributes speaker and recipient roles within the
plural group.

This produces a formal sequence of relations. The human begins `فِى` a state.
The plural group believes without an expressed surface object, acts directly
upon a named object, and then exchanges two named contents `بـ`. The lexical
definitions add, without replacing those surface relations, three further
directions: belief can orient the self `لـ` truth; counsel can advance `إلى`
another; patience can hold the self `عن` distress. The prepositions are not
decorative variants. `فِى` makes loss a surrounding condition, `لـ` gives
assent an endpoint, `إلى` gives counsel an addressee or destination, `بـ` gives
the reciprocal act its content, and `عن` marks what restraint keeps away from.
The passage moves from containment to directedness, effect, exchange, and
separation.

Its pattern of case and definiteness gives that movement a quieter frame. The
oath ends in the definite genitive `ٱلْعَصْرِ`; the central condition ends in
the indefinite genitive `خُسْرٍ`; the whole passage ends in the definite
genitive `ٱلصَّبْرِ`. All three line endings return to the consonantal closure
of `ر`, but the middle term alone carries indefiniteness and tanwin. Between
two definite limits stands an unbounded loss. The final line does not define
that loss by a commodity, quantity, or single failure. It defines the people
excluded from it by an articulated plural practice.

## Time invoked, pressure withheld

The primary temporal field of `ع ص ر` is already multiple. `العصر` can name an
age or extended time, an interval, a day or night, the afternoon, or the time
of the afternoon prayer. The dual `العصران` can join night and day or morning
and evening. Even notices of delay or arriving outside an expected time remain
questions of placement within an interval. The oath therefore invokes more
than an abstract substance called time. It opens a field of succession,
division, recurrence, and the difference between a fitting moment and a missed
one.

That temporal multiplicity has an exact counterpart in the field of counsel.
One explanation of `و ص ي` says `وصيت الليلة باليوم وصلتها`, "I joined the
night to the day." The contact does not make the two roots identical. It gives
the passage a precise bridge: the opening field supplies night and day as
successive temporal members, while the closing verbal field supplies the act
of joining one to the other. The first word invokes an interval structured by
succession; the repeated counsel near the end enacts connection within the
human group. Time passes by linked terms, and the exception persists through
linked persons.

The same opening root also names pressing grapes, olives, or another body until
liquid is released. It names the juice, the expressed yield, the press, and the
thing pressed. One source distinguishes undertaking the pressing oneself. This
field gives `ٱلْعَصْرِ` a transformation schema: pressure acts on a bounded
body; what had been interior becomes a visible yield. The oath's surface noun
does not grammatically state that the passage itself is a press, nor does it
license choosing extraction instead of time as its sole sense. Yet pressure
clarifies the ensuing disclosure. The emphatic clause exposes what the general
human condition contains, and the exception exposes what kind of activity can
issue from a group within that condition.

Cloud and air repeat the relation at another scale. The rain cloud is heavy
with or brings rain, then releases it. A whirlwind raises dust like a column;
the wake of perfume rises behind a passing garment. Interior content, carried
content, and released trace are distinct positions in this field. They keep
the passage from treating effect as instantaneous. A cloud may bear before it
rains, just as counsel may contain a content before any later action taken in
response. The grammar names the truth and patience counseled; it does not name
the later uptake. Bearing, release, and effect must remain separate.

The root's opposite-looking branches make that distinction stronger. `العصر`
can be restraint or confinement; a person can be prevented, property can be
extracted from a hand, withheld from its possessor, reclaimed after a gift, or
held by one characterized as withholding. The same family also names gift,
good yield, and extracted gain. Refuge and attachment occupy the boundary
between these directions: one clings to a person or place as a means of
escape. Thus the opening field does not offer a flat metaphor of pressure. It
organizes transfers across a boundary: holding in, taking out, giving forth,
taking back, and holding on for safety.

Other branches mark thresholds rather than transfers. A person drinks water
little by little to make obstructing food pass. A girl reaches or approaches a
stage of maturity, recognizes the increase in herself, and in one account is
confined in a house. A crop becomes enclosed in its sheath. Lineage returns a
person to an origin, while a low social relation marks rank by contrast.
Names of a tree, a tongue dried by thirst, and bodily wind occupy still more
distant edges of the lexical family. Their value is partly restrictive. They
show that no single concrete picture can simply be substituted for the
surface oath. What survives their differences is a set of relations involving
interval, boundary, ripening, enclosure, pressure, and issue.

The oath holds these possibilities before the predication has named its human
subject. It is definite, but its definiteness does not reduce the invoked field
to one object inside the next clause. It establishes the frame in which
containment, diminishment, action, connection, and restraint can be compared.
Only at the end will the passage disclose why an opening capable of naming
both release and confinement is apt.

## The human inside an indefinite loss

`إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ` is compact but not static. `إِنَّ` and the
emphatic `لَ` intensify the predication. The definite singular
`ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ` is governed as the noun of `إِنَّ`, while the preposition places
that human term within an indefinite genitive condition. The statement does
not merely say that the human has lost something. Its syntax makes loss the
surrounding relation in which the human is found.

The field of `ء ن س` enlarges the subject without dissolving its grammar. It
includes the human being as one and humanity as a collective, the visible
human in contrast to what is wild or unseen, and the absence of any human
presence in a dwelling. It also includes recognizing by sight, hearing, or
sense; perceiving a sign of soundness; and finding something within oneself.
A further branch concerns familiarity and companionship, the nearness that
removes estrangement. Another names the side of an animal or bow facing the
human user. The image seen in the pupil, the fingertip or part of the hand, the
self, the intimate, and the chosen companion extend the family from species to
perception, orientation, miniature image, body part, inwardness, and social
proximity.

These branches do not populate the clause with a viewer, rider, companion, or
second self. They reveal capacities gathered under the human noun: appearing,
perceiving, facing, becoming familiar, and referring inward. That makes the
predication severe in a particular way. The being capable of detecting and
approaching is nevertheless grammatically enclosed by loss. Perception is not
yet exemption, and companionship is not yet the reciprocal practice named in
the final line.

The field of `خ س ر` explains why the condition remains indefinite. Its base
is diminution or the going away of worth. In trade it is loss of capital or a
transaction without profit. In weighing and measuring it is making the scale,
measure, or weighed thing deficient. A small group of marked expanded forms
can be used for ruin, meanness, or weakness, but those formations remain
distinct from the surface noun. Across the central branches, loss is a
relation between what should have remained or been rendered and what is now
less.

The clause supplies no scale against which the deficit is calculated. It names
neither capital nor merchandise, neither weight nor missing quantity. That
absence is not vagueness to be repaired by selecting one lexical example. It
is the force of `خُسْرٍ` as an indefinite condition. Commercial loss and short
measure provide exact models of diminution, but no merchant or measurer enters
the passage. The human is in loss before the sentence identifies any local
account.

A later cross-relation makes the distinction especially clear. The patience
family can name a pile of food sold without weight or measure. The loss family
can name the active diminishing of weight or measure. An unmeasured pile and a
deficient measure are not the same event. Their contact identifies measure as
a governing question while keeping two roles apart: absence of measurement on
one side, and reduction within measurement on the other. The oath's interval
is likewise not itself the loss, but it supplies the span in which diminution
can be disclosed.

The second line therefore establishes the problem in its broadest grammar. A
definite human term is inside an indefinite deficit. Nothing in that line
provides an outward action, an explicit object, another human recipient, or a
content passed between persons. All of those relations arrive only after
`إِلَّا`.

## The exception gives agency a plural form

`إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ` changes the clause's distribution without denying the
generality that precedes it. The excepted group is plural, but its members are
not introduced by names, ranks, lineages, bodily positions, or possessions.
They are defined predicatively. The four perfect verbs are the content of the
relative expression and together complete the exception's scope.

That syntactic fact prevents two reductions. First, the exception cannot be
localized in the first predicate alone, as though belief made the remaining
three clauses supplementary. The relative group is still being specified
through the final word. Second, the four predicates cannot be detached into
four independent populations. Every plural suffix returns to the same
controller. The ones who believe are the ones who act, and the acting group is
the group within which truth and patience circulate reciprocally.

The conjunctions mark addition, but the ordered constructions add a semantic
direction. The first predicate establishes an orientation of assent. The
second gives that group an effective relation to a definite object. The third
and fourth transform the group into a field in which each member can occupy
both sides of counsel. The repeated form does not just double an instruction.
It changes the architecture from an agent facing an object to members facing
one another around a shared content.

The perfect aspect gives the definition a completed verbal profile. It does
not by itself state that every consequence of counsel has already occurred.
In particular, the field of recommendation can specify something another is
to act upon, but the surface clause does not name the later act or its result.
The perfect verbs define what the group has done; they do not close every
future route opened by that doing.

Nor does the exception function as one of the lexical thresholds supplied by
the opening family. A girl's maturation, a crop entering its sheath, a camel
reaching carrying age, or an animal crossing a bodily threshold may illuminate
changes of state, but none becomes the grammatical mechanism of `إِلَّا`.
The exception is a scope relation. Its force is to exclude a group from the
general predication by means of the whole coordinated description.

## The self, the deed, and the fitting object

The first predicate, `ءَامَنُوا۟`, opens the field of security and affirmation.
One branch of `ء م ن` gathers safety against fear, trust against betrayal,
something entrusted, a secure dwelling, seeking another's protection, the
trustworthy person, and even the dependable camel whose stumbling or fatigue
is not feared. Another gives the surface verb's central relation: belief as
affirmation, confirmation of a report or promise, and, in an exact definition,
`إذعان النفس للحق`. A third concerns saying `آمين` as a request for response,
"let it be so," or "respond." These branches distinguish settled security,
assent to truth, and a speech act that requests realization.

The exact definition of belief establishes an important inward direction. The
self is not truth, and truth is not a feeling produced by the self. The self
yields assent `للحق`, toward or to truth. The passage does not surface that
prepositional complement beside `ءَامَنُوا۟`, so it should not be inserted as
though written there. Yet when `ٱلْحَقِّ` appears later as the content of
reciprocal counsel, the exact lexical recurrence matters. What first orients a
self becomes what the group passes among its members.

The self itself crosses several lexical fields in changing roles. Security is
`طمأنينة النفس`, the self's calm after fear is removed. Belief is the self's
assent to truth. The work family notes `اعتمل إذا عمل لنفسه`, one acting for
oneself, although that derived form is not the surface `عَمِلُوا۟`. The human
family uses the self in a question about one's inward condition; the opening
family lets a maturing girl perceive change `في نفسها`; patience is
`حبس النفس عن الجزع`. These exact recurrences produce no single hidden
participant. They produce a sequence of self-relations: a self settled, a self
directed, a self potentially made beneficiary of work, a self perceived from
within, and a self held away from distress.

The surface sequence makes a decisive choice within that field. After belief,
it does not say that the group worked "for itself." `عَمِلُوا۟` takes the
explicit direct object `ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ`. The lexical return to `لنفسه` can show
that work is capable of bending toward the agent as beneficiary, but the
clause's grammar bends outward toward a definite object. The relation to self
remains possible within the root field, not supplied as the syntax of this
predicate.

`ع م ل` begins with intentional doing, every act an animal performs with
purpose, and explicitly includes good and bad works. Its other branches
differentiate causing another to work, requesting work, operating the mind,
opinion, speech, spear, or building material, holding an office, receiving a
wage, entering a transaction, and doing manual labor with the hands. Exertion
in an affair, a disposition fitted to labor, the working section of a spear,
an animal's limbs or a far-seeing eye, a trodden road, and travelers walking on
their legs extend action into instrument, capacity, part, route, and sustained
movement.

The breadth of the field makes the direct object more, not less, important.
The passage does not praise undirected busyness. It names the object as
`ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ`. The action family supplies intention and execution; the
goodness family qualifies what is done. Their grammatical bond is stronger
than a thematic association because the feminine plural accusative is the
verb's explicit object.

`ص ل ح` names soundness or goodness against corruption, repairing rather than
spoiling, and the goodness of a person or work. It also names reconciliation,
the removal of estrangement or dispute between people. A thing can be fitting
for someone, `يصلح لك`, and improving treatment toward an animal can be
described as doing good to it. Personal and place names occupy separate
nominal branches. Those proper names do not identify the passage's object;
they instead mark the limit between a lexical shape and its contextual role.

Three operational aspects of goodness meet the sequence. Repair changes a
damaged state. Fitness relates a thing to the one for whom it is suitable.
Reconciliation changes a relation between persons. The surface object keeps
these from floating as abstractions: the group does the righteous things. At
the same time, the reconciliation branch anticipates a social field without
being confused with counsel. Reconciliation removes estrangement or dispute;
reciprocal counsel passes a content. They can support one another, but they
are different edge types.

The first half of the exception has thus already changed the center twice.
Belief orients the self; action reaches a named object. Neither relation yet
requires the internal role rotation that the repeated form VI verbs will
introduce.

## Truth becomes shared content

The first `وَتَوَاصَوْا۟` changes the group's geometry. The field of `و ص ي`
begins with connection: joining one thing to another, joining night to day,
connected plants, continuous ground, and one open tract leading into another.
Its instruction branch treats a testament or recommendation as speech joined
and delivered to another. It distinguishes what is bequeathed, a testament
after death, appointing another as executor, and the roles of testator and
recipient. One definition is especially exact: `التقدم إلى الغير بما يعمل به
مقترنا بوعظ`, advancing toward another with something to be acted upon,
accompanied by admonition.

The prepositional distinctions inside this field prevent the relation from
becoming generic transfer. `أوصيت له بشئ` gives something for a beneficiary;
`أوصيت إليه` appoints or directs toward the executor. The reciprocal branch
says that a people counsel one another, `أوصى بعضهم بعضا` or
`أوصى بعضهم إلى بعض`. The pasture branch, in which grazing suits livestock and
brings it ease, is a separate relation of fitness. The passage selects the
reciprocal verbal form and supplies its content with `بـ`. It does not name a
bequest, death, executor, pasture, or external beneficiary.

Reciprocity is consequently more precise than collective speaking. Within the
same plural group, a member can be source with respect to another and recipient
with respect to a different or later act. `بعضهم إلى بعض` supplies the
directional structure even though the individual pairs remain unexpressed.
The surface `بِٱلْحَقِّ` supplies a different role: not recipient but content.
Truth is what the reciprocal act is about or with. Collapsing `إلى` and `بـ`
would erase the difference between whom counsel reaches and what it conveys.

The field of `ح ق ق` makes that content internally rigorous. Its first branch
gives truth against falsehood, certainty, reality, and the correspondence of
belief, speech, or action to what is established. Further branches distinguish
what is obligatory or due, a specific right held against another, and dispute
in which each side claims that truth is with it. Still others concern
establishing truth, confirming speech, completing or correcting an affair,
and defending what one is bound to protect. Truth can therefore be
correspondence, obligation, possession, contested claim, establishment, and
protected responsibility. Reciprocal circulation does not make these roles
identical. It places a demanding content between speakers who can test one
another's belief, speech, action, claim, and obligation.

The material branches turn these tests into structures. A camel reaches the
age and strength at which it deserves or is able to bear a load. A straight
thrust reaches the interior without deviation. Cloth is tightly woven; speech
is firm and well composed; an affair is made sound. A joint is the meeting of
bones, a container fits its place, a door leg matches its socket, and a center
is named as the middle of head or winter. Severe travel can burden a weak
animal beyond what it can bear. Full strength or fatness marks completion, and
a horse can place its hind hoof where its fore hoof had been.

These are not illustrations that replace propositional truth. They articulate
conditions under which a shared content holds: correspondence rather than
deviation, fit rather than mismatch, firm composition rather than looseness,
completion rather than prematurity, capacity rather than overburden, and
traceable placement rather than drift. The dispute branch adds a social
warning. Saying that truth is among a group does not guarantee agreement; each
side can claim it. The preceding righteous action and the reciprocal form of
counsel place truth within a practice that can establish, correct, and protect
it rather than merely possess its name.

The return of truth from belief to counsel is the exception's deepest internal
relay. In the lexical definition of belief, an individual self assents to
truth as endpoint. In the surface counsel clause, the same exact noun becomes
definite genitive content passed reciprocally. The participant type also
changes. `النفس` in the definition is singular and inwardly oriented;
`تَوَاصَوْا۟` is plural and role-rotating. Truth moves from the relation by
which a self is aligned to the relation by which a community maintains that
alignment among its members.

Counsel also points onward. Its definition includes something `يعمل به`, to be
acted upon. That exact phrase returns to the action field, but it cannot be
used to fill the surface direct object retroactively. `ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ` is the
known object of the earlier action; whatever later act follows a particular
counsel remains unexpressed. The ordered sequence is therefore neither a
closed circle nor a loose list. Action prepares counsel with an enacted field
of goodness, and counsel opens later action without specifying its result.

## Patience keeps the exchange from breaking

The second `وَتَوَاصَوْا۟` repeats the same verb, person, number, aspect, and
reciprocal measure. Only the governed content changes, from `ٱلْحَقِّ` to
`ٱلصَّبْرِ`. Repetition makes the parallel exact, while the new complement
prevents redundancy. Truth and patience are not two labels for one act. The
same social machinery must circulate both.

The central branch of `ص ب ر` defines patience as holding the self away from
distress, `حبس النفس عن الجزع`, or holding it upon what reason and law require.
Its opposites can be distress, cowardice, or restlessness according to context.
This introduces another directed relation. Belief turns the self toward truth;
patience restrains the self from a countermovement. Truth specifies what must
remain in correspondence, while patience specifies how a self does not abandon
that orientation under pressure.

The surface grammar again transforms an inward definition into reciprocal
content. It does not say only that the group members are patient. It says they
mutually counsel one another `بِٱلصَّبْرِ`. Patience is inward in operation but
social in maintenance. Each member may need another's speech to sustain the
holding relation, and each may become the one who provides that speech. The
group's final quality is therefore neither private endurance nor external
command alone. It is reciprocal maintenance of an inward capacity.

The root field carefully distinguishes that capacity from coercion. A living
being can be confined for killing, held toward death, or forced by authority
into an oath. That is imposed restraint, not the voluntary holding of the self
away from distress. Retaliation and execution by legal requital occupy another
branch. By placing these beside the central definition, the field identifies
agency as decisive. The passage's plural group performs and exchanges counsel;
it is not grammatically the captive object of another's force.

Other branches distribute holding across relations and materials. `الصبير`
can be a guarantor or one who remains with a people in their affairs. The root
can name the side or upper edge of a thing, the borders of a vessel or grave,
rough stone, stony ground, a severe matter with no route out, and the intense
cold of winter. It can name a mountain or its middle, a clan name, and the
stopper that closes a vessel. These senses give restraint a topology: someone
who stays with, a boundary that contains, a hardness that resists, a condition
without exit, an elevated mass, and a seal at an opening.

Plant, cloud, and food branches add product and accumulation. With a different
vocalization, `الصبر` can be the bitter extract of a plant; `الصبار` can be a
sour fruit borne by a tree. A white cloud can be layered above dense cloud,
one part held over another in stages. A broad sheet can lie beneath food, and
a `صبرة` can be a pile heaped part upon part or bought without weighing and
measuring. The surface `ٱلصَّبْرِ`, with its given vocalization and syntax,
must not be replaced by extract, fruit, cloud, or pile. Those branches instead
show recurrent relations of issue, support, layering, boundary, and measure.

Two of them return exactly to the opening. Pressing in the `ع ص ر` field yields
`العصارة`, expressed liquid. The differently vocalized plant term in the
`ص ب ر` field is also `عصارة شجرة`, a tree's extract. This is a genuine lexical
contact through `عصارة`, but not an identity between the oath and the final
surface word. It establishes a pressure-product relation across the passage's
limits. The opening can invoke the interval or pressure under which an interior
is made manifest; the closing names the practiced restraint required not to
lose orientation under that pressure.

Cloud gives the relation a second shape. The opening family names cloud that
bears and releases rain. The patience family names cloud held in layers, one
part above another. Release and support are different phases of a shared
material architecture. The group needs both: truth must be passed, yet the
capacity to keep passing it must be held. The exact grammar still remains
human and reciprocal; the cloud relation supplies structure, not participants.

## Loads, routes, and bounded roles

Several exact recurrences across the lexical fields disclose secondary
architectures in the passage. They matter because they test role assignments.
They do not authorize importing their camels, clouds, hands, houses, or
merchants into the three lines.

The base `حمل` recurs in four relational contexts across three root fields. In
the opening field, clouds or winds bear rain. In the truth field, a mature
camel becomes able to have a load borne upon it; severe travel can impose what
a weak bearer cannot endure. In the patience field, `حمل شجرة` is the fruit or
yield borne by a tree. The four relations are carrying content before release,
attained capacity, overburden beyond capacity, and organic yield. Together
they refine the role of pressure. A content may be borne, a bearer may become
fit, a demand may exceed fitness, and a living source may produce. None
permits saying that the surface `بـ` means "carry" or that counsel's content is
grammatically a load.
The contact instead asks whether a shared content is supportable and whether
reciprocal patience prevents demand from becoming destructive overburden.

The hand provides another bounded circuit. Property can be extracted
`من يده` in the opening field. Workers act `بأيديهم` in the action field. The
truth field's horse places a later hoof in the position of an earlier "hand,"
the forefoot. Possession, instrument, and positional trace are distinct. The
passage moves from what can be taken out of a hand, through effective work by
hands, to a criterion of exact placement. Yet no hand is a surface participant.
The circuit clarifies action as embodied effect and truth as accountable fit.

`أمر` crosses work, truth, and patience. One can labor or exert oneself in an
affair; an affair can be established, completed, corrected, or made sound; a
severe affair can offer no exit. The sequence is operational: a matter is
worked in, brought into firmness, and endured when it cannot simply be escaped.
It makes the order of action, truth, and patience intelligible without turning
all three into the same operation. Work supplies exertion, truth supplies
establishment and correction, patience supplies persistence at the closed
limit.

Death yields a stricter contrast. Counsel can be a testament operative after
death. Coercive patience can be confinement toward death. In the first, death
is a temporal condition under which connected speech remains effective; in
the second, it is an imposed destination. The exact noun recurs, but its edge
type changes from time to endpoint. This difference prevents the testament
branch from sentimentalizing forced restraint and prevents the captivity
branch from defining reciprocal counsel.

The house behaves similarly. A maturing girl may be confined in a house in one
opening-root account; the truth family includes the expression `حق الكهول`
for a spider's house. Both are bounded structures, but one is a site of social
confinement and the other a named web-like dwelling. Neither is the
`فِى خُسْرٍ` container. They show why exact lexical recurrence alone cannot
license participant identity or syntactic substitution.

Routes provide the broadest contact. A penetrating thrust reaches an interior
without deviation. A trodden road is made by work, and travelers become "sons
of work" while walking on their legs. Connected open country leads one tract
into another. A severe matter has no route of escape. Incremental water makes
blocked food pass the throat. These relations distinguish directed arrival,
made passage, continuous terrain, closed exit, and restored passage through an
obstruction. The exception's own route is grammatical rather than geographic:
from a containing state, through four coordinated acts, to a final content.
The surrounding lexical routes clarify how much the exact prepositions matter.

The prepositional system can now be seen as a set of bounded role changes.
`فِى` encloses the human in a state. Lexical `لـ` directs assent to truth or
marks beneficiary and fitness. Lexical `إلى` directs trust or counsel toward
another and can mark improved treatment. `بـ` supplies the surface contents of
the two reciprocal acts and elsewhere can mark instrument, accompaniment,
guarantee, or refuge. `عن` marks what is kept away or what has no route of
escape. `على` can mark obligation, imposed burden, confinement toward death,
or the demand under which the self holds. Their exact uses cannot be freely
exchanged. Together they disclose a passage concerned not only with nouns and
acts but with where states, selves, contents, burdens, and other persons stand
in relation to one another.

## The ending reopens the oath

Read forward, the passage continually redistributes its center. The oath first
invokes a definite field of interval and transformation. The emphatic clause
then centers a definite singular human inside indefinite diminution. The
exception releases a plural group from being only the affected term of that
predication. Belief directs the self; righteous action gives the group an
object; reciprocal counsel distributes source and recipient roles among its
members; truth and patience give those exchanges distinct contents. The last
content restores an inward relation, but now that relation is socially kept.

Read backward, the dependencies become equally clear. Patience is needed for
the reciprocal exchange not to break under distress. Truth gives that exchange
a criterion beyond the changing claims of its speakers. Righteous deeds keep
counsel connected to enacted goodness rather than speech alone. Belief supplies
the prior assent by which truth is already an endpoint before it becomes shared
content. The four predicates together complete the relative group; the group
alone completes the exception; the exception answers the human condition in
loss; and that condition gives the oath its field of consequence.

The repeated forms support this return. The two `تَوَاصَوْا۟` clauses are
formally identical until their `بـ` complements differentiate them. The
conjunction `وَ` repeatedly extends the predicate chain, while the opening
`وَ` performs the different work of an oath particle. The final line therefore
reuses a sound and shape without flattening grammatical function. Likewise,
the definite genitives `ٱلْحَقِّ` and `ٱلصَّبْرِ` parallel one another, while
the opening definite genitive `ٱلْعَصْرِ` places the whole sequence under a
different governing relation.

The three line endings condense the design. `ٱلْعَصْرِ` is definite and
governed by oath; `خُسْرٍ` is indefinite and governed within containment;
`ٱلصَّبْرِ` is definite and governed as reciprocal content. The same final root
consonant returns through invocation, condition, and counsel, but case alone
does not make the words equivalent. The middle tanwin keeps loss unbounded;
the articles at the limits give the opening and closing fields determinate
positions.

The closing patience finally specifies one of the opening oath's latent
relations. `العصر الحبس` names restraint in the opening family;
`الصبر حبس النفس عن الجزع` names the object and direction of restraint in the
closing family. The first is a bare holding relation among many possibilities.
The second is an inward operation: the self is held away from distress. The
surface then gives that inward operation a reciprocal carrier, because the
group counsels one another with it. The ending thus returns to the opening not
by repeating its word, but by articulating its unresolved structure.

The return also preserves release. The opening field joins pressure to yield,
cloud to rain, interval to succession, confinement to refuge, withholding to
gift. The final field joins restraint to self-command, guarantee, boundary,
support, accumulation, and closure. Between them, the passage has moved truth
from assent into circulation and action from intention into a definite object.
What must be held is not held so that nothing can pass. Patience holds the
conditions under which truth and righteous action can continue to pass among
persons.

`وَٱلْعَصْرِ` can therefore be heard differently after the last word. It still
opens as an oath by its definite genitive term. It now also frames a measured
human interval in which deficit becomes visible, selves take direction, deeds
acquire objects, and responsibility is distributed through mutual speech. The
opening's pressure is answered by a yield, its succession by connection, and
its bare restraint by the self's deliberate holding away from distress. The
passage closes at `بِٱلصَّبْرِ`, but the relation it completes leads back to the
oath that first held the whole utterance open.
