**The useful lessons**

1. **Evidence completeness and discovery quality are separate.** Early v2 made
   the reader reconstruct joins between occurrences, roots, and inventories, while
   also requiring more exact reporting. Its instructions still permitted unusual
   readings, but weakened v1's emphatic discovery-first posture and changed the
   summary contract. The subsequent recovery is consistent with these costs
   mattering; it does not isolate which change caused it. Keep the restored grouped
   layout and complete evidence. Do not infer that another global prompt rewrite
   or removal of scopes will improve quality.

2. **Fuller packets also create more material to discriminate among.** The saved
   legacy 29:38 packet is 328,017 bytes; the restored packet is 937,494 bytes,
   about 2.86 times larger. Both contain the focus and 68 context ayat. Added scopes,
   glosses, and variants explain substantive differences; byte size is not token
   size or proof of overload. The optical branch was already present in the legacy
   focus inventory. Its earlier-v2 omission cannot be explained by missing that
   branch. Any packet-format ablation must preserve evidence and separately test
   presentation, language support, and scope availability.

3. **Two-stage discovery helps expose candidates, but count is a poor objective.**
   Legacy 29:38 has 15 entries. Restored one-pass Sol has 17 and Luna 22. Two-stage
   Sol has 46 and Luna 58. These are not comparable units of semantic recall:
   a single integrated v1 finding can contain a mechanism distributed across
   several later entries. Luna's 41 deltas frequently follow successive ayat or
   small context ranges. That shows coverage, but does not establish equally useful
   integration. More output also increases reporting and editorial work.

4. **Luna's latest result is better than “it still cannot find the eye-film.”**
   Its final `outlier_eye_film_path` cites س ب ل / B010 and ب ص ر and explicitly
   describes sight persisting behind an obscuring film. That recovery is already
   present in its discovery notes. However, its contextual support is 29:49's
   inward signs; it does not connect this finding to 29:41's spider. The weak-house
   finding remains separate. This is substantial partial recovery, rather than
   either a complete miss or full recovery of v1's optical-web mechanism.

5. **Integration is a distinct remaining quality target.** V1's
   `delta_socialized_obstruction` connects fabricated value (29:17), affection
   (29:25), public performance and road-cutting (29:29): the diverted become
   participants in reproducing diversion. Both latest discovery inventories retain
   related ingredients, but leave them distributed. Similarly, Luna's protected
   enclave reading retains safety misread as rightness, while v1 explicitly uses
   the shared security/believing root to explain misallocated trust. Its inward-signs
   reading is also less explicit about knowing denial. Mentioning the same ayah or
   root is not the same as retaining its explanatory relationship.

6. **Some new Sol findings are credible improvements worth evaluating.**
   Two-stage Sol retains the optical web and adds a cosmetic-kohl analogy, deeds
   wearing their own road, and intact sight with compromised steering. The latter
   connects the rudder branch to diversion despite discernment; Luna finds the
   rudder too but develops stabilization/survival more generally. These are
   exploratory candidates, not established interpretations. Sol still does not
   preserve every v1 mechanism or every prior alternative. Its result deserves a
   quality experiment rather than blanket rejection because its ledger failed.

**A concrete reliability improvement for both models**

The current response asks the model to reproduce values that are already derivable
from its evidence selection. The validator requires `source_word_indices` to
contain the entire packet occurrence group, and `trigger_roots` to equal the
non-focus roots in the activation trace. This creates avoidable consistency errors.

| Two-stage output | Independently checked raw result | Disposable in-memory diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| Sol | 245/245 branch citations resolve; three trigger-root lists disagree. | Deriving those three lists from the citations makes the response pass the response validator. |
| Luna | 262/279 branch citations resolve; 16 index-list errors, one nonoccurring root, and 36 trigger-root list disagreements. | Deriving occurrence lists and trigger-root lists removes 52 reported consistency diagnostics. Validation still fails on ر ء ي at 29:61. |

No output was repaired, exported, or accepted during this review. The diagnostic
does not certify semantic grounding, candidate preservation, or a completed job
receipt. In particular, deriving a trigger list from citations cannot supply an
uncited branch contribution that the prose actually needs.

For a future version, assign exact evidence handles to permitted occurrence/root/
branch combinations. Let the model select handles and explain their contribution.
Have an offline compiler expand the handles into the existing v1 citation fields,
derive redundant lists, and run the existing validator. Preserve all source variants
and the reader's explanation of the image used. An unknown or semantically wrong
handle must fail; the compiler must never guess another ayah or invent support.
This changes the model-facing reporting interface, while allowing the downstream
v1 ledger to remain compatible. It is more than formatting-only compaction and
should be versioned and tested as a new interface.

This is the highest-confidence engineering lesson. It reduces a demonstrated
failure class. Whether the reduced burden also improves discovery requires a
generation experiment.

**The smallest useful prompt experiment**

Keep v1's permission for competing and unusual grounded readings. Add a short
integration instruction within discovery, before notes are frozen:

> Before finalizing discovery, revisit the focus's peripheral branches against
> the context and your current candidates. Where separate observations form an
> additional supported causal chain, reversal, or material relationship, record
> that integrated mechanism and its evidence. Preserve the component discoveries
> and competing alternatives. Name the new change to the focus reading and the
> links supplied by your inference. Do not force connections or add a finding for
> every ayah or branch.

This is a proposed ablation, not a tested improvement or model-specific guarantee.
It contains no optical-web, security, or other target answer. The current ledger
follow-up explicitly says discovery is complete and forbids a new discovery round;
an integration step belongs before that boundary. Do not expect a faithful ledger
formatter to discover relationships missing from its notes.

For Luna, test the same addition with lightweight candidate output and deterministic
evidence expansion. A generic demand to “think harder,” another max setting, or
more findings does not specifically address the observed gap. All existing Luna
comparison runs already use max. A focus-root revisit targets branch activation;
the integration instruction targets connections among activations. Test these
separately if the combined experiment improves.

For v1/Sol, start with the small instruction addition and the compiler as separate
experiments. Preserve the original prompt as the control. Keep the two-stage mode
as a candidate for high-recall discovery rather than immediately making it the
production default.

**Fix inputs before interpreting model differences**

The documented 83:1 failure is upstream: QAC's مطففين does not intersect the frozen
bridge's لمطففين / للمطففين / مطفف forms under its current matching rule. The
lexical database has ten branches for ط ف ف, but the packet cannot reach them.
The legacy baseline then cites a context root to work around missing focus
evidence; early v2 instead permitted an empty baseline trace. Neither is a clean
complete-evidence comparison. Repair the bridge with explicit provenance and
ambiguity checks, test nearby clitic/inflection cases, then regenerate affected
packets. Do not globally strip affixes and assume every new match is correct.
The separate missing QAC root for ويل needs its own annotation policy.

**How to decide whether either objective has been achieved**

Use the four existing comparison ayat as development cases; they have already
influenced diagnosis. Reserve roughly 12–20 unseen ayat spanning short and long
contexts, competing senses, shared roots, social mechanisms, and remote analogies.
Use at least three independent samples per condition in an initial pilot and
expand if variation leaves the decision uncertain. Keep model profile, evidence
window, delivery method, and packet contents fixed within each ablation.

Compare original v1/Sol, restored grouped-full-packet Sol and Luna controls, then
the compiler interface and integration instruction as separate changes. Test the
combined configuration after identifying useful individual changes. This avoids
repeating early v2's confounding of input, prompt, schema, and orchestration.

Evaluate distinct mechanisms, not entry count, length, root overlap, or fluency.
Blind the reviewer to model and condition; supply packet evidence. Record ordinary
reading fidelity, useful grounded mechanism coverage, competing readings,
exploratory breadth and containment, unsupported claims, duplication, artifact
acceptance, and semantic preservation into the final ledger. Allow a grounded new
discovery to count even when v1 did not produce it. If output is later rendered
into commentary, check that the important mechanisms survive that downstream step.

A proposed operational meaning of “almost similar” is at least 90% of the
reviewer-approved valuable baseline mechanisms, no material degradation in
grounding or containment, and high acceptance after deterministic compilation.
Measure unusual discoveries separately so common readings cannot mask their loss.
The threshold is a proposal, not a measured result or an assumed user preference.

Cost should be measured per accepted, useful output, including both passes and
any review or repair. The saved two-stage calls consumed 76,828 output tokens
including reasoning for Sol and 86,429 for Luna; their one-pass counterparts
consumed 31,979 and 41,727. Luna's lower rates can still make extra passes economical:
standard input/output rates are $0.20/$1.20 per million tokens for Luna versus
$4/$20 for Sol. Caching and the >272K input pricing rule matter; the recorded Luna
ledger request is just above that threshold. These counters are not billing receipts.

and [Sol](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-sol) pages, checked
2026-09-06. Luna fine-tuning is currently unsupported, so prompt/interface work is
the immediately applicable route. General GPT-5.6 guidance also recommends testing
leaner prompts on representative tasks; its coding results do not establish HFT
gains. [Official prompting guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model?model=gpt-5.6).

My recommended next experiment is v1 discovery semantics plus deterministic
citation expansion, followed by the neutral integration ablation on Sol and Luna.
The repository evidence makes Luna a plausible candidate for approaching v1 after
these changes. It does not yet establish that it can preserve v1's most valuable
unusual mechanisms consistently.
