## The object of discovery

A useful secondary activation does not replace the primary meaning. It changes how a valid primary proposition is perceived.

Search for relations of this form:

```text
secondary branch or construction
+ exact passage trigger
+ another word, form, position, or later cue
→ a changed perception of the primary reading
```

Examples of changed perception include newly visible direction, source, recipient, force, duration, containment, interiority, instrument, standard, reversal, role reassignment, exposure, maintenance, or closure. Do not import a literal scene merely because a branch image is vivid.

The central question is:

> Which branch seems irrelevant by itself but becomes functional after another word, construction, or later cue creates a role for it?

## Primary baseline

Begin by privately establishing the strongest direct reading available without secondary branch activation. Preserve it throughout the run. This is the control against which branch-dependent synthesis will later be judged.

Do not let the baseline become the final answer.

## Progressive recitation state

Encounter the passage in order. Maintain awareness of:

- what has been activated;
- what is fading but still available;
- unresolved roles and expectations;
- participant and referent continuity;
- changes of voice, number, person, agency, and grammatical role;
- opening frames, oath scope, repetition, pauses, and closure pressure.

At each new cue, ask not only what it means locally, but what earlier material it reorganizes.

## Constructive search

Use four seed families:

1. every passage occurrence × every supplied branch of its root;
2. actual constructions and attachments in the passage;
3. morphosyntactic patterns such as voice, agency, parallelism, containment, exception, coordination, repetition, and pronoun return;
4. temporal and acoustic events such as order, ayah boundary, delayed completion, repeated form, and opening-to-closing return.

Do not generate all branch pairs in advance. Begin from singleton seeds and actual textual constructions.

For each seed:

1. Let its exact branch prose generate an initial operation or image.
2. Ask what roles that image opens: source, target, interior, container, force, medium, instrument, direction, duration, standard, resistance, maintenance, result, knower, or another passage-specific role.
3. Read the other passage-root dossiers as continuous branch-preserving prose.
4. Recruit only branches that transform, complete, oppose, reverse, or meaningfully fork the developing model.
5. Keep genuinely different models separate. Do not blend them into a broad theme.
6. Allow incomplete models to remain alive while later material may complete them.
7. Once a model becomes specific enough to make predictions, freeze its current construction.
8. Test unused passage features and later cues against those predictions.
9. Perform backward replay: state how the later cue changes the hearing of the earlier expression.
10. Continue searching from the new open roles exposed by the enlarged model.

The query must change after each discovery. Do not merely retrieve branches similar to the original seed.

## Construction and corroboration

Keep a practical distinction between:

- branches and constructions that built the model;
- later or unused evidence that the model successfully anticipated;
- evidence that narrows, resists, or defeats the model.

Do not require this distinction to be expressed as a formal ledger. Use it to prevent one vivid branch from absorbing the passage without prediction.

A branch used to build a feature cannot also count as independent support for that same feature.

## Non-local branches

Never apply this invalid rule:

```text
not the direct local gloss
→ inactive in the passage
```

A branch may be productive as a secondary root-field resonance while the primary contextual sense remains unchanged. State the boundary naturally:

```text
the word does not translate as the secondary branch,
but the branch becomes active through a specific coalition or later replay
```

Do not reject a branch until it has been tested against local form, adjacency, the whole-passage coalition, later replay, opening/closing position, and same-root alternatives.

## Passage architecture during discovery

Do not decide too early that the passage is one continuous movie or one dominant theme.

An early scene may function as:

- an enacted operation later transferred to another domain;
- a frame or global operator;
- an analogy at the level of roles and direction;
- the first phase of a reversal;
- a prediction whose completion arrives later;
- one channel among several simultaneous channels.

Preserve participant changes and grammatical discontinuities. Relational correspondence never licenses literal identity.

For an oath opening, test how the oath remains active across the whole proposition. For a vivid opening scene, abstract the operation it makes the listener experience before asking what later passage field applies, reverses, or exposes that operation.

## Short and long passages

Use the smallest architecture that the evidence requires.

- When one repeated word, pronoun, contrast, or closing construction performs the backward reactivation, keep the model compact.
- When the passage changes participant, voice, agency, domain, scale, temporal frame, or epistemic status, preserve the resulting macroblocks and test their transformations.

Do not manufacture complexity from a rich branch inventory, and do not flatten a multi-block passage into a single summary.
